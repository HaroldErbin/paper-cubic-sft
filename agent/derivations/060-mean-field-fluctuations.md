**What is answered, and relative to what (fix pass 1, critic #14).** Questions (i) and (iii) are answered here relative to the *eliminated* potential $V_{\rm eff}=S_n(\Psi,\Sigma_*(\Psi))$ — at $S_3$ the rational function $A+\tfrac{g^2}{24}u^\top K^{-1}u$ with pole divisor $\{\det K=0\}$ — not relative to the non-polynomial $S^{(n+2)}_{\rm eff}$. ## 1. Block quadratic form and the Schur identity (C1)

\subsection*{Setup}
A1–A3. Fluctuations $\Psi=\Psi_0+x$, $\Sigma=\Sigma_0+y$, $x\in\mathcal U$, $y\in\mathcal S$; $(\Psi_0,\Sigma_0)$ arbitrary (not necessarily a solution). $J_{\Psi_0}:\mathcal U\to\mathcal S$, $J_{\Psi_0}x:=\Psi_0\odot x$ (so $\delta P=2J_{\Psi_0}x$; $J_{\Psi_0}x\in\mathcal S$ for Veronese-closed $\mathcal S$). Operators are written as bilinear forms; $V_4$ on $\mathcal S$ is the matrix $A$ of 057 when truncated.

\subsection*{Derivation: the block form}
$$
S_n(\Psi_0+x,\Sigma_0+y)=S_n(\Psi_0,\Sigma_0)+(\text{linear})+\tfrac12x^\top H_{\Psi\Psi}x+y^\top H_{\Sigma\Psi}x+\tfrac12y^\top H_{\Sigma\Sigma}y+O(3),
\tag{1.1}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{\Psi\Psi}&=V_2+g\,V_3(\Psi_0,\cdot,\cdot)+\tfrac{g^2}{6}\,V_4(\Sigma_0,\cdot,\cdot), &&\text{(1.2)}\\
H_{\Sigma\Psi}(y,x)&=\tfrac{g^2}{6}\,V_4(y,\Psi_0\odot x)+\tfrac{g^3}{60}\,V_5(y,\Sigma_0,x), &&\text{(1.3)}\\
H_{\Sigma\Sigma}&=-\tfrac{g^2}{12}\Big[V_4-\tfrac g5V_5(\cdot,\cdot,\Psi_0)-\tfrac{g^2}{10}W_6(\Sigma_0,\cdot,\cdot)\Big]=:-\tfrac{g^2}{12}\,\mathcal K(\Psi_0,\Sigma_0), &&\text{(1.4)}
\end{aligned}
$$
with the $V_5$ terms present from $S_3$ on and the $W_6$ term at $S_4$. At $S_3$, $\mathcal K=K_{\Psi_0}=V_4-\tfrac g5V_5(\cdot,\cdot,\Psi_0)$ (R17's dressed kernel, independent of $\Sigma_0$); at $S_4$ and $\Sigma_0=P_0$, $\mathcal K=K_6$ of R19. The Schur complement over the auxiliary block (exists iff $\mathcal K$ invertible):
$$
H_{\rm eff}(\Psi_0,\Sigma_0):=H_{\Psi\Psi}-H_{\Sigma\Psi}^\top H_{\Sigma\Sigma}^{-1}H_{\Sigma\Psi}
=H_{\Psi\Psi}+\tfrac{12}{g^2}\,H_{\Sigma\Psi}^\top\mathcal K^{-1}H_{\Sigma\Psi}.
\tag{1.5}
$$

\subsection*{Derivation: the Schur identity on the saddle}
Let $E(\Psi,\Sigma):=\partial_\Sigma S_n$ and let $\Sigma_*(\Psi)$ solve $E(\Psi,\Sigma_*(\Psi))=0$ near $\Psi_0$ with $H_{\Sigma\Sigma}$ invertible there. Differentiating the identity $E(\Psi,\Sigma_*(\Psi))=0$ and $V_{\rm eff}(\Psi)=S_n(\Psi,\Sigma_*(\Psi))$ twice:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma_*'&=-H_{\Sigma\Sigma}^{-1}H_{\Sigma\Psi}, &&\text{(1.6)}\\
V_{\rm eff}'&=\partial_\Psi S_n+E\cdot\Sigma_*'=\partial_\Psi S_n\big\vert_{\Sigma_*}, &&\text{(1.7)}\\
V_{\rm eff}''&=H_{\Psi\Psi}+H_{\Sigma\Psi}^\top\Sigma_*'=H_{\Psi\Psi}-H_{\Sigma\Psi}^\top H_{\Sigma\Sigma}^{-1}H_{\Sigma\Psi}=H_{\rm eff}(\Psi_0,\Sigma_*(\Psi_0)). &&\text{(1.8)}
\end{aligned}
$$
This is exact, for every $S_n$ and every background $\Psi_0$ at which $\mathcal K(\Psi_0,\Sigma_*(\Psi_0))$ is invertible, on or off the $\Psi$-shell, on every smooth branch $\Sigma_*$ of the $\Sigma$-equation. At $S_2$ and $S_3$ the $\Sigma$-equation is linear and the branch is unique where $\mathcal K$ is invertible. At $S_4$ it is quadratic (057 C2.9, fold): (1.8) holds on any sheet (up to $2^{\dim\mathcal S}$ by Bézout) away from the fold locus $\det\mathcal K=0$, and (1.11) below is the statement on the perturbative sheet (the one `saddle_series` constructs, starting from $\Sigma=0$). The comparison with the non-polynomial theory is then the comparison of $V_{\rm eff}$ with $S^{(n+2)}_{\rm eff}$, known from R1, R17, R19, R61:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_2:&\quad H_{\rm eff}=\mathrm{Hess}\,S^{(4)}_{\rm eff}(\Psi_0)\ \text{exactly}; &&\text{(1.9)}\\
S_3:&\quad H_{\rm eff}=\mathrm{Hess}\,S^{(5)}_{\rm eff}(\Psi_0)+\tfrac{g^4}{600}\,\mathrm{Hess}\big(vV_4^{-1}v\big)(\Psi_0)+O(g^5); &&\text{(1.10)}\\
S_4:&\quad H_{\rm eff}=\mathrm{Hess}\,S^{(6)}_{\rm eff}(\Psi_0)+O(g^5)\quad(\text{perturbative sheet; untruncated, or }\mathcal S=\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal U\text{ with }W_6^{(\mathcal S)}), &&\text{(1.11)}\\
&\quad H_{\rm eff}=\mathrm{Hess}\,S^{(6)}_{\rm eff}\vert_{\mathcal U}(\Psi_0)-\tfrac{g^4}{600}\,\mathrm{Hess}\big(w^\top M^{-1}w\big)(\Psi_0)+O(g^5)\quad(\text{full-}\mathcal C\text{ vertex in a truncation}). &&\text{(1.12)}
\end{aligned}
$$
*Explicit form at $S_2$.* With $\Sigma_0=P_0$, $H_{\Sigma\Sigma}=-\tfrac{g^2}{12}V_4$ and $H_{\Sigma\Psi}=\tfrac{g^2}6V_4J_{\Psi_0}$, the Schur term is $\tfrac{12}{g^2}\tfrac{g^4}{36}J^\top V_4J=\tfrac{g^2}3V_4(\Psi_0\odot x,\Psi_0\odot x)=\tfrac{g^2}{3}V_4(P_0,x,x)$, so
$$
H_{\rm eff}=V_2+gV_3(\Psi_0,\cdot,\cdot)+\underbrace{\tfrac{g^2}6V_4(P_0,\cdot,\cdot)}_{\Psi\text{-block (mean field)}}+\underbrace{\tfrac{g^2}3V_4(P_0,\cdot,\cdot)}_{\delta\Sigma\text{ exchange}}=V_2+gV_3(\Psi_0)+\tfrac{g^2}2V_4(P_0,\cdot,\cdot). \tag{1.13}
$$
The quartic contact $\tfrac{g^2}{4!}V_4(\Psi^4)$ has three channels (pairings of four legs into two pairs); fixing two legs on $\Psi_0$ and two on the fluctuation, one channel pairs $(\Psi_0\Psi_0)(xx)$ — carried by the classical auxiliary in the $\Psi$-block — and two pair $(\Psi_0x)(\Psi_0x)$ — carried by the exchange of $\delta\Sigma$. By total symmetry of $V_4$ the three are equal: $1/3+2/3$. This requires $\Psi_0\odot x\in\mathcal S$ for all $x\in\mathcal U$, i.e. Veronese closure (057 C1.1).

*At $S_3$.* $H_{\Sigma\Sigma}=-\tfrac{g^2}{12}K_{\Psi_0}$ is independent of $\Sigma_0$: the dressed kernel is the propagator of the $\Sigma$-fluctuation. $H_{\Sigma\Psi}=\tfrac{g^2}6[V_4J_{\Psi_0}+\tfrac g{10}V_5(\cdot,\Sigma_0,\cdot)]$ and $H_{\rm eff}=H_{\Psi\Psi}+\tfrac{g^2}3[V_4J+\tfrac g{10}V_5(\Sigma_0)]^\top K_{\Psi_0}^{-1}[V_4J+\tfrac g{10}V_5(\Sigma_0)]$. The $\tfrac g5$ of $K_{\Psi_0}$ is what makes the $O(g^3)$ coefficient equal to $\tfrac{g^3}{6}V_5(\Psi_0^3,\cdot,\cdot)$ (the break leg with $\tfrac g4$ fails at $g^3$); the $O(g^4)$ remainder is the Hessian of R17's feedback.

*The joint form (fix pass 1, critic #5).* The Schur identity is a statement about the Schur complement, not about the spectrum of the joint Hessian $H$ on $(\delta\Psi,\delta\Sigma)$, which carries the extra auxiliary block. Completing the square in $y$ at any point where $\mathcal K$ is invertible, with $y':=y+H_{\Sigma\Sigma}^{-1}H_{\Sigma\Psi}x$ ($=y-\Sigma_*'x$ on the saddle, the linearisation of $\Sigma\to\Sigma-\Sigma_*(\Psi)$):
$$
x^\top H_{\Psi\Psi}x+2y^\top H_{\Sigma\Psi}x+y^\top H_{\Sigma\Sigma}y=x^\top H_{\rm eff}\,x+y'^\top H_{\Sigma\Sigma}\,y',\qquad H=L^\top\big(H_{\rm eff}\oplus(-\tfrac{g^2}{12}\mathcal K)\big)L,\quad L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\H_{\Sigma\Sigma}^{-1}H_{\Sigma\Psi}&1\end{pmatrix}. \tag{1.15}
$$
$L$ is unipotent ($\det L=1$), so $H$ is congruent to $H_{\rm eff}\oplus(-\tfrac{g^2}{12}\mathcal K)$: by Sylvester's law (Haynsworth's inertia additivity) $\mathrm{In}(H)=\mathrm{In}(H_{\rm eff})+\mathrm{In}(-\tfrac{g^2}{12}\mathcal K)$, and $\det H=\det(-\tfrac{g^2}{12}\mathcal K)\,\det H_{\rm eff}$. On the saddle $H_{\rm eff}=\mathrm{Hess}\,V_{\rm eff}$ (1.8). $L$ is block-unipotent and invertible wherever it is defined, i.e. iff $\mathcal K$ is invertible, so the zero modes of $H$ are exactly those of $H_{\rm eff}$ (of $\mathrm{Hess}\,V_{\rm eff}$ on the saddle). The auxiliary summand is an invertible quadratic form with no kinetic operator (an algebraic, non-propagating sector); its inertia is that of $-\mathcal K$ (the opposite-sign form of PH15), and its determinant depends on $\Psi_0$ from $S_3$ on — the one-loop factor $\det\mathcal K$ of `## Next`. Where $\mathcal K$ is singular but $u\in\operatorname{im}K$ (C3's locus, critic #13), the Schur complement does not exist, the joint Hessian has $(0,n)$ as a null vector of the $\Sigma$ block only (C3.3), and $V_{\rm eff}$ is a direction-dependent limit (3.3): $\Sigma$ is not algebraically eliminable there.

\subsection*{Derivation: off the saddle}
For $S$ quadratic in $\Sigma$ ($S_2$, $S_3$) the square completes exactly (R17's exact rewriting): $S=V_{\rm eff}(\Psi)+\tfrac12(\Sigma-\Sigma_*)^\top H(\Psi)(\Sigma-\Sigma_*)$ with $H=H_{\Sigma\Sigma}(\Psi)$ affine in $\Psi$. With $\Delta:=\Sigma_0-\Sigma_*(\Psi_0)$, second derivatives at $(\Psi_0,\Sigma_0)$: $S_{\Sigma\Sigma}=H$, $S_{\Sigma\Psi}=-H\Sigma_*'+H'\Delta$, $S_{\Psi\Psi}=V_{\rm eff}''+\Sigma_*'^\top H\Sigma_*'-2\Delta^\top H'\Sigma_*'-\Delta^\top H\Sigma_*''$ (using $H''=0$). The cross terms cancel in the Schur complement:
$$
H_{\rm eff}(\Psi_0,\Sigma_0)-\mathrm{Hess}\,V_{\rm eff}(\Psi_0)=-\big\langle H\Delta,\Sigma_*''\big\rangle-(H'\Delta)^\top H^{-1}(H'\Delta),\qquad H\Delta=\partial_\Sigma S(\Psi_0,\Sigma_0). \tag{1.14}
$$
The first term is linear in the $\Sigma$-EOM residual, the second quadratic in $\Delta$ and present only at $S_3$ ($H'=-\tfrac{g^3}{60}V_5$). At $S_2$ ($H'=0$, $\Sigma_*''=2\,x\otimes x$): $H_{\rm eff}-\mathrm{Hess}\,S^{(4)}_{\rm eff}=\tfrac{g^2}6V_4(\Sigma_0-P_0,\cdot,\cdot)$. At $S_4$ ($S$ cubic in $\Sigma$) the difference is again $O(\Delta)$ with coefficient $\partial_\Sigma$ of the Schur complement; not written in closed form here.

## 2. The auxiliary as a BRST deformation (C2)

\subsection*{Setup}
A1–A3, A4. Shifted operator of the non-polynomial theory at $\Psi_0$ (Zwiebach 1993 (4.112); in our normalisation, Conventions):

Q_{\Psi_0}=Q+g\,\ell_2(\Psi_0,\cdot)+\tfrac{g^2}2\,\ell_3(\Psi_0,\Psi_0,\cdot)+\tfrac{g^3}{3!}\ell_4(\Psi_0^3,\cdot)+\dots,\qquad \omega(x,Q_{\Psi_0}y)=\mathrm{Hess}\,S_{\rm eff}(\Psi_0)(x,y)\ \text{on fields}. \tag{2.1}

At $\Sigma_0=P_0$ the $\Sigma_0$ term reproduces **one third** of the $\ell_3$ part of $Q_{\Psi_0}$, not all of it (the brief anticipated all of it; the factor fixed from R1 is $\tfrac{g^2}6$ against the $\tfrac{g^2}2$ that $\ell_3$ carries). and at $S_3$ the $\ell_4$ part $\tfrac{g^3}{6}\ell_4(\Psi_0^3,\cdot)$ splits as $\tfrac15$ in the $\Psi$-block — through the $O(g)$ shift $\Sigma_*=P_0+\tfrac g5V_4^{-1}v+\dots$, $\tfrac{g^2}6V_4(\tfrac g5V_4^{-1}v,x,x)=\tfrac{g^3}{30}V_5(\Psi_0^3,x,x)$ — and $\tfrac45$ in the exchange ($\tfrac{2g^3}{15}V_5(\Psi_0^3,x,x)$; the $V_5$ coupling is linear in $\Psi$ and has no $\Psi\Psi$ entry of its own). $$
For $c=1$ and $\Sigma_0\neq P_0$ this is not an operator the cubic theory produces (critic #12): R1's block is $c=\tfrac13$, and nothing in $S_2$ or $S_3$ generates $c=1$ with $\Sigma_0\neq P_0$. Statements about it concern a candidate "mean-field BRST operator" that no construction proposes, not a property of the theory.
Then, exactly,

None of these terms is proportional to an equation of motion. At a Maurer–Cartan point ($Q\Psi_0=O(g)$, so $Q^{(2)}P_0=2\,Q\Psi_0\odot\Psi_0=O(g)$) the relation gives the structural form (critic #7)
$$

the Jacobiator of $\ell_2$ on $\Psi_0$ (signs modulo the grading). ## 3. The degenerate branch (C3)

\subsection*{Setup}
A1–A3, $S_3$ restricted to $(\mathcal U,\mathcal S)$, $\mathcal S=\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal U$, $A=V_4\vert_{\mathcal S}$ invertible. $K_\Psi=A-\tfrac g5W(\Psi)$, $W(\Psi)=V_5(\cdot,\cdot,\Psi)\vert_{\mathcal S}$, $u(\Psi)=V_4(\cdot,P)\vert_{\mathcal S}$ (057 §3). Scaled variables $\tau=g\Psi$, $s=g^2\Sigma$ (057 C2.8).

\subsection*{Derivation}
*The $\Sigma$-EOM and its solution set.* $\partial_\Sigma S_3=-\tfrac{g^2}{12}\,[K_\Psi\Sigma-u(\Psi)]$ (057 (2.4), general form). For fixed $\Psi_0$ the solution set $F_{\Psi_0}=\{\Sigma:K_{\Psi_0}\Sigma=u(\Psi_0)\}$ is
$$
F_{\Psi_0}=\begin{cases}\{K_{\Psi_0}^{-1}u\} & \det K_{\Psi_0}\neq0,\\ \emptyset & \det K_{\Psi_0}=0,\ u\notin\operatorname{im}K_{\Psi_0},\\ \Sigma_p+\ker K_{\Psi_0} & \det K_{\Psi_0}=0,\ u\in\operatorname{im}K_{\Psi_0}.\end{cases}\tag{3.1}
$$
Cases exhaustive: $K$ invertible or not; if not, $u$ in its image or not. For $K$ symmetric of corank one with null vector $n$, $u\in\operatorname{im}K\iff n\cdot u=0\iff\operatorname{adj}(K)u=0$ ($\operatorname{adj}K=c\,nn^\top$, $c\neq0$). The **degenerate locus** $\mathcal B=\{\Psi:\det K_\Psi=0,\ n\cdot u=0\}$ is cut out by two equations, of expected codimension 2 in $\mathcal U$ (shown for $\dim\mathcal U=2$; codimension exactly 2 needs $n\cdot u\not\equiv0$ on every component of $\{\det K=0\}$); over it $F_\Psi$ is the line $\Sigma_p+\lambda n$, $\Sigma_p=(K+nn^\top)^{-1}u$. The degenerate branch $\{(\Psi,\Sigma):\Psi\in\mathcal B,\Sigma\in F_\Psi\}$ has dimension $\dim\mathcal U-2+1=\dim\mathcal U-1$ (for corank one; a line over each point of $\mathcal B$).

*Order in $g$.* $\det K_\Psi=\det(A-\tfrac15W(\tau))$ and $u(\Psi)=g^{-2}u(\tau)$: $\mathcal B$ is a fixed set in $\tau$, i.e. $\Psi=O(1/g)$, $\Sigma=O(1/g^2)$, $S_3=g^{-2}\,\hat S_3(\tau,s)$. The branch is non-perturbative, at the scale of the tachyon vacuum, the pole and the fold of 057.

*The action on the branch.* $S_3$ is exactly quadratic in $\Sigma$; along $\Sigma_p+\lambda n$: $S_3=S_3(\Psi_0,\Sigma_p)+\lambda\,n\cdot\partial_\Sigma S_3+\tfrac{\lambda^2}2\,n^\top H_{\Sigma\Sigma}n=S_3(\Psi_0,\Sigma_p)$ (the first vanishes by the EOM, the second since $H_{\Sigma\Sigma}n=-\tfrac{g^2}{12}K n=0$). Value:
$$
S_3\big\vert_{\rm branch}=A(\Psi_0)+\tfrac{g^2}{24}\,u^\top K^+u,\qquad K^+=(K+nn^\top)^{-1}\ \text{on}\ n^\perp. \tag{3.2}
$$
$n$ is a flat direction of the auxiliary: the $\Sigma$-block of the Hessian is degenerate along it (C3.3), so $\Sigma$ is not algebraically eliminable there — the auxiliary "propagates" in exactly one direction. It is not a null direction of the full Hessian: $H_{\Psi\Sigma}n=\tfrac{g^2}{12}\partial_\Psi(K_\Psi\Sigma-u)\cdot n\neq0$ generically.

*Blow-up.* $V_{\rm eff}=A+\tfrac{g^2}{24}u^\top K^{-1}u$ is a rational function whose polar divisor is $\{\det K=0\}$; $\mathcal B$ is where its numerator's pole part $(n\cdot u)^2$ vanishes too — a base point (point of indeterminacy) of the rational map $\Sigma_*=K^{-1}u$. Along $\Psi_0+t\hat d$, with $K=K_0+tK_1+\dots$, $u=u_0+tu_1+\dots$, first-order perturbation of the small eigenvalue and of its eigenvector gives
$$
\lim_{t\to0}\Sigma_*(\Psi_0+t\hat d)=\Sigma_p+\lambda(\hat d)\,n,\qquad \lambda(\hat d)=\frac{n\cdot(u_1-K_1\Sigma_p)}{n^\top K_1n}, \tag{3.3}
$$
a ratio of two linear forms in $\hat d$: as $\hat d$ turns, $\lambda$ sweeps the whole line. So the degenerate fibre is the exceptional line of the graph closure of $\Sigma_*$ over the base point, and $V_{\rm eff}\to(3.2)$ along every direction with $n^\top K_1n\neq0$. The branch is **not** disconnected from the eliminated theory: every point of it is a limit of perturbative-branch configurations $(\Psi,\Sigma_*(\Psi))$.

*Full EOM on the branch.* The $\Psi$-EOM at $(\Psi_0,\Sigma_p+\lambda n)$ is $E(\lambda)=E_0+\lambda E_1+\lambda^2E_2$ ($\dim\mathcal U$ components; quadratic through $\tfrac{g^3}{5!}V_5(\Sigma,\Sigma,\Psi)$). Unknowns: $\Psi_0\in\mathcal B$ ($\dim\mathcal U-2$) and $\lambda$ (1); equations $\dim\mathcal U$. Overdetermined by one for every $\dim\mathcal U$: generically **no** joint critical point lies on the branch (a theorem, *Overdetermination* below); it is a family of solutions of the $\Sigma$-EOM only. $V_2,V_3$ enter the $\Psi$-EOM and not the $\Sigma$-EOM, so the locus is fixed while one combination of $V_2$ is tuned: on the wall in coupling space (codimension one in the instance) a joint critical point sits on the branch, at an isolated $\lambda_*$. It is nondegenerate (full Hessian invertible, C3.4-tuned), so it persists under a perturbation of the couplings off the wall — as an ordinary critical point with $\det K\neq0$ and $\Sigma=K^{-1}u$, at distance $O(\epsilon)$ (C3.4-continuation). The degenerate branch carries no critical points of its own: those on it are the wall-crossings of regular critical points through a base point.

*Overdetermination (theorem; fix pass 1, critic #8).* Fix $(V_3,V_4,V_5)$ such that $\mathcal B$ has codimension 2 and corank one (the theorem's hypothesis; C3.1: shown for $\dim\mathcal U=2$, expected for $\dim\mathcal U\ge3$; $V_3$ genericity is not used, any $V_3$ works); $\mathcal B$, $\Sigma_p$ and $n$ are then fixed, since the $\Sigma$-EOM involves $V_4,V_5$ only. $V_2$ enters $S_3$ only through $\tfrac12V_2(\Psi^2)$, so on the branch the $\Psi$-EOM is
$$
E(\Psi_0,\lambda;V_2)=V_2\Psi_0+E'(\Psi_0,\lambda;V_3,V_4,V_5). \tag{3.4}
$$
(i) $\Psi_0\neq0$ on $\mathcal B$: at $\Psi_0=0$, $K=A$ is invertible (A2). (ii) For $\Psi_0\neq0$ the linear map $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal U^*\to\mathcal U$, $V_2\mapsto V_2\Psi_0$, is onto: for any $w\in\mathcal U$, $V_2=(w\otimes\Psi_0+\Psi_0\otimes w)/|\Psi_0|^2-(w\cdot\Psi_0)\,\Psi_0\otimes\Psi_0/|\Psi_0|^4$ is symmetric and $V_2\Psi_0=w$. (iii) Hence for each branch point $b=(\Psi_0,\lambda)$ the set $\{V_2:E(b;V_2)=0\}$ is an affine subspace of $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal U^*$ of codimension $\dim\mathcal U$ (never empty). (iv) The incidence set $Z=\{(b,V_2):b\text{ on the branch},\ E(b;V_2)=0\}$ is an affine bundle over the branch, of dimension $\le(\dim\mathcal U-1)+(\dim\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal U-\dim\mathcal U)=\dim\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal U-1$. Its projection to $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal U^*$ — the set of $V_2$ admitting a joint critical point on the branch — is semi-algebraic (Tarski–Seidenberg) of dimension $\le\dim\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal U-1$, hence contained in a proper algebraic subset, its Zariski closure: the wall. For fixed $(V_3,V_4,V_5)$ with $\mathcal B$ of codimension 2 and corank one, and $V_2$ off the wall, no joint critical point lies on the branch. This makes "overdetermined by one" a proven obstruction within the class; the genericity it uses is that of $V_2$ (Scope). The instance (C3.4-tuned) shows the wall is non-empty, of codimension one there.

- (3.1): only $S_3$ is treated. At $S_2$, $K=V_4$ is constant and invertible (A2): no branch. - (3.3): $n(t)=n-tK_0^+K_1n+O(t^2)$, $\kappa(t)=t\,n^\top K_1n+O(t^2)$, $n(t)\cdot u(t)=t\,n\cdot(u_1-K_1\Sigma_p)+O(t^2)$ using $n\cdot u_0=0$ and $K_0\Sigma_p=u_0$.
