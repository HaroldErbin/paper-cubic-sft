## §1 Candidate 1 – the constant auxiliary direction of R68 (C1)

\subsection*{Setup}

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(1.1) The direction is not null, at either $\beta$.* From 064 Table 1.1,
$$Ae_2=\begin{pmatrix}h_1\\h_2\\h_3\end{pmatrix},\qquad\det A\ne0\ \text{(companion value, not reproduced here)},\tag{1.1}$$

*(1.2) Robustness against the data caveat.* $h_1$, $h_3$ are weight-scheme values with a section band (A3). *(1.3) From the block to $p_{2,2}$.* The block is not a closed sector (A3), so a null vector of the block would not be a null vector of $p_{2,2}$; the converse direction is what a negative verdict needs and holds without closedness: $N\in\ker p_{2,2}$ means $V_4(N,A')=0$ for *every* $A'$ (`eq:hs4:kernel`, 104 (1.3)), and $V_4(e_2,e_2)=h_2\ne0$ is one such pairing. ## §2 Candidate 3 – the ghost dilaton (C2)

\subsection*{Setup}
A1, A3–A5. $D=(c_1c_{-1}-\bar c_1\bar c_{-1})\vert0\rangle$ the zero-momentum ghost dilaton, $T=c_1\bar c_1\vert0;k\rangle$ at $k=0$ unless a momentum is written; in the $\vert\downarrow\downarrow\rangle=c_1\bar c_1\vert0\rangle$ language, $D=-(c_{-1}\bar b_{-1}-\bar c_{-1}b_{-1})\vert\downarrow\downarrow\rangle$, a sum of two terms carrying holomorphic/antiholomorphic ghost numbers $(+1,-1)$ and $(-1,+1)$ relative to $\vert\downarrow\downarrow\rangle$. Pairs built on $D$ in the space of $\Sigma_2$ (A4): $D\odot\Phi$ with $\Phi\in\mathcal H_0$ at any momentum; by momentum conservation the pair has total momentum $k_\Phi$, so the family $\{D(0)\odot\Phi(P)\}_P$ exists at *every* $P$ – the one candidate of the brief that could realise the first horn (type (a)) directly, which is why it is taken second.

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(2.1) Partners in $\{T,D\}$ at all-zero momenta.* A pair $N=a\,D\odot T+b\,D\odot D=a\,e_2+b\,e_3$ pairs with $e_1,e_2,e_3$ as
$$V_4(N,e_j)=a\,A_{2j}+b\,A_{3j}:\qquad\begin{pmatrix}h_1&h_2\\h_2&h_3\\h_3&h_4\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}=0\iff a=b=0,\tag{2.1}$$
because the two columns are columns of the non-singular $A$ (1.1), hence independent; with $h_1=h_3=0$ the system reads $bh_2=0$, $ah_2=0$, $bh_4=0$, again $a=b=0$. [check: `C2.1-dilaton-pairs`, `C2.1-dilaton-pairs-h1h3zero`; break `C2.1-break`: rank drops to $1$ when $h_1=h_2=h_3=0$.] So no pair with a dilaton strand and a $\{T,D\}$ partner is null at all-zero momenta (sense (ii)), by (1.3)'s one-sided argument.

*(2.2) Plane-wave pairs $D(0)\odot\Phi(P)$ at $P\ne0$.* Take $\Phi=T(P)$ and the partner $T(k_3)\odot D(-P-k_3)$ at total momentum $-P$:
$$\phi(P,k_3):=V_4\bigl(D(0),T(P),T(k_3),D(-P-k_3)\bigr),\qquad\phi(0,0)=h_2\ne0.\tag{2.2}$$
$\phi$ is an entire function of $(P,k_3)\in\mathbb C^{26}\times\mathbb C^{26}$ (104 C2.2: the level-$\le1$ kernel is a finite matrix of integrals over the compact region of $e^{-\mathcal Q}\Pi^{(1)}$, entire in all momenta jointly; a plane-wave matrix element is that kernel at a point). The pair $D(0)\odot T(P)$ is null in sense (ii) only if $\phi(P,k_3)=0$ for all $k_3$ (and all other partners); the set $Z_\phi:=\{P\in\mathbb C^{26}:\phi(P,\cdot)\equiv0\}$ is an analytic subset (an intersection of zero sets of entire functions of $P$), proper because $0\notin Z_\phi$, so it has empty interior in $\mathbb C^{26}$ and its trace on real momenta is a closed null set (the real-analytic function $P\mapsto\phi(P,0)$ on $\mathbb R^{26}$ is not identically zero). By continuity, $\phi(P,k_3)\ne0$ on a real neighbourhood of $(0,0)$: near $P=0$ the pair is non-null outright. The same with $\Phi=D(P)$ and partner $T\odot T$: $\psi(P,k_3)=V_4(D(0),D(P),T(k_3),T(-P-k_3))$, $\psi(0,0)=h_2$. Hence **the family $D(0)\odot\Phi(P)$ does not realise the first horn**: it is non-null at every $P$ off a closed null set, and at every $P$ near $0$. Whether it is null at some isolated real $P$ is not decided (it would need the full partner space, not only $\{T,D\}$ partners, and momentum-dependent data); such a point would be an accidental zero, not a forced one.

*(2.3) Why the dilaton forces nothing: the theorem and the counting.* (a) $D=Q_B\chi$ with $\chi=c_0^-\vert0\rangle$: $\{Q_B,c_0\}\vert0\rangle=(c\partial c)_0\vert0\rangle=-2c_{-1}c_1\vert0\rangle=2c_1c_{-1}\vert0\rangle$, so $Q_B(c_0-\bar c_0)\vert0\rangle=2D$. $\chi$ violates $b_0^-\chi=0$, so it is outside the space on which the vertices and the cyclic $L_\infty$ relations of A5 are defined; the ghost-dilaton theorem (Bergman–Zwiebach 1994; Rahman–Zwiebach 1995) is exactly the evaluation of $\{Q_B\chi,\Phi_1,\dots,\Phi_n\}$ with that boundary term kept: the dilaton insertion into the *full* set of diagrams is the coupling derivative $g_s\partial_{g_s}$, a relation between vertices of adjacent order, not a zero of any vertex – 034 §4 (vii) said so and left the zero-momentum block open for lack of data; (2.1) closes it at level (1,1). (b) The cubic zeros of 064 Table 1.1, $V_3(TTD)=V_3(DDD)=0$ with $V_3(TDD)=\beta^2/2\ne0$, are holomorphic ghost-number counting on the sphere, not a parity: with every strand contributing $(1,1)$ through $c_1\bar c_1$ and each $D$ term $(\pm1,\mp1)$, a cubic vertex (no moduli, no $b$ insertions) needs $3+\sum_i\epsilon_i=3$, i.e. an even number of $D$ legs with $\sum\epsilon_i=0$ – $TTD$ and $DDD$ vanish, $TDD$ survives. At $n=4$ the two real moduli bring $b(v_1)b(v_2)$, each $b(v)=\oint(vb+\bar v\bar b)$ carrying $(-1,0)$ or $(0,-1)$; the condition becomes
$$\textstyle\sum_i\epsilon_i=m-1,\qquad m\in\{0,1,2\}\ \text{the number of holomorphic }b\text{'s},\tag{2.3}$$
solvable for any number of $D$ legs ($T^3D$: $m\in\{0,2\}$; $T^2D^2$, $T^4$, $D^4$: $m=1$ or $m\in\{0,2\}$). The counting that produces the cubic zeros produces none at $n\ge4$, consistent with $h_1,h_3\ne0$ in the data; it is a grading of the vertex by sectors, never a vanishing row.

\subsection*{Commentary}

\subsection*{Result}

## §3 Candidate 2 – zero total momentum and R88 (C3)

\subsection*{Setup}
A2–A4. R88 (Proven, cited): at genus zero and **zero momentum on every leg** (the $p=0$ Fock space), a vertex $V_n$ vanishes when exactly one leg has its matter part outside the matter identity (universal) Virasoro module, whatever the ghost parts; the kinetic term obeys the same rule; at $k\ne0$ the same couplings are $O(k^2)$. Write $\mathcal U\subset\mathcal H_0$ for the universal strands (matter part in the identity module: $T$, $D$, the ghost-oscillator states), $\mathcal U^\perp$ for the strands with a matter oscillator (the trace $x=\eta_{\mu\nu}\alpha^\mu_{-1}\bar\alpha^\nu_{-1}c_1\bar c_1\vert0\rangle$, the traceless graviton, the $B$-field, …), both at $k=0$, and grade the all-zero-momentum pair space by the number of outside strands:
$$S_0=\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal U,\qquad S_1=\mathcal U\otimes\mathcal U^\perp,\qquad S_2=\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal U^\perp.$$
The brief's candidate is a pair of $S_1$ (exactly one strand outside).

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(3.0) Sense (i): R88 constrains a null set of the fibre.* At total momentum $P=0$ the strands of a pair carry $\pm q$ and the partners $\pm q'$ (104 (1.2)). R88's hypothesis – every leg at zero momentum – holds for the matrix element $K_0(q',q)$ only at $q=q'=0$. For $q'\ne0$ the partner legs are not in the $p=0$ Fock space and the rule says nothing (its scope line: the couplings are $O(k^2)$, not zero). A null function in the sense of 104 (1.3) must satisfy $\int K_0(q',q)\sigma(q)\,d^{26}q=0$ for a.e. $q'$; a constraint on the integrand at one point of $q$ for one value of $q'$ is empty for that equation. **In the $L^2$ fibre sense R88 forces nothing at any level.**

*(3.1) Sense (ii): the all-zero-momentum sector and its block form.* On $S_0\oplus S_1\oplus S_2$ (every strand at $k=0$) the rule gives, for the symmetric form $V_4$,
$$V_4\big\vert_{S_0\oplus S_1\oplus S_2}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{00}&0&A_{02}\\0&A_{11}&A_{12}\\A_{20}&A_{21}&A_{22}\end{pmatrix},\tag{3.1}$$
with the single forced block $V_4(S_0,S_1)=0$ (one outside leg among four); $A_{02}$ (two outside legs), $A_{11}$ (two), $A_{12}$ (three), $A_{22}$ (four) are unconstrained by the rule, and $A_{02}\ne0$ at level (1,1) is the sister's statement that $T\otimes T$ and $D\otimes D$ couple to the contracted matter pairs with a momentum-independent coefficient (A3). A selection rule forces a null vector only through a vanishing *row* – a sector that pairs to zero with everything (034 §4, general remark: "a selection rule produces a kernel only if some sector has no partner, or if the block is itself degenerate"). No row of (3.1) vanishes: an $N\in S_1$ is null iff $A_{11}N=0$ and $A_{21}N=0$, two conditions on data the rule does not touch. The same holds for a pair $D(0)\odot x$ (C2's leftover): it lies in $S_1$ and pairs with $S_1\oplus S_2$ through $A_{11},A_{21}$.

*(3.2) Lorentz covariance adds block-diagonality, not zeros.* At all-zero momenta the vertex is invariant under the Lorentz group (Euclidean $O(26)$ in the Wick-rotated oscillator indices, A2, parity included: the bosonic vertices contain no $\epsilon$ tensor; for $SO(26)$ alone the two chiral 13-form irreps, a complex-conjugate pair since $26\equiv2\pmod4$, would need separate care). Decomposing $S_0\oplus S_1\oplus S_2$ into isotypic components $\rho\otimes\mathbb C^{m_\rho}$, Schur's lemma gives $V_4\vert_{\rho\otimes\mathbb C^{m_\rho}}=B_\rho\otimes S_\rho$ with $B_\rho$ the invariant bilinear form on $\rho$ and $S_\rho$ an $m_\rho\times m_\rho$ matrix, and $V_4=0$ between different isotypic components. For $O(26)$ every finite-dimensional irrep is self-dual with a *symmetric* invariant form, so $S_\rho$ is symmetric and unconstrained: no forced zero, only a finer block structure ($A_{11}$ at level (1,1), for instance, splits into the scalar, vector-free, symmetric-traceless and antisymmetric channels of the outside strand times the universal strand's trivial representation). A forced null vector would need a component $\rho$ with $m_\rho$ odd and $B_\rho$ antisymmetric, which does not occur for tensor irreps of an orthogonal group.
$$\ker V_4\big\vert_{\rho\otimes\mathbb C^{m_\rho}}=\rho\otimes\ker S_\rho,\qquad S_\rho=S_\rho^{\!\top}\ \text{free}.\tag{3.2}$$

*(3.3) What would remain: an accidental block zero at $P=0$, and what it would and would not give.* A zero of $S_\rho$ for some channel inside $S_1\oplus S_2$ at all-zero momenta, level (1,1), would be an accident of the declared section, not a consequence of R88 or of covariance. Deciding it needs the level-(1,1) matter-sector block of $V_4$ at zero momentum (the sister's second stage of Track M, `knowledge/open-problems.md` l. 87–92): **Open, barrier data.** Such a block zero is **necessary, not sufficient**, for a sense-(ii) null vector at $(P,q_0)=(0,0)$ (critic #6): by the definition of sense (ii), nullity needs $K_0(q',0)w=0$ for every $q'$, and at $q'\ne0$ the partner legs carry momentum, R88 is silent and the couplings are $O(q'^2)$, not zero; $K_0(q',0)w$ is entire in $q'$ (104 C2.2), so nullity requires every Taylor coefficient in $q'$ to vanish – infinitely many further conditions. If a null vector existed, it would be expected to be isolated, type (b) – the generic expectation, not a consequence of the $O(k^2)$ lifting – and would say nothing about $P\ne0$ or about the $L^2$ fibre operator at $P=0$ (3.0). Type (b) is "$P^2=0$" in the brief's list; here it means $P=0$, and the two coincide for real Euclidean $P$, the record's domain (a lightlike real $P\ne0$ would be a different point for Lorentzian real momenta, not considered).

\subsection*{Commentary}
- (3.0) is the decisive point for the heuristic H2 of record 106: the one rule of the project that *does* force vertex zeros at zero momentum acts on a null set of the fibre, so it cannot produce an $L^2$ null vector and cannot falsify a hypothesis stated in sense (i) at $P=0$. - (3.1) is 034 §4's general remark made concrete with R88 (which post-dates 034); the ghost-number grading of 034 §4 (i) is the same mechanism (a fixed value, no row vanishes) and is not repeated.
- The states polynomial in $x^\mu$ (zero-momentum BRST cohomology) are outside R88's scope and outside $\mathcal H_0$ as used here (momentum eigenstates); they are not a candidate.

\subsection*{Result}
**C3.** R88 forces no null vector of $p_{2,2}^{(L)}(0)$ at any level: nothing in the $L^2$ fibre sense (3.0); in the all-zero-momentum sector one forced zero block and no vanishing row (3.1), with Lorentz covariance adding only block-diagonality (3.2). Closed as a forced kernel. Status: **Proven** (structural; R88 and Schur), with the Open item stated.
