## Task and objective

Literal task (1a). State and prove Lemma L1: a graded-symmetric form $q$ on an enlarged space $E = S \oplus \operatorname{im} D$ with $q \circ D = 0$ and $q|_S = p$ is $q = P^{\mathsf T} p P$, $P$ the projector onto $S$ along $\operatorname{im} D$.
Search a finite model for a counterexample, with a break leg.
Fix the finite model (dimensions, ghost grading, pairing, HS data) that sub-task 1b inherits verbatim.
State L2-model, L2-SFT, the table and the conclusion as open nodes for 1b.

Objective. Decide whether the cubic action, with $\langle \Sigma_2, p_{2,2}(\Sigma_2) \rangle$ and the composite law $\delta_\Lambda \Sigma_2 = 2 \, \delta_\Lambda \Psi \otimes \Psi$, is the gauge-fixed form ($B_2 \Sigma_2 = 0$, $\Lambda_2$ fixed) of a theory with a two-string parameter $\Lambda_2$.
L1 turns the risk "every candidate is the projector construction" into a proven statement about the linear transverse problem, so that any new value of $q$ must sit where L1's hypotheses fail: failures of transversality (E1; split in the fix pass into E1a, $S \cap \operatorname{im} D \neq 0$, and E1b, extra directions outside $S \oplus \operatorname{im} D$), field-dependent $D$ with $\Lambda_2$ acting on $\Psi$ (E2), or a $q$ on the larger space that is a worldsheet object (E3). After the last review (C1.5): within A3 every $\Lambda_2$ is a shift whatever the transversality, so a *non-trivial* $\Lambda_2$ needs an ingredient outside A3 (E2: a field-dependent $D$ or $\Lambda_2$ acting on $\Psi$); a $\Lambda$-law of $\Sigma_2$ differing from the composite one by $D\kappa$ changes the gauge algebra, not the triviality; E1a, E1b and E3 only locate where such an ingredient can change the gauge-fixed theory.

Literal task (1b). ## Finite model M

Decision item of 1a; sub-task 1b inherits it verbatim and adds the HS action on it.
The model follows the graded variant of the finite-model conventions (pre-revision `C-finite-models.tex` l. 9–60; `knowledge/notes/finite-dimensional-models/` §2): a graded space with the paper's pairing, a nilpotent self-adjoint $Q$, graded-symmetric vertices, exact rational arithmetic.
It differs from the six-vector variant in one point, forced by L2: the generic-tensor variants do not satisfy the $L_\infty$ relations, and a model on which the gauge law of $\Psi$ is not a symmetry cannot test L2-model.
M is therefore built so that the truncated action is gauge invariant through $O(g_s^2)$ by construction (node C2), with the smallest field content in which $\Lambda$ acts on the physical fields, the cubic vertex on fields is non-zero, a massive field carries a non-zero kinetic term, and $\ker p_{2,2}$ is non-empty.

The massive basis vector is called $\eta$ (it was $\mu$ before the fix pass, a clash with the trivial-symmetry matrix $\mu^{ij}$; the script keeps the name `mu`).

## Block L1

\subsection*{Setup}

A1–A3. $L$ is the parameter space ($\Lambda_2$ at ghost number 3), $D \colon L \to E$ homogeneous of ghost number $+1$, $S \subseteq E$ a graded subspace, and $E = S \oplus \operatorname{im} D$.
$P \colon E \to E$ is the projector onto $S$ along $\operatorname{im} D$: $P|_S = \mathrm{id}_S$, $P \circ D = 0$, $\operatorname{im}(1 - P) = \operatorname{im} D$; it exists and is unique by transversality, and preserves the ghost number because both summands are graded.
$p$ is a graded-symmetric form on $S$, homogeneous, with $\ker p$ arbitrary (empty or not).
$P^{\mathsf T} p P$ denotes the form $(x, y) \mapsto p(Px, Py)$.

\subsection*{Derivation}

For homogeneous $x \in E$ there is a homogeneous $\lambda_x \in L$ with
$$x = Px + D\lambda_x . \tag{L1.1}$$
By A3, for all $\lambda \in L$ and $y \in E$,
$$q(D\lambda, y) = 0 . \tag{L1.2}$$
By A2 and (L1.2),
$$q(y, D\lambda) = (-1)^{|y| \, |D\lambda|} \, q(D\lambda, y) = 0 . \tag{L1.3}$$
Expanding with (L1.1),
$$\begin{aligned} q(x, y) &= q(Px + D\lambda_x, \, Py + D\lambda_y) \\ &= q(Px, Py) + q(D\lambda_x, Py) + q(Px, D\lambda_y) + q(D\lambda_x, D\lambda_y) \\ &= q(Px, Py) . \end{aligned} \tag{L1.4}$$
Since $Px, Py \in S$ and $q|_S = p$,
$$q(x, y) = p(Px, Py), \qquad q = P^{\mathsf T} p P . \tag{L1.5}$$
Conversely, $q_0 := P^{\mathsf T} p P$ satisfies
$$q_0(y, x) = p(Py, Px) = (-1)^{|Px| \, |Py|} p(Px, Py) = (-1)^{|x| \, |y|} q_0(x, y), \quad q_0(D\lambda, y) = p(PD\lambda, Py) = 0, \quad q_0|_S = p . \tag{L1.6}$$
Its kernel: $q_0(x, \cdot) = 0$ iff $p(Px, s) = 0$ for all $s \in S$ (because $P$ maps onto $S$) iff $Px \in \ker p$, so by (L1.1)
$$\ker q_0 = \ker p \oplus \operatorname{im} D . \tag{L1.7}$$

\subsection*{Commentary}

- (L1.1): transversality is used here and only here; $\lambda_x$ is not unique when $\ker D \neq 0$, but only $D\lambda_x = (1 - P)x$ enters, so reducibility of $\Lambda_2$ plays no role.
- (L1.3): the single place where symmetry enters. $q \circ D = 0$ is a statement about one slot; graded symmetry moves it to the other. Without it, (L1.4) keeps the term $q(Px, D\lambda_y)$, which no hypothesis constrains.
- (L1.6): $|Px| = |x|$ because $P$ is degree preserving; on $E_4$ all signs are $+1$.
- (L1.7): left and right kernels agree by graded symmetry. With $p$ the form of $\mathcal V_4$, $\ker p$ is the kernel of `eq:hs4:kernel`; L1 holds for every $\ker p$, including the empty one that Track D may find.
- The argument is algebraic: it needs the algebraic direct sum $E = S \oplus \operatorname{im} D$ and no topology. In string field theory the decomposition has to hold in the space where $\Sigma_2$ lives; where it fails, the hypothesis of L1 fails (E1).
- The source term. If the enlarged source term is $q(\Sigma_2, \Psi^2)$ with $\Psi^2 \in S$, it is invariant under $\delta \Sigma_2 = D\Lambda_2$ by (L1.3). Observation 1 of the plan forces the totally symmetrized $q_{SS}$ to equal $\mathcal V_4$ on $\mathcal H_2^{\otimes 4}$, i.e. $q_{SS} = p$ on the squares, not as a bilinear form on $S_\Psi$ (check 102a #2); $S \supseteq \operatorname{span}\{\Psi \otimes \Psi\}$ in the setting of L1; the other ghost-4 two-string states (e.g. $\mathcal H_1 \wedge \mathcal H_3$) are not constrained by it.

\subsection*{Result}

$$\boxed{\; E = S \oplus \operatorname{im} D, \;\; q \text{ graded symmetric}, \;\; q \circ D = 0, \;\; q|_S = p \;\Longrightarrow\; q = P^{\mathsf T} p P, \quad \ker q = \ker p \oplus \operatorname{im} D \;}$$

and $P^{\mathsf T} p P$ satisfies the hypotheses: the solution set of the linear transverse problem is the single point $\{P^{\mathsf T} p P\}$, for every $\ker p$.
In words: the enlarged action depends on $\Sigma_2$ only through $P\Sigma_2$, its orbit under $\Lambda_2$ is $\Sigma_2 + \operatorname{im} D$, and the enlargement is the kernel-shift symmetry of 012 §2 with the kernel enlarged from $\ker p$ to $\ker p \oplus \operatorname{im} D$, a Stückelberg enlargement.

\subsection*{Commentary}

- Rigour of the dimension comparison. The matrix and the generators are reduced modulo the prime $2^{31} - 1$; the inclusion $G + K + T \subseteq \mathrm{Sol}$ is checked exactly over $\mathbb Q$. Since a rank modulo a prime cannot exceed the rank over $\mathbb Q$, $\operatorname{rank}_p(G + K + T) \le \dim_{\mathbb Q} \pi_{\rm det}(G + K + T) \le \dim_{\mathbb Q} \mathrm{Sol}_{\rm det} \le \operatorname{null}_p$, and the equality of the two ends found at every instance forces equality throughout. An accident modulo the prime could only produce a mismatch, never a false equality.
- Why the mixed entries matter. With only $\mu^{\psi\psi}$ and $\mu^{\sigma\sigma}$ (the parametrization "$\delta \Sigma_2 \propto \mathcal E_{\Sigma_2}$" with $\psi\psi$ entries), the span has rank 4 instead of 18 (generic) and 44 instead of 54 (degenerate): the 14 missing directions are exactly the $\mu^{\sigma \psi_\eta}$ pairs, which a solve without them would report as solutions outside the span, i.e. false counterexamples (the plan reviewer's M1). A one-sided $\delta \Psi \propto \mathcal E_{\Sigma_2}$ without its partner, or a symmetric $\mu$, is not a symmetry (break legs (a), (b)).
- The undetermined part $U$. It is not a fourth class of symmetries but the part of $\delta$ that the $O(g_s^2)$ action does not constrain; it is fixed by the $O(g_s^3)$ action, which the truncation does not contain. Statement (L2.4) is therefore the strongest one the truncation supports: modulo what it cannot see, every symmetry is in the span.
- Degree sectors. With $g$ of weight $-1$ the action is homogeneous of degree 2, and every homogeneous component of a symmetry is a symmetry; the ansatz is the degree-0 sector, the one in which $\Lambda$ enters as in $Q \Lambda$ and $\Lambda_2$ as in $D \Lambda_2$. Multiplication by $g$ maps the degree-$(d + 1)$ sector into the degree-$d$ sector, so degree $d \ge 1$ is contained in degree 0 up to powers of $g$; the truncation windows match, since a degree-$d$ symmetry at $g^{\le 2}$ maps to degree 0 at $W \le 4 + d$, which contains the window $W \le 4$ solved here, so the containment holds for the truncated systems as well; degree $d < 0$ adds only the $s = -1$ kernel shift $\epsilon N$ excluded in (ii) of the Setup.
- No extension of M was needed. 1a's risk note (one-dimensional abelian gauge algebra) concerned the separation of the composite law from the trivial class: the solve separates them (rank G 1, rank T 17, $G \cap T = 0$ in the projection), so M is used unchanged.
- The quotient formulation. On $\Sigma_2 \in \mathcal H_2^{\wedge 2} / \ker p$ the action is the restriction of the representative action to a complement $C$ of $\ker p$, $\tilde S_{\rm rep}(\Psi, \Sigma_2) = \tilde S_{\rm quot}(\Psi, P_C \Sigma_2)$; a symmetry of the quotient action lifts with $\delta \Sigma_2$ determined up to $\ker p$, and a symmetry of the representative action descends by $P_C$. Hence $\mathrm{Sol}_{\rm quot} = \mathrm{Sol}_{\rm rep} / (\text{all kernel shifts})$, and (L2.4) gives $\mathrm{Sol}_{\rm quot} = P_C(G + T) + U_C$: the class (K) is exactly what the quotient removes. The quotient solve was not run separately.
- The restriction step (L2.3) holds verbatim in closed SFT at the algebraic layer; on M it is checked by C3.3-restriction on a random element of $\mathrm{Sol}$ (all visible generators with random coefficients plus 30 random coefficients in $U$): $S_{\rm el} = S_{\le 4}$ exactly (`eq:hs4:eliminate`, against $S_{\le 4}$ built directly from the vertices), $\delta_{\rm el} S_{\rm el} = 0$ at $W \le 4$ on the saddle $\Psi^2$ and, in the degenerate variant, on the saddle $\Psi^2 + \psi_\chi \psi_{\varphi_1} N$, $N \in \ker p$, while the same element does not preserve the diagonal ($\delta \Sigma_2 \ne \delta(\Psi^2)$ at $\Sigma_2 = \Psi^2$). Break leg: on $\Sigma_2 = 2 \Psi^2$, where $\partial_{\Sigma_2} \tilde S \ne 0$, the restriction is not a symmetry.

$$\omega(bX, X) = 0 \;\; \forall X \in \mathcal H^{(2)} \iff \omega(bX, Y) + \omega(bY, X) = 0 . \tag{θ.2}$$
$\omega$ is non-degenerate on $C$: if $c \in C$ has $\omega(c, \cdot) = 0$, then $\langle Qc, Y \rangle = 0$ for all $Y$ (on $\operatorname{im} Q$ trivially), so $Qc = 0$ and $c \in \operatorname{im} Q \cap C = 0$.
