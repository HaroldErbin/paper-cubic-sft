## §2 The fixed-halves form, its block structure and its inverse (C2)

\subsection*{Setup}
Uses A1–A3, A5, (1.3). $\mathcal H^\downarrow:=\ker b_0^+\cap\ker b_0^-$, so that $\mathcal H=\mathcal H^\downarrow\oplus c_0^+\mathcal H^\downarrow\oplus c_0^-\mathcal H^\downarrow\oplus c_0^+c_0^-\mathcal H^\downarrow$; the *block* is $W:=c_0^+c_0^-\mathcal H^\downarrow$. $\Phi:\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0\to\mathcal H_4$ is the linear map $\Sigma\mapsto\Phi_2(\Sigma)$ (071 C1, injective on finite-level tensors by 071 C5.1), $\mathcal I:=\Phi(\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0)$ its image. $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the BPZ pairing, $\omega(x,y)=\langle x,y\rangle$ viewed as a non-degenerate bilinear form on $\mathcal H$; $X^\dagger$ denotes the transpose with respect to it. A bilinear form $B(x,y)=\omega(x,Oy)$ is identified with its operator $O$.

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(2.1) The form and its operator.* By (1.3), $p^{\rm circ}(\Sigma,\Sigma')=\omega\big(\Phi\Sigma',\,G\,\Phi\Sigma\big)$ with
$$
G=-i\kappa\,b_0^+b_0^-\,F,\qquad F:=\int_{Q_V}ds\,d\theta\;e^{-sL_0^+}e^{i\theta L_0^-},\qquad
F\,|h,m\rangle=\hat f_m(h)\,|h,m\rangle,\quad \hat f_m(h)=\int_{Q_V}ds\,d\theta\;e^{-sh+im\theta},
\tag{2.1}
$$
since $e^{-sL_0^+}$ and $e^{i\theta L_0^-}$ are both diagonal in the joint $(L_0^+,L_0^-)$ eigenbasis and the integral of a diagonal operator is diagonal. (For $\theta$-dependent $\sigma_V$ this is 032's remark that $\hat{\mathsf P}$ acts on $\mathcal H$, not $\mathcal H_0$: it is diagonal, with non-zero even-$m$ eigenvalues, (2.7).) $G$ maps $W\to\mathcal H^\downarrow$ and annihilates the other three summands; $\omega$ pairs $\mathcal H^\downarrow$ with $W$ and $c_0^+\mathcal H^\downarrow$ with $c_0^-\mathcal H^\downarrow$ (A1: the vacuum pairing needs both $c_0^\pm$). So the form $\omega(\cdot,G\,\cdot)$ is supported on $W\times W$, where it is non-degenerate wherever $\hat f_m(h)\ne0$. [C2]

*(2.2) The inverse of a form pulled back by an injective map.* As a form on $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0$, $p=\Phi^\dagger G\Phi$ (meaning $p(\Sigma,\Sigma')=\omega(\Phi\Sigma',G\Phi\Sigma)$). Its inverse $p^{-1}$ (on $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0/\ker p$, R38's quotient) defines the operator
$$
\Pi:=\Phi\,p^{-1}\Phi^\dagger:\ \mathcal H_2\to\mathcal H_4,\qquad G\,\Pi\,G\,\Phi=G\,\Phi,\qquad (G\Pi)^2=G\Pi ,
\tag{2.2}
$$
i.e. $G\Pi G=G$ **on $\mathcal I$** and $P_{\mathcal I}:=\Pi G=\Phi p^{-1}\Phi^\dagger G$ is the $G$-orthogonal projector onto $\mathcal I$ (both lines are one-line consequences of $p=\Phi^\dagger G\Phi$). Two caveats. (a) $p^{-1}$ lives on $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0/\ker p$ (R38's quotient) and $\Pi$ needs a section of it; $G\Phi(\ker p)=G(\mathcal I\cap\mathcal I^{\perp_G})\ne0$ in general, so $G\Pi G=G$ "on $\mathcal I$" holds when paired against $\mathcal I$ (which is all C3 uses), $P_{\mathcal I}$ is a projector modulo $\mathcal I\cap\mathcal I^{\perp_G}$ — this is where $\ker V_4$ enters — and non-degeneracy of $G$ on $W$ does not imply non-degeneracy of $p$ on $\mathcal I$. (b) $\Phi^\dagger y=\omega(\Phi\cdot,y)$ is non-zero for $y\in c_0^\pm\mathcal H^\downarrow$ as well, because $\Phi\Sigma$ has components in all four summands (071 C2.2: $b_0^-\Phi_2\ne0$; $c_0^\pm\Phi_2\ne0$ from 071 (B.5)–(B.7)): $\mathcal I\not\subset W$. What matters is that $G$ is supported on $W\times W$, i.e. $G=G\pi_W$ with $\pi_W$ the projection onto $W$ along the other three summands: $p=(\pi_W\Phi)^\dagger G(\pi_W\Phi)$ and $G\Pi G=G(\pi_W\Phi)p^{-1}(\pi_W\Phi)^\dagger G$ see only the $W$-components of the circle states, while the outer factors of every composite ($G$ in §3, $G_5$ in §4) land in $\mathcal H^\downarrow$, so (2.3) is used on $\mathcal H^\downarrow$ only. Hence
$$
\Pi=P_{\mathcal I}\;G_W^{-1},\qquad G_W^{-1}:=\text{the inverse of }G\text{ on the block, }\mathcal H^\downarrow\to W ,
\tag{2.3}
$$
and $GP_{\mathcal I}=G|_W$, i.e. $G\Pi G=G$ on all of $W$ and $\pi_W\Pi=G_W^{-1}$ on $\mathcal H^\downarrow$, under H1 (surjectivity of the extended $\pi_W\Phi_2$ modulo $\ker G$; $\Pi$ itself keeps off-$W$ components, invisible between two $G$'s). Exactly: by (2.2), $G\Pi Gx=Gx$ holds for $x\in\pi_W\mathcal I$ with no hypothesis; what C4 needs is $x:=G_W^{-1}G_5'\Phi_3^{[b]}(m_3')\in\pi_W\mathcal I$ modulo $\ker G$, then $\Pi Gx=\Phi\Sigma_x$ with $\pi_W\Phi\Sigma_x=x$ and $G_5\Pi G_5'\Phi_3^{[b]}=G_5x$. That $x$ has components at every level, so it lies in the image only for $\Sigma$ of unbounded level: H1 is a surjectivity statement about the extended map, not a density statement about the finite-level image (density in the level-by-level topology would not extend $G\Pi G=G$ to the closure, since $\Phi^\dagger$ sums over all levels and is not continuous there), and $p^{-1}$ acts on $\Sigma$ outside A3's finite-level guarantee. Nothing here uses the diagonal form of $F$: the same (2.2)–(2.3) hold for $p_{2,2}$ with the dressed $\tilde G$ of (1.5), $\tilde\Pi=P^{\tilde G}_{\mathcal I}\tilde G_W^{-1}$. Script: `C2.1-gram` (both identities of (2.2) in a $4\times d$-dimensional model with random complex $\hat f$ and random injective $\Phi$), `C2.1-onto` ($\Phi$ with components in all four summands, $\mathcal I\not\subset W$, and $\pi_W\Phi$ bijective onto $W$: $G\Pi G=G$ on all of $W$, $\pi_W\Pi J=\frac{i}{\kappa}c_0^-c_0^+F^{-1}$ on $\mathcal H^\downarrow$, and $\Pi J$ itself differs from that target by its off-$W$ part — the finite-dimensional stand-in for H1; its limits: it is a transcription check of linear algebra (ordering of $c_0^-c_0^+$, the factor $\kappa$), and it tests neither surjectivity of the infinite-dimensional extended $\pi_W\Phi_2$ (H1 itself), nor the kernel case $\ker p\ne0$ of R38's quotient — $\pi_W\Phi$ onto with a kernel, where $\Pi$ needs a section and $G\Pi G$ must be section-independent — nor any $\hat f_m$ near a zero), `C2.1-break` (when $\pi_W\Phi$ does not span $W$, $G\Pi G\ne G$ on a generic vector of $W$ and $\Pi=P_{\mathcal I}G_W^{-1}$ exactly). [C2.1]

*(2.4) The two $c$'s.* On $\mathcal H^\downarrow$, using $\{b_0^\pm,c_0^\pm\}=1$, $\{b_0^\pm,c_0^\mp\}=0$ (A1) and $b_0^\pm|\!\downarrow\rangle=0$:
$$
b_0^+b_0^-\,c_0^-c_0^+\,|\!\downarrow\rangle=b_0^+\big(1-c_0^-b_0^-\big)c_0^+|\!\downarrow\rangle=b_0^+c_0^+|\!\downarrow\rangle+b_0^+c_0^-c_0^+b_0^-|\!\downarrow\rangle=|\!\downarrow\rangle ,
\qquad
c_0^-c_0^+\,b_0^+b_0^-\big|_{W}=1 ,
\tag{2.4}
$$
so $(b_0^+b_0^-)^{-1}=c_0^-c_0^+$ between $W$ and $\mathcal H^\downarrow$, and $G_W^{-1}=\frac{i}{\kappa}\,c_0^-c_0^+F^{-1}$, $F^{-1}|h,m\rangle=\hat f_m(h)^{-1}|h,m\rangle$. The two $c$'s of $p^{-1}$ are therefore the zero modes dual to the two Schiffer antighosts of the circle gluing — dual to $b(\partial_s)=-b_0^+$ and $b(\partial_\theta)=ib_0^-$, i.e. to the dilation and rotation $w\partial_w$, $iw\partial_w$ of the gluing circle, the residual $\mathbb C^*$ of the two-punctured sphere (036 (1.2)): the inverse of the gluing's antighost pair is the CKV pair of the annulus. `C2.2-zero-modes` (4-dimensional Clifford representation). [C2.2]

*(2.5) No inverse region.* $\mathcal S(q)\mathcal S(q')=\mathcal S(qq')$ holds pointwise ($\mathbb C^*$ is abelian), but the $b$-dressed gluings $\mathcal G_R=\int_Rds\,d\theta\,b_0^+b_0^-\mathcal S(q)$ compose to $\mathcal G_R\mathcal G_{R'}\propto(b_0^+b_0^-)^2=0$ in the fixed-halves model: the gluings are not a semigroup, and an inverse must carry the opposite ghost charge — the $c$'s of (2.4) — rather than a reversed parameter. For $p_{2,2}$ itself only the ghost-charge half of this transfers (a $b$-dressed gluing has $N_{\rm gh}=+2$, an inverse $-2$); products of distinct dressed antighost insertions (1.5) need not vanish. At the level of the modulus, the inverse of an integral over a region is not the integral over the inverse region: for one $L_0^+$ sector with constant $\sigma$,
$$
\hat f_{[-\sigma,0]}(h)\,\hat f_{[0,\sigma]}(h)=\frac{(e^{\sigma h}-1)(1-e^{-\sigma h})}{h^2}\ne1 ,
\tag{2.5}
$$
(`C2.3-inverse-region`, three generic complex points). Only a *point* inverts to a point: $\mathcal S(q)^{-1}=\mathcal S(1/q)$ is the frozen-neck statement of R27, not available to a 2-form. [C2.3]

*(2.6) Closed forms.* Constant $\sigma$ (the $\mathcal H_0$, level-matched case of 032 (3.2)):
$$
\hat f_m(h)=2\pi\,\delta_{m,0}\,\frac{e^{\sigma h}-1}{h},\qquad
\frac{1}{\hat f_0(h)}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\frac{h}{e^{\sigma h}-1}
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\begin{cases}\ \ h\sum_{n\ge1}e^{-n\sigma h}, & \Re h>0,\\[2pt] -h\sum_{n\ge0}e^{+n\sigma h}, & \Re h<0,\end{cases}
\tag{2.6}
$$
(`C2.4-laplace`, 59 terms at three complex points, $10^{-10}$). Since $h\,e^{-n\sigma h}=\int ds\,\delta'(s-n\sigma)\,e^{-sh}$ (sign as for $-\partial_s$), the Neumann branch is the Laplace transform of $\sum_{n\ge1}\delta'(s-n\sigma)$: **a discrete tower of cylinders of lengths $n\sigma$, $n\ge1$, each carrying one $\delta'$ in its length**, the operator content being $c_0^-c_0^+L_0^+e^{-n\sigma L_0^+}=c_0^-c_0^+\{Q_B,b_0^+\}e^{-n\sigma L_0^+}$: the zero-mode part of the $\Psi$ kinetic operator $c_0^-Q_B$ (036 (1.2): the two $c$'s of $Q_B$) on a cylinder of length $n\sigma$. The reversed branch is the same tower with negative lengths $-n\sigma$ and $n\ge0$ — the branch that needs bounded level content in the sewn state (the brief's remark; PH10's regime), while the Neumann branch converges for $\Re h>0$, i.e. above the threshold $\alpha'P^2/2>2-N-\bar N$ of the sector. Both are the same meromorphic function:
$$
\frac{1}{\hat f_0}\ \text{is regular at }h=0\ \Big(=\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma}\Big)\ \text{and has simple poles at } h=\frac{2\pi ik}{\sigma},\ k\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\},
\tag{2.7}
$$
the zeros of $\hat f_0$ (`C2.4-poles`, bookkeeping: one $k$ checked, not that these are all the zeros). These are zeros of the block operator $G_W$ on the $(N,\bar N)$ sector, **not** of $\det p^{\rm circ}$ unconditionally: $p^{\rm circ}=\Phi^\dagger G\Phi$ on finite-level inputs is a sum over all sectors (071 C4: every level populated), so the vanishing of one sector's coefficient does not make $\det p$ vanish and $\Pi=\Phi p^{-1}\Phi^\dagger$ is finite there; they are poles of $p^{-1}$ under H1 (where $G_5\Pi G_5'=G_5G_W^{-1}G_5'$, C4). In the $m=0$ sector at constant $\sigma$: no mass-shell pole ($h=0$ is regular — the $\Sigma$ line is not a propagator, R28/R40; the open-string precedent is R3, Erbin–Fırat's auxiliary *propagator* $(1-e^{-2\lambda L_0})/L_0$ regular at $L_0=0$, which in this reading is the region and its kinetic operator the tower), and zeros of $G_W$ at complex momenta $\alpha'P^2/2=2-N-\bar N+2\pi ik/\sigma$ — real part on the plumbing lattice, imaginary part $2\pi k/\sigma$, off the lattice as points of $\mathbb C$ — R39's zeros of $\det V_4(P^2)$ off the mass shells and R40's H2a (existence part), realised in the untruncated model under H1 (not evidence for PH18, which is about the level-truncated composite at the actual vertices; §6.4). Around $h=0$ the Bernoulli expansion $\frac{h}{e^{\sigma h}-1}=\frac1\sigma-\frac h2+\frac{\sigma h^2}{12}-\dots$ converges for $|h|<2\pi/\sigma$: $p^{-1}$ is a convergent power series in $L_0^+$ there, "local" in the sense of finitely many derivatives at each order and non-local as a whole.

*Physical reading (R30, R3).* Up to $c_0^-c_0^+$ versus $Q_B$, the constant-$\sigma$ kernel $F^{-1}|_{m=0}=\frac1{2\pi}\frac{L_0^+}{e^{\sigma L_0^+}-1}$ is R30's (α-rev) kinetic operator $\mathcal K=Q_BL_0^+/(e^{\sigma L_0^+}-1)$ of the single-strand auxiliary $\Phi$, and 071 C5.3's dictionary $\Phi=-g\mathcal K^{-1}b_0^-\delta(L_0^-)\Phi_2(\Sigma)$ gives $p^{\rm circ}(\Sigma,\Sigma')=\omega(\Phi_2\Sigma',\mathcal K^{-1}\Phi_2\Sigma)$ at constant $\sigma$: the tower is the single-strand kinetic operator already in the ledger, and the roles kinetic/propagator are exchanged between the $\Sigma_2$ and the $\Phi$ formulations (region $\leftrightarrow$ tower), the same exchange noted at $n=2$ in (2.9)(i). This is the cleanest physical reading of "the inverse of the $\Sigma$ kinetic form". [C2.4]

$\theta$-dependent $\sigma_V$ (the actual region (1.4)): $\hat f_m(h)=\int_0^{2\pi}d\theta\,e^{im\theta}\frac{e^{\sigma_V(\theta)h}-1}{h}$, the $m$-th Fourier coefficient; $\pi$-periodicity kills odd $m$ (`C2.4-fourier-odd` on a test profile with a non-periodic break leg; `C2.4-fourier-sigmaV` on the computed $\sigma_V$: $|\hat f_{1,3}|/|\hat f_2|=2\times10^{-16}$), consistent with 071 C3 ($\Phi_2(\mathrm{Sym}^2)$ has no odd-$L_0^-$ component, so the odd sectors never enter). With $\nu$ the distribution of $\sigma_V(\theta)$ on $[\ln\tfrac94,\ln4\beta^2]$,
$$
\hat f_0(h)=2\pi\,\frac{\hat\nu(h)-1}{h},\quad \hat\nu(h):=\int d\nu(\sigma)\,e^{\sigma h},\qquad
\frac1{\hat f_0}=\frac1{2\pi}\,\frac{h}{\hat\nu(h)-1}=\frac{-h}{2\pi}\sum_{n\ge0}\hat\nu(h)^n\ (\Re h<0):
\tag{2.8}
$$
the reversed branch is a tower of cylinders whose lengths are sums of $n$ independent draws from the seam distribution; the Neumann branch $\frac{h}{2\pi}\sum_{n\ge1}\hat\nu(h)^{-n}$ is a tower only when $\nu$ is a point mass ($\hat\nu^{-1}$ is a Laplace transform of a positive measure only then). ## §3 $\mathcal V_4p^{-1}\mathcal V_4=\mathcal V_4$ as region arithmetic (C3)

\subsection*{Setup}
Uses A5, (2.1)–(2.4), (2.6). $\mathcal V_4(\Psi^2,\Sigma)=p^{\rm circ}(\Psi\otimes\Psi,\Sigma)$; the composite is $\mathcal V_4p^{-1}\mathcal V_4(\Psi^4)=\omega\big(\Phi(\Psi\Psi),\,G\,\Pi\,G\,\Phi(\Psi\Psi)\big)$ with $\Pi$ of (2.2). $\mu_R$ denotes the indicator measure of a region in the neck length $s$ (one $L_0^-$ sector, constant $\sigma$ for the closed forms).

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(3.1) Operator level.* By (2.2), $G\Pi G\Phi=G\Phi$, so the identity holds for every $\Phi$-image on either side, and the projector $P_{\mathcal I}$ is invisible because both neighbours $\Phi(\Psi\Psi)$ lie in $\mathcal I$. On the block, with (2.4) and $F$ diagonal, the identity reads sector by sector
$$
b_0^+b_0^-\,\hat f\;\cdot\;c_0^-c_0^+\,\hat f^{-1}\;\cdot\;b_0^+b_0^-\,\hat f
=\big(b_0^+b_0^-c_0^-c_0^+b_0^+b_0^-\big)\,\hat f\hat f^{-1}\hat f=b_0^+b_0^-\,\hat f ,
\tag{3.1}
$$
the ghost part by (2.4) ($b_0^+b_0^-c_0^-c_0^+=1$ on $\mathcal H^\downarrow$), the modulus part the identity $\hat f\cdot\hat f^{-1}\cdot\hat f=\hat f$ of *numbers* in each $(h,m)$ sector. "Two $c$'s cancel two $b$'s and two modulus integrals" is exactly this: the $c_0^\pm$ cancel the $b_0^\pm$ of one neighbour's circle gluing (the dilation/rotation antighosts), and one factor of $\hat f$ — one two-real-dimensional modulus integral, as a number — is cancelled by $\hat f^{-1}$. [C3]

*(3.2) Region level.* In the neck length, with $\mu:=\mu_{[-\sigma,0]}$ and $\hat f=\mathcal L[\mu]$ (Laplace transform, (2.6)), the product $\hat f\hat f^{-1}\hat f$ is the transform of the convolution $\mu*\mathcal L^{-1}[\hat f^{-1}]*\mu$, and by (2.6) $\mathcal L^{-1}[\hat f^{-1}]=\sum_{n\ge1}\delta'_{n\sigma}$ on the Neumann branch, so
$$
\mu*\Big(\sum_{n\ge1}\delta'_{n\sigma}\Big)*\mu=\sum_{n\ge1}\partial_S\,(\mu*\mu)(S-n\sigma),\qquad
(\mu*\mu)(S)=\begin{cases}S+2\sigma,&-2\sigma\le S\le-\sigma\\ -S,&-\sigma\le S\le0\\0&\text{else}\end{cases}
\tag{3.2}
$$
(the tent of base $2\sigma$). Its derivative is $+1$ on $(-2\sigma,-\sigma)$ and $-1$ on $(-\sigma,0)$; shifted by $n\sigma$ it is $+1$ on $((n-2)\sigma,(n-1)\sigma)$ and $-1$ on $((n-1)\sigma,n\sigma)$, and the sum over $n\ge1$ telescopes slab by slab:
$$
\sum_{n\ge1}\partial_S(\mu*\mu)(S-n\sigma)=\mathbb 1_{(-\sigma,0)}(S)=\mu(S)
\tag{3.3}
$$
(`C3-telescoping`, exact rational arithmetic at 159 sample points off the breakpoints, 12 terms). This is the "subtraction of a region made exact": the composition $Q_V\cdot(\text{inverse})\cdot Q_V$ is not $Q_V\cdot Q_V^{-1}\cdot Q_V$ in the group of regions (which would be the Minkowski sum $[-2\sigma,\sigma]$ with multiplicity, cf. (2.5)) but the alternating sum of shifted slabs of the convolution $Q_V*Q_V$ (necks of length $s_1+s_2+n\sigma$), the $c$'s acting as $\partial_S$ on the convolved neighbours' neck modulus, with every slab but $(-\sigma,0)$ cancelling against its neighbour. (3.1) holds as numbers in each even $m$; the $\theta$-modulus has a region reading only in the $m=0$, constant-$\sigma$ case, where it is the trivial inverse of $2\pi\delta_{m,0}$ (for constant $\sigma$ there is no $m\ne0$ sector, $\hat f_m\propto\delta_{m,0}$; for the actual $\sigma_V$ the slab picture exists only on the reversed branch, (3.4)(iv)). [C3]

*(3.4) What it needs, and what fails otherwise.* (i) Fixed halves (A5): with $q$-dependent halves the middle factor is $\tilde G_W^{-1}$, not a function of $L_0^\pm$, and (3.1) holds only as the operator identity $G\Pi G=G$ on $\mathcal I$ — true but without a sector-by-sector or region reading. (ii) The same region on both sides: if the two outer factors were gluings over regions $R_1,R_2$ different from $Q_V$, $\hat f_{R_1}\hat f^{-1}\hat f_{R_2}\ne\hat f_{R_i}$ and the telescoping (3.3) is incomplete (`C3-telescoping-break`: slabs of width $\sigma_5=\tfrac12$ against $\sigma=\tfrac34$ leave non-zero weight at $S>0$, i.e. on long necks) — this is the situation of §4. (iii) Nothing about the domain of a Laplace representation: (3.1) is an identity of meromorphic functions of $h$, valid on both branches; the tower picture (3.2)–(3.3) is the Neumann branch $\Re h>0$, and on the reversed branch the slabs are shifted by $-n\sigma$ and telescope the same way. (iv) The seam: $\sigma_V(\theta)$ enters (3.1) only through $\hat f_m(h)$; the region reading (3.3) needs $\hat f^{-1}$ to be a Laplace transform of a discrete measure, which holds for constant $\sigma$ and sector $m=0$, and for the actual $\sigma_V$ only on the reversed branch via (2.8) — on the Neumann branch (3.1) still holds as numbers, but there is no slab picture. (v) Fold arcs: on them the $(h,m)$ expansion is marginal (§1 (1.5)); (3.1) as an operator identity on $\mathcal I$ does not depend on it.

\subsection*{Commentary}
(3.1): the ghost-number count $-2+2-2=-2$ ($\hat d$: $2-2+2=2$, R39's closure corollary) is the ghost part of this line; the modulus part is what R39's calculus calls "removing $4k-6=2$ moduli" and is, in the model, division by a number per sector. (3.2): the Laplace transform turns convolution of neck measures into products, which is why the per-sector identity has a region translation at all; the derivative $\partial_S$ comes from the $L_0^+=\{Q_B,b_0^+\}$ in $F^{-1}$, the standard $\partial_s e^{-sL_0^+}=-L_0^+e^{-sL_0^+}$ of the plumbing family — the mechanism of the boundary terms in the geometric master equation, here acting on the neighbours' neck modulus. (3.3): the break leg shows the telescoping is a property of a width equal to $\sigma$ (here both), not of the sum of the widths — the general criterion is (4.4)(i).

\subsection*{Result}
$$
\boxed{\ \mathcal V_4p^{-1}\mathcal V_4=\mathcal V_4\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \hat f\hat f^{-1}\hat f=\hat f\ \text{per }(h,m)\ \text{and}\ b_0^+b_0^-c_0^-c_0^+b_0^+b_0^-=b_0^+b_0^-\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \sum_{n\ge1}\partial_S(\mu*\mu)(S-n\sigma)=\mu\ }
$$

\subsection*{Verification}
`C3-telescoping` (exact, Piecewise), `C3-telescoping-break` (unequal widths leave weight on long necks). (3.1) is (2.2) plus (2.4), both checked in §2.
