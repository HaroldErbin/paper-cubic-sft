## 1. The equation in components (C1)

\subsection*{Setup}
A1–A5. Write $q:=\tfrac16A\hat\sigma\in\mathbb R^3$, i.e. $q_m=\tfrac16(h_m\hat\sigma_1+h_{m+1}\hat\sigma_2+h_{m+2}\hat\sigma_3)$, $m=0,1,2$, and $u:=1+q_0$. $H_i:=V_4(e_i,\cdot,\cdot)$ is the $2\times2$ matrix $(H_i)_{ab}=h_{i-1+a+b}$: $H_1=\begin{pmatrix}h_0&h_1\\h_1&h_2\end{pmatrix}$, $H_2=\begin{pmatrix}h_1&h_2\\h_2&h_3\end{pmatrix}$, $H_3=\begin{pmatrix}h_2&h_3\\h_3&h_4\end{pmatrix}$.

\subsection*{Derivation}
$$
\mathcal Q_{\hat\sigma}=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)+\tfrac16\sum_i\hat\sigma_iH_i=\begin{pmatrix}1+q_0&q_1\\q_1&q_2\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}u&q_1\\q_1&q_2\end{pmatrix},\qquad \Delta:=\det\mathcal Q_{\hat\sigma}=uq_2-q_1^2, \tag{1.1}
$$
since $\sum_i\hat\sigma_i(H_i)_{ab}=\sum_i h_{i-1+a+b}\hat\sigma_i=(A\hat\sigma)_{1+a+b}$. The potential (069 (1.4), $\omega=\mathbb 1$, $\det(-\mathcal Q)=\det\mathcal Q$ in two dimensions, the chamber constant dropped):
$$
\hat{\mathcal V}(\hat\sigma)=g^2\mathcal V_{\rm eff}=\tfrac1{24}\hat\sigma^\top A\hat\sigma+\tfrac\lambda2\log\lvert\Delta(\hat\sigma)\rvert,\qquad \lambda=\hbar g^2. \tag{1.2}
$$
Gradient: $\partial_i\log\lvert\Delta\rvert=\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal Q^{-1}\partial_i\mathcal Q)=\tfrac16\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal Q^{-1}H_i)=\tfrac16\sum_{ab}(\mathcal Q^{-1})_{ab}V_4(e_i,X_a,X_b)=\tfrac16V_4\big(e_i,\textstyle\sum_{ab}(\mathcal Q^{-1})_{ab}X_a\otimes X_b\big)=\tfrac16\big(A\,\pi(\mathcal Q^{-1})\big)_i$, hence
$$
\nabla\hat{\mathcal V}=\tfrac1{12}A\big[\hat\sigma+\lambda\,\pi(\mathcal Q_{\hat\sigma}^{-1})\big],\qquad \pi(G)=(G_{TT},2G_{TD},G_{DD}), \tag{1.3}
$$
and, $A$ being invertible (R68, $\det A\neq0$), the stationarity of $\hat{\mathcal V}$ is 069's (1.6) with $G=(-\mathcal Q)^{-1}=-\mathcal Q^{-1}$:
$$
\boxed{\;\hat\sigma=-\lambda\,\pi\big(\mathcal Q_{\hat\sigma}^{-1}\big):\qquad \hat\sigma_1=-\lambda\frac{q_2}{\Delta},\quad \hat\sigma_2=+2\lambda\frac{q_1}{\Delta},\quad \hat\sigma_3=-\lambda\frac{u}{\Delta}\;} \tag{1.4}
$$
Hessian, from (1.3) and $\partial_j\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal Q^{-1}H_i)=-\tfrac16\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal Q^{-1}H_j\mathcal Q^{-1}H_i)$:
$$
\mathrm{Hess}\,\hat{\mathcal V}_{ij}=\tfrac1{12}A_{ij}-\tfrac\lambda{72}\operatorname{tr}\big(\mathcal Q^{-1}H_i\,\mathcal Q^{-1}H_j\big). \tag{1.5}
$$
In the linear coordinates $x=(u,q_1,q_2)=e_1+\tfrac16A\hat\sigma$ the gap equation reads, with $P:(u,q_1,q_2)\mapsto(q_2,-2q_1,u)$ (so that $\pi(\operatorname{adj}\mathcal Q)=Px$) and $B:=\tfrac\lambda6AP$,
$$
\Delta\,(x-e_1)+Bx=0,\qquad \Delta=x_1x_3-x_2^2. \tag{1.6}
$$

\subsection*{Commentary}
- (1.1): the factor $\tfrac12$ of $e_2$ is absorbed in $V_4(e_2,X_a,X_b)=h_{1+a+b}$ (two orderings, 064 §1 commentary); $q=\tfrac16A\hat\sigma$ is the statement that the dressing of the kinetic operator is the $\Sigma$-contraction of the same Hankel block that gives the $\Sigma$ mass term.
- (1.3): $\pi$ carries the factor 2 on the off-diagonal entry because $G_{TD}(T\otimes D)+G_{DT}(D\otimes T)=2G_{TD}\,e_2$ (069 §3 A, 064 C2.1). The one-dimensional check: $N=1$, $V_2=-1$, $V_4=v$ gives $\mathcal Q=-(1-a\sigma)$, $a=g^2v/6$, and (1.4) is $\sigma(1-a\sigma)=\hbar$, 069 (2.3a).
- (1.6): multiplication by $\Delta$ adds the spurious solution $x=0$ ($\mathcal Q=0$, $\hat\sigma=-6A^{-1}e_1$), which is not a root of (1.4); it is excluded in §3.
- Scaling: (1.2) is homogeneous of degree 2 in $(\hat\sigma,\sqrt\lambda)$ only in the quadratic term; $\lambda$ is the one parameter left (069 C1.4).

\subsection*{Result}
(1.1)–(1.6).

\subsection*{Verification}
`C1-grad` (symbolic, generic $h$: (1.3) equals the sympy gradient of (1.2); break: $\pi$ without the factor 2), `C1-hess` ((1.5) against the sympy Hessian), `C1-grad-fd` (numerical gradient at seeded points, both $\beta$).

## 3. Reduction to a sextic (C3)

\subsection*{Setup}
(1.6); $P=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&-2&0\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix}$, $B=\tfrac\lambda6AP$, $\chi(w):=\det(wI+B)$, $a(w):=\operatorname{adj}(wI+B)\,e_1$ (first column of the adjugate; $a_1$ of degree 2, $a_2,a_3$ of degree $\le1$ in $w$).

\subsection*{Derivation}
At a root of (1.4), $\Delta\neq0$ and (1.6) holds; write $w:=\Delta$. (1.6) is linear in $x$ at fixed $w$:
$$
(wI+B)\,x=w\,e_1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x(w)=\frac{w\,a(w)}{\chi(w)}\qquad(\chi(w)\neq0). \tag{3.1}
$$
The definition $w=x_1x_3-x_2^2$ then closes the system on the scalar $w$:
$$
w=\frac{w^2\,N(w)}{\chi(w)^2},\quad N:=a_1a_3-a_2^2\ \ (\deg N\le3)\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad w\,S(w)=0,\quad S(w):=\chi(w)^2-w\,N(w),\ \deg S=6. \tag{3.2}
$$
$w=0$ is the spurious solution $x=0$ of §1 ($B$ invertible: $\det B=(\lambda/6)^3\det A\det P=-2(\lambda/6)^3\det A\neq0$). Hence
$$
\boxed{\;\text{roots of (1.4)}\ \longleftrightarrow\ \{w_*\in\mathbb R:\ S(w_*)=0,\ w_*\neq0,\ \chi(w_*)\neq0\},\qquad \hat\sigma_*=6A^{-1}\big(x(w_*)-e_1\big),\quad \det\mathcal Q_{\hat\sigma_*}=w_*\;} \tag{3.3}
$$
at most six roots, real or complex, for each $(\beta,\lambda)$; the sign of $w_*$ is the chamber ($w_*>0$: I or II by the sign of $u_*=x_1(w_*)$; $w_*<0$: III). The case $\chi(w_*)=0$ with $w_*e_1\in\operatorname{ran}(w_*I+B)$ (codimension one in the data) is excluded by the multistart Newton scan on (1.6), which finds no root beyond the lifted sextic roots; such a $w_*$ is generically not a sextic root ($S(w_*)=-w_*N(w_*)\neq0$ for a rank-one adjugate), so the clause "$\chi\neq0$ at every sextic root" (`C3-chi-*`) does not bear on it.

*Small-$\lambda$ structure of $S$.* $\operatorname{tr}B=\tfrac\lambda6(A_{13}-2A_{22}+A_{31})=0$ identically (Hankel: $A_{13}=A_{22}=A_{31}=h_2$), $B_{31}=\tfrac\lambda6h_4$, and every entry of $B$ is $O(\lambda)$, so
$$
S(w)=w^6+\tfrac{\lambda h_4}6\,w^4+O(\lambda^2)\,w^3+\dots:\qquad\text{two roots }w\simeq\pm i\sqrt{\lambda h_4/6}\ (\text{complex for }h_4>0),\quad\text{four roots }w=O(\lambda). \tag{3.4}
$$
The complex pair is the (2.5a) candidate ($\hat\sigma_3=-\lambda u/\Delta\simeq-\lambda/w$, $\hat\sigma_3^2\simeq-6\lambda/h_4<0$): the root at $\hat\sigma\sim\sqrt\lambda$ that a one-dimensional ray analysis predicts in the $A$-negative cone is not a real root of the three-dimensional system. The four $O(\lambda)$ roots are the near-wall class (§4).

*Scaled form and the two limits.* With $v:=w/\lambda$ and $\tilde B:=\tfrac16AP$ ($B=\lambda\tilde B$), $\chi(w)=\lambda^3\tilde\chi(v)$, $a(w)=\lambda^2\tilde a(v)$, $N(w)=\lambda^4\tilde N(v)$ with $\tilde\chi(v)=\det(vI+\tilde B)$, $\tilde a=\operatorname{adj}(vI+\tilde B)e_1$, $\tilde N=\tilde a_1\tilde a_3-\tilde a_2^2$ all $\lambda$-independent ($\deg\tilde N=3$), so
$$
S(w)=\lambda^5\big[\lambda\,\tilde\chi(v)^2-v\,\tilde N(v)\big]. \tag{3.5}
$$
$\lambda\to0$: the real roots of (3.5) at $v=O(1)$ are $v=0$ (the apex root, §4.1, $\Delta_*\sim\lambda^2$) and the three roots of the cubic $\tilde N(v)=0$, which are therefore the exact values of $\lim\Delta_*/\lambda$ of the near-wall roots (second method for the last column of the table of §4.1; `C4b-ntilde-*`: agreement to $10^{-8}$ with $-\operatorname{tr}\mathcal Q_D/(c(1+r^2))$ at both $\beta$); the remaining pair escapes to $v\sim\lambda^{-1/2}$ (the complex pair (3.4)). $\lambda\to\infty$: $\tilde\chi(v)^2=v\tilde N(v)/\lambda\to0$, so the six roots tend pairwise to the double roots $v=-\mu_i$, $\mu_i$ the eigenvalues of $\tilde B=\tfrac16AP$ (real at both $\beta$; values from the companion SL(2,C) data, not reproduced here), split by $\delta v=\pm\sqrt{-\mu_i\tilde N(-\mu_i)/\lambda}\,/\,\tilde\chi'(-\mu_i)$, i.e. $w\to-\lambda\mu_i$: at strong coupling the roots approach precisely the locus $\chi(w)=0$ that the reduction (3.3) excludes, so the sextic is the wrong tool there; the regime is outside the objective (`C4d-large-lambda-*`: the pairing and the splitting verified at $\lambda=10^4,10^6$ to $<3\times10^{-3}$).

\subsection*{Commentary}

*Criterion.* Translate $\Gamma$ through a real root $\hat\sigma_*$ and write $\delta=x+iy$, $x\in V_+$, $y\in V_-$, $H:=\mathrm{Hess}\,\hat{\mathcal V}(\hat\sigma_*)$ (1.5). $$
H\vert_{V_-}<0\qquad\text{and}\qquad H\vert_{V_+}>0, \tag{4.3}
$$
i.e. for $V_-=\mathrm{span}(v)$, $V_+=m^\perp$ ($m\cdot v\neq0$): $v^\top Hv<0$ and $H\vert_{m^\perp}>0$. At tree level, $H=A/12$, (4.3) is exactly the admissibility of $\Gamma$. When $\mathrm{In}(H)=(2,1,0)$,
$$
H\vert_{m^\perp}>0\iff m^\top H^{-1}m<0, \tag{4.4}
$$
by the cofactor identity $\hat m^\top H^{-1}\hat m=\det(H\vert_{m^\perp})/\det H$ ($\det H<0$; a $2\times2$ restriction with positive determinant is definite, and negative is excluded by $n_-(H)=1$ and interlacing). $$

$$
(complementarity $m\cdot v\neq0$ is generic in these open sets), and by Yuan's lemma (Math. Prog. 47 (1990) 53: two quadratic forms have a common strictly negative vector iff no convex combination $tA+(1-t)H$, $t\in[0,1]$, is positive semidefinite) each condition is decided exactly by $\max_{t\in[0,1]}\lambda_{\min}(tA+(1-t)H)<0$, resp. with the inverses. A root with $n_+(H)<2$ satisfies (4.3) on no contour.

on the cubic (4.1). \subsection*{Commentary}
- (4.1): the statement that the mean-field roots collapse onto R1's on-shell points $\Sigma=\Psi_c^{\otimes2}$ as $\lambda\to0$ is the three-dimensional form of 069 (2.3a) ($\sigma_+\to\sigma_D=1/a$, i.e. $\Psi_c^2=6/v$ in the one-dimensional model). It is exact in the model and uses only that $\operatorname{adj}$ of a rank-one-deficient symmetric $2\times2$ matrix is rank one. The $\pm$ sign is the sign of $-c(r)$: the two on-shell points have $c(r)<0$.
- (4.2): the fold is a property of the sextic (two real roots meeting); the $\lambda\to0$ expansion of §4.1 is only valid for $\lambda\ll\lambda_{\rm merge}$, which is why the $\beta=2$ asymptotics needed $\lambda\le10^{-6}$.

(The earlier wording "the log term turns $A$'s negative direction positive" was loose: $A$'s negative direction is not tangent to the wall.)
- (4.3)–(4.5) are the *local* criterion: whether a saddle contributes to the integral over $\Gamma$ is the Picard–Lefschetz question (whether its thimble enters the decomposition of $\Gamma$), which no local test decides; (4.3) is necessary for the thimble to be locally parallel to the chosen admissible representative and, as the table shows, it is representative-dependent. The representative-independent statement is (4.5): the on-shell roots are convergent saddles on *some* admissible contour at every sampled $\lambda$ (explicitly $\Gamma_n$), the apex and $-\Psi^{\otimes2}$ roots on none.
- The tree-level saddle $\hat\sigma_*=p$ of 064 has $H=A/12$, inertia $(2,1,0)$, negative direction $v_-$: with an indefinite HS kernel the stationary point that the $\Sigma$ integral is built on is a $(2,1,0)$ saddle, never a minimum. The on-shell roots are of the same type, with the negative direction rotated from $v_-$ towards the wall normal (weight $1/\lambda$ at small $\lambda$). The brief's criterion "minimum of $\mathcal V_{\rm eff}$" (Weinberg §21.6, written for a real auxiliary field with a definite kernel) is therefore one the tree-level saddle itself would not pass; in this model it decides "no minimum" correctly but is not the criterion that singles out the saddles of the rotated integral. What both criteria give, among the real roots: only the on-shell roots, the one-loop displacements of $\Sigma=\Psi_c^{\otimes2}$, pass the local convergent-saddle test; no real root without a classical antecedent passes. Whether they dominate the $\Sigma$ integral is not decided here: the Picard–Lefschetz decomposition is not computed; at leading order the complex pair (3.4), the one-loop displacement of $\Sigma=0$, passes (4.3) on $\Gamma_{\rm eig}$ and $\Gamma_{TD}$ (with $H\simeq\tfrac1{12}(A+aa^\top/h_4)$, $a=Ae_3$) but is exponentially subleading ($\operatorname{Re}\hat{\mathcal V}=O(\lambda\log\lambda)$ against $-c/4$); and along the wall normal the Gaussian width equals $\sqrt2\,d_{\rm wall}$. The same $\mathbb Z_2$ reading gives the on-shell root at $\langle\Psi\rangle=0$ a plain interpretation: with $V_3$ dropped the model is symmetric under $\Psi\to-\Psi$ and $\Sigma$ is even, so the root with $\Sigma\approx\Psi_c^{\otimes2}$ is the symmetric image of the two classical vacua $\pm\Psi_c$, not a new vacuum. Whether "condensate" is to be read as "minimum" (069's critic, the brief) or as "convergent saddle on the admissible contour" is a decision for the orchestrator; verdict (iii) — the classical structure displaced by one loop, no new stationary point — is the same under both.
