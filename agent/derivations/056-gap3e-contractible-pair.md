## 1. The BV setting (step 1)

### Setup

A1, A2, A4, A5. Fields $\phi^i=(\Psi,Y)$, both even ($Y:=\tilde X$ at $O(g_s^3)$, 016 §1;
$Y=\Sigma_2-\Sigma_2^*(\Psi)$ at $O(g_s^4)$, 015 §4.2 / 053 A1). Ghost $c\in\mathcal H_0$
(odd, $\mathrm{gh}=1$), the gauge parameter $\Lambda$ promoted to a field. Antifields
$\phi^+_i=(\Psi^+,Y^+)$ and $c^+$, odd/even respectively, $\mathrm{gh}(\Psi^+)=\mathrm{gh}(Y^+)=-1$,
$\mathrm{gh}(c^+)=-2$. Pairings $\Psi^+\cdot\delta\Psi$ and $Y^+\cdot\delta Y$ are the
(BPZ / $p$-induced) duality pairings of the finite model, written as matrix contractions
$\Psi^+_a\,\delta\Psi_a$, $Y^+_I\,\delta Y_I$ in the bases of 043/053 (the $\Lambda_I$ basis of
$\mathrm{Sym}^2$).

### Derivation

The classical action, in either truncation (043 (5.5), 053 (5.5)):
$$
S[\Psi,Y]=S_{\rm eff}(\Psi)+\Phi^*(Y;\Psi)+\mathbb F(\Psi),\qquad
\Phi^*=-\tfrac{g_s^2}{4!}\mathcal K_\Psi(Y,Y)+\tfrac{g_s^4}{6!}T(Y,Y,Y)\ \ (T\equiv0\text{ at }O(g_s^3)),
\tag{1.1}
$$
with $\mathbb F=\tfrac{g_s^4}{600}vK_\Psi^{-1}v$ at $O(g_s^3)$ (beyond the truncation order)
and $\mathbb F=\mathbb F_5=O(g_s^5)$ at $O(g_s^4)$ (015 §4.2, dressed). Gauge law (A5,
R17/011 (B.8), R19, 053 (1.2)):
$$
\delta_\Lambda\Psi=Q_B\Lambda+g_s\ell_2(\Psi,\Lambda)+\dots=:u_\Lambda(\Psi),\qquad
\delta_\Lambda Y=F_{u_\Lambda}(Y;\Psi),
\tag{1.2}
$$
with $F_u$ any admissible representative: $F^{(17)}_u=R_uY=-\tfrac12M_\Psi^{-1}(\partial_uM_\Psi)Y$
at $O(g_s^3)$, $F^{(19)}_u$ (implicit, $Y$-nonlinear) at $O(g_s^4)$, or the flat ones
$\hat R_u Y$ (043) and $F^{\rm flat}_u$ (053 (5.6)). The admissible family is
$F_u+\mathcal K^{-1}S_u\nu$, $S_u$ antisymmetric (R45, R57 C1); every member gives
$\delta_\Lambda\Phi^*=0$ exactly, hence $\delta_\Lambda S=\delta_\Lambda S_{\rm eff}+\delta_\Lambda\mathbb F=O(g_s^{k+1})$
at truncation order $k$ (geometric master equation of $S_{\rm eff}$ through $V_{0,k+2}$).

The gauge algebra (R45, R57, 043 §5, 053 (5.7)): with $u_i:=u_{\Lambda_i}$,
$$
[\delta_1,\delta_2]\Psi=u_{\Lambda_{12}}(\Psi)+\mathcal D_\Psi(\Lambda_1,\Lambda_2)\,E_\Psi\text{-terms},\qquad
[\delta_1,\delta_2]Y=F_{u_{12}}(Y)+\partial_wF\big|_{w=\mathcal D_\Psi}+\mathcal E_{12}(Y),
\tag{1.3}
$$
where $\mathcal E_{12}=0$ for the flat representatives and
$\mathcal E_{12}=\tfrac{g_s^2}{100}[A_{u_1},A_{u_2}]Y+O(g_s^3)$ for R17/R19. $\mathcal E$ is
exactly $E_Y$-proportional ($E_Y=\partial_YS=-\tfrac{g_s^2}{12}\mathcal K\nu(Y)$, 053 (2.1)):
$\mathcal E_{12}=\mu^{\mathcal E}_{12}E_Y$ with $\mu^{\mathcal E}$ antisymmetric (R45: the defect
is a trivial symmetry, R57 C2). The minimal BV action, to antifield number 2 (Henneaux–Teitelboim
(17.29)–(17.31); Batalin–Vilkovisky 1983 for an open algebra),
$$
S_{\rm BV}=S+\Psi^+\!\cdot u_c(\Psi)+Y^+\!\cdot F_c(Y;\Psi)+c^+\!\cdot\tfrac12 f(c,c)
+\tfrac14\,\phi^+_i\phi^+_j\,M^{ji}_{\alpha\beta}c^\beta c^\alpha+O((\phi^+)^3),
\tag{1.4}
$$
where $M^{ij}$ are the structure functions of the $E$-terms of (1.3):
$M^{\Psi\Psi}$ from $\mathcal D_\Psi$ (the $L_\infty$ open algebra, Task 7),
$M^{Y\Psi}$ from $\partial_wF|_{w=\mathcal D_\Psi}$ (both representatives), and
$$
M^{YY}_{12}=\mu^{\mathcal E}_{12}=-\tfrac{12}{g_s^2}\,\mathcal E_{12}\,\mathcal K^{-1}\ (\text{at }T=0),
\qquad\text{the }\lambda^\Sigma\text{ term of R45 in BV form},
\tag{1.5}
$$
present in the R17/R19 representative and **absent** for the flat ones. The
$Y^+$-quadratic term $\tfrac14Y^+\mu^{\mathcal E}Y^+cc$ is R45's $\lambda^\Sigma$: this
identification is the object of C3 (§4). The taxonomy of R22 for the *forced* part of
$F^{(17)}$ (symmetric $\mu$, not a trivial symmetry, not a standalone symmetry) is untouched:
§3 shows that in the hatted variables the forced part is absorbed into the shift of
$\hat\Psi^+$, (2.7), and the $\hat Y^+$-term is empty.

### Commentary

- (1.4) is the standard antifield expansion of the solution of the classical master
  equation for an open algebra with $E$-terms; the coefficient of the antifield-quadratic
  term is fixed by $(S_{\rm BV},S_{\rm BV})=0$ at antifield number 1. Only its structure is
  used below; its $\Psi$-sector content ($M^{\Psi\Psi}$, $f$) is Task 7's.
- (1.5): $\mathcal E_{12}Y=\mu^{\mathcal E}_{12}E_Y$ with $E_Y=-\tfrac{g_s^2}{12}\mathcal KY$
  at $O(g_s^3)$ gives $\mu^{\mathcal E}=-\tfrac{12}{g_s^2}\mathcal E\mathcal K^{-1}$; antisymmetry
  of $\mu^{\mathcal E}$ is $\mathcal K\mathcal E$ antisymmetric, which is 043 C2/(4.4) ($\mathcal E$
  is $\mathfrak k$-valued: $K$-antisymmetric). At $O(g_s^4)$ the same with $\nu$ in place of $Y$
  (053 (4.3)).
- The $Y^+$ ghost number: $\mathrm{gh}(Y^+)=-1$ with $\mathrm{gh}(Y)=0$ in the *BV* (target
  space) grading; the worldsheet $N_{\rm gh}$ of $Y^+$ is not pinned (016 §1, flagged) and
  nothing below depends on it.

### Result

The BV data are (1.1)–(1.5). $\square$

## 2. The field redefinition as a canonical transformation (step 2, C1)

### Setup

A1, A4. $G_\Psi:V\to V$ the admissible redefinition, $\hat Y=G_\Psi(Y)$: at $O(g_s^3)$
$G_\Psi(Y)=M_\Psi^{1/2}Y$ (043 §5, $M_\Psi=1-\tfrac{g_s}{5}A_\Psi$), at $O(g_s^4)$
$G_\Psi(Y)=M_{\mathcal K}^{1/2}\tilde G_\Psi(Y)$ (053 (5.3)–(5.5)). $D_YG$ is the Jacobian
(matrix $(D_YG)_{IJ}=\partial G_I/\partial Y_J$), $\partial_\Psi G$ the matrix
$(\partial_\Psi G)_{Ia}=\partial G_I/\partial\Psi_a$, and $\partial_wG:=(\partial_\Psi G)w$.

### Derivation

Take the odd, $\mathrm{gh}=-1$ generating function of the mixed type (old fields, new
antifields), Henneaux–Teitelboim ch. 15 (canonical transformations of the antibracket:
$\hat\phi^i=\partial F/\partial\hat\phi^+_i$, $\phi^+_i=\partial F/\partial\phi^i$),
$$
F(\Psi,Y,c;\hat\Psi^+,\hat Y^+,\hat c^+)=\hat Y^+\!\cdot G_\Psi(Y)+\hat\Psi^+\!\cdot\Psi+\hat c^+\!\cdot c .
\tag{2.1}
$$
The ghost term gives $\hat c=c$, $c^+=\hat c^+$: the ghost sector is unchanged, and it is
omitted from (2.2)–(2.4).
Then
$$
\hat Y=\frac{\partial F}{\partial\hat Y^+}=G_\Psi(Y),\qquad
\hat\Psi=\frac{\partial F}{\partial\hat\Psi^+}=\Psi,
\tag{2.2}
$$
$$
Y^+=\frac{\partial F}{\partial Y}=(D_YG_\Psi)^{\!\top}\hat Y^+,\qquad
\Psi^+=\frac{\partial F}{\partial\Psi}=\hat\Psi^++(\partial_\Psi G_\Psi)^{\!\top}\hat Y^+ ,
\tag{2.3}
$$
and, inverting (the Jacobian is invertible: $D_YG=M^{1/2}(1+O(Y))$, A3/A4 of 053),
$$
\boxed{\;\hat Y^+=\big((D_YG_\Psi)^{-1}\big)^{\!\top}Y^+,\qquad
\hat\Psi^+=\Psi^+-(\partial_\Psi G_\Psi)^{\!\top}\big((D_YG_\Psi)^{-1}\big)^{\!\top}Y^+ .\;}
\tag{2.4}
$$
Explicitly, at $O(g_s^3)$ ($G=M_\Psi^{1/2}Y$, $D_YG=M_\Psi^{1/2}$, $\partial_wG=(\partial_wM_\Psi^{1/2})Y$):
$$
\hat Y^+=(M_\Psi^{-1/2})^{\!\top}Y^+,\qquad
\hat\Psi^+_a=\Psi^+_a-Y^+\!\cdot M_\Psi^{-1/2}(\partial_aM_\Psi^{1/2})\,Y
=\Psi^+_a+Y^+\!\cdot\hat R_{e_a}Y ,
\tag{2.5}
$$
using $\hat R_u=(\partial_uM^{-1/2})M^{1/2}=-M^{-1/2}\partial_uM^{1/2}$ (043 §5). At $O(g_s^4)$
($G=M_{\mathcal K}^{1/2}\tilde G$, $D_YG=M_{\mathcal K}^{1/2}D_Y\tilde G$,
$\partial_wG=(\partial_wM_{\mathcal K}^{1/2})\tilde G+M_{\mathcal K}^{1/2}\partial_w\tilde G$):
$$
\hat Y^+=\big((D_Y\tilde G)^{-1}M_{\mathcal K}^{-1/2}\big)^{\!\top}Y^+,\qquad
\hat\Psi^+_a=\Psi^+_a+Y^+\!\cdot F^{\rm flat}_{e_a}(Y),
\tag{2.6}
$$
since $-(D_YG)^{-1}\partial_aG=F^{\rm flat}_{e_a}$ by definition (053 (5.6)). Both cases read
$$
\hat\Psi^+\!\cdot w=\Psi^+\!\cdot w+Y^+\!\cdot F^{\rm flat}_w(Y)\quad\text{for every }w\in\mathcal H_0 .
\tag{2.7}
$$

**Antibracket preservation.** (2.2)–(2.3) is the cotangent lift of the diffeomorphism
$(\Psi,Y)\mapsto(\Psi,G_\Psi(Y))$ of field space; the odd Liouville form is invariant,
$$
\hat\phi^+_i\,d\hat\phi^i=\hat\phi^+_i\frac{\partial\hat\phi^i}{\partial\phi^j}\,d\phi^j
=\phi^+_j\,d\phi^j
\tag{2.8}
$$
by (2.3), hence so is the odd symplectic form $d\phi^+_i\,d\phi^i$ and the antibracket
$(\cdot,\cdot)$ it defines: $(F\circ\tau,G\circ\tau)=(F,G)\circ\tau$ for the map $\tau$
(2.2)–(2.4). Ghost number and parity: $F$ is odd of $\mathrm{gh}=-1$ (antifield linear, fields
even), so $\tau$ preserves both gradings. Compatibility with the quotient by $\ker p$ and
with reality: $G_\Psi$ has both (053 §5, 043 C3.3), and (2.4) is built from $G$ alone.

### Commentary

- (2.4): the antifields transform as covectors — $Y^+$ by the inverse-transposed Jacobian,
  $\Psi^+$ picking up the $\Psi$-dependence of the redefinition. This is the standard rule; it
  is derived rather than quoted so that (2.7) is available.
- (2.7) is the whole content of §3: the $\Psi^+$-shift is exactly "$Y^+$ contracted with the
  flat law". It holds for any $G$, with $F^{\rm flat}:=-(D_YG)^{-1}\partial G$.
- (2.8): the invariance of the Liouville form under a cotangent lift needs no property of $G$
  beyond invertibility of $D_YG$; parities of the antifields do not enter, since the
  generating function is linear in them and the fields are even.

### Result

C1: the transformation (2.2), (2.4) is canonical, with (2.5)–(2.7). $\square$

## 3. The transformed BV action and the contractible pair (step 3, C2)

### Setup

A1–A5, C1. $S^{\rm flat}_{\rm BV}$ denotes (1.4) written with the flat law
$F_c=F^{\rm flat}_{u_c}$; $\tau$ the canonical map of §2.

### Derivation

**(a) The antifield-linear terms.** By (2.3) and $F^{\rm flat}_u=-(D_YG)^{-1}\partial_uG$,
$$
Y^+\!\cdot F^{\rm flat}_{u_c}(Y)=\hat Y^+\!\cdot(D_YG)F^{\rm flat}_{u_c}=-\hat Y^+\!\cdot\partial_{u_c}G,
\qquad
\Psi^+\!\cdot u_c=\hat\Psi^+\!\cdot u_c+\hat Y^+\!\cdot\partial_{u_c}G ,
\tag{3.1}
$$
so
$$
\boxed{\;\Psi^+\!\cdot u_c+Y^+\!\cdot F^{\rm flat}_{u_c}(Y)=\hat\Psi^+\!\cdot u_c(\Psi)\;}
\tag{3.2}
$$
exactly, with $u_c(\Psi)$ the *unchanged* $\Psi$-law (A5: it does not depend on $Y$, so its
pull-back by $\tau$ is itself). The $\hat Y^+$-term is identically zero — the BV form of
$\delta\hat Y=0$ (043 (5.2), 053 (5.7)). For any *other* admissible representative
$F=F^{\rm flat}+\mathcal K^{-1}S\nu$ the same substitution leaves
$\hat Y^+\!\cdot(D_YG)\mathcal K^{-1}S_{u_c}\nu(Y)\neq0$: the $\hat Y^+$-term vanishes iff the
law is the flat one (break leg of check `C2.1`).

**(b) The classical action.** By 043 (5.5) / 053 (5.5), in the hatted variables
$$
S=S_{\rm eff}(\Psi)+\mathbb F(\Psi)-\tfrac{g_s^2}{4!}\,p(\hat Y,\hat Y),
\qquad \partial_\Psi\big[p(\hat Y,\hat Y)\big]_{\hat Y}=0 .
\tag{3.3}
$$
Consequently the $\Psi$ equation of motion of the full action at fixed $\hat Y$ is the
effective one, $E_\Psi|_{\hat Y}=\partial_\Psi(S_{\rm eff}+\mathbb F)$, whereas at fixed $Y$
it is not: $E_\Psi|_Y\cdot w=E_\Psi|_{\hat Y}\cdot w-E_Y\cdot F^{\rm flat}_w(Y)$ (chain rule
through $\hat Y=G_\Psi(Y)$, $\partial_wG=-(D_YG)F^{\rm flat}_w$). This is why "closes modulo
the $\Psi$ equation of motion alone" (043 §5, 053 (5.7)) is a statement about
$E_\Psi|_{\hat Y}$, and why in the $(\Psi,Y)$ chart the $E$-terms of the algebra mix
$E_\Psi|_Y$ and $E_Y$ even for the flat law.

**(c) The gauge algebra in the hatted chart.** $\delta_\Lambda\hat Y=0$ for every $\Lambda$,
so $[\delta_1,\delta_2]\hat Y=0=\delta_{\Lambda_{12}}\hat Y$: no $E$-term of any kind in the
$\hat Y$ row. The $\Psi$ row is the $L_\infty$ algebra of $S_{\rm eff}$ (R45/R57, Zwiebach
1993): $[\delta_1,\delta_2]\Psi=u_{\Lambda_{12}}+M^{\Psi\Psi}_{12}E_\Psi|_{\hat Y}$, with
$E_\Psi|_{\hat Y}$ the effective EOM by (3.3). Hence the structure functions of (1.4) in the
hatted chart are $M^{\hat Y\hat Y}=M^{\hat Y\Psi}=0$ and $M^{\Psi\Psi}$ that of $S_{\rm eff}$
alone, and the same holds at every antifield number: the BV action in the hatted chart is
$$
\boxed{\;S_{\rm BV}=S^{\Psi}_{\rm BV}\big[\Psi,\hat\Psi^+,c,c^+\big]-\tfrac{g_s^2}{4!}\,p(\hat Y,\hat Y),
\qquad
S^\Psi_{\rm BV}=S_{\rm eff}+\mathbb F+\hat\Psi^+\!\cdot u_c+c^+\!\cdot\tfrac12f(c,c)
+\tfrac14\hat\Psi^+\hat\Psi^+M^{\Psi\Psi}cc+\dots\;}
\tag{3.4}
$$
where $S^\Psi_{\rm BV}$ is the BV action of the truncated $L_\infty$ theory
$S_{\rm eff}+\mathbb F$ (its full antifield tower is Task 7's; only its independence of
$(\hat Y,\hat Y^+)$ is used here).

**(d) Contractible pair with constant coefficients.** With $s:=(\cdot,S_{\rm BV})$,
$$
s\hat Y=\frac{\partial S_{\rm BV}}{\partial\hat Y^+}=0,\qquad
s\hat Y^+=\pm\frac{\partial S_{\rm BV}}{\partial\hat Y}=\mp\tfrac{g_s^2}{12}\,p\,\hat Y ,
\tag{3.5}
$$
$p$ a constant invertible matrix on the quotient (PH1/PH5). So $(\hat Y^+,\,p\hat Y)$ is a
contractible pair for $s$ in the sense of Henneaux–Teitelboim §8.4.1: generators $(u,v)$
with $su=v$, $sv=0$, $v$ a constant invertible linear image of $\hat Y$. Write
$s\hat Y^+=b\,p\hat Y$, $b=\mp\tfrac{g_s^2}{12}$. The contracting homotopy is
$\rho=\mp\tfrac{12}{g_s^2}\,\hat Y^+\!\cdot p^{-1}\partial/\partial\hat Y$ (ghost number $-1$),
with $s\rho+\rho s=N_{\hat Y,\hat Y^+}$ (the counting operator of the pair):
$(s\rho+\rho s)\hat Y=b^{-1}p^{-1}s\hat Y^+=\hat Y$ and
$(s\rho+\rho s)\hat Y^+=\rho(b\,p\hat Y)=\hat Y^+$ ($p$ symmetric). It anticommutes with
$s_\Psi$ (both are odd) because the coefficients are $\Psi$- and antifield-independent. "Constant
coefficients" is exactly (3.3): for the R17/R19 law in the $(\Psi,Y)$ chart one has instead
$sY^+\propto\mathcal K_\Psi Y+\dots$, still a contractible pair but with a $\Psi$-dependent
homotopy that does not anticommute with $s_\Psi$.

**(e) Decoupling of the master equation (lemma, proved).** *Let $S=S_1[\phi_1,\phi_1^+]+S_2[\phi_2]$
with $\phi_2$ even fields, $S_2$ independent of $\phi_1$, $\phi_1^+$ and of $\phi_2^+$. Then
$(S,S)=(S_1,S_1)$.* Proof: the antibracket is bilinear and a derivation in each slot, and
mixed brackets need a field and its own antifield in the two slots:
$(S_1,S_2)=\partial_rS_1/\partial\phi_2\cdot\partial_lS_2/\partial\phi_2^+-\partial_rS_1/\partial\phi_2^+\cdot\partial_lS_2/\partial\phi_2=0$
(the first term because $S_2$ has no $\phi_2^+$, the second because $S_1$ has no $\phi_2^+$);
$(S_2,S_2)=2\,\partial_rS_2/\partial\phi_2\cdot\partial_lS_2/\partial\phi_2^+=0$. $\square$
Applied to (3.4) with $\phi_1=(\Psi,c)$, $\phi_2=\hat Y$:
$$
\boxed{\;(S_{\rm BV},S_{\rm BV})=(S^\Psi_{\rm BV},S^\Psi_{\rm BV})\;}
\tag{3.6}
$$
identically in $g_s$: the master equation for the cubic action in the hatted chart holds
iff it holds for the $\Psi$-sector, at whatever order the latter does (through the truncation
order, by the geometric master equation of $S_{\rm eff}$; Task 7). Properness: $S_{\rm BV}$
is proper iff $S^\Psi_{\rm BV}$ is, since the Hessian of $-\tfrac{g_s^2}{4!}p(\hat Y,\hat Y)$
has full rank on the quotient and the pair adds no gauge direction; that $S^\Psi_{\rm BV}$ is
proper to the truncation order is C2.4 (Task 7), open.

**(f) The elimination theorem (paraphrased, not checked against the source; Henneaux,
Phys. Lett. B 238 (1990) 299; Henneaux–Teitelboim ch. 17).**
*Let $S_0[\phi,y]$ be a classical action and $y^a$ auxiliary fields, i.e. the equations
$\partial S_0/\partial y^a=0$ can be solved algebraically as $y^a=Y^a(\phi)$, and let
$\bar S_0[\phi]=S_0[\phi,Y(\phi)]$. Let $S[\phi,\phi^+,y,y^+,\dots]$ be a proper solution of
the classical master equation for $S_0$ (with its ghosts and antifields). Then (ii) the
action $\bar S$ obtained by eliminating $(y,y^+)$ is a proper solution of the classical
master equation for $\bar S_0$; (iii) the BRST cohomologies of $S$ and $\bar S$ are
isomorphic.* (The source's precise form of the elimination condition, clause (i) of the
first version of this record, is not reproduced: as first stated it asked that
$\partial S/\partial y^+_a=0$ be solved for $y^+_a$, which in the hatted chart determines
nothing, since $\hat Y^+$ drops out of $S_{\rm BV}$.) In the hatted chart no theorem is needed
for the reduced action itself: $\partial S_{\rm BV}/\partial\hat Y^+\equiv0$ and
$\partial S_{\rm BV}/\partial\hat Y=-\tfrac{g_s^2}{12}p\hat Y=0$ gives $\hat Y=0$, so

properness of $\bar S$ and (iii), presupposes that $S_{\rm BV}$, hence $S^\Psi_{\rm BV}$, is a
proper solution: conditional on C2.4 (Task 7), and applied order by order in $g_s$ to the
truncation order, where $\delta S=O(g_s^{k+1})$ (1.2). In the $(\Psi,Y)$ chart the
elimination sets $Y=0$; there $\partial_\Psi G_\Psi(0)=0$, so $\hat\Psi^+=\Psi^+$ and the
reduced action is again $S^\Psi_{\rm BV}$.

### Commentary

- (3.2) is exact, not perturbative: it uses only the chain rule (2.3) and the definition

## 4. $\lambda^\Sigma$ and the trivial symmetry $R^{(17)}-\hat R$ (step 3c, C3)

### Setup

A1–A5, C1, C2. Both BV actions are written in the *same* chart $(\Psi,Y,\Psi^+,Y^+)$, at
$O(g_s^3)$: $S^{\rm flat}_{\rm BV}$ with the law $\hat R_cY$ and $S^{(17)}_{\rm BV}$ with
$R^{(17)}_cY$. $D_u:=R^{(17)}_u-\hat R_u=\tfrac{g_s^2}{200}[A_\Psi,A_u]+O(g_s^3)$ (043 (5.4)),
$\mathcal K D_u$ antisymmetric (043 C3.2, check `D4`).

### Derivation

The two laws differ by the trivial symmetry $D_cY=\mu_cE_Y$ with
$$
\mu_c:=-\tfrac{12}{g_s^2}\,D_c\,\mathcal K^{-1},\qquad \mu_c^{\top}=-\mu_c
\quad(\Leftrightarrow\ \mathcal KD_c\ \text{antisymmetric}),
\tag{4.1}
$$
$E_Y=-\tfrac{g_s^2}{12}\mathcal KY$. Take the odd, $\mathrm{gh}=-1$ generator, quadratic in
antifields (Henneaux–Teitelboim: trivial gauge transformations are generated by
antifield-quadratic canonical transformations),
$$
\Xi:=\tfrac12\,Y^+\!\cdot\mu_c\,Y^+ .
\tag{4.2}
$$
Then, with $S_0=S$ the classical action,
$$
(S_0,\Xi)=\frac{\partial S_0}{\partial Y}\cdot\frac{\partial\Xi}{\partial Y^+}
=E_Y\cdot\mu_c^{\top}Y^+\ \dot=\ Y^+\!\cdot\mu_cE_Y=Y^+\!\cdot D_cY ,
\tag{4.3}
$$
(the sign $\dot=$ absorbs the convention A4 and the antisymmetry of $\mu_c$; whichever it is,
$\Xi\to-\Xi$ fixes it, but the sign and normalisation this then induces at antifield number
two are not computed, C3.3): the antifield-linear terms of $S^{(17)}_{\rm BV}$ and
$S^{\rm flat}_{\rm BV}$ differ exactly by $(S_0,\Xi)$. At antifield number two, the term of
$S^{(17)}_{\rm BV}$ absent from $S^{\rm flat}_{\rm BV}$ is R45's $\lambda^\Sigma$, in BV form
$\tfrac14Y^+\mu^{\mathcal E}_{\alpha\beta}Y^+c^\beta c^\alpha$ with (1.5)
$\mu^{\mathcal E}_{12}=-\tfrac{12}{g_s^2}\mathcal E_{12}\mathcal K^{-1}$. The canonical
transformation $e^{{\rm ad}_\Xi}$ produces at antifield number two, from the antifield-linear
part $S_1^{\rm flat}=\Psi^+\!\cdot u_c+Y^+\!\cdot\hat R_cY+c^+\!\cdot\tfrac12f(c,c)$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(S_1^{\rm flat},\Xi)\big|_{Y^+Y^+}
&=\tfrac12\,Y^+\!\cdot\big(\partial_{u_{c}}\mu_{c}\big)Y^+ + Y^+\!\cdot\hat R_c\mu_cY^+
+\tfrac14\,Y^+\!\cdot\mu_{f(c,c)}Y^+ ,
\\
\tfrac12\big((S_0,\Xi),\Xi\big)\big|_{Y^+Y^+}&=\tfrac12\,Y^+\!\cdot D_c\mu_cY^+ ,
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
$$
the third term being $(c^+\!\cdot\tfrac12f(c,c),\Xi)$, the $-\mu_{[1,2]}$ term of the
covariant exterior derivative (it was missing from the first version of this list); signs
and normalisations in (4.4) are as far as tracked, not computed (C3.3). Here $\partial_{u_c}\mu_c$ is the $\Psi$-derivative of $\mu_{c}$ along $u_{c}$, antisymmetrised
by the two ghosts: in components $\tfrac14Y^+(\partial_{u_\alpha}\mu_\beta-\partial_{u_\beta}\mu_\alpha)Y^+c^\beta c^\alpha$.
Orders in $g_s$: $\mu=O(1)$, $\partial_u\mu=O(1)$, $\hat R\mu=O(g_s)$,
$\mu_{f(c,c)}=O(g_s)$ ($f=O(g_s)$), $D\mu=O(g_s^2)$, and
the $Y^+Y^+$ term of $S^{\rm flat}_{\rm BV}$ itself is $\tfrac14(\Theta^{\top}Y^+)^2M^{\Psi\Psi}=O(g_s^4)$
(§3(b): $\Theta=(\partial_\Psi G)^\top(D_YG)^{-\top}=O(g_s)$, $M^{\Psi\Psi}=O(g_s^2)$). Hence the
classical content at leading order is the matrix identity
$$
\boxed{\;\mu^{\mathcal E}_{12}=\partial_{u_1}\mu_{u_2}-\partial_{u_2}\mu_{u_1}+O(g_s)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal E_{12}\big|_{g_s^2}=\big(\partial_{u_1}D_{u_2}-\partial_{u_2}D_{u_1}\big)\big|_{g_s^2}\;}
\tag{4.5}
$$
which is the leading term of 043 (4.3) applied to $R^{(17)}=\hat R+D$ with $\mathcal E[\hat R]=0$:
$\mathcal E[\hat R+D]=\mathcal E[\hat R]+\mathrm dD+[\hat R,D]+[D,D]$, the last two of order
$g_s^3$, $g_s^4$. Analytically, $D_u=\tfrac{g_s^2}{200}[A_\Psi,A_u]$ gives
$\partial_{u_1}D_{u_2}-\partial_{u_2}D_{u_1}=\tfrac{g_s^2}{200}\big([A_{u_1},A_{u_2}]-[A_{u_2},A_{u_1}]\big)
=\tfrac{g_s^2}{100}[A_{u_1},A_{u_2}]=\mathcal E_{12}|_{g_s^2}$ (R45): the coefficient
$-1/200$ of the shift and $1/100$ of the defect are the same number seen from the two sides
of (4.5). Machine: `C3` (exact $g_s^2$ coefficients via nilpotent $g_s$, $N=2,3$, two seeds;
break: a factor $\tfrac12$ in the generator fails), `C3-antisym`, `C3-E2`. (4.5) is a
classical identity; its identification with the antifield-number-two coefficient of the BV
action — sign, the $\tfrac12$ vs $\tfrac14$ normalisation of (4.4) relative to (1.5) — is not
tracked (C3.3). The $f$-term of (4.4) is $O(g_s)$ and does not affect (4.5) at leading
order.

**All orders (conditional on C2.4, Task 7).** Beyond leading order, (4.5) receives the
$[\hat R,D]$, $[D,D]$, $\hat R\mu$, $\mu_{f(c,c)}$, $D\mu$ terms of (4.4) and the
$O(g_s^4)$ term of $S^{\rm flat}_{\rm BV}$, and $\Xi$ must be completed by higher antifield
terms; that both actions are related by *some* canonical transformation follows from the
uniqueness theorem (Henneaux–Teitelboim ch. 17; proof by homological perturbation theory in
Fisch–Henneaux, CMP 128 (1990) 627, from memory, bibliographic details unverified; *the proper
solution of the classical master equation with a given classical action $S_0$ is unique up to
canonical transformations*: if $S$ and $S'$ are proper solutions with $S|_{\phi^+=0}=S'|_{\phi^+=0}=S_0$,
there is a canonical transformation $\sigma$, connected to the identity and of the form
$e^{{\rm ad}_\Xi}$ order by order in the antifields, with $S'=S\circ\sigma$), whose
antifield-linear part is (4.3). The theorem presupposes that both actions are proper
solutions, which holds only if $S^\Psi_{\rm BV}$ is (C2.4), and for the truncated action only
modulo $O(g_s^{k+1})$: it is applied order by order in $g_s$ to the truncation order. The
removability of $\lambda^\Sigma$ is this consequence of C3.2, not of (4.3)–(4.4). The
classical leading-order identity (4.5) is what is derived here; the existence of the full
$\sigma$ is cited, not derived.

### Commentary

- (4.3) is the standard "trivial symmetry $\leftrightarrow$ antifield-quadratic canonical
  transformation" correspondence; its content is the antisymmetry (4.1), which is 043 C3.2.
- (4.4): $(\Psi^+\!\cdot u_c,\Xi)$ contracts $\partial_{\Psi^+}$ with $\partial_\Psi\Xi$, giving
  the $\Psi$-derivative of $\mu_c$ along $u_c$; $(Y^+\hat R_cY,\Xi)$ contracts $\partial_Y$ with
  $\partial_{Y^+}\Xi$.
- (4.5) settles the classical side of the classification asked by 043 §7 item 5: the
  defect $\mathcal E$ is $\mathrm dD$ of the trivial symmetry $D=R^{(17)}-\hat R$ at leading
  order. That the $\lambda^\Sigma$ term is removable by a canonical transformation in the BV
  complex follows from C3.2, conditionally on C2.4; the explicit $({\rm ad}_\Xi)$-form of
  that coefficient at antifield number two is C3.3, not tracked. In the hatted chart
  $\lambda^\Sigma$ is absent altogether (C2).

### Result

C3: the classical leading-order identity (4.5), with (4.1); the BV normalisation at
antifield number two open (C3.3); all orders and removability by the cited uniqueness
theorem, conditional on C2.4 (Task 7) and to the truncation order. $\square$
