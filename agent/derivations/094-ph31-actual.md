The mechanism needs one $q$-independent map $\Phi$ and one operator $G$.

*What survives for (i.1).* (a) For R80's derivation as written there is no single $G$: $p=\int_q\Phi_q^\dagger\tilde G_q\Phi_q$ is a superposition of Gram forms with different maps. The lift $\hat\Phi:\Sigma\mapsto(\Phi_q\Sigma)_{q\in Q_V}$, $\hat G=\int^\oplus\tilde G_q$ restores $p=\hat\Phi^\dagger\hat G\hat\Phi$, but the transplant of PH31 to it — $\hat\Phi$ onto the $W$-valued functions on $Q_V$ modulo $\ker\hat G$ — is (L) below, of which the pointwise F1 tested here is a necessary condition (fix pass #12; an earlier version called it false at every finite level, on the wrong premise that the $q$-dependence is finite-dimensional). At fixed finite $(K,L)$ the set of $q$ where (F1) holds is open (maximal rank is an open condition), and where the dependence on $q$ is real-analytic its complement is an analytic set, so on each connected piece (F1) holds either nowhere or almost everywhere (no longer used: C4.6 now argues through (v.6), which needs no regularity of the section, fix pass #8).

*Relation to the lifted statement (fix pass 3, critic #1).* The integrated $p_{2,2}=\hat\Phi^\dagger\hat G\hat\Phi$ needs a *joint* statement, not a pointwise one:

- **(L)** the lifted map $\hat\Phi:\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0\to L^2(Q_V,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}\theta;W^{\rm even})$, $\hat\Phi\Sigma=(\pi_W\Phi_q\Sigma)_{q\in Q_V}$, is onto modulo $\ker\hat G$ in the topology in which $p^{-1}$ is built (image-alone form: its image is dense).

*Necessity.* Write F1$_K(q)$ for $\pi_{\le K}\pi_W\Phi_q(\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0)=W^{\rm even}_{\le K}$ (inputs of unbounded level; the finite-level F1$(q)$ at $(K,L)$ implies it). If F1$_K(q)$ fails on a set $S\subset Q_V$ of positive measure, choose measurable unit vectors $\chi_q\in W^{\rm even}_{\le K}$ with $\chi_q\perp\pi_{\le K}\pi_W\Phi_q(\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0)$ for $q\in S$. Then
$$
f\;\longmapsto\;\int_S\langle\chi_q,\pi_{\le K}f(q)\rangle\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}\theta \tag{i.2}
$$
is a non-zero continuous functional on $L^2(Q_V;W^{\rm even})$ ($\pi_{\le K}$ is continuous, $S$ has finite measure, and it is non-zero on $f=\chi\mathbf 1_S$) that annihilates the lifted image, which is therefore not dense. So **F1 (F1$_K$ for a.e. $q$, every $K$) is necessary for the image-alone form of (L)**; (F2) (constant functionals only) is the weaker necessary condition. The measurable selection of $\chi_q$ and the $L^2$ setting are taken as given. [C0.1]

*Non-sufficiency.* F1 is pointwise surjectivity and says nothing about reaching independent values at different $q$: by C2 a lifted image vector depends on $q$ only through $(\lambda(q),|\kappa(q)|)$, and on $\Gamma$ (see (a)) $f(q)=f(\bar q)$, so (L) fails in the uniform topology whatever F1 does; in $L^2$ that particular mechanism is null. F1 is therefore *the pointwise necessary condition for the integrated analogue of PH31*, with R84/R86's evidence bearing on it, and not sufficient. [C0.1]

*What stage 2 must test (the formulation question).* (1) The finite-level form of (L): at $n$ sampled $q_j$, the joint rank of the stacked map $(\pi_{\le K}\pi_W\Phi_{q_j})_{j\le n}$ on $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0^{\le L}$, the columns including sampled input momenta $\Delta$, against $n\cdot\dim W^{\rm even}_{\le K}$ rows, **with the plane-wave prefactor $|\kappa(q_j)|^{\alpha'(P^2/4+\Delta^2)}$ restored**. By C2.5 each column is a polynomial of bidegree $\le(N_1+N_2,\bar N_1+\bar N_2)$ in $(\lambda_j,\bar\lambda_j)$ times $|\kappa_j|^{c+\alpha'\Delta^2}$, so at $L=2$ the $q$-dependence available is 25 monomials times functions of $\Delta$; a deficit at small $n$ would locate what the pointwise F1 misses. (2) A kernel-side condition: the composites $G_5\,p^{-1}G_5'$ of R80's use need the other vertices' $\Sigma$-legs to lie in $\operatorname{im}\hat\Phi^\dagger=(\bigcap_q\ker\Phi_q)^\perp$; this is automatic in the fixed-halves model (one $\Phi$), not for the actual theory, where by the reasoning of C2 a general two-puncture circle state is the rigid state at $(\lambda_1,\lambda_2;\bar\lambda_1,\bar\lambda_2)$ with $U_{\kappa_1}\otimes U_{\kappa_2}$ (no Klein relation). Neither is computed in this record; no number of it changes.

*Which one is tested.* (F1), for two reasons (fix pass #13: an earlier third reason, "the only one that survives where $p_{2,2}$ is evaluated pointwise", was not an argument as written and is dropped). It is the pointwise analogue of PH31 on which R84's and R86's evidence bears (R84's failure and R86's removal of it are pointwise statements). And it is checkable at finite level by a rank computation per $q$, which (F2) is not without choosing a $q$-sample; at $|q|\to1^+$ it reduces to the model's PH31 row at $\alpha'_{\rm v}=\tfrac12$ (R86's boundary clause). F1 is defined through $\pi_W$; its relation to the inverse of the actual $p_{2,2}$ is now fixed in one direction (necessary, not sufficient for (L), (i.2) above) and **open in the other**: stage 2 item 3 asks whether the projection onto $W$ is still the relevant one once the dressed antighosts are included. It is therefore not presented as PH31 for the actual vertex. (F2) is recorded as the weaker necessary condition: a failure of (F2) would locate a direction of $W^{\rm even}$ that $p_{2,2}$'s zero-mode part never sees. Neither gives the closed form of R80; what the paper's inverse needs beyond them, given (b), is (L) and the kernel-side condition, stage 2's question.

**Stage 1 tests (F1)** at $K\le2$, $L=2$, $d_{\rm eff}=2$, pointwise in $q$, through the reduction of §(iii) where it holds; a full-rank $q$ also establishes (F2) at that level.

## §(iii) The candidate reduction (C2)

\subsection*{Setup}
A2–A5, §(ii). $h_1^{(\alpha)}$ the engine's map (A3) at $\gamma'=\beta$; $\mu:=\alpha/\gamma'$; $U_\kappa:=\kappa^{L_0}\bar\kappa^{\bar L_0}$ the operator of $W\mapsto\kappa W$ ($(\kappa\,\cdot)\circ\phi(0)=\kappa^h\bar\kappa^{\bar h}\phi(0)$ for a primary of weights $(h,\bar h)$, single-valued since $h-\bar h\in\mathbb Z$).

\subsection*{Derivation}
*Step 1 – Möbius data.* A Möbius $F$ is fixed by $F(0)$, $F'(0)$, $F(\infty)$. The dictionary fixes $F_1^{(u)}(0)=u_1=z$ (and $F_2^{(u)}(0)=-z$) at every $q$; $h_1^{(\alpha)}(0)=z$, $h_1^{(\alpha)\prime}(0)=1/(\beta\gamma')$ for every $\alpha$, and $\alpha\mapsto h_1^{(\alpha)}(\infty)=\frac{\alpha+1}{2\beta(\alpha-1)}$ is a bijection of $\mathbb C$ onto $\hat{\mathbb C}\setminus\{z\}$ ($\alpha=1\mapsto\infty$; $R=z$ would need $\alpha=\infty$). Hence
$$
F_1^{(u)}(W;q)=h_1^{(\lambda)}\big(\kappa W\big),\qquad \lambda(q)=\frac{2\beta R_1(q)+1}{2\beta R_1(q)-1}=\frac{R_1-u_2}{R_1-u_1},\qquad \kappa(q)=\beta\gamma'\,a_1^{(u)}(q), \tag{iii.1}
$$
exactly (complex $\lambda$, $\kappa$). [C2.1]

*Step 2 – Klein.* On the FD the package defines $F_j=K_j\circ F_1$ for $j=2,3,4$, with $K_2,K_3,K_4$ the Möbius maps realising $(12)(34)$, $(13)(24)$, $(14)(23)$ on $(0,1,\infty,Z)$ (`section_FD`; 092 (iv.11)(a)); hence $F_j=K_jK_i^{-1}\circ F_i$ for all $i,j$, and $K_jK_i^{-1}$ is the Klein element sending $i\mapsto j$ (the full $V_4$ symmetry is needed, not $F_2=K_2\circ F_1$ alone: fix pass #5). Off the FD, `relabel` gives $F'_j=N\circ F_{\pi^{-1}(j)}$ (conjugation first); so $F_{\pi^{-1}(2)}=K\circ F_{\pi^{-1}(1)}$ with $K$ the Klein element sending $\pi^{-1}(1)$ to $\pi^{-1}(2)$, so $F'_2=(NKN^{-1})\circ F'_1$ with $NKN^{-1}$ realising $(12)(34)$ on the new frame. In the circle frame the unique Möbius map realising $(12)(34)$ on $(z,-z,2\beta/q,-2\beta/q)$ is $u\mapsto-u$. Hence, at every $q\in Q_V$, as maps of the same $W$ (phase included):
$$
F_2^{(u)}=-F_1^{(u)}=h_2^{(\lambda)}(\kappa W),\qquad R_2=-R_1,\quad a_2^{(u)}=-a_1^{(u)},\quad \kappa_2=\kappa_1,\quad \lambda_2=\lambda_1 . \tag{iii.2}
$$
The brief's $R_2(q)=-R_1(q)$ holds, and more: the scales agree too, so one $U_\kappa$ acts on both legs. [C2.2]

*Step 3 – the scale.* By (iii.1)–(iii.2), $F_i^{(u)}\circ A=h_i^{(\lambda)}\circ(U_\kappa A)$, so
$$
\Phi_2(A_1,A_2;q)=\Phi_2^{\rm rig}\big(U_\kappa A_1,\,U_\kappa A_2\big)\Big|_{\rm hol:\ \lambda,\ antihol:\ \bar\lambda},\qquad U_\kappa\big|_{\mathcal H_0}=|\kappa|^{L_0^+}e^{i\arg\kappa\,L_0^-}=|\kappa|^{L_0^+} . \tag{iii.3}
$$
On $\mathcal H_0$, $U_\kappa$ is real, positive, diagonal in the Fock basis (the momentum part $|\kappa|^{\alpha'k^2/2}$ is a non-zero scalar per input momentum *at fixed $q$*; any joint statement over several $q$ must restore it, §(i)(a) and *Relation to the lifted statement*), commutes with $b_0^\pm$, $c_0^\pm$ and $L_0^\pm$, and is invertible level by level; $U_\kappa\otimes U_\kappa$ maps $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0^{\le L}$ onto itself. So the image of $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0^{\le L}$ under $\pi_{\le K}\pi_W\Phi_2(\cdot\,;q)$ equals the rigid image at the parameter $\lambda(q)$. Off $\mathcal H_0$ the factor carries the phase $e^{i\arg\kappa(N-\bar N)}$ (still invertible; the break leg shows it is there). [C2.3]

*Step 4 – what "the rigid image at a complex parameter" means (correction to the brief).* The antiholomorphic sector transforms with $\bar F$, so the rigid state at complex $\lambda$ is the engine's construction with $\alpha=\lambda$ in the holomorphic and $\alpha=\bar\lambda$ in the antiholomorphic sector, not the holomorphic continuation of the real-$\alpha$ engine (whose single data set serves both sectors; the break leg `BREAK-C2.3-holomorphic-lambda` shows the difference, (companion value, not reproduced here) at a point of $Q_V$). The correction rests on the standard transformation law of a closed-string field under $(f,\bar f)$, with $\overline{h_1^{(\lambda)}(\kappa W)}=h_1^{(\bar\lambda)}(\bar\kappa\bar W)$ because $h_1$'s coefficients are real apart from $\lambda$; the checks `C2.3-rescaling` and `BREAK-C2.3-holomorphic-lambda` test only its implementation (both routes build in the conjugate map), not the law itself (fix pass #4; a test of the law, the plumbed four-point function against the package's direct amplitude at one complex $q$, is not run). Define the rank function $r_K(\lambda,\tilde\lambda)$ on $\mathbb C^2$ with independent sectors; the physical family is the real 2-plane $\Pi:=\{\tilde\lambda=\bar\lambda\}$, and
$$
E_K:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb C:\ r_K(\lambda,\bar\lambda)<\dim W^{\rm even}_{\le K}\}. \tag{iii.4}
$$
Its finiteness is **not** automatic: the deficiency locus $\mathcal D_K\subset\mathbb C^2$ is algebraic (common zeros of the maximal minors), and a curve component $\{P(\lambda,\tilde\lambda)=0\}$ meets $\Pi$ in a real-algebraic set. For a generic complex polynomial $P$, $P(\lambda,\bar\lambda)=0$ is two real equations in two real unknowns and the intersection is generically finite; it is a curve when $P$ is self-conjugate under $(\lambda,\tilde\lambda)\mapsto(\bar{\tilde\lambda},\bar\lambda)$ (e.g. $\lambda\tilde\lambda=c$, $c>0$, gives $|\lambda|^2=c$). Here self-conjugacy is the generic case: $\mathcal D_K$ is invariant under this antiholomorphic involution (conjugate the engine data and swap the chiralities: $\overline{N(\alpha)}=N(\bar\alpha)$ for real-coefficient polynomials, so $r_K(\lambda,\tilde\lambda)=r_K(\bar{\tilde\lambda},\bar\lambda)$), and its fixed set is exactly $\Pi$; the same fact is why a self-conjugate component of $\mathcal D_2$ would have appeared as a curve in C4.2 (fix pass 3, critic #7). What is automatic is finiteness on the real diagonal ($\lambda=\tilde\lambda\in\mathbb R$, the engine's family), which is how the known rows ($\alpha'_{\rm v}=0,\tfrac12,\tfrac14,-\tfrac12$) sit. [C2.4]

*Step 5 – polynomial structure.* $h_1^{(\lambda)}=h_1^{(0)}\circ m_\mu$ with $m_\mu(W)=W/(1+\mu W)$, $\mu=\lambda/\gamma'$ (direct composition: $(h_1^{(0)})^{-1}(y)=\gamma'\frac{2\beta y-1}{2\beta y+1}$), and $h_2^{(\lambda)}=h_2^{(0)}\circ m_\mu$ with the same $m_\mu$. $m_\mu$ is the time-one flow of $-\mu W^2\partial_W$, realised on a state by $e^{\pm\mu L_1}$ (sign by convention; every rank statement here is insensitive to it, the exceptional set being at most reflected by $\lambda\to-\lambda$, and C4.3's $E_2\cap(\mathbb R\cup i\mathbb R)=\{0\}$ and the symmetric points $\pm\tfrac12$ are symmetric; the sign must be fixed against the book's convention before (iii.5) is used as an operator identity, stage 2 item 3; critic #8), and on the antiholomorphic side by $e^{\pm\tilde\mu\bar L_1}$. So
$$
\Phi^{\rm rig}_{\lambda,\tilde\lambda}(A_1,A_2)=\Phi^{\rm rig}_{0,0}\big(e^{\pm\mu L_1\pm\tilde\mu\bar L_1}A_1,\ e^{\pm\mu L_1\pm\tilde\mu\bar L_1}A_2\big), \tag{iii.5}
$$
a polynomial in $(\lambda,\tilde\lambda)$ of bidegree $\le(N_1+N_2,\ \bar N_1+\bar N_2)$ on inputs of levels $(N_i,\bar N_i)$ ($L_1$ lowers the level and annihilates $c_1|k\rangle$). The $\lambda$-dependence of the rank is the statement that $e^{\mu L_1}$ does not preserve $\mathcal H_0$: the rigid map at $\alpha=0$ on the subspace $(e^{\pm\mu L_1\pm\tilde\mu\bar L_1})^{\otimes2}\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0$. [C2.5]

*Step 6 – $\lambda$ against $\mu_i$.* By (iii.1) and 089 (1.4), $\mu_1=\beta a_1^{(u)}\frac{R_1-u_2}{R_1-u_1}=\beta a_1^{(u)}\lambda$, so (package phase)
$$
\lambda(q)=\frac{\mu_1(q)}{\beta\,a_1^{(u)}(q)}=\frac{\beta\gamma'}{\beta\kappa(q)}\,\mu_1(q),\qquad \lambda=0\iff\mu_1=0, \tag{iii.6}
$$
so R86's $\mu_1$ and the rigid-family parameter $\mu=\lambda/\gamma'$ of (iii.5) are related by $\mu_1(q)=\kappa(q)\,\mu(q)$ (package phase; $\mu_1^{\rm A4}=|\kappa|\mu$ in 089's phase, see *Conventions*); in the package phase they agree only where $\kappa=1$, e.g. for the rigid halves in the engine's frame ($\lambda=\alpha'_{\rm v}$, $\mu_1=\mu=\alpha'_{\rm v}/\gamma'$), not over $\mathcal F_s$, where the halves are rigid but $\kappa=-1$ (fix pass #3; fix pass 2, critic #9). On the FD, with 092's anchors ($1/R=A+L(B-A)$, (iv.3)) and the cross-ratio $\lambda=\frac{R-1}{R}\cdot\frac{u_3-u_2}{u_3-u_1}$, $\frac{u_3-u_2}{u_3-u_1}=\frac{1+x}{1-x}$, $x=q/4\beta^2$:
$$
\lambda=\frac{4(1+x^2)-L\,(1+7x^2)}{6(1-x^2)}=\frac{(1+7x^2)\,(g_1-L)}{6(1-x^2)},\qquad L\in[0,1] , \tag{iii.7}
$$
on the segment from $\tfrac12$ ($L=1$, the wall $|q|=1$) to $\frac{2(1+x^2)}{3(1-x^2)}$ ($L=0$); non-zero on the FD since $g_1\notin[0,1]$ there (092 (iv.4)). R86's every-point clause is therefore $0\notin\lambda(Q_V)$, given the package. [C2.6]

\subsection*{Commentary}
(iii.1): the content is the positions ($F_i^{(u)}(0)=\pm z$ at every $q$, which is the dictionary's gauge); the decomposition itself is then forced. (iii.2) is stronger than $R_2=-R_1$: equality of the scales is what makes one operator $U_\kappa\otimes U_\kappa$ act on the symmetric tensor; with $\kappa_2\ne\kappa_1$ the map would still be invertible on $\mathcal H_0\otimes\mathcal H_0$ but would not preserve $\mathrm{Sym}^2$ (it would mix $A_1\odot A_2$ with $A_1\wedge A_2$ components). (iii.3): the brief's invertibility claim holds, on $\mathcal H_0$ and also off it; what is special to $\mathcal H_0$ is that the factor is phase-free. (iii.4) is where the brief's step "rank questions reduce to the exceptional set $E_K\subset\mathbb C$, finite at each $K$" needs a proof that the record supplies in §(v), at $K=2$, partly exactly and partly numerically. (iii.7) also shows that on the FD $\lambda$ is close to the real axis when $x$ is (the $Z$-real walls).

\subsection*{Result}
The reduction holds, with one correction. $\pi_{\le K}\pi_W\Phi_2(\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0^{\le L};q)$ equals the image of the rigid $\gamma'=\beta$ state with parameter $\lambda(q)$ in the holomorphic and $\bar\lambda(q)$ in the antiholomorphic sector, $\lambda(q)=\mu_1(q)/(\beta a_1^{(u)}(q))$; so F1 at $(K,L)$ holds at $q$ iff $\lambda(q)\notin E_K$, with $E_K$ the real-algebraic slice (iii.4) of an algebraic set in $\mathbb C^2$, whose finiteness has to be shown. [C2]

*(v.2) No curve component.* On a line $\ell=\{(a+bt,c+dt)\}$, the column of pair $(i,j)$ has $t$-degree $\le2(N_i+N_j)$, so every maximal minor has $t$-degree $\le584$ (the 73 largest column degrees).
