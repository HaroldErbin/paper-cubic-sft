## §0 The stub change at fixed level (C0)

\subsection*{Setup}

A1–A4. $K^{(L)}_P[s_0]$ the fixed-level operator of 104 (1.2) for the vertex with stub length $s_0$ in description (A) (fixed local coordinates, region $\mathcal V_{0,4}(s_0)$); $K^{(L),B}_P[s_0]$ the same in description (B). Strand momenta $k_{1,2}=\tfrac P2\pm q$, $k_{3,4}=-\tfrac P2\pm q'$ (104 A2); $l_a$ the level of strand $a$; $\hat l$ the level operator on $W_L$, $\hat l(a\otimes b)=(l_1+l_2)(a\otimes b)$. $\Pi_L$ the projector on level-$\le L$ pairs, $\Pi_n$ the projector on level-$n$ single-string states. $\mathcal V_3$ the cubic vertex of the family, as a map $\mathcal H^{\otimes2}\to\mathcal H^*$ (two strands in, one out), extended by linearity and continuity to entangled pairs. $\Delta_{[a,b]}:=b_0^+b_0^-\delta_{L_0^-,0}\int_a^b\mathrm ds\,\mathrm e^{-sL_0^+}$ the finite-strip propagator (C9).

\subsection*{Derivation}

*(0.1) Per-leg factor.* In description (B) every leg of every vertex carries $\mathrm e^{-\frac{s_0}{2}L_0^+}$ (book l. 1044–1047). On a level-matched strand of momentum $k_a$ and level $l_a$, $L_0^+=\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}k_a^2+2(l_a-1)$ (C9, A2), so the factor is $\mathrm e^{-\frac{\alpha's_0}{4}k_a^2}\,\mathrm e^{-s_0(l_a-1)}$ – diagonal in the Fock basis, momentum part included, as the brief states. For the pair $(k_1,k_2)$: $k_1^2+k_2^2=\tfrac{p^2}{2}+2q^2$, the cross term $\pm p\,n\cdot q$ cancelling between the two strands; the same for $(k_3,k_4)$ with $q'$. Hence the product of the four leg factors is
$$\mathrm e^{-\frac{\alpha's_0}{4}p^2}\;\Lambda_{s_0}(q')\,\Lambda_{s_0}(q),\qquad\Lambda_{s_0}(q):=\mathrm e^{-\frac{\alpha's_0}{2}q^2}\,\mathrm e^{-s_0(\hat l-2)}\quad\text{on }L^2(\mathbb R^{26})\otimes W_L .\tag{0.1}$$
$\Lambda_{s_0}$ is a bounded, positive, injective multiplication operator (Gaussian in $q$ times a positive diagonal finite matrix in the level basis), self-adjoint for the auxiliary structure of 104 A4, commuting with $\mathcal S$ (even in $q$, level-symmetric under $\tau$) and with $U$; its inverse $\mathrm e^{+\frac{\alpha's_0}{2}q^2}\mathrm e^{s_0(\hat l-2)}$ is unbounded, defined on $\mathrm{ran}\,\Lambda_{s_0}=\{\sigma:\mathrm e^{\frac{\alpha's_0}{2}q^2}\sigma\in L^2\}$, a dense non-closed subspace.

*(0.2) The two descriptions and the congruence.* In description (A) the amplitude is stub-independent (book l. 1052–1056) and the family of A3 is built so: for $s,s_1\in J$, at fixed level and total momentum,
$$K^{(L)}_P[s]=K^{(L)}_P[s_1]+\sum_{\rm ch\in\{s,t,u\}}\Pi_L\,\mathcal V_3\,\Delta_{[s_1,s]}\,\mathcal V_3\,\Pi_L\Big|_P ,\tag{0.2a}$$
the strip of each channel between the two stub lengths (a definition for the one-sided family of A3, an identity for the book's family). The (B)-form of the same family at length $s$ is description (A) at $s$ with the added leg factors absorbed (book l. 1001: "rescaling the local coordinates by a constant parameter is equivalent to change the stub parameter"; on the internal leg of a propagator graph, $\mathrm e^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}L_0^+}$ from each adjacent vertex shifts the plumbing range from $[s_0,\infty)$ back to $[s_0+\sigma,\infty)$, so both descriptions assign the same surfaces to vertex and propagator). With $\sigma=s-s_0$,
$$K^{(L),B}_P[s]=\mathrm e^{-\frac{\alpha'\sigma}{4}p^2}\;\Lambda_{\sigma}\,K^{(L)}_P[s]\,\Lambda_{\sigma}=\mathrm e^{-\frac{\alpha'\sigma}{4}p^2}\;\Lambda_{\sigma}\Bigl(K^{(L)}_P[s_0]+\sum_{\rm ch}\Pi_L\mathcal V_3\Delta_{[s_0,s]}\mathcal V_3\Pi_L\Bigr)\Lambda_{\sigma},\qquad K^{(L),B}_P[s_0]=K^{(L)}_P[s_0].\tag{0.2}$$
The stub dependence, read in the rescaled-coordinate form, is thus a **congruence by $\Lambda_{\sigma}$** (times the non-zero scalar $\mathrm e^{-\alpha'\sigma p^2/4}$, irrelevant for kernels at every complex $p$) **of the fixed-coordinate operator**, whose own stub dependence is **the strip of the three exchange channels**. The congruence alone – the brief's "rescaling multiplies each leg by $\lambda^{-L_0}$" – is the first factor; it is exact, with $\lambda=\mathrm e^{\sigma/2}$. The project's operator is the base point $s=s_0$ of both forms, where the congruence is the identity; every kernel statement of §1–§2 is made for it directly.

*(0.3) The shell.* Differentiating (0.2a) in $s_0$, now a generic point of $J$ (one-sided derivative at the base point; the name $s_0$ is kept for the variable in this item only) (the integrand of $\Delta$ is continuous in $s$; the intermediate sum converges uniformly on compacts of $\operatorname{Re}s>0$ by A4):
$$\partial_{s_0}K^{(L)}_P[s_0]=\mathcal E^{(L)}_P[s_0]:=\sum_{\rm ch}\Pi_L\,\mathcal V_3\,b_0^+b_0^-\delta_{L_0^-,0}\,\mathrm e^{-s_0L_0^+}\,\mathcal V_3\,\Pi_L\Big|_P ,\tag{0.3}$$
the sewn two-cubic-vertex surface at plumbing length $s_0$, summed over channels – the plumbing boundary of $\mathcal V_{0,4}(s_0)$ (A3). Written out on the fibre: in the $s$-channel the intermediate state carries the pair momentum $P$, and
$$\bigl(\mathcal E^{s}_P[s_0]\sigma\bigr)(q')=\sum_{n\ge0}\mathrm e^{-s_0h_n(P)}\;\Pi_L\,\mathcal V_3\,\Pi_n\,\mathcal V_3\,\sigma ,\qquad h_n(P)=\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}P^2+2(n-1),\tag{0.3s}$$
where $\mathcal V_3\sigma\in\mathcal H^*_{P}$ is the single-string functional $\phi\mapsto\int\mathrm d^{26}q\,\mathcal V_3(\phi;\tfrac P2+q,\tfrac P2-q)\,\sigma(q)$ (the product $\ell_2$ applied to the entangled pair, with $b_0^+b_0^-$ and the level-matching projection absorbed into $\Pi_n$), $\Pi_n\mathcal V_3\sigma$ its level-$n$ component (a finite-dimensional vector space at fixed $P$), and $\Pi_L\mathcal V_3(\cdot)$ the map back to a level-$\le L$ pair function of $q'$. Each term is a finite-rank operator on the fibre; the sum over $n$ converges by A4 for $s_0>0$. In the $t$ and $u$ channels the intermediate momentum is $q+q'$ and $q-q'$ (strand labels of the Setup), so the exponent is $h_n^{t,u}=\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}(q\pm q')^2+2(n-1)$, a function on the fibre, and $\mathcal E^{t,u}_P[s_0]$ is an integral operator in $(q,q')$ with kernel $\sum_n\mathrm e^{-s_0h^{t,u}_n(q,q')}\,\pi^{t,u}_n(q,q')$, $\pi_n$ polynomial-weighted Gaussians (the two cubic vertices' matter factors). All exponents are real on the Euclidean fibre (PH25) and the whole shell is entire in $s_0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s_0>0$ and, for each $s_0$, entire in $p$ (the same argument as 104 (2.3): compact region, exponential–polynomial integrand); it inherits the transposition and reflection structure 104 (1.4)–(1.5).

*(0.4) Kernel correspondence under the congruence.* Let $K$ be any operator on the fibre and $\Lambda$ bounded injective with dense range. Then $\Lambda K\Lambda\sigma=0\iff K\Lambda\sigma=0$ (injectivity of $\Lambda$ on the left), so
$$\ker(\Lambda K\Lambda)=\Lambda^{-1}(\ker K)=\{\sigma\in L^2:\Lambda\sigma\in\ker K\},\qquad\Lambda:\ \ker(\Lambda K\Lambda)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\ker K\cap\mathrm{ran}\,\Lambda ,\tag{0.4}$$
and $\dim\ker(\Lambda K\Lambda)=\dim(\ker K\cap\mathrm{ran}\,\Lambda)\le\dim\ker K$. Applied to (0.2) with $\Lambda=\Lambda_{\sigma}$: the null vectors of the (B)-form at length $s=s_0+\sigma$ are exactly the null vectors $N$ of the fixed-coordinate operator $K^{(L)}_P[s]$ with Gaussian decay $\mathrm e^{\frac{\alpha'\sigma}{2}q^2}N\in L^2$, mapped by $\Lambda_{\sigma}^{-1}$. Three consequences. (i) The *level factor* $\mathrm e^{-\sigma(\hat l-2)}$ is a positive invertible matrix on the finite-dimensional $W_L$: it moves no kernel and changes no dimension. The mass-sum suppression $\lambda^{-h}$ of de Lacroix et al. 2017 §3.7 is, at fixed external level, exactly this factor on the external legs – **it says nothing about $\ker p_{2,2}^{(L)}(P)$ through the congruence**. (ii) The *Gaussian factor* acts through the domain only. **At fixed $K$**, the congruence by $\Lambda_\sigma$ has kernel $\Lambda_\sigma^{-1}(\ker K\cap\mathrm{ran}\,\Lambda_\sigma)$, which decreases as $\sigma$ grows ($\mathrm{ran}\,\Lambda_\sigma\downarrow$): null vectors with Gaussian decay at every rate (compact support, $\mathrm e^{-q^4}$) are kept at every $\sigma$, while a null vector of the type polynomial $\times\,\mathrm e^{-aq^2}$ with fixed $a$ – the natural type for these kernels – is lost once $\alpha'\sigma/2\ge a$; Schwartz decay is *not* enough ($\mathrm e^{-\sqrt{1+q^2}}$ is Schwartz and $\mathrm e^{\alpha'\sigma q^2/2}\mathrm e^{-\sqrt{1+q^2}}\notin L^2$). This monotonicity is a statement about the congruence factor alone (critic #1): the stub change also moves $K$ by the strip (0.2a), which can create or remove null vectors, so nothing is claimed about the stub change as a whole. This is the third place of plan review M3; it bears on D2 only and not on D1 (dimensions only drop under the congruence). H1's regularity clause ($\ker\mathcal T_L\subseteq\mathrm{ran}\,\mathbb K_0^{1/2}$, $\mathbb K_0^{1/2}\ker K\subseteq D$) is a statement in the same scale and is left as in 104. (iii) At every $s\in J$ the kernel of the (B)-form is contained, after $\Lambda_\sigma$, in the kernel of the fixed-coordinate operator at the same $s$. At the base point $s_0$ the two coincide (the project's operator), so no transfer is needed for the statements of §1–§2, which are made at $s_0$; the inclusion is used only to read the selection-rule sectors of §1 in both descriptions.

*(0.5) Verdict and what H2′ acts on.* The stub change at fixed level **is** a congruence on the part of the vertex common to the two stubs, exactly (0.2), with the kernel correspondence (0.4); the part that is not a congruence is the strip (0.2a), whose derivative is the shell (0.3) of single-string exchanges in the three channels – the brief's "$s$, $t$, $u$ pieces". The mass sum enters there (A4): the shell is a convergent sum over intermediate states of every mass, with the weight $\mathrm e^{-s_0h}$ beating the $\mathrm e^{c\sqrt h}$ growth. **H2′ acts on the shell** (§1): on how a null vector behaves under the strip, not on the congruence. The limit $L\to\infty$ is the second place (C5); the domain effect is the third, used only for D2 (C3).

\subsection*{Commentary}

- (0.1): the cancellation of $p\,n\cdot q$ between the two strands of a pair is what makes the congruence factor a function of $q^2$ and $p^2$ only, even in $q$ – so it preserves the fibre space (1.1) of 104 and the structures (1.5). Without the cancellation (unequal stubs on the two strands) the factor would break the $\mathcal S$ symmetry; the stub is the same on every leg by construction.

\subsection*{Derivation}

*(2.1) Laplace separation: a stub-independent null vector is exchange-decoupled (C2.1).* Let $N\in\mathcal H^{(L)}$, $N\ne0$, with $K^{(L)}_P[s]N=0$ for all $s\in J=[s_0,s_0+\varepsilon)$ (a one-sided interval suffices: only its open interior $(s_0,s_0+\varepsilon)$ is used). Differentiating (0.2a) in $s$ gives $\mathcal E^{(L)}_P[s]N=0$ on $(s_0,s_0+\varepsilon)$. By C0.2, $s\mapsto\mathcal E^{(L)}_P[s]N$ is holomorphic on $\operatorname{Re}s>0$ (A4: the intermediate sum converges uniformly on compacts; the $t,u$ kernels are dominated by their value at the left end of any compact $s$-interval), so it vanishes for all $s>0$. Fix $q'$ and pair with any test vector $w\in W_L$; by (0.3s) and the form of the $t,u$ shells,
$$0=\sum_{n\ge0}\mathrm e^{-s\,h_n(P)}\,\langle w,v_n(q')\rangle+\int_{-2}^{\infty}\mathrm e^{-sh}\,\mathrm d\mu_{q',w}(h)\quad\forall s>0,\qquad v_n:=\Pi_L\mathcal V_3\Pi_n\mathcal V_3N ,\tag{2.1}$$
where $\mu_{q',w}$ is the push-forward under $q\mapsto h^{t,u}_n(q,q')=\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}(q\pm q')^2+2(n-1)$, summed over $n$ and the two channels, of the measures $\langle w,\pi_n^{t,u}(q,q')\,N(q)\rangle\,\mathrm d^{26}q$ – absolutely continuous in $h$ (an $L^2$ function of $q$ puts no mass on the spheres $(q\pm q')^2=$ const), supported in $[-2,\infty)$, finite by A4 after multiplication by $\mathrm e^{-sh}$. The left side of (2.1) is the Laplace–Stieltjes transform of the signed measure $\sum_n\langle w,v_n(q')\rangle\,\delta_{h_n(P)}+\mu_{q',w}$, convergent for all $s>0$ and identically zero there; by the uniqueness theorem for the Laplace transform (Widder, *The Laplace Transform*, ch. II, §6) the measure vanishes. Its atoms sit at the distinct points $h_n(P)=\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}P^2+2(n-1)$ ($h_{n+1}-h_n=2$), which the absolutely continuous part cannot cancel; hence $\langle w,v_n(q')\rangle=0$ for every $n$, every $w$, a.e. $q'$, and the absolutely continuous part vanishes too:
$$\Pi_L\,\mathcal V_3\,\Pi_n\,\mathcal V_3\,N=0\ \ \forall n\ge0,\qquad(\mathcal E^t_P[s]+\mathcal E^u_P[s])N=0\ \ \forall s>0 ,\tag{2.2}$$
which is (1.1): $N$ is exchange-decoupled. For complex $p\in\Omega_L$ the $h_n(P)$ are complex with a common imaginary part $\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}\operatorname{Im}p^2$ and still distinct, and the $t,u$ measure is complex-valued; the uniqueness theorem applies to each of the real and imaginary parts after factoring $\mathrm e^{-\mathrm i s\,\alpha'\operatorname{Im}p^2/2}$ from the atoms only if the continuous part carries the same phase – it does not in general, so (2.2) at complex $p$ is established by analytic continuation of (2.2) from real $p$ (both sides entire in $p$, C0.2) when $N$ is taken entire in $p$, and otherwise only the real-$P^2$ case is claimed. **Status: Proven for real $P^2$** (both signs), from (0.2a), (0.3), A4 and the uniqueness theorem; for complex $p$, Proven when $N_P$ is holomorphic in $p$ (which the first horn under H1 provides locally, 104 (4.6): a holomorphic Fredholm family with a kernel everywhere has locally holomorphic null-vector families), and that is the case used in (2.3).

(The $\Lambda$-invariance (ii) is not used here; it is what makes the statement description-independent, Setup of §1.) In words: **a kernel at every momentum at the project's stub, if any, contains no null-vector family that is fixed along the stub family – a kernel at every momentum cannot be a conservation law of the family, because every conserved sector would have to be blind to the whole single-string exchange tower, which (S) forbids.** This is the record's result on the first horn that does not rest on (R′). **(2.2b) First-horn exclusion (Conditional(H2′, PH25, A4) – Proven as an implication; see the Logical status of §1).** Suppose FH$_{\mathbb R}$ at level $L$: $\ker K^{(L)}_P[s_0]\ne0$ at every real $P^2$. By (R′) each such kernel contains a selection-rule sector $S_P\ne0$; by (2.2a) there is none: contradiction. Hence **under H2′, at no level is there a kernel at every real $P^2$**, and a fortiori no kernel at every $p\in\Omega_L$ (FH $\Rightarrow$ FH$_{\mathbb R}$): the first horn of 104 (4.6) is excluded at every $L$ for the project's operator directly – no transfer by (0.4)(iii) is needed, since the project's operator is the base point of the family. H1 is not needed: the contradiction is reached on the real axes, where (2.1) is unconditional. ## §3 The tachyonic pair (C6)

Where the UV-finiteness argument uses $m^2\ge0$, and what survives for the bosonic pair kernel under PH25:

- *The hypothesis of de Lacroix et al. 2017 §3.7* is "for fixed momentum, the conformal weight of the vertex operators is bounded from below", not $m^2\ge0$: it is what makes $\mathrm e^{-sh}$ a suppression for all but finitely many states, and the paper establishes it for the superstring spaces $\widehat{\mathcal H}_T,\widetilde{\mathcal H}_T$ (picture-number choice, Ramond zero modes cut by $\mathcal X_0$). For the bosonic string, $L_0^+=\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}k^2+2(l-1)\ge\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}k^2-2$ at fixed Euclidean momentum: bounded below, with exactly one level ($l=0$, the tachyon) below the massless value. A4 therefore holds for the bosonic string as stated, and every finite-stub statement of §0–§2 – the shell (0.3), the Laplace separation (2.1), C2–C4 – is unaffected by the tachyon: a negative exponent $h_0(P)<0$ is one atom among the others, and the uniqueness theorem does not care about its sign. (In §8.1 the paper writes $\exp(-\Lambda_im_i^2)$ with $m^2\ge0$ implicit; that is the superstring form; the bosonic form has the same structure with $m_0^2<0$.)
- *Where the tachyon bites.* The $s$-channel exponent $h_0(P)=\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}P^2-2$ is negative for Euclidean $\alpha'P^2<4$ (every timelike $P$ and the spacelike ones below the tachyon threshold), and the $t,u$ exponents $h_0^{t,u}=\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}(q\pm q')^2-2$ are negative on the balls $(q\pm q')^2<4/\alpha'$ of the fibre, at every $P$. In the shell this gives factors $\mathrm e^{+s|h_0|}$, finite at finite $s$; in the limit $s_0\to\infty$ they diverge: the strip $\int^{s_0}\mathcal E$ does not converge, $\mathcal V_4[s_0]$ does not tend to the full amputated four-point function, and no heuristic of the form "at large stub the vertex is the amplitude, whose kernel is read off its factorisation" is available for the bosonic string. H2 is formulated on finite intervals of stub length and uses no limit; this is why.
- *What PH25 does.* With Euclidean $q,q'$ the transfers $(q\pm q')^2$ are real and $\ge0$, so the exponents are real, bounded below by $-2$, and the tachyon ball is a bounded region of the fibre; the shells are real-symmetric with the structure 104 (1.5) and the Laplace transform in (2.1) is a transform of a measure supported in $[-2,\infty)$, where uniqueness holds. In Lorentzian relative momenta the transfer is unbounded below, the exponents have no lower bound, the measure in (2.1) has unbounded support on both sides, and the two-sided Laplace transform may fail to exist for any $s$: PH25 is what makes A4 and (2.1) statements at all. Nothing in this record decides PH25 (record 105 §1 states the prescription).
- *The pair kernel at level 0.* R53 holds at every real $s$, both sides of the tachyon threshold; the tachyonic pair enters H2′ only through the atom $h_0(P)$, and (S) at $L=0$ follows from R53's mechanism regardless of its sign (motivation (b) of §1).

Status: Proven where it restates A4's hypothesis and the exponents; the divergence of the $s_0\to\infty$ limit is Proven (a positive exponent integrated to infinity); the absence of a large-stub heuristic is a consequence, not an exclusion of D2.
