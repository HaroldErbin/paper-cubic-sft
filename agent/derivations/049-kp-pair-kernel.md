### Block 1 — The kernel, its symmetry, its compactness, its partial waves (C1)

\subsection*{Setup}

A1–A4. $\rho=\lvert\ell\rvert$, $\lambda=\lvert\ell'\rvert$, $x=\cos\theta=\hat\ell\cdot\hat\ell'$. $F(s,t,u)$ of A3. $\lvert S^{n}\rvert=2\pi^{(n+1)/2}/\Gamma(\tfrac{n+1}2)$. $C^{(\nu)}_n$ the Gegenbauer polynomials, $\nu=\tfrac D2-1=12$.

\subsection*{Derivation}

*(1.1) The kernel.* The tachyon-pair block of $V_4$ at fixed $P$ is, by A2 and R48,
$$K_P(\ell,\ell')=C\,F\bigl(P^2,\,(\ell+\ell')^2,\,(\ell-\ell')^2\bigr),\qquad
 t=\rho^2+\lambda^2+2\rho\lambda x,\quad u=\rho^2+\lambda^2-2\rho\lambda x.\tag{1.1}$$
It acts on the component $\sigma(\ell)$ of $\Sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0$ along $T(\tfrac P2+\ell)T(\tfrac P2-\ell)$; that state is invariant under $\ell\to-\ell$, so $\sigma$ is **even** and the space is $L^2_{\rm even}$ once an $L^2$ structure is chosen. $K_P(\ell,-\ell')=K_P(\ell,\ell')$ because $\ell'\to-\ell'$ is $t\leftrightarrow u$ and $F$ is totally crossing symmetric (R48): the kernel is consistent with evenness and annihilates odd functions, which are not in $\operatorname{Sym}^2$ in the first place.

*(1.2) $O(26)$ invariance (C1.1).* By (1.1), $K_P$ depends on $(\ell,\ell')$ through $\rho,\lambda,x$ only. $P$ has dropped out entirely: without R48 the kernel would carry $\ell\cdot P$, $\ell'\cdot P$ through the individual $k_a^2$ and only the $O(25)$ stabiliser of $P$ would survive. So the fixed-$P$ block is a rotation-invariant kernel on $\mathbb R^{26}$ although $P$ singles out a direction.

*(1.3) Symmetry and reality.* $F$ is real for real $(s,t,u)$ and symmetric in $t\leftrightarrow u$, hence $K_P(\ell,\ell')=K_P(\ell',\ell)$ (exchange $\ell\leftrightarrow\ell'$ maps $(t,u)\to(t,u)$ — nothing to check — while $\ell\to-\ell$ or $\ell'\to-\ell'$ maps $(t,u)\to(u,t)$).

*(1.4) $G_2$ on the block.* The BPZ pairing of tachyon pairs is, with the momentum deltas, $\langle T(k_1)T(k_2),T(k_3)T(k_4)\rangle\propto\delta(k_1+k_3)\delta(k_2+k_4)+\delta(k_1+k_4)\delta(k_2+k_3)$; at fixed total momentum this is $g_T^2\,[\delta(\ell-\ell')+\delta(\ell+\ell')]$ in the relative momenta ($g_T^2$: two single-tachyon pairings), i.e. $2g_T^2$ times the identity on even functions. So on the level-0 block $G_2$ is a constant, $m_2=G_2^{-1}V_4\propto K_P$, and the bilinear form $\int\sigma\sigma'\,d^{26}\ell$ on even functions is the BPZ pairing of this block (pairing $k$ with $-k$). **The inner product is not auxiliary at level 0**: it is the restriction of the BPZ form, and PH1's "no auxiliary inner product" clause is not touched. The BPZ form is bilinear; the sesquilinear $\int\bar\sigma\sigma'$ used for the Hilbert-space statements of (1.5) coincides with it on real functions, and since $K_P$ is real symmetric at real $s$ its eigenfunctions can be taken real, so at real $s$ the two structures give the same kernels, ranges and eigen-expansions. At complex $s$ (D1 below) only the bilinear form is meaningful, and "isotropic" refers to it.

*(1.5) Gaussian bound and compactness (C1.2).* Record 045 (3.7) bounds $\lvert F\rvert$ at every real $(s,t,u)$, and on the quadrant $t,u\ge0$ the sum reduces to $\lvert F\rvert\le c_s\,\beta^{-\alpha'(t+u)/2}\,2^{\alpha'\lvert t-u\rvert/4}$, $c_s$ depending on $s$ only (045 (3.10)). With (1.1), $t+u=2(\rho^2+\lambda^2)$ and $\lvert t-u\rvert=4\rho\lambda\lvert x\rvert\le2(\rho^2+\lambda^2)$, so
$$\lvert K_P(\ell,\ell')\rvert\le C c_s\,\exp\bigl[-\alpha'(\rho^2+\lambda^2)(\ln\beta-\tfrac12\ln2)\bigr],\tag{1.2}$$
a Gaussian in $(\ell,\ell')\in\mathbb R^{52}$ iff $\beta>\sqrt2$ — both declared values qualify, $\beta=\tfrac32$ with rate $0.0589$, $\beta=2$ with $0.347$. Hence $\int\!\!\int\lvert K_P\rvert^2\,d^{26}\ell\,d^{26}\ell'<\infty$: $K_P$ is Hilbert–Schmidt, so compact and self-adjoint on $L^2_{\rm even}(\mathbb R^{26})$. (Trace class is **not** claimed: a Gaussian bound on the kernel gives Hilbert–Schmidt only; trace class would need a derivative bound, which 045 does not supply, or positivity, which does not hold. Nothing in Blocks 2–3 uses it — the $\det_2$ of D4 is a Hilbert–Schmidt object.) Its spectrum is real, discrete, of both signs in general, accumulating only at $0$. This holds at every real $s=P^2$, timelike included (045 C4.1). For complex $s$: $\lvert Z^{p}\rvert=\lvert Z\rvert^{\operatorname{Re}p}e^{-\operatorname{Im}p\arg Z}$ with $\arg Z\in[0,\pi]$ on $R$, so the bound (1.2) holds with $\operatorname{Re}s$ times a factor $e^{\pi\alpha'\lvert\operatorname{Im}s\rvert/2}$ per tile, uniform on compacts; $K_P(s)$ is an entire Hilbert–Schmidt-operator-valued function of $s$ (each $J$ is entire of exponential type, 045 C1), complex-symmetric rather than self-adjoint off the real axis.

*(1.6) Partial waves (C1.3).* By (1.2) the kernel is $O(26)$-invariant, so it commutes with the decomposition $L^2(\mathbb R^{26})=\bigoplus_n L^2(\lambda^{25}d\lambda)\otimes\mathcal Y_n$ into spherical harmonics of degree $n$ on $S^{25}$ ($\dim\mathcal Y_n=\binom{n+25}{25}-\binom{n+23}{25}$). Funk–Hecke (Atkinson–Han, *Spherical harmonics and approximations on the unit sphere*, Thm 2.22) gives, for $f(\ell')=g(\lambda)Y_n(\hat\ell')$,
$$(K_Pf)(\ell)=Y_n(\hat\ell)\int_0^\infty\lambda^{25}\,k_n(\rho,\lambda)\,g(\lambda)\,d\lambda,\qquad
 k_n(\rho,\lambda)=\lvert S^{24}\rvert\int_{-1}^{1}K_P(\rho,\lambda,x)\,\frac{C^{(12)}_n(x)}{C^{(12)}_n(1)}\,(1-x^2)^{23/2}\,dx.\tag{1.3}$$
The weight $(1-x^2)^{23/2}dx=\sin^{24}\theta\,d\theta$ is 045's (4.4). Since $K_P$ is even in $x$ (the $t\leftrightarrow u$ symmetry), $k_n=0$ for odd $n$: **only even partial waves occur**, which is the evenness of (1.1) again. Each $k_n$ is a real symmetric kernel on $L^2([0,\infty),\lambda^{25}d\lambda)$; the unitarily equivalent symmetrised kernel $\tilde k_n(\rho,\lambda)=(\rho\lambda)^{25/2}k_n(\rho,\lambda)$ acts on $L^2(d\lambda)$ with the same spectrum. The spectrum of $K_P$ is the union over even $n$ of $\operatorname{spec}(k_n)$, each eigenvalue with multiplicity $\dim\mathcal Y_n$.

*(1.7) Where the mass sits.* On the diagonal $\rho=\lambda$ the symmetrised s-wave kernel is $\rho^{25}k_0(\rho,\rho)$, with $k_0$ an average of $F$ over $x$ against $(1-x^2)^{23/2}$. At large $\rho$ the angular integrand is $\propto2^{\alpha'\rho^2\lvert x\rvert}$ (the edge growth of 045 C3.7), so the Laplace-type average contributes $2^{\alpha'\rho^2}(\alpha'\rho^2\ln2)^{-25/2}\Gamma(\tfrac{25}2)2^{23/2}\,[1+O(\tfrac{72}{\alpha'\rho^2\ln2})]$: the $(\rho^2)^{-25/2}$ **cancels the $\rho^{25}$ phase-space factor exactly**, and with the prefactor $\beta^{-2\alpha'\rho^2}$
$$\rho^{25}k_0(\rho,\rho)\ \asymp\ \exp\bigl[-\alpha'\rho^2(2\ln\beta-\ln2)\bigr]\times(\text{powers from the corner asymptotics of }J),\tag{1.4}$$
rate $0.1178$ at $\beta=\tfrac32$, $0.6931$ at $\beta=2$ — the **edge** rate of 045 (3.10), not the diagonal rate $2\ln\beta$ ($0.811$, $1.386$). The upper bound is rigorous ($J(p,q)\le\operatorname{Vol}(R)2^q$ at $p+q\le0$, 045 (3.6)). Two corrections to the slope, of opposite signs, are made explicit in (1.8)–(1.10): the corner power $q^{-3/2}$ of $J$ (steeper, $-\tfrac32/(\alpha'\rho^2)$) and the angular Laplace crossover (shallower: the expansion of the average in $1/c$, $c=\alpha'\rho^2\ln2$, has first relative term $-\tfrac{23}4\cdot\tfrac{25}{2c}=-72/c$, so its derivative pulls the slope *up*, and the expansion is useless below $c\sim10^2$; the exact form is a Bessel function, (1.10)). Off the diagonal the angular average of $\beta^{-\alpha'(\rho^2+\lambda^2)}2^{\alpha'\rho\lambda\lvert x\rvert}$ is a ridge $\exp[-\alpha'\ln\beta\,(\rho-\lambda)^2-\alpha'(2\ln\beta-\ln2)\rho\lambda]$ of transverse width $(\alpha'\ln\beta)^{-1/2}\approx1.6$ at $\beta=\tfrac32$: the s-wave kernel is a narrow Gaussian ridge along $\rho=\lambda$ decaying slowly along it. **Consequence for any discretisation:** the radial rule must resolve the width $1.6$ (spacing $\lesssim0.4$), and the cutoff must reach $\alpha'\rho^2\sim$ several times $1/(2\ln\beta-\ln2)$ — $\Lambda\gtrsim16$ at $\beta=\tfrac32$, $\gtrsim8$ at $\beta=2$ — so the tile exponents run to $\lvert p\rvert,\lvert q\rvert\sim\alpha'\Lambda^2\sim250$, beyond the $170$ at which R50's evaluator was validated; Part 3 revalidates it there (check `C0-Jvec-deg`).

*(1.8) Corner asymptotics of $J$ at $Z=-1$ (C1.6; fix pass, critic F19).* $J(p,q)=\int_R\lvert Z\rvert^p\lvert1-Z\rvert^q\,d^2Z$ (045 (3.0)). Let $q\to+\infty$ with $\delta:=-(p+q)\ge0$ fixed — the regime of the tiles $(u,t,s)$ and $(s,t,u)$ on the diagonal at $x\to1$, where $q=\alpha'\rho^2x-\alpha's/4$ and $\delta=\alpha's/2$, resp. $\alpha'u/2$. The rate function of 045 (3.6) is attained at the corner $Z=-1$, where the unit-circle arc and the real segment of $\partial R$ meet; $R$ lies outside the unit circle there. Write $Z=-1+\epsilon$, $\epsilon=x+\mathrm iy$, $y\ge0$; the constraint $\lvert Z\rvert\ge1$ reads $x\le\tfrac12(x^2+y^2)$, i.e. $x\le\tfrac12y^2+O(y^4)$. With $\ln\lvert Z\rvert=-x-\tfrac12(x^2-y^2)+O(\lvert\epsilon\rvert^3)$ and $\ln\lvert1-Z\rvert=\ln2-\tfrac x2-\tfrac18(x^2-y^2)+O(\lvert\epsilon\rvert^3)$,
$$\Phi:=p\ln\lvert Z\rvert+q\ln\lvert1-Z\rvert=q\ln2+x\bigl(\tfrac q2+\delta\bigr)+(x^2-y^2)\bigl(\tfrac{3q}8+\tfrac\delta2\bigr)+O(q\lvert\epsilon\rvert^3).\tag{1.8}$$
$\Phi$ increases in $x$, so its maximum over $\bar R$ near the corner lies on the circle $x=\tfrac12y^2$, where $\Phi=q\ln2-\tfrac q8y^2+O(qy^4)$: the corner is a strict maximum, of width $y\sim q^{-1/2}$ along the circle and $\xi:=x-\tfrac12y^2\sim-1/q$ transverse to it. Laplace's method in $(\xi,y)$ — the terms $\xi^2,\xi y^2$ of (1.8) are $O(1/q)$ relative in this scaling —
$$J(-q-\delta,q)=2^q\int_0^\infty dy\,e^{-qy^2/8}\int_{-\infty}^0 d\xi\,e^{\xi(q/2+\delta)}\,[1+O(1/q)]
 =2^q\cdot\frac1{q/2+\delta}\cdot\frac12\sqrt{\frac{8\pi}q}\,[1+O(1/q)],$$
$$\boxed{\ J(-q-\delta,q)=2\sqrt{2\pi}\;2^q\,q^{-3/2}\Bigl[1+\frac{c_1(\delta)}q+O(q^{-2})\Bigr]\ }\tag{1.9}$$
with $c_1$ not derived here; numerically (check `C1.6`, $q=200,400,800$), consistent with $c_1(\delta)=c_1(0)-2\delta$, the $-2\delta$ being the expansion of $1/(q/2+\delta)$. The corner is $\beta$-independent, and so is the leading asymptotics: $J(-250,250)$ agrees at $\beta=\tfrac32$ and $\beta=2$ to $12$ digits (the rest of $R$ is suppressed by $2^{-q(1-\cdots)}$). *Contribution to the diagonal slope (iteration-2 residue, critic finding N1).* On the diagonal $\rho=\lambda$ at angle $x$, the $(u,t,s)$ tile has $q=\alpha'\rho^2x-\alpha's/4\to\infty$ at fixed $\delta=\alpha's/2$, exactly the regime of (1.9): $J\simeq2\sqrt{2\pi}\,2^qq^{-3/2}$. The $(s,t,u)$ tile shares the same $q$ but its own $\delta_{stu}=\alpha'u/2=\alpha'\rho^2(1-x)$, which is $O(q)$ rather than fixed on the angular window $x\in[x_0,1)$ — the $\delta\ll q$ corner formula (1.9) does **not** apply to it at leading order, and applying it there anyway (the first version of this record) understates the second tile's contribution. Its own corner evaluation, keeping $\delta_{stu}$ unexpanded in the Laplace exponent of (1.8) rather than expanding it as (1.9) does, gives instead $J\simeq2\sqrt{2\pi}\,2^{q}q^{-1/2}(q/2+\delta_{stu})^{-1}$, with $q/2+\delta_{stu}=\alpha'\rho^2(1-x/2)-\alpha's/8$; both tiles' corner factors are $O(\rho^{-3})$ on the diagonal, not one leading and one $O(1/q)$-subleading to the other. The symmetrised diagonal is modelled by the two-tile sum
$$d_{\rm model}(\rho)=\rho^{25}\beta^{-2\alpha'\rho^2}\int_{x_0}^1 2^{\alpha'\rho^2x}\Bigl[q^{-3/2}+q^{-1/2}\bigl(\tfrac q2+\delta_{stu}\bigr)^{-1}\Bigr](1-x^2)^{23/2}\,dx\qquad(x_0=0.3;\ \text{the rest is }O(2^{-0.7\alpha'\rho^2})),\tag{1.10}$$
whose log-slope in $\alpha'\rho^2$ is, to leading order, $-(2\ln\beta-\ln2)-\tfrac32/(\alpha'\rho^2)+(\text{angular crossover})$, the two-tile correction changing the coefficient of the $1/(\alpha'\rho^2)$ term but not its sign. Without either corner factor, the angular factor is exactly $\int_{-1}^1e^{cx}(1-x^2)^{23/2}dx=\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(\tfrac{25}2)(2/c)^{12}I_{12}(c)$, $c=\alpha'\rho^2\ln2$, whose log-derivative $I_{13}(c)/I_{12}(c)$ tends to $1-\tfrac{25}{2c}+\tfrac{575}{8c^2}+\dots$: the $\tfrac{25}{2c}$ cancels the $\rho^{25}$ and the remainder pushes the slope **above** the edge rate ($-0.0985$ at $\alpha'\rho^2=60$, $-0.1157$ at $250$, against the edge $-0.1178$): the measured slopes are below the edge rate because of the tiles' corner factors, and the residual is the sum of the two effects, with the corner sign. Model (1.10) against measurement (3.1): the five slopes agree (max deviation $1.05\times10^{-3}$, the genuine $O(1/q)$ subleading terms of (1.9) in each tile, not carried by either corner factor at leading order); the crossover-only model (no corner factor at all) deviates by $2.4\times10^{-2}$ with the wrong sign (checks `C3.5-corner`, `BREAK-C3.5-corner`).

\subsection*{Commentary}

- (1.1) is R48 applied to 045's assignment; nothing new. Its $O(26)$ content (1.2) is what makes 18c a one-dimensional (radial) problem per partial wave and is the practical payoff of R48.
- (1.4) settles a question the ledger left open in words ("no auxiliary inner product"): the level-0 block has a canonical $L^2$ structure, the BPZ one. At higher levels $G_2$ is indefinite and the statement changes; see Part 2.
- (1.5) is the first statement of what kind of object $V_4^{(0)}(P^2)$ is: a compact operator with a Gaussian kernel. Everything in (c) below follows from that.
- (1.8)–(1.10) settle the sign question of the residual: the first version of this record attributed the $4$–$5\%$ excess of the measured decay rate to the angular Laplace crossover, whose sign is in fact the opposite (it makes the slope *less* negative); the corner power $q^{-3/2}$ is what makes it *more* negative, and the model (1.10) with both reproduces the five slopes to $2\times10^{-3}$. The same corner controls the complex-$s$ behaviour of the tiles that 18c.6 continues into the strip, since $\operatorname{Im}s$ enters through $e^{-\operatorname{Im}p\arg Z}$ and $\arg Z=\pi$ at the corner.
- (1.7) corrects an expectation one would take from 045 Block 4: the row mass in the *full* kernel peaks at $\alpha'T_*=12/\ln\beta$ ($T=\rho^2+\lambda^2$) only along the diagonal direction of the $(t,u)$ quadrant; the s-wave average is dominated by the edge, its decay rate is $2\ln\beta-\ln2$ per $\alpha'\rho^2$, and the $D=26$ power is absent. At $\beta=\tfrac32$ this is a factor $6.9$ slower than the diagonal rate — 18c is expensive there and cheap at $\beta=2$.

### Block 2 — The stand-ins for PH1 (ii), PH18/PH19, PH3 at continuous relative momentum (C2)

**(c1) Quotient by $\ker V_4$.** At level 0 and fixed $P$, under H1: $\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0\rvert_{TT,P}=L^2_{\rm even}(\mathbb R^{26})$ in the Euclidean relative momenta (odd functions are absent by definition, not quotiented), and $\ker V_4^{(0)}(P)=\ker K_P=\{\sigma\in L^2_{\rm even}:K_P\sigma=0\}$, the null space of a compact self-adjoint operator: closed, of finite or infinite dimension, equal to $\bigoplus_n\ker k_n\otimes\mathcal Y_n$. The quotient is $(\ker K_P)^\perp$ in the BPZ/$L^2$ structure of (1.4). Two candidate definitions differ only if $\ker K_P$ is infinite-dimensional: (i) the algebraic quotient, (ii) the closure of the range $\overline{\operatorname{ran}K_P}=(\ker K_P)^\perp$. PH1 (ii) is stated on (i); for a self-adjoint compact operator (i) and (ii) coincide, so no choice is needed **at level 0**. (At level $L>0$, $G_2$ is indefinite and $V_4$ is BPZ-symmetric rather than self-adjoint; kernel and range need not be complementary — 036 §5.2 last item — and the choice matters. Not this sub-task.)

**(c2) $m_2^{-1}$ on the quotient (PH1).** $m_2=(2g_T)^{-1}K_P$ on $(\ker K_P)^\perp$ is injective with dense range and an unbounded inverse (eigenvalues accumulate at 0). "Invertible on the quotient" can mean: (α) injective (no null vector besides $\ker$) — always true on $(\ker K_P)^\perp$ by construction; (β) injective **and** $\ker K_P=0$, i.e. no null vector at all at the external $s$; (γ) bounded inverse — false for every compact operator, and disowned by the ledger's restatement (2026-09-22). The level-0 stand-in for PH1 (ii) is (β): **$\ker K_P(s)=0$ at the external $P^2$, the on-shell $s=16/3$ included**. (β) is **sufficient for PH1 (ii) at level 0, not equivalent to it**: PH1 quotients by $\ker V_4$, so a level-0 null vector that is a null vector of the full $V_4$ is exactly what PH1 permits and removes; only a null vector of $K_P$ that is *not* in $\ker V_4$ — one that couples to level $>0$ — would be a failure of the kernel-stability clause. At level 0 alone that clause has no content beyond (α), and deciding it for a candidate null vector needs the level-$(0,1)$ block, the same external data as 18c.7. Hence the routing of Part 2: a positive (β) verdict (no null vector) supports PH1 (ii) at level 0; a negative one (a converged null vector of $K_P$) is reported as *a candidate element of $\ker V_4$, stability test pending the level-$(0,1)$ data*, never as PH1 failing. The Nyström side of the stability question — that a discrete null vector converges to one of $K_P$ rather than being a discretisation artefact — is 18c.4's convergence criterion. What the identity $m_2^{-1}m_2=\mathrm{id}$ needs at the telescoping collapse is (β) on the states $m_2\Phi$ actually inserted, so (β) restricted to the partial waves those states excite — for external tachyon pairs, all even $n$.

**(c3) $\det V_4^{(L)}(P^2)$ (PH18, PH19).** For the level-0 block with continuous momentum the determinant does not exist: $\prod_j\mu_j(s)$ over the spectrum of a compact operator with $\mu_j\to0$ is $0$ or undefined, and $K_P$ is not of the form $1+$(trace class), so no Fredholm determinant applies either. The candidate stand-ins, with what each hypothesis needs:

- (D1) *Eigenvalue branches.* $s\mapsto K_P(s)$ is an entire compact-operator family, self-adjoint at real $s$ and complex-symmetric off the axis; away from exceptional points its isolated eigenvalues $\mu_{n,j}(s)$ are analytic branches (Kato, *Perturbation theory for linear operators*, VII §1, holomorphic families of closed operators, applied to the finite-rank projections onto isolated eigenvalues; for the self-adjoint real-$s$ family through crossings VII §3, Rellich's theorem). "A zero of $\det V_4^{(L)}$" becomes "a zero of some $\mu_{n,j}(s)$", equivalently a non-trivial $\ker K_P(s_0)$ — a well-posed statement, the same as (β) failing at $s_0$. **PH18 needs D1 with the isotropy clause**: a complex $s_0$ off the lattice $\alpha's\in\{4,0,-4,\dots\}$ with a null vector $\lambda_0$ of $K_P(s_0)$ that is **non-isotropic** in the bilinear BPZ form of (1.4), $\int\lambda_0\lambda_0\,d^{26}\ell\ne0$ (at real $s_0$ the null vector is real and this is automatic; at complex $s_0$, where PH18 lives, it is not: the family is complex-symmetric and a simple zero of a branch with $\int\lambda_0\lambda_0=0$ is exactly the case the PH18 row excludes, the one that produces no pole in 035/036's isotropy test). *Which object the zeros stand in for:* the record's own (2.1) shows that the composite $V_4m_2^{-1}V_4$ carries $\mu_I$, not $\mu_I^{-1}$, so a branch zero produces no pole in that representation; PH18's pole lives in the representation of $m_2^{-1}$ itself (R29's closing sentence), i.e. in $\mathcal C^{(L)}(P)$ resolved on the $\Sigma$-propagator, where $\mu_{n,j}(s)^{-1}$ appears with the residue $\lambda_0\otimes\lambda_0/\int\lambda_0\lambda_0$. 18c.6's zeros are the zeros of that denominator; the isotropy test is what decides whether the residue is finite. **PH19 needs D1 plus its level-$L$ version**: the zeros of the branches of $V_4^{(L)}$ converging as $L\to\infty$.
- (D2) *Nyström determinant.* $\det M_N(s)$ for the $N\times N$ symmetrised Nyström matrix is an entire function of $s$ whose zeros are the union of the zeros of the $N$ discrete branches $\mu^{(N)}_j(s)$; the low-$j$ branches converge to D1's (Nyström convergence for continuous kernels, Atkinson 1997 Thm 4.1.1; the Gaussian bound gives uniform continuity on the truncated domain), the high-$j$ ones are $\sim10^{-16}\times$ the leading one and their zeros are noise. **Usable only branch by branch, never as the full product.** This is what a naive transcription of PH18 into code would compute, and it is the wrong object.
- (D3) *Frozen-momentum model* (035 §4.2): $\ell=\ell'=0$, block $1\times1$, $V_4^{TT}(s)=F(s,0,0)$, entire in $s$ with computable zeros. A model, not a truncation: it is the $\rho=\lambda=0$ corner of the kernel and sees no partial-wave or radial structure. Its zeros are not those of any $\mu_{n,j}$.
- (D4) *Regularised determinant* $\det_2$ or $\det(1+\epsilon^{-1}K_P)$: zeros in $s$ at fixed $\epsilon$ are the points where $-\epsilon$ is an eigenvalue — a different question from a null vector, useful only as $\epsilon\to0$, where it reduces to D1.

**Choice:** D1, computed through D2 branch by branch, per even partial wave, with D3 as a cheap first scan. "The level-0 tachyon block is the first truncation" means: $L=0$ on both strands, all even partial waves, radial momentum continuous — the double limit (radial rule $N$, cutoff $\Lambda$) is a discretisation of the fixed-$L$ block, not a level truncation, and its convergence is C3.4's business. The level-$L$ blocks for $L>0$ add the oscillator states $\{T,D,\dots\}\otimes\{T,D,\dots\}$ at each $(\ell,\ell')$; they couple partial waves only through the tensor structure of the excited states, so the partial-wave reduction survives as a block structure in the $O(26)$ representation content.

**(c4) The partial sums $s_N$ of PH3.** In the eigenbasis of $K_P$ (which is $m_2$'s, $G_2$ being constant): $I=(n,m,j)$ with $n$ even, $m$ the magnetic index ($\dim\mathcal Y_n$ values), $j$ the radial eigen-index of $k_n$; $\lambda_I(\ell)=\phi_{n,j}(\rho)Y_{n,m}(\hat\ell)$; $\mu_I=\mu_{n,j}$. Then $V_4(\Phi,m_2^{-1}\lambda_I)\langle\lambda_I,m_2\Psi\rangle=\langle\Phi,\lambda_I\rangle\,\mu_I\,\langle\lambda_I,\Psi\rangle$ (constants $2g_T$ cancel), so
$$s_N(\Phi,\Psi)=\sum_{I\le N}\mu_I\,\langle\Phi,\lambda_I\rangle\langle\lambda_I,\Psi\rangle\ \longrightarrow\ V_4(\Phi,\Psi)\tag{2.1}$$
is the eigen-expansion of the kernel. For $\Phi,\Psi\in L^2$ it converges (spectral theorem); the external states of the four-point amplitude are momentum eigenstates $\Phi=\delta(\ell-\ell_0)$, **not** in $L^2$, and (2.1) becomes the pointwise expansion $\sum_I\mu_I\lambda_I(\ell_0)\lambda_I(\ell_0')\to K_P(\ell_0,\ell_0')$, which for an *indefinite* kernel is not guaranteed by Mercer's theorem (positive kernels only). **PH3 at level 0 is therefore: absolute convergence of $\sum_I\lvert\mu_I\rvert\,\lvert\lambda_I(\ell_0)\lambda_I(\ell_0')\rvert$ at the external relative momenta**, ordered by $\lvert\mu_I\rvert$ (the natural $N$). Two moves relative to the PH3 row, both deliberate: (i) a *change of basis* — the row's $s_N$ sums over the strands' oscillator levels and the pair's relative momentum, i.e. the momentum basis, in which at fixed level the sum is the integral $\int d^{26}\ell\,K_P(\ell_0,\ell)\,(\cdots)$ and the finite-node identity is 023's tautology; the eigenbasis of $K_P$ is the one in which "partial sum" and "ordered by size" have a basis-independent meaning, and in which the collapse $m_2^{-1}m_2$ is diagonal; (ii) a *strengthening* — absolute convergence instead of the row's "convergence of numbers", because the eigen-expansion of an indefinite kernel with no canonical ordering other than $\lvert\mu_I\rvert$ has a well-defined sum only if it converges absolutely (a conditionally convergent rearrangeable series would make the verdict ordering-dependent). A positive verdict on the strengthened form implies the row; a negative one on the strengthened form is reported as such, with the conditional convergence in the $\lvert\mu\rvert$ ordering examined separately — a well-posed question with a numerical falsifier: the number of terms needed for a fixed error at $(\ell_0,\ell_0')$ must stabilise under Nyström refinement (if it grows with $N_{\rm nodes}$, the continuum sum does not converge — the "saturation drift" of PH3's falsifier column). The finite-matrix identity at the nodes is tautological (023's trap); only the drift is informative. The level part of $I$ ($L>0$) multiplies this by the finite oscillator sum, unchanged.

**Answer to "is 18c well posed as written":** not literally — "$\det V_4^{(L)}(P^2)$" and "invert on the quotient" do not exist for the continuous relative momentum — but it is well posed in the D1/(β)/(2.1) form, with the level-0 block a compact self-adjoint operator family whose eigenvalue branches, null vectors and eigen-expansions are the objects; every hypothesis maps onto one of them without a regulator, and the Nyström discretisation is a convergent approximation of each (branch by branch), never of a determinant.

- The convergence table is the deliverable of Part 3: a design rule for 18c. - The definiteness of the leading eigenvalues (all of one sign: positive at $C=+1$, negative at the ledger's $C<0$) is a numerical observation at two $s$ per $\beta$ and says nothing about the small end of the spectrum, where PH1 (ii)'s null vectors would sit (18c.4). - The $4$–$5\%$ residual in (3.1) is resolved by (1.8)–(1.10): the corner power $q^{-3/2}$ of $J$ at $Z=-1$, checked against the evaluator at $q$ up to $800$ (`C1.6`), not the angular crossover, whose sign is the opposite. The residual fades as $\tfrac32/(\alpha'\rho^2)$, so the slopes approach the edge rate from below; the window of `C3.5` is right on both sides for that reason (the crossover, $O(\rho^{-4})$, never lifts the slope above the edge rate once the corner power is included). It does not affect the design rule.
- The `C3.5-ang` failure of the first run (32 angular nodes) is recorded as a finding: the angular integrand $2^{\alpha'\rho\lambda\lvert x\rvert}$ needs $n_\theta\gtrsim\sqrt{c}$ nodes with $c=\alpha'\rho\lambda\ln2$; at $\rho\lambda\le60$ (where the mass is) $24$ nodes give $10^{-9}$, at $\rho\lambda=250$ they do not.
