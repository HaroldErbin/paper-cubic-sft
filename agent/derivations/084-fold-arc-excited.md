## §1 OPE exponent and integrand (C1)

\subsection*{Setup}
Uses A1–A3 and 079 (1.1)–(1.4): $w_a=2\beta$, $w_b=-2\beta$, $w_c=2\beta y$, $w_d=-2\beta y$, $y=q/4\beta^2$; $\kappa_{ij}=\alpha'k_i\cdot k_j$; $h_0=\tfrac{\alpha'}2(k_c+k_d)^2-2$, $a_-=1+\tfrac12(\kappa_{ac}+\kappa_{bd})$, $a_+=1+\tfrac12(\kappa_{ad}+\kappa_{bc})$, $a_-+a_+=-h_0$ (079 C4.1, conservation only). An input $j$ is $c\bar c\,V_j$, $V_j=\prod_{i}(\epsilon_{j,i}\cdot\partial^{n_{j,i}}X)\prod_{l}(\bar\epsilon_{j,l}\cdot\bar\partial^{\bar n_{j,l}}X)\,e^{ik_jX}$ (normal ordered; general states are linear combinations), holomorphic level $N_j=\sum_in_{j,i}$, antiholomorphic $\bar N_j=N_j$. $N_{\rm tot}:=\sum_jN_j$. $\nu_-$ ($\nu_+$) := order of the pole at $y=1$ ($y=-1$) of the holomorphic oscillator factor, $\bar\nu_\mp$ the antiholomorphic ones.

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(1.1) Wick contractions.* From $X^\mu(z,\bar z)X^\nu(w,\bar w)\sim-\tfrac{\alpha'}2\eta^{\mu\nu}\ln|z-w|^2$:
$$
\partial^nX^\mu(z)\,e^{ikX}(w)\sim-\frac{i\alpha'}2\,k^\mu\,\frac{(-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!}{(z-w)^n}\,e^{ikX}(w),\qquad
\partial^nX^\mu(z)\,\partial^{n'}X^\nu(w)\sim-\frac{\alpha'}2\eta^{\mu\nu}\,\frac{(-1)^{n-1}(n+n'-1)!}{(z-w)^{n+n'}}
\tag{1.1}
$$
(both follow from the book's `cft:eq:scalar-ope-X-X`, $X(z)X(w)\sim-\tfrac{\epsilon\ell^2}2\ln(z-w)$ with $\epsilon=+1$, $\ell^2=\alpha'$, by differentiation and Wick's theorem; live source commit 1dbb721. $n=1$ reproduces the active `cft:eq:scalar-ope-dX-V`, $J(z)V_k(w)\sim\tfrac k2V_k(w)/(z-w)$, for $J=\tfrac{i}{\ell^2}\partial X$ (the normalisation under which the two agree; the complex components of `cft:eq:scalar-current-U1` were not re-derived here), and `cft:eq:scalar-ope-dX-dX`, $\partial X\partial X\sim-\tfrac{\alpha'}2(z-w)^{-2}$; equivalently Polchinski vol. I §2.2, $\partial X^\mu(z)e^{ikX}(0)\sim-\tfrac{i\alpha'}2k^\mu e^{ikX}(0)/z$.) The sphere correlator of the four inputs is the Koba–Nielsen factor of 079 (1.1) times the sum over complete contractions of the oscillators, each oscillator $\partial^nX(w_j)$ contracted either with an exponential $e^{ik_lX}(w_l)$, $l\ne j$ (factor $\propto(w_j-w_l)^{-n}$), or with an oscillator at $w_l\ne w_j$ (factor $\propto(w_j-w_l)^{-n-n'}$); no self-contraction (normal ordering). Holomorphic and antiholomorphic oscillators contract separately ($\langle\partial X\bar\partial X\rangle$ is a contact term), so the oscillator factor is $\sum_r\mathcal P_r(w)\,\overline{\tilde{\mathcal P}_r(w)}$, a finite sum of products of a holomorphic and an antiholomorphic rational function of the $w_j$.

*(1.2) Pole order at the cross-channel collisions.* At $y\to1$ exactly the pairs $(a,c)$ and $(b,d)$ collide: $w_a-w_c=2\beta(1-y)$, $w_b-w_d=-2\beta(1-y)$, every other $w_j-w_l$ stays $\ge2\beta$ in modulus. A contraction factor is singular at $y=1$ only if it connects the two members of a colliding pair, and then has pole order $n$ (oscillator–exponential) or $n+n'$ (oscillator–oscillator), i.e. each oscillator contributes at most its own $n$. Hence the pole order of each $\mathcal P_r$ at $y=1$ is at most the total holomorphic level of the colliding pairs, $\nu_-\le N_a+N_c+N_b+N_d=N_{\rm tot}$; at $y=-1$ the pairs are $(a,d),(b,c)$, again all four inputs, $\nu_+\le N_{\rm tot}$; the same for $\bar\nu_\mp$. The maximal pole is reached by the term in which every oscillator of $a$ ($b$) contracts within its pair; its coefficient factorises, at leading order in $1-y$, into the leading OPE coefficients of the two pairs onto the tachyons $e^{\pm i(k_a+k_c)X}$:
$$
V_a(w_a)V_c(w_c)=\big[C_{ac}\,(w_a-w_c)^{\frac{\kappa_{ac}}2-N_a-N_c}\times\text{c.c.}\big]\,e^{i(k_a+k_c)X}(w_c)+\dots,\qquad
C_{ac}\ni\big(-\tfrac{i\alpha'}2\big)^{\#}\prod_i\big(\epsilon_{a,i}\cdot k_c\big)\prod_{i'}\big(\epsilon_{c,i'}\cdot k_a\big)\times(\text{non-zero integers}),
\tag{1.2}
$$
the exponent $\tfrac{\kappa_{ac}}2-N_a-N_c=h_{k_a+k_c}-h_a-h_c$ being the weight difference to the lowest operator in that channel. $C_{ac}$ is a polynomial in the polarisations that is not identically zero: exactly one contraction pattern (every oscillator of $a$ contracted with $e^{ik_cX}$, every oscillator of $c$ with $e^{ik_aX}$) produces the monomial $\prod_i(\epsilon_{a,i}\cdot k_c)\prod_{i'}(\epsilon_{c,i'}\cdot k_a)$ – any other pattern carries some $\epsilon_a\cdot\epsilon_c$, $\epsilon_a\cdot k_{b,d}$ or a lower pole – so no two patterns cancel it; hence for generic polarisations $\nu_\mp=\bar\nu_\mp=N_{\rm tot}$; on special loci (e.g. $\epsilon_a\cdot k_c=0$ for every oscillator of $a$ and no compensating $\epsilon_a\cdot\epsilon_c$) the order is lower, never higher.

*(1.3) Subleading terms.* Near $y=1$ the holomorphic factor of the integrand is
$$
\Phi_r(y):=(1-y)^{a_-}(1+y)^{a_+}\,P_r(y)=(1-y)^{a_--\nu_-}\sum_{k\ge0}e_{r,k}(1-y)^k ,
\tag{1.3}
$$
convergent for $|1-y|<2$: the exponents are integer-spaced above $a_--\nu_-$. For one level-(1,1) input at $a$ with tensor polarisation $\epsilon_{\mu\nu}$, $\epsilon_{\mu\nu}k_a^\mu=\epsilon_{\mu\nu}k_a^\nu=0$ (the primary case, A3):
$$
P^\mu(y)=\frac{k_b^\mu}2+\frac{k_c^\mu}{1-y}+\frac{k_d^\mu}{1+y},\qquad
I_{(1,1)}=-\frac{\alpha'^2|f'_a(0)|^2}{16\beta^2}\,\mathcal N\,|y|^{h_0}\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\,\Phi^\mu(y)\,\overline{\Phi^\nu(y)},
\tag{1.4}
$$
from $\mathcal F^\mu=-\tfrac{i\alpha'}2\sum_{j\ne a}k_j^\mu/(w_a-w_j)$, $w_a-w_b=4\beta$, $w_a-w_{c,d}=2\beta(1\mp y)$, and the extra weight $(1,1)$ of $V_a$, whose Jacobian $|f'_a(0)|^2$ is $q$-independent (copy 1, A2). Near $y=1$, with $k_b+k_d=-k_a-k_c$ and $\epsilon\perp k_a$,
$$
\epsilon\cdot\Phi(y)=2^{a_+}(\epsilon\cdot k_c)\,(1-y)^{a_--1}\Big[1-\tfrac{1+a_+}2(1-y)+O\big((1-y)^2\big)\Big],
\tag{1.5}
$$
so $\nu_-=1$ iff $\epsilon_{\mu\nu}k_c^\mu\ne0$ (and $\bar\nu_-=1$ iff $\epsilon_{\mu\nu}k_c^\nu\ne0$); at $y=-1$ the same with $k_d$ and $a_+\leftrightarrow a_-$. For two level-one inputs at $a$ and $c$ (holomorphic polarisations $e_a\perp k_a$, $e_c\perp k_c$, primary), the holomorphic factor is $y\big[\mathcal F_a\mathcal F_c-\tfrac{\alpha'}2\,e_a\cdot e_c\,(w_a-w_c)^{-2}\big]$ (the $y$ from $c$'s Jacobian $\phi_c'(0)\propto q$, which cancels the $1/(w_c-w_d)$ pole of $\mathcal F_c$ at $y=0$), with leading pole at $y=1$
$$
\frac{1}{4\beta^2(1-y)^2}\Big[\tfrac{\alpha'^2}4(e_a\cdot k_c)(e_c\cdot k_a)-\tfrac{\alpha'}2\,e_a\cdot e_c\Big],
\tag{1.6}
$$
order $2=N_a+N_c$, and order 2 at $y=-1$ through $(e_a\cdot k_d)(e_c\cdot k_b)$ ($\mathcal F_c\ni e_c\cdot k_b/(w_c-w_b)$, $w_c-w_b=2\beta(1+y)$).

*(1.4) Non-primary inputs and $y=0$.* For a non-primary $V_j$ the local-coordinate map adds, at the same point, operators of lower level with coefficients built from $\phi_j^{(k)}(0)$. In copy 1 these are $q$-independent; in copy 2 $\phi_j=q\,g_j(\xi)$ (A2), so they are powers of $q=4\beta^2y$, regular and non-zero at $y=\pm1$. Lower level means lower pole order by (1.2), so the bound $\nu_\mp,\bar\nu_\mp\le N_{\rm tot}$ holds for all matter-oscillator inputs. At $y=0$ the integrand is $|y|^{h_0}$ times a double power series in $y,\bar y$ (the circle-state expansion: the lowest $L_0^+$ in the channel is that of $c\bar c\,e^{i(k_c+k_d)X}$, the same ghost content as for tachyons; excitations at $c,d$ bring $q^{N_c+N_d}$ from the Jacobians and at most $(w_c-w_d)^{-N_c-N_d}\propto y^{-N_c-N_d}$ from the contractions). $h_0$ and $a_\pm$ are 079's.

\subsection*{Commentary}
(1.2): this is the standard statement that the most singular term of an OPE is fixed by the lowest-weight operator of the channel, $(z-w)^{h_p-h_i-h_j}$. In the $t$-channel the lowest operator is the tachyon of momentum $k_a+k_c$ for any matter-oscillator inputs, so, when both pairs couple to it, excitations lower the exponent by their weight, one unit per $\partial$; when a pair does not couple to it (parity-odd $B$–$T$, C4.3), the leading term of the pair OPE is the lightest operator, of any spin, that it does couple to, and the statement holds per chirality: the holomorphic exponent is set by the lightest holomorphic weight, which for $\bar\partial X\,e^{ipX}$ is still the tachyon's (C4.3, (4.5)). The factorisation into the two pairs' OPEs holds because both pairs collide at the same rate ($|w_a-w_c|=|w_b-w_d|$) and the cross contractions between the pairs are regular. (1.4)–(1.5): $\epsilon_{\mu\nu}k_c^\mu k_c^\nu$ is the three-point coupling $\langle V_a\,e^{ik_cX}\,e^{-i(k_a+k_c)X}\rangle$; it vanishes on a codimension-one locus of polarisations, which contains the whole antisymmetric part ($\epsilon_{[\mu\nu]}k_c^\mu k_c^\nu=0$): for $B$-fields the coupling to the tachyon vanishes for the entire irrep, not on a special locus (C4.3). The bound uses matter oscillators only (A3); ghost oscillators among the $c$'s do not raise the singularity ($c(z)c(w)$ is regular), $b$-ghost excitations are outside A3.

\subsection*{Result}
$$
\boxed{\ I=\mathcal N|y|^{h_0}|1-y|^{2a_-}|1+y|^{2a_+}\sum_rP_r(y)\overline{\tilde P_r(y)},\quad
P_r,\tilde P_r\ \text{rational, poles only at }y=\pm1,\ \text{orders }\nu_\mp,\bar\nu_\mp\le N_{\rm tot},\ =N_{\rm tot}\ \text{generically}\ }
$$
so near $y=1$ the integrand is at most as singular as $|1-y|^{2a_--\nu_--\bar\nu_-}$ ($\nu_-,\bar\nu_-$ the pole orders per $r$), with equality iff $\sum_re_{r,0}\tilde e_{r,0}\ne0$ (for a sum of rank above one the per-$r$ orders bound the singularity but do not fix it: in the $B$ example of (4.5) every term has double poles at $y=-1$, yet $|I|\sim|1+y|^{2a_+-2}$); generic: $|1-y|^{2(a_--N_{\rm tot})}$, with integer-spaced subleading exponents (1.3) and the explicit first correction (1.5).

## §2 Sector-coefficient growth (C2)

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(2.1) Coefficients.* With $\Phi_r(y)=\sum_NG^{(r)}_Ny^N$ and $\tilde\Phi_r(\bar y)=\sum_{\bar N}\tilde G^{(r)}_{\bar N}\bar y^{\bar N}$ (both holomorphic in $|y|<1$ by C1),
$$
c_{h,m}(4\beta^2)^h=\mathcal N\sum_rG^{(r)}_N\tilde G^{(r)}_{\bar N},\qquad h=h_0+N+\bar N,\ m=N-\bar N,
\tag{2.1}
$$
exactly as 079 (1.6) (the $(4\beta^2)^h$ from $|y|^h$). For one level-(1,1) input at $a$, (1.4) gives $G^\mu_N=\tfrac{k_b^\mu}2g_N[a_-,a_+]+k_c^\mu g_N[a_--1,a_+]+k_d^\mu g_N[a_-,a_+-1]$, $g_N[a,b]:=[y^N](1-y)^a(1+y)^b$, and $c\propto\epsilon_{\mu\nu}G^\mu_NG^\nu_{\bar N}$; for general inputs $\Phi_r=Q_r(y)\,(1-y)^{a_--\nu}(1+y)^{a_+-\nu}$ with $Q_r$ a polynomial, so $G^{(r)}=Q_r*g[a_--\nu,a_+-\nu]$.

*(2.2) Darboux.* By (1.3) the singular expansion of $\Phi_r$ at $y=1$ is $\sum_ke_{r,k}(1-y)^{\alpha^L_-+k}$, and at $y=-1$ likewise with $\alpha^L_+$. The transfer of 079 (1.7) applies term by term ($[y^N](1-y)^b=\Gamma(N-b)/\Gamma(-b)\Gamma(N+1)$):
$$
G^{(r)}_N=\frac{e_{r,0}}{\Gamma(-\alpha^L_-)}N^{-\alpha^L_--1}\big(1+O(N^{-1})\big)+(-1)^N\frac{e^+_{r,0}}{\Gamma(-\alpha^L_+)}N^{-\alpha^L_+-1}\big(1+O(N^{-1})\big),
\tag{2.2}
$$
the subleading $e_{r,k\ge1}$ entering at relative order $N^{-k}$. Hence, exactly as in 079 (1.7)(i)–(iii) with the exponents replaced:

(i) fixed $m$: the product of (2.2) with its antiholomorphic counterpart has four terms $(\sigma,\tau)\in\{-,+\}^2$ (write $e^-_{r,0}:=e_{r,0}$, $\tilde e^\mp_{r,0}$ likewise),
$$
c_{h,m}(4\beta^2)^h\simeq\mathcal N\sum_{\sigma,\tau}s_{\sigma\tau}\,\frac{\sum_re^\sigma_{r,0}\tilde e^\tau_{r,0}}{\Gamma(-\alpha^L_\sigma)\Gamma(-\alpha^R_\tau)}\,N^{-\alpha^L_\sigma-1}\bar N^{-\alpha^R_\tau-1},\qquad s_{--}=1,\ s_{++}=(-1)^{m},\ s_{-+}=(-1)^{\bar N},\ s_{+-}=(-1)^{N},
$$
so $c_{h,m}(4\beta^2)^h\sim h^{-\min(\alpha^L_\sigma+\alpha^R_\tau)-2}$, the minimum over the $(\sigma,\tau)$ with non-vanishing coefficient $\sum_re^\sigma_{r,0}\tilde e^\tau_{r,0}$; the same-point terms ($\sigma=\tau$) have a fixed sign at fixed $m$, the cross terms ($\sigma\ne\tau$, $y=1$ with $\bar y=-1$ and conversely) alternate as $(-1)^N$. Keeping only $\sigma=\tau$ is the error of the withdrawn C3.3; in C3.5 a cross term dominates. Generically the minimum is the same-point term at the nearer singularity, $h^{-2\alpha_{\rm eff}-2}$ (non-oscillating when $a_-\ne a_+$; for $a_-=a_+$ all four terms share the exponent), with amplitude $\mathcal N\sum_re_{r,0}\tilde e_{r,0}/\Gamma(-\alpha_{\rm eff})^2$ (single input: $\propto\epsilon_{\mu\nu}k_c^\mu k_c^\nu\,4^{a_+}/\Gamma(1-a_-)^2$ when $a_-<a_+$);

(ii) fixed $\theta$ on the arcs: $T_n(\theta)\sim n^{-\min(\alpha^L,\alpha^R)-1}$, oscillating, $\alpha^{L,R}:=\min_\mp\alpha^{L,R}_\mp$; generically $n^{-\alpha_{\rm eff}-1}$;

(iii) arc midpoint $\theta=\pi/2$: the coalescing singularities ($y=1$ of $\Phi$ with $\bar y=-1$ of $\tilde\Phi$, and $y=-1$ with $\bar y=1$) give $T_n(\tfrac\pi2)\sim n^{-\min(\alpha^L_-+\alpha^R_+,\ \alpha^L_++\alpha^R_-)-1}$; generically $n^{-(a_-+a_+)+2N_{\rm tot}-1}=n^{h_0+2N_{\rm tot}-1}$.

\subsection*{Commentary}
(2.2): nothing in 079's transfer argument used the specific form $(1-y)^{a_-}(1+y)^{a_+}$ beyond the location and type of the singularities; the rational factor of C1 only lowers the leading exponents by integers and adds integer-spaced corrections. The midpoint exponent (iii) is new for excited inputs: it shifts by $2N_{\rm tot}$, not $N_{\rm tot}$, because both factors' singularities lower it. Finite-$N$ caveat (found while choosing test points): when $|a_+-a_-|$ is small the $y=\pm1$ terms of (2.2) have nearby exponents and their cross term in (i) can cancel the leading term at accessible $N$ (a first choice $(a_-,a_+)=(0.3,0.5)$ gave $c_{N,N-m}$ crossing zero near $N\sim1600$, slope fits meaningless); the asymptotics is unaffected, the test points below have $|a_+-a_-|\ge1.1$ or $a_-=a_+$.

\subsection*{Result}
$$
\boxed{\ c_{h,m}(4\beta^2)^h\sim h^{-2\alpha_{\rm eff}-2}\ (\text{fixed }m),\quad T_h(\theta)\sim h^{-\alpha_{\rm eff}-1}\ (\text{arcs}),\quad T_h(\tfrac\pi2)\sim h^{h_0+2N_{\rm tot}-1},\qquad \alpha_{\rm eff}=\min(a_-,a_+)-N_{\rm tot}\ }
$$
for generic polarisations; in general, with $\alpha^{L,R}_\mp=a_\mp-\nu_\mp,\ a_\mp-\bar\nu_\mp$, the forms (i)–(iii), where at fixed $m$ the exponent is the minimum over all four $(\sigma,\tau)$ products with non-zero coefficient $\sum_re^\sigma_{r,0}\tilde e^\tau_{r,0}$, the cross ($\sigma\ne\tau$) terms alternating as $(-1)^N$ (C3.5). ## §3 Thresholds (C3)

\subsection*{Setup}
Uses C2 and 079 §2 ((2.1)–(2.5), the region $Q_V$, $\hat F_m(h)$, the three summation orders). $J:=\int_{Q_V}I\,ds\,d\theta$ for the excited integrand.

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(3.1) What transfers from 079 §2.* 079's convergence analysis uses, of the integrand, only (a) the factorised form (1.6) of the coefficients, (b) the Darboux exponents of $g_N$ at $y=\pm1$, (c) smoothness of $I$ on $\bar Q_V$ (for $J$ finite and for Abel). (a) is (2.1) (a finite sum of factorised terms), (b) is (2.2), (c) holds because the poles of $P_r$ sit at $y=\pm1$ only, outside $\bar Q_V$ (C1). The $\theta$-side ($|\hat F_m|\le8/|m|$, the boundary layer, the endpoint and midpoint phases) is a property of the region, unchanged. So 079 (2.3)–(2.5) hold with the exponents of C2.

*(3.2) Generic polarisations ($\nu_\mp=\bar\nu_\mp=N_{\rm tot}$).* Substituting $\alpha\to\alpha_{\rm eff}=\alpha-N_{\rm tot}$ at the endpoints and $a_-+a_+\to a_-+a_+-2N_{\rm tot}$, i.e. $h_0\to h_0+2N_{\rm tot}$, at the midpoint:
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{every order (absolute), }\int_{Q_V}\leftrightarrow\sum_{h,m}: & \alpha_{\rm eff}>-1\\
\text{level order, absolute:} & \alpha_{\rm eff}>-2\ \text{and}\ h_0+2N_{\rm tot}<2\\
\text{level order, conditional:} & \alpha_{\rm eff}>-3\ \text{and}\ h_0+2N_{\rm tot}<3\\
\text{no order; }J=\text{Abel sum of the level series:} & \text{otherwise}
\end{array}
\tag{3.1}
$$
(for $\alpha_{\rm eff}>-1$ the midpoint condition is automatic, $h_0+2N_{\rm tot}=-(a_--N_{\rm tot})-(a_+-N_{\rm tot})<2$). Fixed-$m$ sector sums diverge like $H^{-2\alpha_{\rm eff}-2}$ for $\alpha_{\rm eff}<-1$; level-order error $O(H^{\max(-\alpha_{\rm eff}-3,\ h_0+2N_{\rm tot}-3)})$. Abel: $\sum_nL_nr^n=r^{-h_0}\int_{Q_V}I(ry)$ for $r<1$ (the series (2.1) converges absolutely on $|y|\le r$) and $I(ry)\to I(y)$ boundedly on $\bar Q_V$, so 079 C2.4 transfers verbatim.

*(3.3) Special polarisations (rewritten in the fix pass; the original unbalanced criterion is withdrawn, C3.3).* The original text kept, of the fixed-$m$ products $G^{(r)}_N\tilde G^{(r)}_{N-m}$, only the same-point Darboux products ($y=1$ with $\bar y=1$, $y=-1$ with $\bar y=-1$), "of fixed sign", and concluded "sector order iff $\min_\mp(\alpha^L_\mp+\alpha^R_\mp)>-2$". It dropped the cross products ($y=1$ of $\Phi_r$ with $\bar y=-1$ of $\tilde\Phi_r$ and the converse), which by (2.2) are
$$
e_{r,0}\tilde e^+_{r,0}\,(-1)^{N}\,N^{-(\alpha^L_-+\alpha^R_+)-2}\big(1+O(N^{-1})\big),\qquad e^+_{r,0}\tilde e_{r,0}\,(-1)^{N}\,N^{-(\alpha^L_++\alpha^R_-)-2}\big(1+O(N^{-1})\big)
\tag{3.3}
$$
at fixed $m$, alternating in $N$. In the balanced case ($\alpha^L_\mp=\alpha^R_\mp$) they are harmless, $\alpha_-+\alpha_+\ge2\min_\mp\alpha_\mp$; unbalanced, they can dominate.

*Counterexample (C3.5).* Level-(1,1) inputs at $a$ and $b$ (copy 1), rank-one polarisations $\epsilon_a=u_a\otimes v_a$, $\epsilon_b=u_b\otimes v_b$ with $u_a\perp k_a,k_d$, $v_a\perp k_a,k_c$, $u_b\perp k_b,k_c$, $v_b\perp k_b,k_d$. By (1.2): at $y=1$ the pairs $(a,c),(b,d)$ collide, $u_a\cdot k_c\ne0$, $u_b\cdot k_d\ne0$, so $\nu_-=2$; at $y=-1$ ($(a,d),(b,c)$) $u_a\cdot k_d=u_b\cdot k_c=0$ and the $u_a\cdot u_b$ contraction is regular ($w_a-w_b=4\beta$), so $\nu_+=0$; likewise $\bar\nu_-=0$, $\bar\nu_+=2$. With $a_-=a_+=\tfrac14$ ($h_0=-\tfrac12$): $\alpha^L_-=\alpha^R_+=-\tfrac74$, $\alpha^L_+=\alpha^R_-=\tfrac14$ (one $r$: the factor is a single product). The withdrawn criterion gives $\min_\mp(\alpha^L_\mp+\alpha^R_\mp)=-\tfrac32>-2$ and $\min(\alpha^L,\alpha^R)=-\tfrac74>-2$: "every order". But by (3.3) the fixed-$m$ coefficients are $\sim(-1)^NN^{3/2}$, and with the radial factor $\hat F_m(h)/h\sim h^{-1}$ ($\hat F_m(h)\to$ its arc value, non-zero for the tested $m$) the sector terms are $\sim(-1)^Nh^{1/2}$: they do not tend to zero, so every fixed-$m$ sector sum diverges. The withdrawn text's own midpoint clause gave $T_n(\tfrac\pi2)\sim n^{-(\alpha^L_-+\alpha^R_+)-1}=n^{5/2}$, level-order divergence, at the same point – it was self-contradictory. 079 (4.3)'s $\min_\mp(a_\mp-\tfrac12(\nu_\mp+\bar\nu_\mp))>-1$ fails on the same example ($-\tfrac34>-1$ predicted convergence).

*What survives (C3.4).* For any matter-oscillator inputs every exponent obeys $\alpha^{L,R}_\mp=a_\mp-\nu_\mp,\ a_\mp-\bar\nu_\mp\ge a_\mp-N_{\rm tot}$ (C1), for every $r$. By (2.2) each $G^{(r)}_N,\tilde G^{(r)}_N$ is then a finite sum of Darboux terms $N^{-\alpha-1}$ with $\alpha\ge\alpha_{\rm eff}$ at the endpoints and, in every product, $\alpha+\alpha'\ge a_-+a_+-2N_{\rm tot}$ at the midpoint. The every-order threshold uses the coefficient bounds only, and these are dominated by the generic ones; the level-order argument of 079 §2 applies term by term to each Darboux term with an exponent at least the generic one; each threshold of (3.1) is monotone in the exponents. Hence the generic thresholds (3.1) are sufficient for all polarisations: special polarisations only enlarge the convergence regions. The exact thresholds for unbalanced polarisations involve all four products $(\sigma,\tau)\in\{\mp\}^2$ (e.g. at the diagonal $\min\alpha^L+\min\alpha^R$; sector order also the cross exponents) and are not derived here; the objective does not need them (generic polarisations give the binding case).

\subsection*{Commentary}
(3.1) is the reason the extension is cheap: every step of 079 §2 is either a property of the region or of the singularity exponents at $y=\pm1$. (3.2) contains one statement 079 (4.3) did not make: the arc-midpoint threshold moves too, by $2N_{\rm tot}$ in $h_0$ (both factors' singularities coalesce there). \subsection*{Result}
$$
\boxed{\ \text{every order}\iff\min(a_-,a_+)>N_{\rm tot}-1;\qquad \text{level order}\iff\min(a_-,a_+)>N_{\rm tot}-3\ \text{and}\ h_0+2N_{\rm tot}<3\ (\text{abs.: }-2,\ 2);\qquad J=\text{Abel sum always}\ }

for generic polarisations ($N_{\rm tot}$ = total holomorphic level of the four inputs); for special polarisations these thresholds remain sufficient, the regions only larger (C3.4; the exact unbalanced thresholds are not derived). Level zero is 079 (2.3)–(2.4) / R83 exactly.

## §4 R83 on shell (C4)

\subsection*{Setup}
Uses C3 and 079 §4. On shell: $\alpha'k_j^2=4(1-N_j)$ (closed-string mass shell, $c\bar cV_j$ with $L_0^\pm=0$), $s=-(k_c+k_d)^2$, $t=-(k_a+k_c)^2$, $u=-(k_a+k_d)^2$.

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(4.1) On-shell exponents.* $\alpha'k_a\cdot k_c=-\tfrac{\alpha'}2(t+k_a^2+k_c^2)=-\tfrac{\alpha't}2-2(2-N_a-N_c)$ and likewise for $(b,d)$ ($(k_b+k_d)^2=(k_a+k_c)^2$), so
$$
a_--N_{\rm tot}=-3-\tfrac{\alpha'}2t,\qquad a_+-N_{\rm tot}=-3-\tfrac{\alpha'}2u,\qquad \alpha'(s+t+u)=-16+4N_{\rm tot},\qquad h_0=-\tfrac{\alpha'}2s-2 .
\tag{4.1}
$$
The level enters $a_\mp$ through the masses and cancels exactly against the OPE shift of C1: on shell the effective cross-channel exponent is the same function of $t$ ($u$) as for tachyons. This is (1.2)'s exponent $h_{k_a+k_c}-h_a-h_c$ with $h_a=h_c=1$ (matter weights of on-shell inputs): it depends only on the exchanged momentum, and the lowest exchange in the $t$-channel is the tachyon whenever both colliding pairs couple to it (otherwise see (4.5), where the statement is made per chirality).

*(4.2) R83's on-shell regimes for excited inputs.* Substituting (4.1) into (3.1):
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{every order:} & \alpha't<-4\ \text{and}\ \alpha'u<-4\quad(\text{unchanged; then }\alpha's>-8+4N_{\rm tot})\\
\text{level order, absolute:} & \alpha't,\alpha'u<-2\ \text{and}\ \alpha'(t+u)<-8\ \ (\Leftrightarrow\alpha's>-8+4N_{\rm tot})\\
\text{level order, conditional:} & \alpha't,\alpha'u<0\ \text{and}\ \alpha'(t+u)<-6\ \ (\Leftrightarrow\alpha's>-10+4N_{\rm tot})\\
\text{else:} & \text{Abel sum only.}
\end{array}
\tag{4.2}
$$
Written in $t,u$ every line is level-independent; R83's $s$-form of the midpoint condition, "$\alpha's>-10$" ("$>-8$"), is the tachyon case of $\alpha's>-10+4N_{\rm tot}$ ($-8+4N_{\rm tot}$), because the on-shell $s+t+u$ depends on the levels. For special polarisations these conditions remain sufficient (C3.4). They are necessary for generic polarisations: the every-order lines when the colliding pairs of each channel couple to the channel tachyon (the endpoint same-point coefficients are then non-zero, and the midpoint cross products are dominated); the level-order lines when moreover the arc-midpoint coefficient $\sum_re^\mp_{r,0}\tilde e^\pm_{r,0}$ is non-zero. That coefficient pairs the holomorphic $t$-channel leading term with the antiholomorphic $u$-channel one and is not a channel coupling: for a single level-(1,1) input it is $\propto\epsilon(k_c,k_d)$, which vanishes on a codimension-one locus where $\epsilon(k_c,k_c),\epsilon(k_d,k_d)\ne0$; there the necessity of $\alpha'(t+u)<-6$ is not established (critic iteration 2, #4c).

*(4.3) Off shell.* At fixed momenta (fixed $a_\mp,h_0$) excitations lower the endpoint exponent by $N_{\rm tot}$ and raise the midpoint one by $2N_{\rm tot}$: the regime is level-dependent, and on all matter-oscillator inputs at fixed momenta neither every-order nor level-order convergence holds (high enough $N_{\rm tot}$ violates both); the Abel statement holds for every matter-oscillator input (A3). Ghost-oscillator states of $\mathcal H_0$ at ghost number 2 (e.g. $b_{-1}c_0^+c_1\bar c_1$-type) are outside A3: $b(z)c(w)\sim(z-w)^{-1}$ can raise the pole order, so the bound of C1 is not established for them; the exponent rule $h_p-h_a-h_c$ of the (1.2) commentary with total $L_0$ weights is expected to cover them as long as the lightest exchanged state with non-zero couplings is still $c\bar c\,e^{i(k_a+k_c)X}$ (general rule of (4.5), per chirality; not derived). This confirms 079 (4.3)'s conclusion ("every-order exchange only on level-bounded subspaces; on all of $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0$ level order or Abel") and sharpens it: level order also fails on unbounded levels, through the midpoint.

*(4.4) Status of 079 (4.3).* (i) The shift: verified; $\nu_\mp\le N_a+N_b+N_c+N_d$ with equality generically, i.e. "half the total level of the colliding pairs" counts, at each of $y=\pm1$, all four inputs (both pairs collide). (ii) ~~The threshold $\min_\mp(a_\mp-\tfrac12(\nu_\mp+\bar\nu_\mp))>-1$ is the sector-order condition; the every-order condition adds $\min(\alpha^L,\alpha^R)>-2$.~~ [withdrawn in the fix pass: 079's threshold, like (3.3)'s, drops the cross Darboux products and fails on the counterexample C3.5. What holds: for generic (balanced) polarisations it reduces to $\alpha_{\rm eff}>-1$, which is (3.1); for special ones (3.1) is sufficient (C3.4).] (iii) Missing from 079: the midpoint shift $h_0\to h_0+2N_{\rm tot}$ in the level-order threshold. (iv) Missing: the on-shell cancellation (4.1), which makes R83's on-shell every-order condition sufficient for all on-shell matter-oscillator inputs, and necessary for generic polarisations (every order: when the colliding pairs couple to the channel tachyon; level order: moreover a non-zero midpoint coefficient; (4.2), (4.5)).

*(4.5) Inputs whose colliding pairs do not couple to the channel tachyon (fix pass, critic #4, #9).* The "only if" halves of (4.2) rest on C1.2's leading coefficient being non-zero, i.e. on both colliding pairs coupling to the channel tachyon. That fails on whole irreps: worldsheet parity forbids $B$–$T$–$T$, equivalently the tachyon coupling $\epsilon(k,k)$ of a level-(1,1) state vanishes on the antisymmetric part.
