## Task and objective

**Literal task.** Decide whether the records give a complete off-shell argument that "the inverse of the quartic form $p_{2,2}^{-1}$ is a dressed gluing of two copies of a cubic vertex along a circle", and if not, derive the strongest off-shell statement with its assumptions.

**Restatement (flagged).** The object that the records show to be a dressed gluing is $p_{2,2}$ itself, restricted to its vertex region: two copies of the cubic vertex $\mathcal V_3$ (the halves $\mathcal V_3'$ at the theory's parameters) glued along a circle over $Q_V$, with $q$-dependent input operators and dressed antighosts (R93). Its inverse $p_{2,2}^{-1}$ – the $\Sigma_2$ line – is not a gluing in any sense the records establish: in the fixed-halves model it is the CKV pair times the reciprocal of the region's transform times a projector, explicitly *not* a gluing over an inverse region (R80 C2.3), and for the actual form no closed form is known (R80 annotation 2026-10-05). The brief's phrase conflates the two; The motivating expectation that the gluing should be dressed concerns $p_{2,2}$. The record therefore treats two questions: (A) is "$p_{2,2}=$ dressed gluing" a theorem off shell, with which inputs; (B) what is established off shell about $p_{2,2}^{-1}$ from the gluing picture.

**Motivating objective.** The worldsheet section is being rewritten to present the off-shell dressed gluing first and the on-shell discussion (Sonoda–Zwiebach) as a separate subsection. The deliverable says what that section may state as a theorem, as a conditional result, and as a heuristic.

## §2 Decomposition of the off-shell question (part 2)

Two questions (restatement above). For (A), "$p_{2,2}=$ dressed gluing off shell", the records already hold the route (L7); what remains is the measure identity L5, the one link still "Argued", and its place in the off-shell statement. For (B), "$p_{2,2}^{-1}$ off shell", the candidate routes of the brief, with the obstruction each meets, ordered by cost:

1. **Direct: the Schiffer decomposition** (for A; and for L5 as its $h_i=0$ corollary). Cost: low – a Leibniz rule and the linearity of the Schiffer insertion (A4). Obstruction: none foreseen; the only inputs are the standard transformation law and the definition of $\Omega$. Failure would look like: the decomposition of the tangent vector into neck and disc parts not representing the section's tangent vector (it does, since the family of surface states is the composite of the two one-parameter families). Taken first.
2. **Analytic continuation from the Feynman disc** (for L5 alone, on shell). Cost: low. The rigid gluing's integrand and $Z^*\Omega$ are real-analytic 2-forms on $\{\lvert q\rvert<4\beta^2\}$ agreeing on $\lvert q\rvert\le1$. Obstruction: none; it gives L5 but not the off-shell form. Taken second, as the independent route for C2.
3. **Direct computation of a dressed gluing against the Schwinger-parameter form of the inverse** ($p^{-1}=\int_0^\infty\mathrm dt\,\mathrm e^{-tp}$, paper `eq:ws:propagator-sigma`) (for B). Cost: high. Obstruction, known before starting: the exponent $\mathrm e^{-tp}$ expands in chains of $k$ dressed gluings sewn along two necks each, with weight $(-t)^k/k!$, and the $t$-integral does not commute with the expansion (each term diverges); so no term-by-term surface representation of $p^{-1}$ arises (the paper's own paragraph). The route can at most reproduce the Neumann series (route 5) by resummation. Not taken.
4. **Algebraic argument from the sewing axioms** (for B). Cost: medium. The axioms (factorisation at a node, the standard insertions $b_0^-\delta_{L_0^-,0}$, $b_0^+b_0^-/L_0^+$) are what R28 uses, and they give an *exclusion*: no connected bounded-type kernel with standard insertions has $N_{\rm gh}=-2$. Within the gluing picture the same counting reads: a gluing carries $N_{\rm gh}=+2$ (two antighosts, two moduli), an inverse $-2$; the $b$-dressed gluings do not compose (R80 C2.3: $\mathcal G_R\mathcal G_{R'}=0$ in the model by $(b_0^\pm)^2=0$; for the dressed gluing the same count). Obstruction: the axioms exclude, they do not construct. What they give positively is the factorisation identity $\hat G\hat\Pi\hat G=\hat G$ (L11), a tautology for any factorisation. Taken as far as it goes (§4): the strongest positive off-shell statement about the inverse from the gluing picture is this identity plus the count.
5. **Neumann series** (for B). Cost: low to state, open to control. $p^{-1}=\sum_k(D^{-1}R)^kD^{-1}$: each term a chain of $k$ dressed gluings (the off-diagonal parts) with $k+1$ algebraic factors. Obstruction: convergence is PH21 (one block supported); the terms are surfaces only up to the $D^{-1}$ factors (R28's loophole, R27 (3)(ii)). Cited, not re-derived.
6. **Reduction to the on-shell case plus a dressing lemma** (for B). Cost: low for the lemma on primaries (L8: $\lvert\kappa(q)\rvert^{2\sum h_i}$), high beyond. Obstruction: the inverse needs the form on all of $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0$, not on primaries; on non-primary inputs the dressing has $b_1^{(i)}$, $\bar b_1^{(i)}$ and $L_1$ terms that mix levels, so the on-shell block inverse (R80, Conditional(PH31)) does not lift (R80 annotation 2026-10-05, L9). The lemma gives the weight on primaries only; it does not reduce the inverse. Not taken beyond L8.

Order taken: 1, 2 (both close A and L5), then 4 and 5 for B as statements of what is established, with 3 and 6 recorded as the two further angles whose obstructions are known – three distinct angles for the negative part of (B) (4: counting exclusion; 3: the Schwinger expansion does not commute with the $t$-integral; 6: the block inverse does not lift), so the negative conclusion "no gluing representation of $p_{2,2}^{-1}$ is established, and the model's inverse is not a gluing" is a documented exclusion with its scope (§4), not an impossibility claim.

## §3 Derivation: the theorem and the measure identity (part 3)

\subsection*{Setup}
A1–A5. $\langle\Sigma(q);F|$ denotes the surface state of the glued four-punctured sphere at $q$ with local coordinates $F=(F_1,\dots,F_4)$ at the external punctures: on finite-level inputs $\langle\Sigma(q);F|A_1\otimes\cdots\otimes A_4\rangle=\langle\prod_iF_i\circ A_i(0)\rangle_{\Sigma(q)}$. $h=(h_1,h_2)$ are the fixed coordinates of the half at its ordinary punctures (the same half for both copies), $\Phi_2^h$ its circle state (071 C1.1), $\mathcal S(q)=q^{L_0}\bar q^{\bar L_0}$. For a germ $g$ fixing $0$, $U_g$ is the operator of A4, and $T^{(i)}[v]$, $b^{(i)}[v]$ the stress-tensor and antighost contours of a vector field $v$ in the frame of input $i$. $\Omega$ is the section's form (A3) with its normalisation $N$.

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(3.1) The vertex region in the coordinate $q$.* By L1 the restriction of $Z$ to $Q_V$ is a bijection onto $\mathcal V_{0,4}$ up to a null set, holomorphic, hence orientation preserving; so
$$
\int_{\mathcal V_{0,4}}\Omega=\int_{Q_V}Z^*\Omega=\int_{Q_V}\mathrm ds\,\mathrm d\theta\;\Omega\big(Z_*\partial_s,Z_*\partial_\theta\big). \tag{3.1}
$$
The family of pointed surfaces with coordinates over $\mathcal V_{0,4}$, $Z\mapsto(\Sigma_Z,f_i(\cdot\,;Z))$, pulled back along $Z(q)$, is the family $q\mapsto(\Sigma(q),F_i(\cdot\,;q))$ with the transported germs $F_i=N_q^{-1}\circ f_i(\cdot\,;Z(q))$ (096 (1.1)), because $N_q$ is an isomorphism of the glued sphere onto the section's sphere at $Z(q)$ carrying the four punctures to $(0,1,\infty,Z(q))$: the two pointed surfaces with coordinates are the same point of $\hat{\mathcal P}_{0,4}$ and have the same surface state, $\langle\Sigma_{Z(q)};f|=\langle\Sigma(q);F(q)|$. The tangent vectors $Z_*\partial_s$, $Z_*\partial_\theta$ are the tangent vectors $\partial_s,\partial_\theta$ of the pulled-back family. By A3,
$$
\Omega(Z_*\partial_s,Z_*\partial_\theta)=N\,\big\langle\Sigma(q);F(q)\big|\,B[\partial_s]\,B[\partial_\theta]\,\big|A_1\otimes\cdots\otimes A_4\big\rangle, \tag{3.2}
$$
with $B[\partial_s]$ the antighost insertion of any Schiffer vector representing the tangent vector $\partial_s$ of the family $q\mapsto\langle\Sigma(q);F(q)|$ (A4: the class modulo holomorphic fields is what the insertion sees).

*(3.3) A Schiffer vector of the family, by the Leibniz rule.* Split $F_i(q)=h_i\circ g_i(q)$ with $g_i(q)$ a germ fixing $0$ (096 (1.2), exact for every admissible $h_i$). By A4,
$$

## §4 The inverse off shell (part 3, continued)

\subsection*{Setup}
A1, A5; C1. $p:=p_{2,2}$ on $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0/\ker p$ at fixed total momentum, invertible there by definition of the quotient (PH5 for the kernel's absence at generic momentum is not needed: the inverse is taken on the quotient, as the paper does). ## §5 Sonoda–Zwiebach connection (part 4)

\subsection*{Setup}

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(5.1) The on-shell sewing over a non-disc domain.* By (1.1) and the Feynman disc, on four on-shell primaries at finite level,

with $\Phi_2$ the circle state of the theory's $\mathcal V_3$ – at $\lambda=\tfrac12$, $\gamma'=\beta$ it is SZ's symmetric Hermitian vertex with $a=2\beta$ (paper, `sec:ws:seams`). The right-hand side is SZ's sewing of that vertex over the domain $D_s=\{\lvert q\rvert<\mathrm e^{\sigma_V(\theta)}\}$ in the $s$-channel, with the $t$- and $u$-channels keeping their unit discs. *(5.2) The square point.* $Z(q)=-1$ with $\lvert q\rvert<4\beta^2$: $(q+4\beta^2)^2=-16\beta^2q$, i.e. $q^2+24\beta^2q+16\beta^4=0$, $q=-4\beta^2(3\mp2\sqrt2)$, the sheet's root being

*(5.3) Off shell.* The rigid sewing (5.1) continued off shell is the fixed-halves model, and R91 shows it is not seam-consistent: at the outer seam the mapping radii of the two descriptions differ by $\ln\frac{4\beta^2-1}{4\beta}$ at $\theta=\pi$ for every $\beta>\beta_\circ$ (exact), vanishing only at the pinch.
