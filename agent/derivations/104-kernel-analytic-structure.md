## Task and objective

**Literal task.** Write the claim tree C1–C6 of the brief: the fixed-level operator as an integral operator in the relative momentum with its space (C1, with the range clause for $p_{3,2}$); entire dependence on $P^2$ and on the relative momenta and the Hilbert–Schmidt property at every level (C2); the Schur reduction over the level-0 block of R53 and the definiteness question for the complement (C3); a formulation in which D1 – the singular set $Z_L=\{P^2:\ker p_{2,2}^{(L)}(P)\ne0\}$ is discrete – is a theorem, or a precise gap (C4); what carries over to $k\ge3$ and the upward propagation of a kernel (C5); the structural statement on $L\to\infty$ (C6, Statement D0).

**Motivating objective.** The Track D question (`knowledge/decisions.md`, 2026-10-17): are the kernels of the auxiliary fields' quadratic forms always empty, or can the paper's kernel conditions always be met, with the tools of the UV-finiteness proofs? The paper assumes "no kernel at generic momentum" (`09-discussion.tex` l. 57). This record is angle A (analyticity): it owns the object definition the other angles reuse, and it must turn D1 into a precise theorem or a precise gap, and say what D1 does and does not give for D2, D3, D0.

**The analogy, restated in the record's terms.** The UV-finiteness proofs (de Lacroix et al. 2017 §8) use three facts about a vertex: (a) its momentum dependence is $\exp[-\sum g_{ij}k_i\cdot k_j]$ times a polynomial, with $g_{ij}$ positive definite once stubs are added (their (8.1)–(8.2)); (b) that dependence is entire in the momenta; (c) sums over intermediate states converge because the stub factor $\lambda^{-h}$ beats the growth of the number of states. For the kernel question these become: (a) and (b) give that the fixed-level kinetic operator is an *entire family of Hilbert–Schmidt operators* in the complex pair momentum (C2); (c) is what the limit $L\to\infty$ would use (C6, angle B). What the analogy does not supply is the step from "entire compact family" to "kernel only on a discrete set": that step is a Fredholm statement, and a compact operator has no identity part to make it (C4).

## §1 The object (C1)

\subsection*{Setup}

A1–A5. $\hat V_4^{\alpha'\alpha}(k_1',k_2';k_1,k_2)$ the stripped vertex on basis states (072 §2 Setup), $\alpha,\alpha'\in$ a basis of $W_L$. $\tau$ the strand swap on $W_L$ ($\tau(a\otimes b)=b\otimes a$, $\tau^2=1$). $\mathcal R$ the action on $W_L$ of the reflection $R\in O(26)$ with $Rn=-n$ (a tensor representation of $O(26)$ on the oscillator indices, trivial on the ghosts; $\mathcal R^2=1$).

\subsection*{Derivation}

*(1.1) Fibre space.* By R78 (072 (1.1)–(1.7), (2.2)) a level-$\le L$ two-string state of total momentum $P$ is a function $\sigma:\mathbb R^{26}\to W_L$ of the relative momentum with the strand symmetry $\tau\sigma(-q)=\sigma(q)$ (072 (1.7) at $n=2$, all Koszul signs $+1$). The fixed-level fibre space is
$$\mathcal H^{(L)}:=\bigl\{\sigma\in L^2(\mathbb R^{26},d^{26}q)\otimes W_L:\ \mathcal S\sigma=\sigma\bigr\},\qquad(\mathcal S\sigma)(q):=\tau\,\sigma(-q),\tag{1.1}$$
the $+1$ eigenspace of the involution $\mathcal S$ (an orthogonal projection $\tfrac12(1+\mathcal S)$ in the $L^2\otimes$Fock structure of A4). At $L=0$, $W_0=\mathbb CTT$, $\tau=1$ and (1.1) is $L^2_{\rm even}(\mathbb R^{26})$ of R51. The $L^2$ structure in $q$ is the Euclidean one of PH25 (A2); the inner product on $W_L$ is auxiliary (A4).

*(1.2) The operator.* By 072 (2.2) at $n=2$, the quadratic form restricted to level $\le L$ and total momentum $P$ is
$$V_4(\Sigma,\Sigma')\big|_{P}=\int\frac{d^{26}q'\,d^{26}q}{(2\pi)^{52}}\;\sigma(-P,q')^{\alpha'}\,K^{(L)}_P(q',q)_{\alpha'\alpha}\,\sigma'(P,q)^{\alpha},\qquad K^{(L)}_P(q',q)_{\alpha'\alpha}=\hat V_4^{\alpha'\alpha}\bigl(-\tfrac P2+q',-\tfrac P2-q';\ \tfrac P2+q,\tfrac P2-q\bigr),\tag{1.2}$$
and $p_{2,2}^{(L)}(P)$ is the integral operator $(K^{(L)}_P\sigma)(q')=\int\frac{d^{26}q}{(2\pi)^{26}}K^{(L)}_P(q',q)\sigma(q)$ on $\mathcal H^{(L)}$ (the kernel commutes with $\mathcal S$ by the $S_4$ symmetry of $V_4$, 072 Commentary (2.2), so it preserves (1.1)). It depends on $P$ through $P^2=p^2$, $P\cdot q=p\,n\cdot q$, $P\cdot q'=p\,n\cdot q'$ and the explicit vectors $P^\mu$ contracted with oscillator indices (072 (2.2); the $P\cdot q$ dependence drops at level 0 under PH29, R48). At level 0 it is $K_P(q',q)=C\,F(P^2,(q+q')^2,(q-q')^2)$ (049 (1.1)).

*(1.3) Kernel of the form = null functions.* $N_2\in\ker p_{2,2}$ means $V_4(N_2,A)=0$ for all $A$ (`eq:hs4:kernel`). Fibrewise (072 (1.9)): $N_2\in\ker p_{2,2}$ iff $\sigma_N(P,\cdot)\in\ker K^{(L)}_P$ for a.e. $P$, where
$$\ker K^{(L)}_P:=\{\sigma\in\mathcal H^{(L)}:\ K^{(L)}_P\sigma=0\ \text{a.e.}\},\tag{1.3}$$
using that the pairing $\int\tau(q')\,(K\sigma)(q')$ against all $\tau\in\mathcal H^{(L)}$ at $-P$ vanishes iff $K\sigma=0$ a.e. (the $L^2$ pairing is non-degenerate; the paper's "the $2$-string pairing is non-degenerate" at `02-2-quartic.tex` l. 92). This definition uses no inner product on $W_L$: a null function is a null function in any basis of $W_L$. **The kernel question is a property of the bilinear form; the $L^2\otimes$Fock structure is a tool for compactness, not part of the definition.**

*(1.4) Transpose, reflection, and the two self-adjoint structures.* Three identities, all exact:
$$\text{(i)}\ \ K^{(L)}_P(q',q)^{\!\top}=K^{(L)}_{-P}(q,q')\ \text{(symmetry of }V_4\text{; 072 (2.2))};\qquad\text{(ii)}\ \ K^{(L)}_{-P}(q',q)=\mathcal R\,K^{(L)}_P(Rq',Rq)\,\mathcal R\ \text{(}O(26)\text{ covariance, }Rn=-n\text{)};\qquad\text{(iii)}\ \ \overline{K^{(L)}_{p}}=K^{(L)}_{\bar p}\ \text{(real coefficients, (2.2) below)}.\tag{1.4}$$
Let $U:=\mathcal R\circ(q\mapsto Rq)$ on $\mathcal H^{(L)}$, $U^2=1$, $U$ orthogonal. Then (i)–(ii) give $K_p^{\!\top}=UK_pU$ as operators, so
$$\text{real }p:\quad \tilde K_p:=K_pU\ \text{ is real symmetric (self-adjoint, compact)};\qquad\text{imaginary }p:\quad K_p^*=\overline{K_p}^{\!\top}=K_{-p}^{\!\top}=K_p\ \text{ (self-adjoint)}.\tag{1.5}$$
In both cases $\dim\ker K_p$ is the dimension of the kernel of a compact self-adjoint operator ($U$ is invertible), and $K_{-p}=UK_pU$ shows $\ker K_{-p}=U\ker K_p$: the singular set in the $p$-plane is even, hence a set in $P^2=p^2$ (A3). Consequence: **at every real $P^2$, of either sign, the kernel question for the indefinite form at level $L$ is the kernel question of a compact self-adjoint operator** – PH1's "no auxiliary inner product" clause is not touched, since (1.3) is basis-free, but the counting of null vectors can use the spectral theorem.

*(1.5) "Invertible on the quotient" for a compact operator.* R51 (049 (c2)) at level 0, verbatim at level $L$: on $\mathcal H^{(L)}/\ker K_p$ the operator is injective by construction; its range is dense in $\overline{\mathrm{ran}\,K_p}=(\ker K_p^*)^\perp$ ($=(U\ker K_p)^\perp$ at real $p$, $=(\ker K_p)^\perp$ at imaginary $p$); the inverse exists on $\mathrm{ran}\,K_p$ and is unbounded (eigenvalues accumulate at $0$, C2). Three readings as in 049 (c2): (α) injective on the quotient – automatic; (β) $\ker K_p=0$ – the content of D2 at $(L,P)$; (γ) bounded inverse – false for every compact operator and disowned by PH1. The paper's assumption 1 (`02-3-quintic.tex` l. 480, 487: "the inverse of $p_{2,2}$ exists on the quotient, level by level") is, for a compact operator, the statement that the vectors on which the inverse is applied lie in $\mathrm{ran}\,K_p$, not only in its closure: see (1.6).

*(1.6) The range clause for $p_{3,2}$.* The source of $\Sigma_2$ at order $g_s^3$ is $v_p(A,B):=p_{3,2}(A,B)\big|_P$, the function $q'\mapsto\hat V_5(\text{pair at }(-P,q');A,B)$ (072 (2.3) with one $\Sigma$ strand replaced). The kernel condition `eq:hs5:kernel-condition` says $\langle N,v_p\rangle=0$ for all $N\in\ker K_p$, i.e. $v_p\in(\ker K_p)^\perp$. In finite dimensions $(\ker K)^\perp=\mathrm{ran}\,K^{\!\top}$; for the compact $K_p$, $(\ker K_p)^\perp=\overline{\mathrm{ran}\,K_p^*}$ only. What `eq:hs5:neumann` and the saddle $\Sigma_2^*=K_p^{-1}v_p$ need is $v_p\in\mathrm{ran}\,K_p$, a strictly stronger statement (Picard's criterion: with the singular system $K_p=\sum_j\mu_j\,u_j\langle w_j,\cdot\rangle$, $v\in\mathrm{ran}\,K_p$ iff $\sum_j|\langle u_j,v\rangle|^2/\mu_j^2<\infty$, while $v\in\overline{\mathrm{ran}}$ iff $v\perp\ker K_p^*$). The candidate argument of the brief – the Gaussian decay of $\mathcal V_5$ in $q'$ (workflow C's H-C1) – is **not sufficient**, and the comparison is two-sided. The model in the record's own class (compact, (3.2)) is the Mehler kernel $E_{a,c}(q',q)=e^{-a(q'^2+q^2)+c\,q\cdot q'}$, $|c|<2a$, in one dimension: eigenfunctions the Hermite functions at scale $\omega=\sqrt{4a^2-c^2}$, eigenvalues $\mu_j=\mu_0r^j$ with $c/2a=2r/(1+r^2)$. A Gaussian source $e^{-bq^2}$ is a squeezed vacuum in that basis, with coefficients $|v_{2n}|\asymp n^{-1/4}t^{\,n}$, $t=|\omega-2b|/(\omega+2b)$, so Picard's series $\sum_n|v_{2n}|^2/\mu_{2n}^2$ converges iff $t<r^2$, which unwinds to the **window**
$$e^{-bq^2}\in\mathrm{ran}\,E_{a,c}\iff a-\frac{c^2}{4a}<b<a\tag{1.6}$$
(directly: the Gaussian ansatz $f=e^{-\beta q^2}$ solves $E_{a,c}f=e^{-bq^2}$ with $b=a-c^2/4(a+\beta)$, and $f\in L^2$ iff $\beta>0$). Both edges are real: a source decaying *faster* than the kernel's diagonal rate ($b>a$) is outside the range, and so is one decaying *too slowly* ($b<a-c^2/4a$); the rank-one limit $c\to0$ gives $b=a$ and the limit $c\to2a$ gives the one-sided $b<a$ of the convolution model, which is not compact and not in the class. For the actual operators the relevant comparison is therefore not "Gaussian rate of $\mathcal V_5$ against Gaussian rate of $\mathcal V_4$" but the decay of the Hermite coefficients of $v_p$ in the eigenbasis of $K_p$ against the geometric decay of the eigenvalues of $K_p$ (R55's rate at level 0; for $\mathcal V_5$ the rates are set by the mapping radii of the two pair punctures and the cross terms with the other three); no five-vertex data exists. **Status: Open**, with the criterion stated. Two mitigations, recorded for Task 7 and the paper: (a) the *value* of the exchange term $\langle v_{-p},K_p^{-1}v_p\rangle$ (the quintic-exchange vertex $\mathcal V_{5\cdot5}$) is finite under the weaker condition $v_p\in\mathrm{ran}\,|K_p|^{1/2}$ (the form domain; at level 0 with $K_p>0$: $\sum_j|\langle u_j,v\rangle|^2/\mu_j<\infty$; at level $L$ and real $p$ the self-adjoint object is $\tilde K_p=K_pU$, the condition is $v_p\in\mathrm{ran}\,|\tilde K_p|^{1/2}$ and the pairing is $\langle v_{-p},U\tilde K_p^{-1}v_p\rangle$), which is what the eliminated action needs, while the saddle $\Sigma_2^*$ in $L^2$ needs the full range; (b) the condition is a comparison of decay rates with two edges, and a stub on the five-vertex longer than the one on the four-vertex pushes $v_p$ towards the fast-decay edge – the opposite of the UV intuition.

\subsection*{Commentary}

- (1.1)–(1.2) transcribe R78 to the fixed-level fibre; nothing is computed. The only choice is A3's frame, which makes "analytic in $P$" a one-variable statement.
- (1.4)(ii) is Lorentz covariance of the vertex under the single reflection $R$; it needs no $O(26)$ invariance of the kernel at fixed $P$ (which fails above level 0) – only that the vertex is a scalar under the simultaneous rotation of all four momenta and oscillator indices. (iii) is the statement that every coefficient of the kernel – exponents $\ln|z_{ij}|$, mapping radii, Taylor coefficients of the local coordinates, ghost correlators – is real on the real vertex region (P2 is the reality of the vertex; (iii) is slightly more, the reality of the integrand, which the construction of §2 shows).
- (1.5): the two structures of (1.5) are the reason the real-$P^2$ kernel question is a self-adjoint one although $G_2$ is indefinite; 049 (c1)'s caution ("kernel and range need not be complementary at level $>0$") is answered: they are complementary up to the fixed unitary $U$.
- (1.6): the paper's sentence "by analogy with operators in finite dimensions" (`02-3-quintic.tex` l. 480) is exactly where the closure is dropped; the record makes the gap precise and shows that H-C1's Gaussian decay does not fill it.

\subsection*{Result}

$p_{2,2}^{(L)}(P)$ is the integral operator (1.2) on the fibre space (1.1); its kernel is the space of null functions (1.3), basis-free; at every real $P^2$ it is, up to the fixed involution $U$, a compact (under C2's hypotheses) self-adjoint operator (1.5); "invertible on the quotient" is (α)–(γ) of (1.5) with (β) the content of D2; the range clause for $p_{3,2}$ is Picard's criterion, Open, not implied by Gaussian decay: in the Mehler model the admissible source rates form the two-sided window (1.6).

\subsection*{Verification}

## §2 Entire Hilbert–Schmidt family (C2)

\subsection*{Setup}

A1–A5. $\mathcal V_{0,4}$ the vertex region with its section $m\mapsto(z_a(m),f_a(m))$ (punctures and local coordinates), real-analytic on the twelve tiles and $C^1$ across the arcs (PH34), compact and at positive distance from the collisions $z_a=z_b$ (P1; 045 C1 for the declared section). $\rho_a(m)=1/|f_a'(0)|$ the mapping radii. Strand momenta $k_a$ as in (1.2); $h_a=\tfrac{\alpha'}4k_a^2+l_a-1$ the weight of a level-$l_a$ state.

\subsection*{Derivation}

*(2.1) Structure of the integrand.* The vertex is the sphere correlator of the four states, each inserted through its local coordinate, integrated over $\mathcal V_{0,4}$ with the antighost insertions of the section (conv. C4; Erbin 2023). For states $c\bar c\,\mathcal O_a e^{ik_a\cdot X}$ with $\mathcal O_a$ a level-$l_a$ polynomial in $\partial^nX,\bar\partial^nX$ and ghost modes, the matter correlator is the free-boson Wick expansion:
$$\Bigl\langle\prod_a f_a\circ\bigl[\mathcal O_ae^{ik_a\cdot X}\bigr]\Bigr\rangle=\prod_{a<b}|z_{ab}|^{\alpha'k_a\cdot k_b}\prod_a|f_a'(0)|^{\,\alpha'k_a^2/2}\;\times\;\mathcal P^{(L)}(m;k_1,\dots,k_4),\tag{2.1}$$
where $\mathcal P^{(L)}$ is a polynomial in the momenta of total degree $\le2\sum_al_a\le8L$ (a level-matched level-$l_a$ state carries up to $2l_a$ oscillators, $N=\bar N=l_a$; each oscillator contracted with an exponential gives one factor $k_b^\mu$; oscillator–oscillator contractions give momentum-independent factors; the weights $h_a\sim k_a^2$ of the non-primary transformation laws stay within the same count), with coefficients that are rational functions of the $z_{ab}$ with poles only at collisions, polynomials in the Taylor coefficients of the $f_a$ (the finite conformal transformation of a non-primary of level $l_a$ involves finitely many derivatives of $f_a$ at $0$ and the weight $h_a$, polynomially in $k_a^2$), and the ghost correlator with the antighost insertions. Every coefficient is real on the real section (the free-boson Green function $\ln|z|^2$ and the section data are real) – this is (1.4)(iii). On $\mathcal V_{0,4}$ (P1, PH34) all coefficients are bounded continuous functions of $m$, and the exponent factors satisfy $0<c_-\le|z_{ab}|,|f_a'(0)|\le c_+<\infty$.

*(2.2) The kernel as an exponential–polynomial integral.* Substituting $k_{1,2}=\tfrac P2\pm q$, $k_{3,4}=-\tfrac P2\pm q'$, $P=p\,n$, the exponent of (2.1) is a quadratic form in $(p,q,q')$ with real coefficients continuous in $m$:
$$K^{(L)}_P(q',q)=\int_{\mathcal V_{0,4}}d^2m\;\exp\bigl[-\mathcal Q_m(p,q,q')\bigr]\;\Pi^{(L)}(m;p,q,q'),\qquad\mathcal Q_m=a_m(q^2+q'^2)-c_m\,q\cdot q'+\ell_m(p)\cdot(q,q')+e_m\,p^2,\tag{2.2}$$
with $\Pi^{(L)}$ a $W_L\otimes W_L$-valued polynomial of degree $\le8L$ in $(p,q,q')$. Under PH29 (equal mapping radii at the four punctures) the linear term $\ell_m(p)\cdot(q,q')$ – the $P\cdot q$ dependence – vanishes (R48), and after the $S_3$ tile assembly (PH30) the six exponents are those of 050's table (4.1): $a_m$ and $c_m$ are the $a_i(Z)$, $c_i(Z)$ there, with $2a_m-|c_m|\ge\alpha'(2\ln\beta-\ln2)$ on the region. Without PH29 the linear term is present, bounded by $|\ell_m(p)|\le c\,|p|$; it does not affect any statement below (a linear term in the exponent is dominated by the Gaussian).

*(2.3) Entire dependence.* For each fixed $m$ the integrand of (2.2) is an entire function of $(p,q,q')\in\mathbb C^{1+26+26}$ (exponential of a polynomial times a polynomial). On a compact set $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^{53}$ it is bounded uniformly in $m$ by $\sup_\Omega|\Pi|\cdot\exp[\sup_{m,\Omega}|\mathcal Q_m|]<\infty$ (coefficients bounded, region compact). By dominated convergence and Morera (or differentiation under the integral), $K^{(L)}_P(q',q)$ is entire in $(p,q,q')$ jointly, of order $2$: $|K^{(L)}|\le C_L\exp[B_L(|p|^2+|q|^2+|q'|^2)]$. Where entireness could fail, and does not: (i) the $q$-integration of the operator moves no boundary of $\mathcal V_{0,4}$ – the region is fixed and the kinematics enter the integrand only; (ii) PH34's kinks of the section across the arcs are a loss of smoothness in $m$, which the $m$-integral absorbs (continuity in $m$ suffices for (2.3)); (iii) the non-primary transformation laws are finite polynomials in the section data; (iv) collisions, where $\ln|z_{ab}|$ would diverge, are at positive distance (P1). A boundary of the region *would* enter if the region depended on the momenta (it does not) or if the kinematics reached a collision (they cannot: the exponent is a finite real number at every point of $\overline{\mathcal V_{0,4}}$).

*(2.4) Hilbert–Schmidt at every level.* For real $(q,q')$ and $p$ in a compact $\Omega\subset\mathbb C$, by (2.2) and the bound $2a_m-|c_m|\ge2a_*>0$ (050 C3.1 under PH29/PH30; under P1 alone, $a_*>0$ is the statement that the stubbed Gaussian wins – the review's (8.2), $g_{ij}$ positive definite, l. 185–187),
$$\bigl\|K^{(L)}_P(q',q)\bigr\|_{W_L\otimes W_L}\le C_L(\Omega)\,(1+|q|+|q'|)^{8L}\,e^{c_\Omega(|q|+|q'|)}\,e^{-a_*(q^2+q'^2)},\tag{2.3}$$
the factor $e^{c_\Omega(|q|+|q'|)}$ coming from $\operatorname{Re}\ell_m(p)$ – the only $p$–$q$ coupling in the exponent, $p$ not entering the quadratic part in $(q,q')$ – and absent identically under PH29, for every complex $p$ (049 (1.5) for the complex-$s$ growth of the prefactor). The right-hand side is square integrable on $\mathbb R^{52}$ for every polynomial degree and every $c_\Omega$: **a Gaussian of any positive rate beats any polynomial**, so the Hilbert–Schmidt property at level $L$ holds under the level-0 condition, $a_*>0$, i.e. $\beta>\sqrt2$ for the declared section (049 C1.2), unchanged by the level. At $L=0$ the condition is also necessary (R51: positive integrand, no cancellation); at $L\ge1$ necessity is not argued here – the integrand $e^{-\mathcal Q_m}\Pi^{(L)}$ changes sign in $m$ and across oscillator components, and a cancellation making the superposition decay faster than its worst Gaussian is not excluded. Hence $K^{(L)}_P$ is Hilbert–Schmidt (compact) on $L^2(\mathbb R^{26};W_L)$, and on $\mathcal H^{(L)}$, for every $p\in\mathbb C$, with $\|K^{(L)}_p\|_{HS}$ locally bounded; combined with (2.3) and the entire dependence of the matrix elements, $p\mapsto K^{(L)}_p$ is an entire Hilbert–Schmidt-operator-valued function (049 (1.5) extended: the HS norm of the difference quotient is controlled by the same Gaussian bound). At $L=0$ this is R51. Trace class is not claimed (049 C1.2's remark; R55 obtains it at level 0 from positivity, which is not available above level 0, C3).

*(2.5) What the level does.* The level enters (2.2) only through the polynomial $\Pi^{(L)}$ and the finite-dimensional $W_L$; the exponential factor – the Gaussian rate, the cross term $c_m$, the dependence on $p^2$ – is the level-0 one. This is the structural fact the remaining sections use: $K^{(L)}$ is a *finite matrix of polynomial-weighted Gaussian integral operators sharing the level-0 exponent*.

\subsection*{Commentary}

- (2.1): standard free-boson Wick contraction with non-primary insertions; the only property used is that oscillator modes contract with exponentials to give momentum factors and with each other to give constants, which bounds the degree. The ghost sector contributes a momentum-independent factor; the antighost insertions of the section depend on $m$ only.
- (2.3): the statement is about *joint* entireness in $(p,q,q')$; 050 (8.1)(a) stated the level-0 case. The $O(26)$ structure is not needed.
- (2.4): the condition on $\beta$ is the same at every level because the HS norm is an $L^2$ norm of the kernel and polynomial factors are harmless under a Gaussian. Operator-norm or trace-norm statements would be level dependent; none is used.

\subsection*{Result}

$$\boxed{\ p\mapsto K^{(L)}_p\ \text{is an entire Hilbert–Schmidt-operator-valued function on }\mathcal H^{(L)}\text{, for every }L\text{, with kernel entire in }(p,q,q')\text{ and bound (2.3), under P1 (PH34 for the section) and }a_*>0\ (\beta>\sqrt2\text{ under PH29, PH30}).\ }$$

\subsection*{Verification}

`C2.4-HS`: for a Gaussian×polynomial toy kernel the quadrature HS norm converges under cutoff growth for $2a>|c|$ at polynomial degree 4, and diverges for $2a<|c|$ (`BREAK-C2.4-HS`). The entireness argument (2.3) is analytic and has no machine check beyond the toy's explicit form.

## §3 Schur reduction (C3)

\subsection*{Setup}

A1–A5. Split $\mathcal H^{(L)}=\mathcal H_0\oplus\mathcal H_{>0}$ with $\mathcal H_0=L^2_{\rm even}(\mathbb R^{26})\,TT$ (the level-0 block) and $\mathcal H_{>0}$ its orthogonal complement (all strand-level pairs other than $(0,0)$, strand-symmetrised). Blocks $K_{00},K_{0L},K_{L0},K_{LL}$ of $K:=K^{(L)}_p$; $K_{00}=K_P$ of R51/R53. Sign convention: $C=+1$ (the normalisation used in this project), so $K_{00}>0$ at real $P^2$ (R53); the ledger's $C<0$ reverses every sign below.

\subsection*{Derivation}

*(3.1) Exact reduction, with the range condition built into the domain.* Let $\sigma=(\sigma_0,\sigma_1)\in\ker K$:
$$K_{00}\sigma_0+K_{0L}\sigma_1=0,\qquad K_{L0}\sigma_0+K_{LL}\sigma_1=0.\tag{3.1}$$
At real $P^2$, $K_{00}$ is injective (R53, Conditional(PH25, PH29, PH30)), so $K_{00}^{-1}$ exists on $\mathrm{ran}\,K_{00}$ (dense, unbounded inverse). The first equation says $K_{0L}\sigma_1=-K_{00}\sigma_0\in\mathrm{ran}\,K_{00}$ and determines $\sigma_0=-K_{00}^{-1}K_{0L}\sigma_1$. Define
$$\mathcal D_L:=\{\sigma_1\in\mathcal H_{>0}:\ K_{0L}\sigma_1\in\mathrm{ran}\,K_{00}\},\qquad S_L:=K_{LL}-K_{L0}K_{00}^{-1}K_{0L}:\ \mathcal D_L\to\mathcal H_{>0}.\tag{3.2}$$
Then the map $\sigma_1\mapsto(-K_{00}^{-1}K_{0L}\sigma_1,\sigma_1)$ is a linear bijection
$$\ker K^{(L)}_p\;\cong\;\ker\bigl(S_L\big|_{\mathcal D_L}\bigr),\tag{3.3}$$
with inverse $(\sigma_0,\sigma_1)\mapsto\sigma_1$ (injective on $\ker K$ because $\sigma_1=0$ forces $K_{00}\sigma_0=0$, hence $\sigma_0=0$ by R53). (3.3) is exact: no hypothesis on $\mathrm{ran}\,K_{0L}$ is needed, because a kernel vector supplies the range condition itself. At $L=0$, $\mathcal H_{>0}=0$ and (3.3) reads $\ker K_P=0$: R53.

*(3.2) The range condition $\mathcal D_L=\mathcal H_{>0}$.* It is needed only to make $S_L$ an everywhere-defined operator (and, in C4 (ii), to speak of $S_L$ as "compact minus compact"). By (2.2), $K_{0L}(q',q)$ and $K_{00}(q',q)$ are integrals over the *same* region of the *same* Gaussian factor $e^{-\mathcal Q_m}$ times polynomials ($\Pi_{0L}$ of positive degree, $\Pi_{00}$ a constant). The question "is $\int d^2m\,e^{-\mathcal Q_m}\Pi_{0L}\sigma_1$ in the range of $\int d^2m\,e^{-\mathcal Q_m}\Pi_{00}\,\cdot$" is C1.5's Picard question with both sides of the same exponent. Evidence in favour, not a proof: for a *single* Mehler kernel $e^{-a(x^2+y^2)+cxy}$ ($|c|<2a$) the eigenfunctions are scaled Hermite functions with geometrically decaying eigenvalues $\mu_j=\mu_0r^j$, and multiplication by a polynomial maps into $\mathrm{ran}$ the vectors whose Hermite coefficients decay geometrically, $|v_j|\le Cs^j$ with $s<r$ (it shifts the coefficients by a bounded number of steps and multiplies them by $O(j^{d/2})$, harmless against a geometric margin – not on all of $\mathrm{ran}$: $\sum_j|v_j|^2r^{-2j}<\infty$ does not imply $\sum_jj\,|v_j|^2r^{-2j}<\infty$; the Gaussian sources of (1.6) are in the geometric class); the actual $K_{00}$ is a positive superposition over $m$ of such kernels with different $(a_m,c_m)$, and the range of a sum of positive operators is not the sum of the ranges, so the single-kernel argument does not transfer. C2 does **not** give $\mathcal D_L=\mathcal H_{>0}$; it gives that $S_L$ is densely defined ($\mathcal D_L\supseteq$ the finite-level-support vectors whose $K_{0L}$-image is a finite sum of Gaussian×polynomial functions … which is again the Picard question). **Status: Open**, the same gap as C1.5, and *not needed* for (3.3).

*(3.3) Definiteness at real $P^2$ – the positivity route to D2.* The symmetric object at real $P^2$ is $K_p$ itself at $p=0$ and at imaginary $p$ (1.5), and $\tilde K_p=K_pU$ at real $p\ne0$. Where the level-0 block is positive and the form is symmetric – $p=0$ and imaginary $p$, with $K_{00}>0$ by R53 – completing the square gives, for $\sigma_1\in\mathcal D_L$, the variational identity
$$\langle\sigma_1,S_L\sigma_1\rangle=\inf_{\sigma_0\in\mathcal H_0}\bigl\langle(\sigma_0,\sigma_1),K(\sigma_0,\sigma_1)\bigr\rangle\qquad(p=0\ \text{or}\ p\in i\mathbb R,\ K\ \text{itself, the sesquilinear form at imaginary }p),\tag{3.4}$$
so there $S_L\ge0$ iff the full form is $\ge0$ on the vectors with a non-zero higher-level component, and $S_L>0$ would give D2 at level $L$ by (3.3). At real $p\ne0$ (3.4) does **not** hold as an infimum: the level-0 block of $\tilde K_p$ is $\tilde K_{00}=K_{00}U_0$ with $U_0:\sigma(q)\mapsto\sigma(Rq)$, which commutes with $K_{00}$ ($O(26)$ invariance of the level-0 kernel) and has a $-1$ eigenspace (even functions odd under $q_n\to-q_n$), so $\tilde K_{00}$ is indefinite and the infimum over $\sigma_0$ is $-\infty$ for every $\sigma_1$; the completed square gives only the *stationary value* $\langle\sigma_1,\tilde S_L\sigma_1\rangle$ of the full form in $\sigma_0$, with $\tilde S_L=S_LU_1$ the Schur complement of $\tilde K_p$ ($U$ is block diagonal in the levels), and no inequality. The positivity route is therefore stated for the full symmetric form, which needs neither (3.4) nor $\mathcal D_L$: D2 at level $L$ on a set of real $P^2$ would follow from the definiteness of $K_0$, of $K_{p}$ at imaginary $p$, or of $\tilde K_p$ at real $p$, there. The route is closed near $P^2=0$ at $L\ge1$, in two steps. (a) *The level-0 mechanism does not extend.* 050 (4.3) proves $K_{00}>0$ because each point $m$ of the region contributes a positive-semidefinite kernel $w\,\phi\otimes\phi\,e^{c\,q\cdot q'}$ on even functions (sum of squares of moments, 050 (4.2)). At level $L$ the contribution of a point $m$ is $e^{-\mathcal Q_m}\Pi^{(L)}(m;\cdot)$, and the matrix polynomial $\Pi^{(L)}$ is not positive semidefinite pointwise in $m$: its diagonal entries change sign between oscillator components. (b) *The full symmetric form is indefinite near $P^2=0$ at level $\ge1$.* At $P=0$, level $(1,1)$, $q=q'=0$ (all momenta zero), R68 gives $V_4|_{\mathrm{Sym}^2\{T,D\}}$ invertible with inertia (companion value, not reproduced here) on $\{TT,TD,DD\}$ (the frozen $3\times3$ block of R68: entries and signs are companion values, not reproduced here). By Haynsworth's inertia additivity with the positive $TT$ entry, the frozen Schur complement on $\{TD,DD\}$ has inertia (companion value, not reproduced here). For the operator: take $\sigma=c\otimes g$ with $c\in\{TT,TD,DD\}$ the negative direction of the frozen form and $g$ an even $L^1$-normalised Gaussian concentrated at $q=0$; $g$ even and $\mathcal R$ trivial on $T,D$ put $\sigma$ in the $U=+1$ sector, so $\langle\sigma,\tilde K_p\sigma\rangle=\langle\sigma,K_p\sigma\rangle$ for every real $p$. By continuity of the kernel (C2), $\langle\sigma,K_0\sigma\rangle\to c^{\!\top}Ac<0$ as $g$ narrows, so for $g$ narrow enough $\langle\sigma,K_0\sigma\rangle<0$; on the other hand $\langle TT\otimes g,K_0\,TT\otimes g\rangle=\langle g,K_{00}g\rangle>0$ (R53). Hence $K_0$ is indefinite at level $1$, and by continuity of $p\mapsto\langle\sigma,\tilde K_p\sigma\rangle$ and $p\mapsto\langle g,K_{00}(p)g\rangle$ (bounded entire family, C2; $U_0g=g$) so is $\tilde K_p$ on a real neighbourhood of $p=0$, and $K_p$ on an imaginary one: **the full symmetric form is indefinite near $P^2=0$, on both sides, at level $L\ge1$.** An indefinite compact form is not excluded from having a kernel by definiteness: this is what closes the positivity route to D2 at all real $P^2$. Equivalently, at $P=0$ exactly, where (3.4) holds, $S_1$ has a negative direction on $\mathcal D_1$ whenever the test vector $\sigma_1=(c_{TD},c_{DD})\otimes g$ lies in $\mathcal D_1$ (the open range question C3.2; the statement on the full form does not need it). Conditional on PH29, PH30 and the declared section (R68 is at the declared section, with R68's own scope: the numerical values of R68 are not independently verified, $h_2$ is a weight-scheme value and the inertia holds on a range of $h_2$ that is a companion value, not reproduced here), and on the identification of R68's zero-momentum block with $K^{(1)}_{P=0}(0,0)$ restricted to $\{T,D\}$ (072 C5.2). What is not touched: a positivity route to D2 on a sub-range of real $P^2$ away from $P^2=0$ (large spacelike, or the $P^2\to-\infty$ corner (4.6)(b)); nothing here says the form is indefinite there.

*(3.4) What C3 achieves.* The kernel question at level $L$ is, exactly, the kernel question of $S_L$ on its natural domain – a finite-dimensional block of integral operators in $q$ over a positive reference $K_{00}$ with a known, geometrically decaying spectrum (R53, R55, 050 C2). What it is not: a positivity statement. The reduction removes the one block whose definiteness is known and leaves an indefinite problem of the same type (compact, no identity part), which is C4's subject.

\subsection*{Commentary}

- (3.1): the point of writing the domain $\mathcal D_L$ explicitly is that the equivalence (3.3) needs no range hypothesis – the brief's worry ("state the range condition that $K_{00}^{-1}K_{0L}$ needs") is answered by moving the condition into the definition of the object; the condition then matters for *operator* statements about $S_L$ (C4 (ii)), not for the kernel count.
- (3.3)(a) is the precise reason PH1's scope line says "the level-0 positivity mechanism does not carry over": the sum-of-squares needs a pointwise PSD integrand in $m$, and the oscillator polynomials break it.
- (3.3)(b) uses R68 only through the inertia of a $3\times3$ matrix and the continuity of the kernel; the indefiniteness of the full symmetric form is rigorous under R68's data and needs neither (3.4) nor the domain $\mathcal D_1$; the statement on $S_1$ itself holds at $P=0$ only, and only on $\mathcal D_1$. ## §4 D1 (C4)

\subsection*{Setup}

A1–A5, C1–C3. $Z_L:=\{p\in\mathbb C:\ker K^{(L)}_p\ne0\}$ (even under $p\to-p$, C1.3; its image in $P^2=p^2$ is the brief's $Z_L$; $p\mapsto p^2$ is proper and $2:1$, so discreteness in $p$ and in $P^2$ are equivalent). $\mathbb K_0:=\bigl(K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})\,\Pi_{\rm even}+|K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})|\,\Pi_{\rm odd}\bigr)\otimes1_{W_L}$ on $L^2(\mathbb R^{26};W_L)$, with $\Pi_{\rm even/odd}$ the parity projectors in $q$ (they commute with $K_{00}$, $U$ and $\mathcal S$) (the level-0 Gaussian operator acting on every oscillator component; it commutes with $\mathcal S$ since $K_{00}$ is even and $O(26)$-invariant, so it preserves $\mathcal H^{(L)}$). On the even sector it is positive and injective at real $P^2$ (R53); at $L\ge1$ the fibre space (1.1) also has a $q$-odd sector ($f$ odd times $w$ with $\tau w=-w$, e.g. the antisymmetric $(1,0)-(0,1)$ strand pair), on which R53 says nothing (its sum-of-squares mechanism uses evenness, and the sign of $c_m$ is not fixed, (5.2)), hence $|K_{00}|$ there and the injectivity clause of H1. $h_j$ the Hermite functions in the one-dimensional toy.

\subsection*{Derivation}

*(4.1) The barrier, made precise (C4.1).* "Discrete" is used throughout in the strong sense: *closed and discrete in $\Omega_L$* (no accumulation point inside the domain; the exceptional real set is then locally finite). The plan's D1 is read in this sense, which is what (4.7) and the paper's "generic momentum" need; in the weak, purely topological sense (every point isolated) a set accumulating inside the domain would still count as discrete. Two facts. (a) *Analyticity and compactness do not imply discreteness.* Let $(h_j)_{j\ge0}$ be the Hermite functions, $0<r<1$, $p_j\to p_*$ a convergent sequence with $p_0:=p_*$, and
$$K(p):=\sum_{j\ge0}(p-p_j)\,r^j\;h_j\langle h_j,\cdot\rangle=p\,M_r-\sum_{j\ge0}p_jr^jh_j\langle h_j,\cdot\rangle,\tag{4.1}$$
with $M_r=\sum_jr^jh_j\langle h_j,\cdot\rangle$ the Mehler kernel. $K(p)$ is self-adjoint at real $p$, entire and Hilbert–Schmidt-valued ($\sum_j|p-p_j|^2r^{2j}<\infty$ locally uniformly), its kernel function $\sum_j(p-p_j)r^jh_j(x)h_j(y)$ is entire in $(x,y)$ of order $2$ with Gaussian decay on the real axes (the Mehler piece in closed form; the second piece dominated by $r^j$ against the growth $|h_j(x)|\lesssim e^{\sqrt{2j}|\operatorname{Im}x|}$), so the family has every property of C2 – whereas the weights $j^{-2}$ in place of $r^j$ would give an $L^2$ kernel that is *not* entire in $(x,y)$, since $\sum_jj^{-2}e^{2\sqrt{2j}|\operatorname{Im}x|}$ diverges. Yet $\ker K(p_j)\ni h_j$ for every $j\ge0$: $Z=\{p_j\}_{j\ge0}$ accumulates at $p_*\in Z$, so $Z$ is not closed-discrete and, with $p_*$ a non-isolated point of $Z$, not even discrete in the weak sense. The repaired family is exactly of the form (4.3): $K(p)=M_r^{1/2}(p-\hat P)M_r^{1/2}$ with $\hat P=\sum_jp_jh_j\langle h_j,\cdot\rangle$ bounded self-adjoint, $\sigma_{\rm ess}(\hat P)=\{p_*\}$, so $\mathcal T(p)=p-\hat P$ is Fredholm exactly on $\mathbb C\setminus\{p_*\}$: H1 fails precisely at the accumulation point, and is sharp against this failure mode. So P1–P3, through C2, cannot give D1: **D1 is not a consequence of entire dependence plus compactness**, and any proof needs an input beyond C2. (b) *The kernel question sits at the essential spectrum.* For a compact operator, $0$ is the accumulation point of the spectrum; analytic perturbation theory (Kato VII §1, §3; 049 D1 stand-in) controls *isolated* eigenvalues, so an eigenvalue branch $\mu_j(p)$ is analytic only while $\mu_j(p)\ne0$: "a zero of a branch" is not a notion the theory supplies at the point where it matters, and the union over infinitely many branches is not controlled, as (4.1) shows (there every branch is analytic through its zero, and the zeros still accumulate). The analytic Fredholm theorem (Reed–Simon I, Thm VI.14) applies to $1+C(p)$, $C$ compact analytic: the identity part is what makes $\ker(1+C(p))\ne0$ a closed, isolated condition. $K^{(L)}_p$ has no identity part. The task of C4 is therefore to *supply* one.

*(4.2) Formulation (i): the level-diagonal split (C4.2).* PH21's split, in its sign $K=D-N$, with $D=\operatorname{diag}(K_{ll})$ over strand-level pairs $l=(l_1,l_2)$, $N$ the off-diagonal remainder; $K=D(1-D^{-1}N)$. For D1 one needs on a connected neighbourhood $\Omega$ of the real axis: (α) $D(p)$ injective, (β) $D^{-1}N$ defined ($\mathrm{ran}\,K_{ll'}\subseteq\mathrm{ran}\,K_{ll}$), compact and analytic. Then $\ker K=\ker(1-D^{-1}N)$ and the analytic Fredholm theorem gives: either $\ker\ne0$ on all of $\Omega$ or on a discrete subset; PH21 ($\rho(D^{-1}N)<1$) at one point $p_0$ makes $1-D^{-1}N(p_0)$ invertible by the Neumann series and excludes the first horn. Barriers: (α) is the kernel question for each diagonal block – settled at $l=(0,0)$ by R53 and open for $l\ne(0,0)$; the frozen level-$(1,1)$ diagonal block $\{TD,DD\}$ at $P=0$ has diagonal entries whose signs are companion values, not reproduced here (R68 data, (3.3)(b)); it is indefinite and no positivity mechanism is available for it; moreover $D$ injective on $\Omega$ is D2 for the diagonal blocks, which is circular as a route to D1. (β) fails in the Hermite-scale toy: for the Mehler kernel $K_0=\sum_mr^mh_m\langle h_m,\cdot\rangle$ and polynomial weights, $\langle h_j,M_{x^{d_1}}K_0M_{y^{d_2}}h_k\rangle$ is banded ($|j-m|\le d_1$, $|m-k|\le d_2$) with magnitude $j^{d_1/2}k^{d_2/2}r^m$, so
$$\bigl(K_0^{-1}M_{x^{d_1}}K_0M_{y^{d_2}}\bigr)_{jk}=O\bigl(j^{d_1/2}k^{d_2/2}\,r^{m-j}\bigr),\quad|m-j|\le d_1:\qquad\text{entries growing like }j^{(d_1+d_2)/2}\ \text{along the band},\tag{4.2}$$
an *unbounded* operator, not a compact one: **a polynomial-weighted Gaussian operator is relatively unbounded with respect to the unweighted one** – in the toy, $K_0^{-1}aK_0=r\,a$ and $K_0^{-1}a^\dagger K_0=r^{-1}a^\dagger$, so $K_0^{-1}M_xK_0=(ra+r^{-1}a^\dagger)/\sqrt2$, a polynomial in the ladder operators. Since the off-diagonal block $K_{ll'}$ carries polynomial weights of degree up to $2(l_1+l_2+l_1'+l_2')$, $K_{ll}^{-1}K_{ll'}$ is of this type: $1-D^{-1}N$ is not a compact perturbation of the identity. It might still be Fredholm as an unbounded operator, but that statement presupposes (α) and is no simpler than H1 below; formulation (i) adds the inversion of unknown blocks to the problem without removing anything. (This is also why PH21's Neumann chain cannot be expected to converge in operator norm at continuous relative momentum; its support is a frozen $3\times3$ block.)

*(4.3) Formulation (ii): the Schur complement (C4.3).* $S_L=K_{LL}-R$, $R=K_{L0}K_{00}^{-1}K_{0L}$ on $\mathcal D_L$ (3.2). $K_{LL}$ is compact with unknown kernel, $R$ is the composition of a compact operator with $K_{00}^{-1}K_{0L}$, which by (4.2) is unbounded in the Hermite scale (the output weight of $K_{0L}$ has positive degree, $K_{00}$'s has degree $0$); $S_L$ is "compact minus (compact∘unbounded)" with no identity part. Same barrier as (i), on one block, plus the domain question (3.2). C3's reduction is exact for counting kernels (3.3) and does not by itself turn D1 into a Fredholm statement.

*(4.4) Formulation (iii): dividing out the universal Gaussian on both sides (C4.4).* By (2.5) every block of $K^{(L)}_p$ shares the level-0 exponent; the obstruction in (4.2) is that polynomial weights are unbounded relative to a *one-sided* Gaussian reference. Place the reference symmetrically:
$$K^{(L)}_p=\mathbb K_0^{1/2}\,\mathcal T_L(p)\,\mathbb K_0^{1/2},\qquad\mathcal T_L(p):=\mathbb K_0^{-1/2}K^{(L)}_p\,\mathbb K_0^{-1/2}\ \text{ on }\ \mathrm{ran}\,\mathbb K_0^{1/2},\tag{4.3}$$
with $\mathbb K_0:=\bigl(K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})\,\Pi_{\rm even}+|K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})|\,\Pi_{\rm odd}\bigr)\otimes1_{W_L}$ at a **fixed** reference momentum $p_{\rm ref}$ with $p_{\rm ref}^2$ real (one per level; $p_{\rm ref}=0$ is the natural choice), positive and injective on the even sector by R53 and positive on the odd sector, injective there by H1's clause, so that $\mathbb K_0^{\pm1/2}$ are defined once and for all and $\mathcal T_L(p)$ makes sense for every complex $p$ – the version with $\mathbb K_0$ at the same $p$ exists only on the real axes, where $K_{00}(p)>0$, and is not available on the connected open set the dichotomy (4.6) needs. The $00$ block of $\mathcal T_L(p)$ is then $\mathcal T_{00}(p)=K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})^{-1/2}K_{00}(p)K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})^{-1/2}$, equal to $1$ at $p=p_{\rm ref}$ and otherwise controlled as follows. Under PH29, PH30 the level-0 kernel is $K_{00}(p)=\int d^2m\,w_m(p^2)E_m$ with $E_m$ the pointwise positive-semidefinite kernel of 050 (4.3) on even functions and $w_m(s)$ a scalar tile weight, continuous in $(m,s)$, positive for real $s$ and nowhere zero on the compact region. (α) For $s=p^2$ in a compact set $I\subset\mathbb R$, $0<c_I\le w_m(s)/w_m(s_{\rm ref})\le C_I$, hence the form comparison $c_I\,K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})\le K_{00}(p)\le C_I\,K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})$, and by Douglas' lemma $K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})^{-1/2}K_{00}(p)^{1/2}$ is bounded with bounded inverse: $\mathcal T_{00}(p)$ is bounded with bounded inverse at every real $P^2$, with $c_I\le\mathcal T_{00}(p)\le C_I$. (β) For complex $p$ in a compact $\Omega$, $|w_m(p^2)|\le C_\Omega\,w_m(s_{\rm ref})$ and Cauchy–Schwarz on the positive-semidefinite $E_m$ give $|\langle f,K_{00}(p)g\rangle|\le C_\Omega\langle f,K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})f\rangle^{1/2}\langle g,K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})g\rangle^{1/2}$, so $\mathcal T_{00}(p)$ is bounded, with $\|\mathcal T_{00}(p)\|\le C_\Omega$, and holomorphic in $p$ (matrix elements entire, locally uniformly bounded); its inverse is bounded on the real axes by (α), hence on an open neighbourhood of them by the openness of invertibility. At $L=0$ this makes H1 below *exact*: $\mathcal T_0(p)=\mathcal T_{00}(p)$ is a bounded holomorphic family, invertible on a connected neighbourhood $\Omega_0$ of $\mathbb R\cup i\mathbb R$. The kernel count is unchanged by the choice of $p_{\rm ref}$, since $\mathbb K_0^{1/2}$ is injective and fixed. In the Hermite-scale toy, (4.2) gives for the $00$ block at $p=p_{\rm ref}$ $\mathcal T_{00}=1$ and for a block with weights $x^{d_1}y^{d_2}$
$$\bigl(K_0^{-1/2}M_{x^{d_1}}K_0M_{y^{d_2}}K_0^{-1/2}\bigr)_{jk}=O\bigl(j^{d_1/2}k^{d_2/2}\,r^{\,m-(j+k)/2}\bigr),\quad|m-\tfrac{j+k}2|\le\tfrac{d_1+d_2}2:\qquad\text{banded, entries }O(j^{(d_1+d_2)/2}),\ \text{no Gaussian factor left},\tag{4.4}$$
i.e. the oscillator-basis matrix of a *differential operator with polynomial coefficients* (a polynomial in the ladder operators $a,a^\dagger$ of degree $d_1+d_2$; in the toy (4.4) is exact, not only a scaling: $K_0^{-1/2}M_{x^{d_1}}K_0M_{y^{d_2}}K_0^{-1/2}=(K_0^{-1/2}M_{x^{d_1}}K_0^{1/2})(K_0^{1/2}M_{y^{d_2}}K_0^{-1/2})$ is a product of two polynomials in $(r^{\pm1/2}a,r^{\mp1/2}a^\dagger)$): unbounded, densely defined, and Fredholm when it is globally elliptic in the sense of Shubin (*Pseudodifferential operators and spectral theory*, ch. IV; the anharmonic oscillator is the standard instance). Global ellipticity gives the Fredholm property, **not** index $0$ and not a compact resolvent by itself: $a=(x+i\xi)/\sqrt2$ is globally elliptic of order $1$ with $\ker a$ one-dimensional and $a$ surjective, index $+1$, and $a-\lambda$ is never invertible. What gives index $0$ here is the symmetric structure (1.5) together with self-adjointness at one point: $U$ commutes with $\mathbb K_0$ ($K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})$ is even and $O(26)$-invariant, and $\Pi_{\rm odd}$ commutes with $U$), so $\mathcal T_L(p)U$ is symmetric on $D$ at real $p$ and $\mathcal T_L(p)$ at imaginary $p$; a closed symmetric Fredholm operator has index $\le0$, with $0$ iff it is self-adjoint (a self-adjoint Fredholm operator restricted to a domain of finite codimension is closed, symmetric, of negative index), so index $0$ holds once $\mathcal T_L(p)U$ is self-adjoint at one point of $\Omega_L$ (part of the discharge), and then on all of the connected $\Omega_L$ by local constancy. Since $\mathcal T_L$ is a *matrix* of such operators with blocks of different degrees ($0$ on $00$, $d_l$ on $0l$, $d_l+d_{l'}$ on $ll'$), the relevant notion is Douglis–Nirenberg (mixed-order) global ellipticity – invertibility of the weighted principal-symbol matrix – not the top symbol of one block. This is the same operator type as (4.2) – both are unbounded – so the gain of the symmetric split is not boundedness; it is threefold: only $K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})$ is inverted (R53), never an unknown diagonal block; the symmetry (1.5) is preserved, which is what fixes the index; and the level-0 block of $\mathcal T_L$ is bounded with bounded inverse at real $P^2$ (α) – the identity at $p=p_{\rm ref}$ –, the level-$L$ dependence being, in the toy scaling, a relatively bounded, not compact, perturbation of it. Dividing out the Gaussian on both sides thus converts "compact with no identity part" into "unbounded Fredholm with an invertible level-0 block": the right type for the analytic Fredholm theorem is **a holomorphic family of Fredholm operators of index $0$**, not a compact family. Kernel correspondence: $\sigma\in\ker K^{(L)}_p\iff\mathbb K_0^{1/2}\sigma\in\ker\mathcal T_L(p)$, by injectivity of $\mathbb K_0^{1/2}$, provided $\ker\mathcal T_L(p)\subseteq\mathrm{ran}\,\mathbb K_0^{1/2}$ (a regularity statement on null vectors) – and the very definition of $\mathcal T_L(p)$ on a domain inside $\mathrm{ran}\,\mathbb K_0^{1/2}$ presupposes that $K^{(L)}_p$ maps that domain into $\mathrm{ran}\,\mathbb K_0^{1/2}$, which is the Picard/range question of C1.5 and C3.2. (4.4) is a scaling statement in the toy, not a proof for the string kernel, whose $K_{00}$ is a superposition over $m$ of Mehler kernels (3.2): it motivates the hypothesis and fixes its form.

*(4.5) The hypothesis (declared).*

> **H1 (Fredholm form of the fixed-level kinetic operator).** Fix, for each $L$, a reference momentum $p_{\rm ref}$ with $p_{\rm ref}^2$ real and $\mathbb K_0:=\bigl(K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})\,\Pi_{\rm even}+|K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})|\,\Pi_{\rm odd}\bigr)\otimes1_{W_L}$; at $L\ge1$, $K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})$ is injective on $L^2_{\rm odd}(\mathbb R^{26})$ (not given by R53, whose mechanism uses evenness). There is a connected open $\Omega_L\subset\mathbb C$ containing $\mathbb R\cup i\mathbb R$ (every real $P^2$) on which $p\mapsto\mathcal T_L(p)=\mathbb K_0^{-1/2}K^{(L)}_p\mathbb K_0^{-1/2}$ of (4.3) is a holomorphic family of closed Fredholm operators of index $0$ on $\mathcal H^{(L)}$ (holomorphic in the sense of Kato VII §1.2, type (A): a common domain $D\subseteq\mathrm{ran}\,\mathbb K_0^{1/2}$ on which $K^{(L)}_p$ takes values in $\mathrm{ran}\,\mathbb K_0^{1/2}$ – in particular the range condition of C3.2 for the vectors of $D$), with $\ker\mathcal T_L(p)\subseteq\mathrm{ran}\,\mathbb K_0^{1/2}$ and $\mathbb K_0^{1/2}\ker K^{(L)}_p\subseteq D$ (satisfied when $D$ is the common maximal domain $\{u\in\mathrm{ran}\,\mathbb K_0^{1/2}:K^{(L)}_p\mathbb K_0^{-1/2}u\in\mathrm{ran}\,\mathbb K_0^{1/2}\}$), so that $\ker K^{(L)}_p=\mathbb K_0^{-1/2}\ker\mathcal T_L(p)$ (at $L=0$, $D=\mathcal H$ and the last clause is automatic).
> *Motivation.* (a) Exact at $L=0$: $\mathcal T_0(p)=\mathcal T_{00}(p)$ is bounded with bounded inverse at every real $P^2$ (form comparison of the tile weights and Douglas' lemma, (α)), bounded and holomorphic for all complex $p$ (β), hence invertible on a neighbourhood $\Omega_0$ of the axes; it is $1$ at $p=p_{\rm ref}$ (R53). (b) Structure (2.5): all blocks share the level-0 Gaussian; after dividing it out on both sides the toy scaling (4.4) leaves a matrix of polynomial-coefficient differential operators in the oscillator basis, Fredholm under (Douglis–Nirenberg) global ellipticity, which does not fix the index; the structure (1.5) makes it symmetric by (1.5); index $0$ once $\mathcal T_L(p)U$ is self-adjoint at one point of $\Omega_L$ (part of the discharge), then on all of $\Omega_L$ by local constancy; the known failure mode of D1, (4.1), is a kernel escaping to high modes, exactly what Fredholmness forbids. (c) The UV-finiteness analogy in the record's terms: the stub Gaussian is what controls the high-momentum behaviour of every vertex (review l. 185–187); H1 says the level dependence is a polynomial dressing of that Gaussian, controlled by it. (d) H1 is sharp against the known failure mode: for the repaired counterexample (4.1), $\mathcal T(p)=p-\hat P$ is Fredholm exactly off the accumulation point $p_*$, where H1 fails.
> *Falsified by.* An accumulation point of $Z_L$ at finite $p$ in $\Omega_L$; equivalently a sequence of normalised near-null vectors $\|K^{(L)}_{p_j}\sigma_j\|\to0$, $p_j\to p_*$, with no $L^2$-convergent subsequence after rescaling by $\mathbb K_0^{1/2}$ (a kernel escaping to large relative momentum or high partial wave); or a weighted principal-symbol matrix of $\mathcal T_L$ degenerate on a cone of the phase space (not Douglis–Nirenberg elliptic), which would deny the Fredholm property; or a vector of the natural domain whose $K^{(L)}_p$-image leaves $\mathrm{ran}\,\mathbb K_0^{1/2}$ (the range condition failing); or a null vector of $K_{00}(p_{\rm ref})$ on odd functions. *Discharged by.* The symbolic computation of $\Pi^{(1)}$ for the level-$(1,1)$ block (a Wick-contraction computation needing the section data, not momentum-dependent vertex numerics), the Douglis–Nirenberg global ellipticity of the resulting weighted symbol matrix of $\mathcal T_1$, and the self-adjointness of $\mathcal T_1(p)U$ at one real $p$ (which fixes the index at $0$ on $\Omega_1$); or a proof of the Fredholm property from the explicit six-tile form (2.2) directly.

*(4.6) Theorem under H1 (C4.5).* Under H1 and the hypotheses of C1–C3 (PH25, PH29, PH30 through R53 for $\mathbb K_0$): for each $L$, **either $\ker K^{(L)}_p\ne0$ for every $p\in\Omega_L$, or $Z_L\cap\Omega_L$ is discrete.** Proof: for a holomorphic family of Fredholm operators of index $0$ on a connected domain, the set where the kernel is non-trivial is either the whole domain or discrete (closed and discrete in the domain). This is where H1's type (A) clause is used: type (A) means a common domain $D$ for all $p$; equipped with the graph norm of $\mathcal T_L(p_0)$ for one $p_0\in\Omega_L$, $D$ is a Banach space, and $p\mapsto\mathcal T_L(p)\in B(D,\mathcal H^{(L)})$ is a holomorphic family of *bounded* Fredholm operators of index $0$ between two Banach spaces, to which Gohberg–Goldberg–Kaashoek, *Classes of linear operators* I, XI.8, or Zworski, *Semiclassical analysis*, Thm C.8, apply directly; Reed–Simon I Thm VI.14 is the special case $1+C(p)$ with $C$ compact, obtained when one member $\mathcal T_L(p_0)$ is invertible and $\mathcal T_L(p_0)^{-1}(\mathcal T_L(p)-\mathcal T_L(p_0))$ is compact. The kernel correspondence of H1 transfers the dichotomy to $K^{(L)}_p$. The first horn is a kernel at every momentum – a *forced* kernel in the sense of angle D (a selection rule, a decoupled direction), which is Task 7's object; it is excluded by one reference point:
$$\exists\,p_0\in\Omega_L:\ \ker K^{(L)}_{p_0}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Z_L\cap\Omega_L\ \text{discrete (D1 at level }L\text{)}.\tag{4.5}$$
*Reference point.* $L=0$: every real $P^2$ (R53). $L\ge1$: **open**; candidates, none established: (a) PH21 at one real momentum, if the split were Fredholm (it is not, (4.2)); (b) the limit $P^2\to-\infty$ (timelike, imaginary $p\to\infty$), where the tile weights $|Z|^{\alpha's/4}|1-Z|^{-\alpha's/4}$ concentrate at the corner $Z=-1$ (050 C3.1: $|1-Z|/|Z|\le2$ with equality there; 049 (1.8)) and the kernel tends to a single Mehler-type kernel with $c=\pm\alpha'\ln2$ dressed by the top power of $p$ in $\Pi^{(L)}$ – a simpler kernel question, still open; this candidate is a point at infinity, not in $\Omega_L$, and what it would give is injectivity for $|p|$ large by perturbation from the corner kernel, hence a finite $p_0$; (c) a point of the complex plane where the level-$L$ Nyström determinant of a *discretised momentum* is non-zero does not qualify (a discretisation is not the operator, 049 (c3)).

*(4.7) What D1 gives and does not (C4.6).* Given (4.5): the set of real $P^2$ with a kernel at level $L$ is discrete in $\mathbb R$ in the strong sense (closed, no accumulation at finite points) – the paper's "no kernel at generic momentum" at fixed level, with the exceptional set locally finite, not merely of measure zero. It does not give D2 (real zeros are allowed, each isolated); it does not give D3; and it says nothing about $L\to\infty$ (C6). At a point $p_*\in Z_L\cap\mathbb R$ that is a *simple* zero – $\dim\ker K_{p_*}=1$, spanned by $N$, with left null vector $UN$ (1.5) and $\langle UN,\partial_pK_{p_*}N\rangle\ne0$ – the singular part of $K_p^{-1}v_p$ is $N\langle UN,v_{p_*}\rangle/\bigl((p-p_*)\langle UN,\partial_pK\,N\rangle\bigr)$, so the exchange vertex $\langle v_{-p},K_p^{-1}v_p\rangle$ has a pole at $p_*$ unless $\langle UN,v_{p_*}\rangle=0$ exactly: D3 at a simple real zero is the kernel condition `eq:hs5:kernel-condition` at that one point, and nothing in the analytic structure forces it (it is a property of $\mathcal V_5$ at $p_*$). For a higher-order zero or $\dim\ker>1$ the singular part involves $\partial_pv$ and the Jordan chain (one-dimensional example: $K=(p-p_*)^2$, $v$ constant), and the kernel condition at $p_*$ is only necessary. (The self-adjoint structure makes $N$ real; "non-isotropic" was not checked against 049 C2(ii) and is not used.)

\subsection*{Setup}

\subsection*{Derivation}

*(5.2) What needs a new ingredient: the level-0 reference.* R53's mechanism at $k=2$ uses (a) one scalar cross term $e^{c\,q\cdot q'}$ per point of the region, (b) evenness of the fibre functions, under which odd powers of $q\cdot q'$ drop and $e^{c\,q\cdot q'}$ becomes a sum of squares whatever the sign of $c$ (050 (4.2)). The level-0 definiteness of $\mathcal K_k$ at real $P^2$ is therefore **open at $k\ge3$**, with (a') as the statement to check on the $\mathcal V_6$ section – no computation here. Everything in C3 and in the reference point of C4 that cites R53 is, at $k\ge3$, conditional on that open statement.

## §6 The limit $L\to\infty$ (C6)

\subsection*{Setup}

A1–A5. $\mathcal H^{(L)}\subset\mathcal H^{(L+1)}$ (per-strand level), $K^{(L)}=\Pi_LK^{(\infty)}\Pi_L$ the compression of the full fibre operator – the full form on finite-level vectors; on vectors of infinite level support it is defined on whatever domain the convergence of the mass sum gives it (angle B, PH1), which this record does not have –, $Z_L$ as in §4, $Z_\infty:=\{p:\ker K^{(\infty)}_p\ne0\}$ on that domain; only $Z_\infty^{\rm fin}$ below is used.

\subsection*{Derivation}

*(6.1) No monotonicity.* For $\sigma\in\mathcal H^{(L)}$: $\sigma\in\ker K^{(L+1)}\iff\sigma\in\ker K^{(L)}$ and $K_{L+1,\le L}\sigma=0$ (the new rows). Hence $\ker K^{(L)}\cap\mathcal H^{(L)}$ and $\ker K^{(L+1)}$ are related only through
$$\ker K^{(L)}\cap\ker K^{(L+1)}=\ker K^{(L)}\cap\ker K_{L+1,\le L},\tag{6.1}$$
and a null vector of $K^{(L+1)}$ with a non-zero level-$(L+1)$ component is not in $\mathcal H^{(L)}$ at all. Neither $Z_L\subseteq Z_{L+1}$ nor the converse holds in general: a level-$L$ null vector is lost at level $L+1$ unless the new rows annihilate it, and level $L+1$ can create null vectors of its own. (At $L=0$: $Z_0\cap\{\text{real }P^2\}=\emptyset$ by R53 while $Z_1$ may be non-empty, C3.3.) This is the precise content of "a null vector of the restriction is not a null vector of the full form".

*(6.2) Stabilisation (PH1 (i)) in these terms.* Two readings of "a kernel that stabilizes". The strong one, taken here: $\ker K^{(L)}=\ker K^{(\infty)}\cap\mathcal H^{(L)}$ for $L\ge L_0$ – every level-$L$ null vector survives all higher rows. The weak one, which a level-truncation practitioner means and which is PH19's object: $\dim\ker K^{(L)}$ eventually constant and the kernels converging as subspaces of $\mathcal H^{(\infty)}$, which allows the null vector to rotate out of $\mathcal H^{(L)}$ and is not covered by what follows. The strong reading means: every level-$L$ null vector is annihilated by *all* higher rows, $K_{l,\le L}\sigma=0$ for all $l>L$ – a condition on infinitely many blocks, which no finite-level computation decides (the pitfall of `knowledge/open-problems.md` l. 41 in kernel form). Under stabilisation, $Z_L=Z_\infty^{\rm fin}:=\{p:\ker K^{(\infty)}_p\text{ contains a finite-level vector}\}$ for $L\ge L_0$ – but only for finite-level null vectors.

*(6.3) The union.* Under D1 at every $L$ (C4.5 with a reference point at every level), each $Z_L$ is discrete, hence countable, and $\bigcup_LZ_L$ is countable: of measure zero in $\mathbb C$ and in $\mathbb R$ – "generic momentum" in the measure sense – but possibly dense (a countable union of discrete sets has no reason to be discrete; the zeros of the level-$L$ truncations may approach every real point as $L$ grows, which is exactly the alternative PH19 leaves open: convergence to finite points, or densification). The relation to the full operator: $Z_\infty^{\rm fin}\subseteq\liminf_LZ_L\subseteq\bigcup_LZ_L$ (a finite-level null vector of $K^{(\infty)}$ is a null vector of every $K^{(L)}$ from its level on), and a null vector of $K^{(\infty)}$ with infinite level support is not detected by any $Z_L$ (its truncations are near-null, not null; the momentum may lie in some $Z_L$ for another reason). So D1 at every level gives, for the full operator, only: *the finite-level part of $Z_\infty$ is countable*; it gives neither $Z_\infty$ discrete nor the paper's "generic momentum" for $K^{(\infty)}$ in the locally-finite sense of (4.7). What would: a uniform-in-$L$ version of H1 (Fredholm with index $0$ for $\mathcal T_\infty$, with the mass sum converging – angle B's tool (c) of the restated analogy), which is the $L\to\infty$ statement this record does not make.

\subsection*{Result}

Restricted kernels are not monotone in $L$ (6.1); stabilisation is a condition on all higher rows (6.2); under D1 at every level $\bigcup_LZ_L$ is countable, generic in measure, possibly dense, and controls only the finite-level null vectors of the full operator (6.3). Structural (Proven as statements about compressions of a fixed operator); the behaviour of $Z_L$ as $L\to\infty$ (PH19) is untouched.
