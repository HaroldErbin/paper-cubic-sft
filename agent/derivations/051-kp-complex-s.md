## Task and objective of the line

**Literal task (whole line).** Three sequential sub-tasks of 049 Part 2 items 4–5: 18c.5, the zeros of the frozen-momentum contact term $F(s,0,0)$ on $\alpha's\in[-40,20]$ and in the strip $\lvert\operatorname{Im}\alpha's\rvert\le10$ over the same real range, by argument principle on rectangles and Newton refinement, read against the plumbing lattice $\alpha's\in\{4,0,-4,-8,\dots\}$ ($P_I^2=-\tfrac2{\alpha'}(N+\bar N-2)$), both $\beta\in\{\tfrac32,2\}$; 18c.6a, a Chebyshev-in-$s$ tabulation of $F$ on the strip per node pair, with its interpolation error; 18c.6b, the zeros of the leading eigenvalue branches $\mu_{n,j}(s)$ of the Nyström s-wave (then $n=2$) in the strip, each with the bilinear square of its null vector (isotropy verdict), read against the lattice — the level-0 form of PH18.

**Motivating objective.** PH18 (a non-isotropic zero of $\det V_4^{(L)}(P^2)$ off the plumbing lattice) decides through records 035/036 whether the $\Sigma$-channel composite $\mathcal C^{(L)}$ has a pole where no string state sits. At level 0 its faithful stand-in (R51, form D1 of 049 Block 2) is a complex-$s$ zero of an eigenvalue branch of $K_P(s)$ with a non-isotropic null vector. 18c.5 is the cheapest model of that computation, named in 035 §4.2 ("freeze the relative momentum, the block is $1\times1$"): at $\ell=\ell'=0$ the kernel collapses to the number $F(s,0,0)$, whose zeros show whether off-lattice zeros occur at all in this contact term and where. **It is a model, not the level-0 verdict**: the level-0 block is an integral operator in $(\ell,\ell')$, and a zero of the scalar $F(s,0,0)$ is neither necessary nor sufficient for a zero of an eigenvalue branch (see §18c.5, *Physical reading*). 18c.5 also tests, on a scalar, the complex-$s$ evaluation path (complex exponents in the boundary evaluator, growth $e^{\pi\alpha'\lvert\operatorname{Im}s\rvert/2}$ per tile, 049 (1.5)) that 18c.6 uses on matrices.

## 18c.5 — The frozen-momentum zero scan of $F(s,0,0)$

### Block 5 — Reduction, continuation, positivity, growth (C1, C2, part of C3/C4)

\subsection*{Setup}

A1, A3, A5, A6. $R=\mathrm{FD}\cap\mathcal V_{0,4}$ (047 A1: unit-circle arc from $-1$ to $e^{i\pi/3}$, vertical segment $\operatorname{Re}Z=\tfrac12$, ellipse quarter with semi-axes $a=\beta^2+\tfrac1{16\beta^2}$, $b=\beta^2-\tfrac1{16\beta^2}$ centred at $\tfrac12$, real segment from $\tfrac12-a$ to $-1$). $J(p,q)=\int_R\lvert Z\rvert^p\lvert1-Z\rvert^q\,d^2Z$. $\alpha'=1$.

\subsection*{Derivation}

*(5.1) Reduction.* At $t=u=0$ the six permutations $(x,y,z)$ of $(s,0,0)$ give the exponent pairs $\tfrac14(x-y-z,\,y-x-z)$: $(s,0,0),(s,0,0)\mapsto(\tfrac s4,-\tfrac s4)$; $(0,s,0),(0,s,0)\mapsto(-\tfrac s4,\tfrac s4)$; $(0,0,s),(0,0,s)\mapsto(-\tfrac s4,-\tfrac s4)$. Hence
$$F(s,0,0)=4\beta^{8-s/2}\,G(s),\qquad G(s):=J(\tfrac s4,-\tfrac s4)+J(-\tfrac s4,\tfrac s4)+J(-\tfrac s4,-\tfrac s4).\tag{5.1}$$
$\beta^{8-s/2}=e^{(8-s/2)\ln\beta}$ is entire and nowhere zero, so the zeros of $F(s,0,0)$ are the zeros of $G$, with multiplicities. At $s=0$, $G(0)=3\operatorname{Vol}(R)$ and $F(0,0,0)=12\operatorname{Vol}(R)\beta^8$, 045's normalisation anchor. Note $J(p,q)\neq J(q,p)$ in general ($R$ is not $Z\to1-Z$ symmetric: $\operatorname{Re}Z\le\tfrac12$ on $R$), so the first two terms of $G$ are distinct.

*(5.2) Continuation to complex exponents.* $\lvert Z\rvert^p\lvert1-Z\rvert^q=\exp(p\ln\lvert Z\rvert+q\ln\lvert1-Z\rvert)$ with $\ln\lvert Z\rvert,\ln\lvert1-Z\rvert$ real: the integrand is entire in $(p,q)$ and carries **no phase from $\arg Z$**. Consequences: (i) $J$ is entire in $(p,q)$ and $G$ entire in $s$ (045 C1 restated); (ii) the direct 2D quadrature continues verbatim; (iii) the sharp bound is
$$\lvert J(p,q)\rvert\le J(\operatorname{Re}p,\operatorname{Re}q),\tag{5.2}$$
without the factor $e^{\pi\lvert\operatorname{Im}p\rvert}$ of 049 (1.5) — that factor belongs to the *boundary representation's* integrand $f=Z^{p/2}(1-Z)^{q/2}$, which does carry $e^{-\operatorname{Im}p\,\arg Z/2}$, not to $J$. 049 (1.5)'s bound is correct but not sharp; nothing in 049 rests on its sharpness. (iv) Schwarz reflection: $G(\bar s)=\overline{G(s)}$, since $G$ is real on the real axis; complex zeros come in conjugate pairs.
The boundary representation continues as follows. For complex $(p,q)$ set $f(Z)=\exp[\tfrac p2(\ln\lvert Z\rvert+i\theta_0)+\tfrac q2(\ln\lvert1-Z\rvert+i\theta_1)]$ with 047's branches $\theta_0=\arg Z\in[0,\pi]$, $\theta_1=\arg(1-Z)\in[-\pi,0]$, and $f^*(Z):=\exp[\tfrac p2(\ln\lvert Z\rvert-i\theta_0)+\tfrac q2(\ln\lvert1-Z\rvert-i\theta_1)]=\overline{f(Z;\bar p,\bar q)}$. Then $ff^*=\lvert Z\rvert^p\lvert1-Z\rvert^q$ exactly, $f$ is holomorphic and $f^*$ anti-holomorphic in $Z$ on $R$. With $G=\int_{-1}^Zf\,dZ$ and $G^*(Z):=\overline{G(Z;\bar p,\bar q)}$ (so $\partial_{\bar Z}G^*=f^*$), Stokes on the $(1,0)$-form $G^*f\,dZ$ gives $d(G^*f\,dZ)=f^*f\,d\bar Z\wedge dZ=2i\,ff^*\,dx\,dy$, hence
$$J(p,q)=\frac1{2i}\oint_{\partial R}G^*\,dG.\tag{5.3}$$
At real exponents $G^*=\overline G$ and, since $\operatorname{Re}\oint\overline G\,dG=\tfrac12\oint d\lvert G\rvert^2=0$, (5.3) is R50's $\tfrac12\operatorname{Im}\oint\overline G\,dG$. Both sides of (5.3) are entire in $(p,q)$ (the right side is a finite integral of entire functions of $(p,q)$), so agreement at real exponents is agreement everywhere. The vectorised evaluator `j_vec_c` implements (5.3) with R50's cumulative Chebyshev scheme for both $G$ and $G^*$, summing over all four arcs before dividing by $2i$ (per-arc real parts do not vanish individually).
*Conditioning.* $\lvert f\rvert=\lvert Z\rvert^{\operatorname{Re}p/2}\lvert1-Z\rvert^{\operatorname{Re}q/2}e^{-(\operatorname{Im}p\,\theta_0+\operatorname{Im}q\,\theta_1)/2}$; for the tiles of $G$, $\lvert\operatorname{Im}p\rvert=\lvert\operatorname{Im}q\rvert=\lvert\operatorname{Im}s\rvert/4$, and $\theta_0\in[0,\pi]$, $\theta_1\in[-\pi,0]$, so $\lvert G^*f\rvert$ exceeds the scale of $J$ by up to $e^{\pi\lvert\operatorname{Im}s\rvert/2}$: $\approx7$ digits lost at $\lvert\operatorname{Im}s\rvert=10$, all $16$ at $\lvert\operatorname{Im}s\rvert\approx23$. **The boundary route is usable in double precision on the strip $\lvert\operatorname{Im}\alpha's\rvert\le10$ and not much beyond; the direct 2D quadrature (bounded integrand, (5.2)) has no such loss and is the route for wider strips.** This is the complex-$s$ lesson for 18c.6.

*(5.3) No real zero.* For real $s$ each $J$ is the integral of a strictly positive function over a region of positive area, so $G(s)>0$ for all real $s$ and $F(s,0,0)$ has **no real zero at any $\beta$**. In particular no zero of the frozen block sits on the plumbing lattice (real), and every zero of $G$ is off the real axis, off the lattice, in conjugate pairs.

*(5.4) Zeros exist; where to expect them.* Each $J$ of $G$ is a Laplace transform $\int e^{-sL/4}d\mu(L)$ of a positive measure on a compact interval — $L=\ln\lvert Z(1-Z)\rvert$ for the third tile, $L=\pm\ln\lvert Z/(1-Z)\rvert$ for the first two — so $G$ is entire of exponential type (order $1$). On the real axis: $\lvert Z/(1-Z)\rvert\le1$ on $R$ with equality on the vertical segment, and $\lvert(1-Z)/Z\rvert\le2$ with equality at $Z=-1$; $\lvert Z(1-Z)\rvert\le a^2-\tfrac14$ (at $Z=\tfrac12-a$). So $G(s)\sim 2^{s/4}\times$(power) as $s\to+\infty$ (second tile) and $G(s)\sim(a^2-\tfrac14)^{\lvert s\rvert/4}\times$(power) as $s\to-\infty$ (third tile; $a^2-\tfrac14=4.94$ at $\beta=\tfrac32$, $15.9$ at $\beta=2$): $G\to+\infty$ in both real directions. A zero-free entire function of order $\le1$ is $e^{\alpha s+\gamma}$ (Hadamard), monotone on the real axis for real $\alpha,\gamma$ ($G$ real there); so $G$ **has zeros in $\mathbb C$**, and by the Laplace-transform analogy ($\int_0^1e^{-sL}dL=(1-e^{-s})/s$, zeros at $s=2\pi in$) their imaginary parts scale as $2\pi\cdot4/\Delta L$ with $\Delta L$ the spread of the exponent function on $R$: $\Delta L\approx\ln(a^2-\tfrac14)\approx1.6$ ($\beta=\tfrac32$) gives $\operatorname{Im}s\sim16$, above the strip of the brief; $\Delta L\approx\ln 15.9=2.8$ ($\beta=2$) gives $\operatorname{Im}s\sim9$, inside it. This heuristic is only a guide to where the scan should look; the numbers are in Block 6.

\subsection*{Commentary}

- (5.2) corrects a remark of 049 (1.5) in the safe direction and separates two things 049 ran together: the growth of $J$ in $\operatorname{Im}s$ (none beyond $J(\operatorname{Re}p,\operatorname{Re}q)$) and the growth of the *boundary integrand* (real, $e^{\pi\lvert\operatorname{Im}s\rvert/2}$ for the tiles of $G$), which is a conditioning problem of one evaluator, not a property of the kernel. For the Nyström matrices of 18c.6 this means: the entries of $K_P(s)$ are bounded by those of $K_P(\operatorname{Re}s)$ on the strip, and the evaluator to use at $\lvert\operatorname{Im}s\rvert\gtrsim10$ is the 2D quadrature or a higher-precision boundary route.
- (5.3) is the whole real-axis content of 18c.5: the frozen block is positive on the real line. The 049 remark that $K_P$ is Gaussian-bounded "at every real $s$, timelike included" is consistent with this (nothing singular happens at the lattice points in the contact term; the poles of the amplitude on the lattice come from the exchange diagrams, not from $V_4$).
- (5.4) uses Hadamard's theorem for order-$1$ functions: $G(s)=e^{\alpha s+\gamma}\prod_n(1-s/s_n)e^{s/s_n}$; a zero-free $G$ would be $e^{\alpha s+\gamma}$, which cannot tend to $+\infty$ in both real directions. The Laplace-transform heuristic is the reason the scan is extended beyond the brief's strip (informational checks `C3-wide`, `C4-wide`): the objective is to know where the first zeros of this model are, and at $\beta=\tfrac32$ they are expected just above the strip.

\subsection*{Result}

(5.1)–(5.3): $F(s,0,0)=4\beta^{8-s/2}G(s)$, $G$ entire of exponential type, real and strictly positive on $\mathbb R$, $\lvert J(p,q)\rvert\le J(\operatorname{Re}p,\operatorname{Re}q)$; the boundary representation continues as $(2i)^{-1}\oint G^*dG$ with a digit loss $e^{\pi\lvert\operatorname{Im}s\rvert/2}$.

### Block 8 — The scheme and its justification

\subsection*{Setup}

A1–A6; C2 (the bound $\lvert J(p,q)\rvert\le J(\operatorname{Re}p,\operatorname{Re}q)$, entrywise $\lvert F(s,t,u)\rvert\le F(\operatorname{Re}s,t,u)$, and the conditioning loss $e^{\pi\lvert\operatorname{Im}s\rvert/2}$ of the boundary route); C2.1 (entrywise Schwarz reflection $F(\bar s,t,u)=\overline{F(s,t,u)}$ for real $t,u$). Region $\mathcal S=\{\operatorname{Re}\alpha's\in[-40,20],\ \lvert\operatorname{Im}\alpha's\rvert\le40\}$ (C5, `## Next`). Nyström node triples $(\rho_i,\rho_j,x_k)$, $i\le j$, $x_k>0$, of the $\beta=2$ design $(n_r,\Lambda,n_\theta)$ (050 §18c.2–18c.3; `_kn_job` of `verify-kp-real-s.py`), $t=\rho_i^2+\rho_j^2+2\rho_i\rho_jx_k$, $u=\rho_i^2+\rho_j^2-2\rho_i\rho_jx_k$.

\subsection*{Derivation}

*(8.1) The $s$-dependence of one entry.* By A3 the six tiles of $F(s,t,u)=2\beta^{8-(s+t+u)/2}\sum_{S_3}J(p,q)$ fall into three **types** by their $s$-dependence, each type a pair of tiles: type I, $(s,t,u),(s,u,t)$: $p=\tfrac14(s-t-u)$, $q=\tfrac14(\pm(t-u)-s)$, so $J=\int_Rw_{\rm I}(Z)e^{sL_{\rm I}(Z)/4}d^2Z$ with $L_{\rm I}=\ln\lvert Z\rvert-\ln\lvert1-Z\rvert$; type II, $(t,s,u),(u,s,t)$: $L_{\rm II}=-L_{\rm I}$; type III, $(t,u,s),(u,t,s)$: $L_{\rm III}=-\ln\lvert Z\rvert-\ln\lvert1-Z\rvert$. The weights $w$ are positive and $s$-independent. On $R$ (047 A1: outside the unit circle, $\operatorname{Re}Z\le\tfrac12$): $\lvert Z/(1-Z)\rvert\in[\tfrac12,1]$ (equality $\tfrac12$ at the corner $Z=-1$, $1$ on the vertical segment), and $\lvert Z(1-Z)\rvert\in[1,a^2-\tfrac14]$ ($1$ at $e^{i\pi/3}$, the maximum at $\tfrac12-a$; $a^2-\tfrac14=15.875$ at $\beta=2$). Hence, per unit $s$,
$$\tfrac14L_{\rm I}\in[-\tfrac{\ln2}4,0]=[-0.173,0],\quad\tfrac14L_{\rm II}\in[0,0.173],\quad\tfrac14L_{\rm III}\in[-\tfrac14\ln(a^2-\tfrac14),0]=[-0.692,0]\ (\beta=2).\tag{8.1}$$
Each type-sum $J_{\rm X}(s)$ is a Laplace transform of a positive measure supported on the interval (8.1): entire of exponential type, $\lvert J_{\rm X}(s)\rvert\le J_{\rm X}(\operatorname{Re}s)$, real on the real axis. The centred functions
$$h_{\rm X}(s):=e^{-c_{\rm X}s}J_{\rm X}(s),\qquad c_{\rm I}=-0.0866,\ c_{\rm II}=+0.0866,\ c_{\rm III}=-0.346\ (\beta=2),\tag{8.2}$$
have exponent range $\lvert\tfrac14L-c_{\rm X}\rvert\le\gamma_{\rm X}$ with $\gamma_{\rm I,II}=0.0866$, $\gamma_{\rm III}=0.346$, so that $h_{\rm X}(s_1)/h_{\rm X}(s_2)\le e^{\gamma_{\rm X}\lvert\operatorname{Re}(s_1-s_2)\rvert}$: the dynamic range of what is interpolated is set by the *real* extent of the node set alone. The entry is reassembled exactly as $F=2\beta^{8-(s+t+u)/2}\sum_{\rm X}e^{c_{\rm X}s}h_{\rm X}(s)$.

*(8.2) Why the real-interval Chebyshev interpolant is unsound on $\mathcal S$.* Map $[-40,20]\ni s=-10+30x$. The corner $s=20+40i$ is $x=1+\tfrac43i$, on the Bernstein ellipse $E_r$ with $r\approx3.45$ (the solution of $1/a_r^2+(4/3)^2/b_r^2=1$, $a_r=\tfrac12(r+r^{-1})$, $b_r=\tfrac12(r-r^{-1})$). (i) *Degree.* For $g(x)=e^{30\gamma x}$ the Chebyshev coefficients are $2I_k(30\gamma)$, $\lvert I_k(a)\rvert\le(a/2)^k/k!$, and the interpolation error at $x\in E_r$ is bounded by the tail $\sum_{k>N}2\lvert I_k\rvert r^k$: with $\gamma_{\rm III}=0.346$, $(30\gamma r/2)^k/k!=(17.9)^k/k!$, which reaches $10^{-8}$ relative to $\min_{\mathcal S}\lvert g\rvert=e^{-30\gamma}$ only at $N\approx72$ — not the $\sim40$ of 049 Part 2 item 5, which was estimated for $\lvert\operatorname{Im}\alpha's\rvert\le10$ ($r=1.39$, $N\approx40$). (ii) *Conditioning — the decisive point.* The interpolant $p_N(x)=\sum a_kT_k(x)$ carries rounding errors $\delta a_k\sim\epsilon\max_{[-1,1]}\lvert h\rvert$ from real data, and $\lvert T_k(x)\rvert\approx\tfrac12r^k$ at $x\in E_r$: the continuation error at height $\operatorname{Im}\alpha's=40$ is $\sim\epsilon\max\lvert h\rvert\,r^N=10^{-16}\times3.45^{72}\approx10^{+23}\max\lvert h\rvert$, while $\lvert h(s)\rvert\le h(\operatorname{Re}s)$ there (C2). Analytic continuation from real data over a height larger than the interval's half-length is exponentially ill-posed; no degree fixes it (larger $N$ makes it worse). On the original strip $\lvert\operatorname{Im}\alpha's\rvert\le10$ the same estimate gives $1.39^{40}\approx10^{5.7}$, acceptable — which is why 049's scheme was sound for the strip it was written for and is not for the widened region. **The plan's scheme is replaced.**

*(8.3) The scheme adopted: Chebyshev coefficients from nodes on a Bernstein ellipse enclosing the region, per real panel.* If the samples are taken *on* $E_r$ rather than on $[-1,1]$, the coefficients are Fourier coefficients on the circle $\lvert w\rvert=r$ under Joukowski $x=\tfrac12(w+w^{-1})$: $h(x)=\sum_kb_k(w^k+w^{-k})$, $b_k=\tfrac12a_k$ ($k\ge1$), $b_0=a_0$, and the discrete Fourier transform of $h(x(re^{i\phi_j}))$, $\phi_j=2\pi j/N$, gives $\hat c_m=b_mr^m+b_{N-m}r^{-(N-m)}$: $a_m=2\hat c_mr^{-m}$ ($1\le m<N/2$), $a_0=\hat c_0$, up to the aliasing tail. Rounding errors are now $\delta\hat c_m\sim\epsilon\max_{E_r}\lvert h\rvert$, and evaluating $\sum a_mT_m(x)$ at $x$ inside $E_r$ multiplies $\delta a_m=2\delta\hat c_mr^{-m}$ by $\lvert T_m(x)\rvert\le r^m$: the error at every interior point is $\lesssim N\epsilon\max_{E_r}\lvert h\rvert$ — interpolation from the boundary of the ellipse is well conditioned inside it (the maximum principle; Fejér points). The truncation error is the same tail as in (8.2)(i) with the Bessel bound, and by C2.1 only the nodes with $\operatorname{Im}s\ge0$ are evaluated ($N/2+1$ per ellipse, two of them real), the coefficients $a_k$ being real.
The remaining constraint is the dynamic range $e^{\gamma_{\rm III}\times(\text{real semi-axis of }E_r)}$ against the $10^{-8}$ target at the smallest interior value, which forces **panels in $\operatorname{Re}s$**: for a panel of real half-width $\ell$ and the full height $40$, the cheapest ellipse (minimising the growth parameter $\tfrac12\gamma(A+B)$ of the Bessel tail, $A,B$ the real and imaginary semi-axes, subject to the corner $(\ell,40)$ on the ellipse) has $A/B=(\ell^2/1600)^{1/3}$: $\ell=30$ (one panel): $(A,B)=(44.6,54.0)$, dynamic range $e^{0.346\cdot44.6}=10^{6.7}$, interior minimum $e^{-0.346\cdot30}=10^{-4.5}$, conditioning floor $N\epsilon\cdot10^{6.7+4.5}\approx10^{-4}$ — fails; $\ell=15$: $(25.6,49.3)$, floor $10^{-16+3.85+2.26+1}=10^{-9}$ — marginal; $\ell=10$ (three panels $[-40,-20],[-20,0],[0,20]$): $(A,B)=(18.8,47.3)$, floor $10^{-16+2.8+1.5+1}\approx10^{-11}$, tail parameter $\tfrac12\gamma_{\rm III}(A+B)=11.6$ (for $(A,B)=(19,48)$), $(11.6)^K/K!\le10^{-8}\cdot e^{-3.5}$ at $K\ge49$, and $2.5\times10^{-13}$ at $K=52$. The degree $K$ is half the number of nodes on the ellipse ($N$ samples on the circle $\lvert w\rvert=r$ resolve the Laurent modes $-N/2\le k\le N/2$, and $T_k$ pairs $w^{\pm k}$). **Adopted: three panels, each with $N=104$ nodes on its ellipse (degree $K=52$; $53$ evaluations with $\operatorname{Im}s\ge0$ per panel, $2$ of them real), semi-axes $(A,B)=(19,48)$, i.e. $159$ evaluations of $F$ on all node triples in total ($153$ complex, $6$ real).** A first run with $N=56$ ($K=28$, tail bound $11.6^{28}/28!\approx0.4$) confirmed the estimate the hard way: tail $7\times10^{-3}$, errors up to $6\times10^{-2}$ at $\operatorname{Im}\alpha's\approx39$ (`agent/scratch/c6a-run2.log`). \subsection*{Derivation}

*(12.1) Step 1 — the tool and its analyticity condition.* The projected determinant is
$$d_K(s):=\frac{\det\big(P_K(s)^{\!\top}M^{(n)}(s)P_K(s)\big)}{\det\big(P_K(s)^{\!\top}P_K(s)\big)}=\prod_{k=0}^{K-1}\mu_{(k)}(s),\tag{12.1}$$
the quotient making it independent of the basis (for a bilinearly orthonormal $P_K$ the denominator is $1$, the brief's form); the equality with the product holds because $P_K^{\!\top}MP_K=P_K^{\!\top}P_K\,\Lambda$ on an invariant subspace with $MP_K=P_K\Lambda$ (`C7.6-tool-proj`: relative difference $\le1.6\times10^{-8}$ at $K\le9$ at three seeded points, the smallest factor being at $10^{-8}$–$10^{-9}$ of the largest, within two decades of the floor (9.3)). $d_K$ is a symmetric function of the $K$-set and needs no labels. Three statements fix when its winding on a cell $Q$ is a zero count:
(i) *Continuity on the contour.* If along $\partial Q$ the modulus gap $\gamma_K:=\lvert\mu_{(K-1)}\rvert/\lvert\mu_{(K)}\rvert$ stays $\ge\gamma_{\min}=3$, the $K$ leading eigenvalues at neighbouring contour points are the continuations of one another (an eigenvalue cannot leave or enter the leading set without its modulus crossing that of the $(K{+}1)$-th), so the set is the same analytic set of $K$ branches along $\partial Q$, $d_K$ is continuous (analytic) on $\partial Q$, and $\tfrac1{2\pi}\oint_{\partial Q}d\arg d_K$ is an integer.
(ii) *Single-valuedness inside.* The continuation of the $K$-set into $Q$ is single-valued — and the winding equals the number of zeros in $Q$ of the $K$ leading branches, each with multiplicity — unless an exceptional point inside $Q$ joins a member of the $K$-set to a non-member (a coalescence of the $K$-set with its complement inside $Q$, at which $\gamma_K=1$); exceptional points *within* the $K$-set (the monodromy of (11.2)(iv)) are harmless, the product being symmetric. Since $\gamma_K$ is continuous and $\ge3$ on $\partial Q$, such a coalescence would sit on a closed level curve $\gamma_K=1$ strictly inside $Q$; the modulus-gap sampling used below (the same gap $\gamma_K\ge3$ tested at the four interior points $\tfrac13,\tfrac23$ of the cell) only probes for a coalescence at the sampled points and **does not by itself certify single-valuedness** — a genuine branch zero at the collapse is not distinguished from an exceptional point by the modulus gap alone (18c.6f, (12.14)–(12.18)). The residual condition of the count is properly the continuation/monodromy criterion of (12.13): the continued $K$-set is invariant under a loop's monodromy and no member of it coalesces with a non-member inside, tested by branch continuation rather than by modulus rank; the gap sampling below is retained as a cheap necessary (not sufficient) test, tightened by subdivision. A branch that vanishes inside $Q$ is at its zero the *smallest* eigenvalue, so the pointwise-ordered $d_K$ inside $Q$ is not the continuation from $\partial Q$; only the contour values are used, and the count is that of the continued branches.
(iii) *Choice of $K$ per cell.* $K$ is the largest integer with $\lvert\mu_{(K-1)}\rvert\ge10^{-9}\lvert\mu_{(0)}\rvert$ (the floor of (9.3), so that the phase of every factor is resolved) and $\gamma_K\ge3$ at every contour and interior sample point; a cell with no such $K$ is quadrisected (to level $4$ since the fix pass) and otherwise reported unresolved. \subsection*{Commentary}

- (13.2)–(13.3): the double-precision and DD rule comparisons agree to two figures, so the $60$/$90$ discrepancy is a property of the rules, not of the arithmetic; the smooth rule converges non-monotonically at the $10^{-13}$–$10^{-12}$ level at these exponents (the $90$-point rule is the outlier). The entry-level cause is not investigated; it matters for the profile test, which needs a rule converged far beyond $10^{-13}$.
- (13.4)(ii): the failure of the far bases and of the det drop at $\Lambda=10$ is a resolution effect of the $70$-node DD matrices ($\det K$ noise response $10^{3}$), not a property of the zero: the base-$z_S$ pencil eigenvalue is isolated by a factor $\gtrsim70$ and moves by $\le6\times10^{-9}$ under entry noise up to $10^{-25}$. An analytic $M'$ (the evaluator's node factors $A_ne^{sA_n}$, computed but not accumulated by `m0_dd`) would remove the $\epsilon/h$ noise of the finite-difference derivative.
- (13.5), (13.8): the compressed determinant $g_m$ is entire in $s$ for a fixed basis; its winding counts the zeros of the compressed operator, which approximate those of $\det M$ carried by the head. Its reliability is shown by the control on $(56,8,24)$ (same winding as the full determinant, same convergence of the zero in $m$); on $(70,10,24)$ it is the only resolved count.
