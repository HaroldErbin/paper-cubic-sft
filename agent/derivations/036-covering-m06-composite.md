## 0. Task, objective, conventions

**Literal task.** Determine which part of $\mathcal M_{0,6}$ the composite $\mathcal C$ (R19) covers, by which mechanism, and how it fits the covering requirement "vertices + propagators cover $\mathcal M_{0,6}$ exactly once" together with $W_6=\mathcal V_6-\tfrac65\mathcal C$ and the $\Psi$-propagator Feynman regions. Four sub-tasks: 17.1 CKV/$c$-insertion reading of negative dimension; 17.2 the eight-to-six dimension collapse, modelled on $\mathcal V_4p_{2,2}^{-1}\mathcal V_4=\mathcal V_4$; 17.3 reconciliation with R19 ($W_6\ne\mathcal V_6$) and the covering statement; 17.4 the scalar $\phi^3{+}\phi^4{+}\phi^5{+}\phi^6$ control.

**Objective.** A construction: what $\mathcal C$ covers and what $W_6$ covers, so that the six-point sector of the cubic theory is a covering of $\mathcal M_{0,6}$ in Harold's sense (any gluing counts as a propagator; poles and moduli are not the discriminant). No-go only with the covering statement it would violate.

**Relation to 035's A2.** A2 is *not* used as a hypothesis of any step here, and the factorisation/analyticity falsifier of 035 is not invoked. It is not, however, an independent result: the entirety half of C3.2 runs through "the standard measure is a smooth form whose momentum dependence enters through $e^{ik\cdot X}$ correlators", which is A2's standard-node-insertion content relocated into the qualifier *with the standard measure* of the conclusion. The negative result is scoped by that qualifier, not independent of it. Used from R39: the calculus $\hat d=S-6$, $\hat d_B=6-S_B$, additivity, inverse lemma; degree argument (iv) only as context.

**Conventions.** The project conventions: physical ghost number, $N_{\rm gh}(\Psi)=2$, $N_{\rm gh}(\Sigma_2)=4$, $N_{\rm gh}(p_{2,2})=+2$; $\mathcal V_n(A_1,\dots,A_n)=\langle A_1\otimes A_2,p_{2,2}(A_3,A_4)\rangle$ at $n=4$ (Harold's notes-new); $\Sigma_2\in\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0/\ker p_{2,2}$. Real dimensions throughout. $\mathcal V_n$ denotes both the vertex region $\mathcal V_{0,n}\subset\mathcal M_{0,n}$ and the multilinear form $\int_{\mathcal V_{0,n}}\Omega$.

## 1. CKVs, $c$-insertions and negative dimension (17.1)

\subsection*{Setup}
Uses A1, A3. Genus-0 surface with $n$ punctures; $n_b$ = number of antighost insertions (one per integrated real modulus, $\int dm\,b(v_m)$), $n_c$ = number of $c$-insertions beyond the vertex operators (one per real direction of the residual automorphism group, needed to soak the $c$ zero modes). Legs carry total ghost number $S$. Reference for the CKV/zero-mode statement: Erbin's book, `bos_path_integral_closed_vacuum.tex` §"Conformal Killing vectors" (zero modes $\xi^{(0)}$ of $P_1$; dimension $2n-6$ after fixing punctures); Witten, arXiv:1209.5461, footnote 3: the measure $F(g|\delta g)$ vanishes on a surface with CKVs because $c$ then has zero modes. Witten's paper contains no treatment of unstable components beyond that footnote and the super-analogue (l. 1973–1977); nothing more is attributed to it here.

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(1.1) The anomaly identity.* On a connected genus-0 surface the ghost-number anomaly is $6$, so a non-vanishing correlator has
$$
S - n_b + n_c = 6 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \hat d := S-6 = n_b - n_c .
\tag{1.1}
$$
For $n\ge3$ physical legs ($S=2n$), $n_c=0$ and $n_b=2n-6=\dim_\R\mathcal M_{0,n}$: the $-6$ is $\dim_\R\mathrm{PSL}(2,\C)$, the three punctures it fixes costing the naive $2n$ six real parameters. For $n=2$: $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\C)$ fixes both punctures with four parameters and the residual $\C^*$ ($z\mapsto\lambda z$) is two real: $n_c=2$, $n_b=0$, $\hat d=-2$. In general $\hat d=n_b-n_c=\dim(\text{moduli})-\dim(\text{residual CKV})$ is the Riemann–Roch virtual dimension $h^1-h^0$ of $T_\Sigma(-\sum p_i)$, $=2n-6$ real for all $n\ge0$ at genus 0. This is the content of R39's $\hat d=S-6$: **the calculus counts $b$ minus $c$, and $c$-insertions are the CKV directions.** [C1.1]

*(1.2) Harold's example, made exact.* $\mathcal V_2(\Psi,\Psi)=\omega_1(\Psi,Q_B\Psi)=\langle\Psi,c_0^-Q_B\Psi\rangle$: $n_c=2$ ($c_0^-$ and the $c$ of $Q_B$), $\hat d=-2$. The residual $\C^*$ of the two-punctured sphere $(0,\infty)$ acts on the pair of local coordinates $(w_1,w_2)=(z,1/z)$ as $(\lambda z,1/z)$, i.e. on the sewing relation $w_1w_2=\lambda$. The plumbing fixture that defines the propagator is $w_1w_2=q$, $q=e^{-s+i\theta}$, $|q|<1$: **the plumbing parameter $q$ is a point of the residual $\C^*$**, the propagator integrates the half $|q|\le1$ of that orbit ($s\ge0$; $s=0$ is the identity sewing, and A2 is what makes the other half unnecessary), and its two $b$-insertions $b_0^+b_0^-$ (from $\int ds\,e^{-sL_0^+}b_0^+$, $\int d\theta\,e^{i\theta L_0^-}b_0^-$) replace the two $c$-insertions of $Q_B$. Inversion converts the unfixed automorphism group into moduli, $c\to b$, $\hat d=-2\to\hat d_B=+2$ (R39 inverse lemma). [C1.2]

*(1.3) The $4k-6$ count.* $\Sigma_k\in\mathrm{Sym}^k\mathcal H$ has $N_{\rm gh}=2k$; its kinetic form $\mathcal V_{2k}(\Sigma_k,\Sigma_k)$ is the $2k$-punctured sphere with $S=4k$, so by (1.1)
$$
\hat d(\mathcal V_{2k}(\Sigma_k,\Sigma_k))=4k-6=\dim_\R\mathcal M_{0,2k}\ ,\qquad <0\iff k=1 .
\tag{1.3}
$$
The reading $(n_b,n_c)=(4k-6,0)$ holds for $k\ge2$ only, where the $2k$-punctured sphere is stable. At $k=1$ the surface is the unstable two-punctured sphere and the correct reading is the one of (1.1)–(1.2), $(n_b,n_c)=(0,2)$: $\hat d=-2$ is a CKV deficit, not a negative number of antighosts. The sign flip at $k=1$ is exact: the physical field is the only member of the tower whose kinetic surface is unstable. [C1.3]

*(1.4) Harold's removal law.* Join $\mathcal V_a$ and $\mathcal V_b$ through a colour-$k$ line. By R39 additivity and the inverse lemma,
$$
\hat d(\mathcal V_a\,p_{k,k}^{-1}\,\mathcal V_b)=(2a-6)+(2b-6)-(4k-6)=2(a+b-2k)-6=\dim_\R\mathcal M_{0,a+b-2k},
\tag{1.4}
$$
so the line removes $R(k)=4k-6$ real moduli for every $(a,b)$: $R(1)=-2$ (adds the propagator's $(s,\theta)$), $R(2)=+2$, $R(3)=+6$. Independently verified (script check `C1.4`, all $2\le a,b\le 9$, $1\le k\le\min(a,b)$). (1.3) and (1.4) agree: $p_{k,k}^{-1}$ removes exactly the moduli of the surface it inverts. [C1.4]

*(1.5) Ghost arithmetic of a geometric $s$-strand gluing (the candidate test).* Let two connected genus-0 forms $\Omega_1,\Omega_2$ (here the two $\mathcal V_5$'s, $n_i=5$ punctures, $e_i=3$ external and $s=2$ sewing legs each) be glued by a *geometric* sewing object: a family of (possibly disconnected, possibly nodal) surfaces attached at the $2s$ sewing punctures, with $n_b$ integrated moduli and $n_c$ fixed directions soaked by $c$'s. Write $g$ for the genus of the glued surface, so that its anomaly is $6-6g$. Ghost-number conservation on the glued surface reads
$$
S_1+S_2-S_B-\big(n_{b,1}+n_{b,2}+n_b\big)+\big(n_{c,1}+n_{c,2}+n_c\big)=6-6g,
$$
and using (1.1) for each genus-0 piece ($S_i=6+n_{b,i}-n_{c,i}$),
$$
S_B=6+6g-n_b+n_c\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \hat d_B=6-S_B=n_b-n_c-6g .
\tag{1.5}
$$
This extends R39 (1.5) to $c$-insertions and shows that the $-6g$ is the anomaly of the handles the gluing creates. It is topological — but $g$ is **not** determined by $s$: it depends on the connectivity of the sewing object (1.7). Setting $g=s-1$, as an earlier version of this record did, is the special case "one component per strand". For $p_{2,2}^{-1}$: $S_B=8$ (R39 inverse lemma), $s=2$, hence
$$
n_b-n_c=6g-4+2=6g-2 .
\tag{1.6}
$$
[C1.5]

*(1.7) Genus from connectivity.* Let the sewing object have $C$ components of genera $g_i$, the $i$-th carrying $\ell_i$ of the $2s$ legs, $\sum_i\ell_i=2s$. The glued surface is built from $V=2+C$ components joined at $N=2s$ nodes; if the resulting dual graph is connected,
$$
g=\sum_ig_i+N-V+1=\sum_ig_i+2s-C-1 .
\tag{1.7}
$$
At $s=2$ with genus-0 components: $C=2$ (two cylinders, dual graph a 2-cycle) gives $g=1$; $C=1$ (one four-punctured sphere carrying all four legs, dual graph three vertices and four edges) gives $g=\mathbf 2$; $C=3$ gives $g=0$. This is R15's own Euler-characteristic bookkeeping — a length-$k$ chain of spheres joined by $k-1$ colour-2 edges has $g=k-1$, which is (1.7) with $C=k-1$ components of two legs each. [C1.8]

*(1.8) How many moduli a sewing object may carry.* The $b$-insertions of the whole configuration pair with tangent vectors of $\mathcal M_{g,6}$; more than $\dim_\R\mathcal M_{g,6}$ of them are linearly dependent and the form vanishes. The two vertex families contribute $\dim_\R\mathcal V_{0,5}=\dim_\R\mathcal M_{0,5}=4$ each, independently, so
$$
n_b\ \le\ n_b^{\max}=\dim_\R\mathcal M_{g,e_1+e_2}-\sum_{i=1,2}\dim_\R\mathcal M_{0,e_i+s}=\big(6g-6+2(e_1{+}e_2)\big)-\big(2(e_1{+}e_2)+4s-12\big)=6g+6-4s ,
\tag{1.8}
$$
independent of how the external legs $e_1,e_2$ are distributed; at $s=2$, $n_b^{\max}=6g-2$, i.e. $\dim_\R\mathcal M_{g,6}-8$. Counted directly on the sewing object instead — $2$ real plumbing parameters per node, plus the virtual dimension $6g_i-6+2\ell_i$ of each component (moduli minus automorphisms, valid for unstable components too) — the same number appears:
$$
n_b^{\max}=\sum_i\big(2\ell_i+6g_i-6+2\ell_i\big)=4N-6C+6\textstyle\sum_ig_i=8s-6C+6\sum_ig_i ,
$$
equal to $6g+6-4s$ by (1.7) for every $s$, $C$, $\{\ell_i\}$, $\{g_i\}$ (check `C1.6-bound`). The two cylinders give $4=2+2$ (each cylinder: two nodes, $2\times2$ real, minus the two of its residual $\C^*$), the connected four-punctured sphere gives $10=4\times2+\dim_\R\mathcal M_{0,4}$, and $\dim_\R\mathcal M_{1,6}=12=4+4+4$, $\dim_\R\mathcal M_{2,6}=18=4+4+10$. Combining (1.5) and (1.8),
$$
\boxed{S_B\ \ge\ 4s+n_c}\ ,
\tag{1.9}
$$
with equality iff $n_b$ saturates (1.8) and $n_c=0$ — a floor independent of $g$, of $C$ and of the components' genera. [C1.9]

## 2. The dimension arithmetic and the collapse (17.2)

\subsection*{Setup}
Uses A3, A4, C1.4–C1.6. Write $V_4(A,B)=\langle A,p_{2,2}B\rangle$ on $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0/\ker p_{2,2}$ (finite matrix by A3); $V_5(\cdot,\cdot,\Psi)$ the $\mathrm{Sym}^2$-valued map of R17; "$\mathcal V_a p^{-1}\mathcal V_b$" the contraction of one $\mathrm{Sym}^2$ slot of each form with $p_{2,2}^{-1}$.

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(2.1) Four points: what collapses.* The $\Sigma$-channel four-point kernel is (R14)
$$
\mathcal V_4\,p_{2,2}^{-1}\,\mathcal V_4:\quad \big\langle p_{2,2}(\Psi_1\Psi_2),\,p_{2,2}^{-1}\,p_{2,2}(\Psi_3\Psi_4)\big\rangle=\big\langle p_{2,2}(\Psi_1\Psi_2),\Psi_3\Psi_4\big\rangle=V_4(\Psi_1,\Psi_2,\Psi_3,\Psi_4).
\tag{2.1}
$$
The collapse is $p_{2,2}^{-1}p_{2,2}=\mathrm{id}$ on the quotient: the source coupling $V_4(\Sigma,\Psi^2)=\langle\Sigma,p_{2,2}\Psi^2\rangle$ carries the *same* kernel that the propagator inverts, and one whole copy of $\mathcal V_{0,4}$ is annihilated — not two of the four real directions of a product $\mathcal V_{0,4}\times\mathcal V_{0,4}$. *(2.6) The converse of the collapse criterion (added in the improvement pass, 2026-09-21).* "Collapse" is made precise first. A $\Sigma$ line joins two slot vectors $u,w\in Q:=\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0/\ker p_{2,2}$ (the $\mathrm{Sym}^2$ slots of the two vertices it connects, with the external states held fixed) through $V^{-1}$, $V:=p_{2,2}|_Q$, a symmetric invertible $n\times n$ matrix (A3). The line *collapses* if the contraction
$$
f(V)=u^\top V^{-1}w
\tag{2.6}
$$
equals a polynomial in the vertex data — an identity in the data, as (2.1) and (2.2) are — so that the diagram is a vertex of the target theory. The data are the vertex forms of $S_4$ itself — $\mathcal V_5$ and $W_6$ (R19), the latter a datum of $S_4$ and not a function of $V$ in this count — together with $V$. The vertices of $S_4$ through $O(g_s^4)$ then have slot vectors **affine in $V$**: $V$-independent for a genuine interaction vertex ($\mathcal V_5$, $W_6$, and the target $\mathcal V_n$ in general) and $Vy$ for the source vertex $p_{2,2}\Psi^2$ ($y=\Psi^2$); a chain of such lines through $\mathcal V_5$ insertions (the expansion of $K_\Psi^{-1}$, $K_\Psi=V_4-\tfrac{g_s}5V_5(\cdot,\cdot,\Psi)$, R17) is covered line by line. Write therefore
$$
u=u_0+Vy,\qquad w=w_0+Vz,\qquad u_0,w_0,y,z\ \text{independent of }V .
\tag{2.7}
$$
**Proposition 2.6.** For ends of the form (2.7), $f(V)$ is a polynomial in the entries of $V$ if and only if $u_0=0$ or $w_0=0$, i.e. **iff at least one end is a pure source vertex.**

*Proof.* Expanding (2.7),
$$
f(V)=u_0^\top V^{-1}w_0+y^\top w_0+u_0^\top z+y^\top Vz ,
\tag{2.8}
$$
and the last three terms are polynomial, so $f$ is polynomial iff $u_0^\top V^{-1}w_0$ is. With $V^{-1}=\mathrm{adj}(V)/\det V$, the latter is polynomial iff $\det V$ divides $N(V):=u_0^\top\mathrm{adj}(V)\,w_0$ in the polynomial ring of the $n(n+1)/2$ independent entries of $V$. $N$ is homogeneous of degree $n-1$ and $\det V$ of degree $n$, so divisibility forces $N\equiv0$. Now $N\equiv0$ as a polynomial means $u_0^\top M w_0=0$ for every symmetric invertible $M$ (take $M=V^{-1}$; the invertible symmetric matrices are Zariski-dense), hence for every symmetric $M$ by continuity. Taking $M=e_ie_i^\top$ gives $u_{0,i}w_{0,i}=0$ for all $i$, and $M=e_ie_j^\top+e_je_i^\top$ gives $u_{0,i}w_{0,j}+u_{0,j}w_{0,i}=0$; if some $u_{0,i}\neq0$ then $w_{0,i}=0$ and $w_{0,j}=-u_{0,j}w_{0,i}/u_{0,i}=0$ for every $j$, so $w_0=0$. Conversely $u_0=0$ or $w_0=0$ makes (2.8) polynomial. $\square$

*Remarks.* (a) Irreducibility of the symmetric determinant is **not** needed for affine ends: the degree count $n-1<n$ suffices. It is what one would need for ends of higher polynomial degree in $V$ (none occur in $S_4$ through $O(g_s^4)$), and it holds ($\det$ of a generic symmetric matrix is irreducible for $n\ge2$; script `C2.6-irred` confirms it at $n=2,3,4$). (b) Viewed from the *target* theory instead, $W_6=\mathcal V_6-\tfrac65\mathcal C$ has a slot vector containing $V^{-1}$ through $\mathcal C$. That viewpoint cannot be combined with definition (2.6) in one sentence: a line joining $W_6$ to a *source* end still telescopes, $y^\top VV^{-1}w=y^\top w$, rational through $W_6$ itself — this is what (3.1)(b) uses when the three source–$W_6$ lines of $W_6(\Sigma^3)$ are collapsed — while a line joining $W_6$ to a non-source end is not even rational of degree $-1$ in $V$ (under $V\to tV$ the contraction $u_0^\top(tV)^{-1}(w_0+t^{-1}\tilde w)$ has separate $t^{-1}$ and $t^{-2}$ parts: the scaling argument of R28′/R39 (iv)); such lines first occur at $O(g_s^5)$. With $S_4$'s forms as the data, as above, $W_6$'s slot is affine and the proposition covers both cases verbatim. The earlier sentence "a line ending on $W_6$ never collapses" is withdrawn. (c) The proposition is an identity in the data, uniform in $V$ — exactly the sense in which (2.1) and (2.2) are collapses. At the actual $V_4$ a number can always be rewritten; the actual-data content is (2.9) below. (d) On the quotient $Q$ the pure source end is $\Pi y$, so the "if" direction at five points needs $\Pi\mathcal V_5\Pi=\mathcal V_5$ (R38), as (2.2) records; the "only if" direction does not need R38: it is a statement about $u_0,w_0\in Q$ whatever their origin.

R19 (2)'s "independent invariants" is the six-point instance of Proposition 2.6, no longer an import. [C2.6]

*(2.7) The converse at the actual data.* Proposition 2.6 is uniform in $V$. \tag{2.9}

## 3. Reconciliation with R19 and the covering statement (17.3)

*(3.5) The third member, part one: momentum-dependent regions of sector type (added in the improvement pass, 2026-09-21).* Option (ii) of (3.2) is a family of real six-chains $\Gamma(P)\subset\mathcal M_{0,6}$, depending on the external momenta, integrated against the standard measure $\Omega(P)$. What singularities in $P$ can such an integral have? Two sources only: the compact part of the chain, and its ends at the degeneration divisors. Fix the level $L$ of the intermediate $\Psi$ sum, so that every object is meromorphic in $P^2$ with finitely many poles per collar.

**Class.** (a) $\Gamma(P)$ has bounded multiplicity (a region, or a signed region, counted at most $M$ times); (b) its dependence on $P$ is real-analytic, $\Gamma(P)=\phi_P(\Gamma_0)$ for a real-analytic family of maps — the minimal requirement for "$\int_{\Gamma(P)}\Omega$" to admit the analytic continuation in $P$ that $\mathcal C$ has, $\mathcal C^{(L)}$ being meromorphic by (2.9); (c) in the plumbing collar of each degeneration, with coordinate $q=re^{i\theta}$ and the remaining moduli $m'$, the chain is of **sector type**: $\Gamma\cap\{\text{collar}\}=\{(q,m'):\ \theta\in\Theta(m',P),\ r<\epsilon(\theta,m',P)\}$ with $\epsilon$ real-analytic and $0<\epsilon_{\min}\le\epsilon\le\epsilon_{\max}$ on its angular support. (c) is what the Feynman regions themselves satisfy ($\Theta=[0,2\pi)$, $\epsilon$ the stub length), and what "a region whose boundary reaches a degeneration transversally" means.

**Proposition 3.5.** In this class, $\int_{\Gamma(P)}\Omega(P)$ is meromorphic in $P_I^2$ with poles only on the **plumbing lattice**
$$
P_I^2=-\tfrac{2}{\alpha'}\big(N_\phi+\bar N_\phi-2\big)=:-\mu_\phi^2,\qquad (N_\phi,\bar N_\phi)\in\mathbb N^2,
\tag{3.4}
$$
of the channels whose collars $\Gamma$ reaches — the physical mass shells $m_\phi^2=\tfrac4{\alpha'}(N_\phi-1)$ (the even sublattice $N_\phi=\bar N_\phi$) when $\Theta=[0,2\pi)$, the full lattice, of spacing $2/\alpha'$, for partial angular support. Consequently, **under H2a — a pole of $\mathcal C^{(L)}$ off the plumbing lattice — $\mathcal C$ is not a sector-type momentum-dependent region with the standard measure**, anywhere in $\mathcal M_{0,6}$.

*Proof.* Split $\Gamma=\Gamma_K+\Gamma_\infty$ with $\Gamma_K$ in a compact $K\subset\mathcal M_{0,6}$ and $\Gamma_\infty$ in the collars. On $K$ the integrand is real-analytic in $(m,P)$ jointly (a correlator of $e^{ik\cdot X}$ insertions on a surface away from all degenerations, 035 Prop. 2.1's easy half) and $\int_{\Gamma_K(P)}\Omega(P)=\int_{\Gamma_0}\phi_P^*\Omega(P)$ is an integral of an analytic family over a fixed compact chain, hence real-analytic in $P$: no singularity at any real $P_0$. In a collar, the plumbing expansion of the integrand is $\sum_\phi r^{2h_\phi-1}e^{i\theta(h_\phi^{\rm L}-h_\phi^{\rm R})}c_\phi(m',P)\,dr\,d\theta\,dm'$, the sum running over the full Fock space of the channel (level matching is imposed by the $\theta$ integral only when $\Theta$ is the full circle), with $2h_\phi:=h^{\rm L}_\phi+h^{\rm R}_\phi=\tfrac{\alpha'}2(P_I^2+\mu_\phi^2)$ on the states of levels $(N_\phi,\bar N_\phi)$ — $\mu_\phi^2$ as in (3.4), equal to $m_\phi^2$ iff $N_\phi=\bar N_\phi$ — and $c_\phi$ analytic (products of lower vertex correlators); for a sector-type chain the radial integral is
$$
\int_0^{\epsilon(\theta,m',P)}r^{2h_\phi-1}\,dr=\frac{\epsilon(\theta,m',P)^{2h_\phi}}{2h_\phi}=\frac{e^{2h_\phi\ln\epsilon}}{2h_\phi},
\tag{3.5}
$$
convergent for $h_\phi>0$ and continued by the right-hand side. With $\ln\epsilon$ analytic and bounded (by (c)), the numerator is entire in $P$ and the only pole is $h_\phi=0$, i.e. $P_I^2=-\mu_\phi^2$, a point of the lattice (3.4). The remaining integrals over $\theta\in\Theta$ and $m'$ are over compact sets with analytic integrands and change nothing, except that $\int_\Theta e^{i(N_\phi-\bar N_\phi)\theta}d\theta$ vanishes for $N_\phi\ne\bar N_\phi$ iff $\Theta$ is the full circle, which is what restricts the pole set to the physical shells in that case. Finitely many $\phi$ at fixed level: meromorphic, poles on the plumbing lattice only. $\square$ (The pole set was misstated as "the mass shells" in the improvement pass; the proof was unchanged by the correction.)

*Scope of the exclusion.* The class is (a)–(c). Its physical reason: (a) is what "region" means; (b) is forced by the analyticity of $\mathcal C$; (c) is the way every region of the standard covering meets a degeneration. Relevant cases outside it: none for (a)–(b) — unbounded multiplicity or non-analytic boundaries make $\int_\Gamma\Omega$ a different kind of object from $\mathcal C$, a formal chain with momentum-dependent coefficients (R39's class with $c_\alpha(P)$), which is the density reading; **one for (c)**, treated in (3.6). Weakest relaxation that evades it: dropping the lower bound $\epsilon\ge\epsilon_{\min}$ in (c). With $\epsilon\to0$ on part of the angular support the numerator $e^{2h\ln\epsilon}$ is no longer entire, and the singularities that appear are of a new type and need not be poles — the subject of (3.6). [C3.5]

*(3.6) The third member, part two: cusp-type regions, a construction.* Let the chain touch the divisor in a cusp: on an angular interval $[\theta_0,\theta_0+\delta]$ take $\epsilon(\theta)=c\,(\theta-\theta_0)^k$ with $k>0$, so that the region is pinched between the ray $\theta=\theta_0$ and the curve $r=c(\theta-\theta_0)^k$, both through $q=0$ with the same tangent. Then (3.5) becomes, for the level-$\phi$ term,
$$
\int_{\theta_0}^{\theta_0+\delta}\frac{\big(c(\theta-\theta_0)^k\big)^{2h}}{2h}\,d\theta=\frac{c^{2h}\,\delta^{2hk+1}}{2h\,(2hk+1)} ,
\tag{3.6}
$$
which has, besides the lattice pole $h=0$, **a pole at $2hk=-1$**, i.e. at $P_I^2=-\mu_\phi^2-\tfrac{2}{\alpha'k}=-\tfrac{2}{\alpha'}\big(N_\phi+\bar N_\phi-2+\tfrac1k\big)$ — on the plumbing lattice (3.4) iff $1/k\in\mathbb Z$. For $k=1$ it is the lattice point $N+\bar N+1$ (for the tachyon, $P^2=2/\alpha'$, the $(N,\bar N)=(1,0)$ pole a partial sector already produces): not a distinguishing example. For $k=2$ it is $P_I^2=-\mu_\phi^2-1/\alpha'$, midway between two lattice points (for the tachyon, $P^2=3/\alpha'$): the flagship case. The position is free: a cusp of exponent $k(P)$, real-analytic in $P$, puts a simple pole of the region integral at any prescribed $P_0^2<-\mu_\phi^2$ ($k>0$ needs $P_0^2+\mu_\phi^2<0$; with the tachyon, $\mu^2=m^2=-4/\alpha'$, this is $P_0^2<4/\alpha'$), with residue $\propto c^{2h}\delta^{\,0}/(2h\cdot 2k)$ at the pole times the level-$\phi$ coefficient $c_\phi(m',P)$. So a region with the standard measure *can* carry a simple pole at a zero of $\det V_4$ off the lattice: **the third member is not excluded**. What part one shows is only that any singularity off the lattice requires $\epsilon\to0$ somewhere on the angular support; the power cusp is the profile that yields a simple pole at a prescribed position, and other profiles give other singularities — an exponential one, $\epsilon=c\,e^{-1/(\theta-\theta_0)}$, gives $\int_0^\delta e^{-2h/t}dt=\delta\,E_2(2h/\delta)$, a logarithmic branch point at $h=0$ and no pole (numerically: $(I-\delta)/(h\ln h)\to2$, one-off run, 2026-09-21). "Survives in cusp form only", as the improvement pass wrote, was therefore wrong on two counts: partial sectors already leave the physical shells, and non-power profiles leave the class without producing poles. (Script `C3.6`: the cusp integral computed by tanh–sinh quadrature over $\theta$ grows like $1/(2hk+1)$ near $2hk=-1$ and matches (3.6); `C3.6-break`: the sector integral (3.5) with the same pole detector reports no pole there.)

What is *not* constructed: equality with $\mathcal C$. A cusp region reproduces the pole *position* of one zero of $\det V_4^{(L)}$ in one channel; to equal $\mathcal C^{(L)}$ it must reproduce (i) every zero of $\det V_4^{(L)}(P)$ — a hypersurface in the momenta of the two triples, not a function of one channel momentum $P_I^2$ alone unless $V_4$ depends on the pair states only through $P_I$; (ii) the residue $(u_0\!\cdot\!N)(N\!\cdot\!w_0)/(N^\top\partial V_4N)$ at each, whose rank in (left data)$\times$(right data) is the null-space dimension — one at a simple zero, the multiplicity at a semisimple zero — against a cusp residue of rank $\dim(\text{level }\phi)$; and (iii) analyticity everywhere else. (i)–(iii) is a named computation: **residue matching of $\mathcal C^{(L)}$ against cusp data on the zero locus of $\det V_4^{(L)}$**, a refinement of R39's condition (v) (residue factorisation), decidable at fixed level once the zero locus of 16b is known. Its most likely outcome is negative on (i) alone, but that is an expectation, not a result. And a cusp region is, integrated against $\Omega$, the same thing as the full sector integrated against the indicator weight $\chi_{\text{cusp}}(m;P)$ — by definition of an indicator, true of any subset of any set, so this is a reclassification and not a result: in its surviving form, option (ii) is an instance of option (iii) with a $\{0,1\}$-valued, momentum-dependent, boundary-supported weight. The trichotomy of (3.2) thus reads, by definition, as a dichotomy — momentum-independent region (excluded under H2a), density (adopted) — with the cusp region as a drawable representative of the density class. [C3.6]

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(4.1) The cubic rewriting.* Hubbard–Stratonovich on the quartic, $\tfrac{\lambda_4}{4!}\phi^4=\tfrac{\lambda_4}{4!}(2\sigma\phi^2-\sigma^2)+\tfrac{\lambda_4}{4!}(\sigma-\phi^2)^2$, and replacement of $\phi^5\to\sigma^2\phi$, $\phi^6\to\sigma^3$:

\tag{4.1}
$$
at most cubic in $(\phi,\sigma)$ — the scalar image of $S_4$ (R19). The $\sigma$ "propagator" is $K^{-1}=(\lambda_4/12)^{-1}$, momentum-independent: no pole, no modulus.

*(4.2) Integrating $\sigma$ out at tree level.* $\partial_\sigma V_{\rm cub}=0$ gives $\sigma^*=\phi^2+\delta$, $\delta=\tfrac{\lambda_5}{5\lambda_4}\phi^3+O(\phi^4)$, and

$$
The $\phi^6$ feedback $\lambda_5^2/(600\lambda_4)$ is the scalar image of R17's $\tfrac{g_s^4}{600}vK_\Psi^{-1}v$ — same coefficient — and it is cancelled by
$$
\boxed{\lambda_6'=\lambda_6-\tfrac65\,\frac{\lambda_5^2}{\lambda_4}}\qquad(\theta=6/5,\ \text{R19's value}).
\tag{4.3}
$$
Orders $\phi^3,\phi^4,\phi^5$ are reproduced exactly (the source lines telescope, $\lambda_4\lambda_4^{-1}\lambda_4=\lambda_4$); the $\phi^7$ feedback is non-zero and needs the next auxiliary (scalar image of the tower). [C4.1]

\subsection*{Commentary}
(A.3) is not a continuation of the stable formula $\dim_\C\mathcal M_{0,n}=n-3$: at $n\le2$ it is computed from the two cohomology groups separately, (A.2) or the direct count, and the value $-1$ at $n=2$ is $h^0$ of an honest vector-field space. The stable dimension is the special case $h^0=0$. Only the difference $h^1-h^0$ is affine in $n$; the individual $h^i$ are not, which is why no source that works with $\mathcal M_{0,n}$ (stable, $h^0=0$) states the $n=2$ value.

*Why $n=0,1$ never enter the calculus.* The formula holds there ($\hat d=-6,-4$; with $S=0,2$ they satisfy $\hat d=S-6$, the six resp. four extra $c$'s being $2h^0$). They do not occur because every object the calculus is applied to — the kinetic form ($n=2$), the vertices ($n\ge3$), the sewing objects of 030 §1.3 and their gluings — has at least two legs per component: closed SFT around a conformal background has no linear term ($\mathcal V_1=0$ at genus 0, the background solving the equation of motion), the $n=0$ sphere is a field-independent constant, and gluing through a bivector removes two legs from forms that carry $\ge2$ further legs each. This is a statement about the objects used, not a restriction of (A.3).

$$
\boxed{\ \mathrm{vd}_\R(0,n)=2(h^1-h^0)=2n-6\quad(n\ge0),\qquad (h^0,h^1)_{n=2}=(1,0),\quad\mathrm{vd}_\R(0,2)=-2\ }
$$

### A.2 $n=2$: reading as $n_b-n_c$, and the cylinder

\subsection*{Setup}
A1 (ghost bookkeeping, sphere anomaly 6), the project conventions: $c_0^\pm=\tfrac12(c_0\pm\bar c_0)$, $b_0^\pm=b_0\pm\bar b_0$, $L_0^\pm=L_0\pm\bar L_0$; mode expansion $c(z)=\sum_mc_mz^{-m+1}$.

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(A.4) Ghost zero modes are the cohomology.* The $c$-mode $c_m$ multiplies $z^{1-m}$, i.e. it is the ghost coordinate dual to the vector field $z^{1-m}\partial_z$; $c_1,c_0,c_{-1}$ are dual to $\partial_z,z\partial_z,z^2\partial_z$, the $n=0$ row of the table. An unintegrated vertex operator $c\bar cV$ at $p_i$ soaks the ghost direction of the vector fields *not* vanishing at $p_i$. The $c$'s needed beyond the $n$ vertex operators are therefore dual to $H^0(T(-D))\oplus\overline{H^0(T(-D))}$, and the $b$'s paired with Beltrami differentials are dual to $H^1(T(-D))\oplus\overline{H^1}$:
$$
n_c=2h^0(T(-D)),\qquad n_b=2h^1(T(-D)) .
\tag{A.4}
$$
With (A.2): $(n_b,n_c)=(2n-6,0)$ for $n\ge3$ and $(0,2)$ at $n=2$ — the two readings of 036 (1.1), which (A.4) now fixes individually, not only as the difference. The anomaly identity is then RR itself: $2n-n_b+n_c=6\iff h^0-h^1=3-n=\deg T(-D)+1$, the $2n$ vertex-operator $c$'s being the $-D$ twist counted in both chiral halves; (1.1) $\hat d=S-6=n_b-n_c=2\,\mathrm{vd}_\C$.

*(A.5) The kinetic surface.* Put the two punctures at $0,\infty$. $c\bar cV(0)$ acting on the vacuum gives $c_1\bar c_1$; the BPZ conjugate at $\infty$ gives $c_{-1}\bar c_{-1}$; the zero modes left over are $c_0,\bar c_0$, i.e. $c_0^\pm$, dual to $H^0(T(-D))=\C z\partial_z$ and its conjugate — in real terms the generators $L_0^+$ (dilation, $z\partial_z+\bar z\partial_{\bar z}$) and $L_0^-$ (rotation) of the residual $\C^*$. The kinetic form supplies exactly these: $\mathcal V_2(\Psi,\Psi)=\langle\Psi,c_0^-Q_B\Psi\rangle$ with $Q_B=c_0^+L_0^++c_0^-L_0^-+\dots$ (from $c_0L_0+\bar c_0\bar L_0=c_0^+L_0^++c_0^-L_0^-$), net $n_c=2$, $n_b=0$, $\hat d(Q_B)=-2=2\,\mathrm{vd}_\C(0,2)$.

*(A.6) The cylinder.* Sewing $w_1w_2=q$ adds one complex parameter $q\in\C^*$, $|q|\le1$, $q=e^{-s+i\theta}$ (Erbin 2023, `bos:eq:q-s-th`), and the propagator carries its two $b$'s: $\mathsf P=\int ds\,d\theta\,b_0^+b_0^-e^{-sL_0^++i\theta L_0^-}$, $(n_b,n_c)=(2,0)$, $\hat d_B(\mathsf P)=+2$. The parameter $q$ is a point of the group generated by $H^0(T(-D))$ at $n=2$ (036 (1.2)); the $b_0^\pm$ are dual to the same $L_0^\pm$ as the $c_0^\pm$ of (A.5). So $\hat d_B(\text{cylinder})=-2\,\mathrm{vd}_\C(0,2)=+2$: the automorphism directions of the unstable sphere are the moduli of the node.

\subsection*{Commentary}
(A.4): the dictionary "$c$ zero modes $\leftrightarrow$ CKVs, $b$ zero modes $\leftrightarrow$ quadratic differentials" is the standard one (Polchinski vol. 1 §5.2–5.3, from memory; the book's CKV section, 036 §1 Setup); what is added is the twist by $-D$, which is exactly what unintegrated vertex operators do, and the explicit mode check at $n=2$ in (A.5). (A.6) needs no geometry beyond the plumbing parametrisation, which is sourced. The inverse lemma of R39, $\hat d_B(\Delta)=-\hat d(Q_B)$, is here an output: the $-2$ from $h^0$ at $n=2$ and the $+2$ from the node parameter are computed independently and agree with it.

\subsection*{Result}
$$
\boxed{\ \hat d(\Sigma_{0,2})=2\,\mathrm{vd}_\C(0,2)=-2\ \ [(n_b,n_c)=(0,2),\ c_0^\pm\leftrightarrow z\partial_z],\qquad \hat d_B(\text{cylinder})=-2\,\mathrm{vd}_\C(0,2)=+2\ \ [(n_b,n_c)=(2,0),\ b_0^\pm]\ }
$$

### A.3 Additivity under sewing

\subsection*{Setup}
Components $\Sigma_v=(g_v,n_v)$, $v=1,\dots,V$, sewn at $N$ nodes (each node identifies two punctures, one plumbing parameter), dual graph connected (disconnected: see the Commentary). The sewn surface has
$$
g=\sum_vg_v+N-V+1,\qquad n=\sum_vn_v-2N .
\tag{A.7}
$$

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(A.8) Topological Euler characteristic.* Let $\chi_{\rm top}(\Sigma\setminus D)=2-2g-n$. By (Deg), $\deg T_\Sigma(-D)=\chi_{\rm top}(\Sigma\setminus D)$. Cutting along the $N$ sewing circles ($\chi(S^1)=0$) and using Mayer–Vietoris, or directly from (A.7):
$$
\sum_v(2-2g_v-n_v)=2V-2\Big(g-N+V-1\Big)-\big(n+2N\big)=2-2g-n=\chi_{\rm top}(\Sigma\setminus D) .
\tag{A.8}
$$
*(A.9) Virtual dimension.* RR gives $\mathrm{vd}_\C=-\chi(T(-D))=-\chi_{\rm top}+g-1$ at every genus. With (A.7)–(A.8):
$$
\mathrm{vd}_\C(\Sigma)=-\sum_v\chi_{{\rm top},v}+\sum_vg_v+N-V=\sum_v\mathrm{vd}_\C(\Sigma_v)+N .
\tag{A.9}
$$
Each node adds one complex parameter, $+1=-\mathrm{vd}_\C(0,2)$: a node counts as the inverse of the two-punctured sphere. Consistency: inserting a $\Sigma_{0,2}$ component between two punctures (two nodes instead of one) changes $\mathrm{vd}$ by $-1+2=+1$, the same as one node — $\Sigma_{0,2}$, the component with $\chi_{\rm top}=0$, is the unit for sewing up to one node.

*(A.10) The sphere-equivalent count.* $\hat d:=2\,\mathrm{vd}_\C-6g=2n-6$ (036 (1.1), (1.5); R39's "handles at $-6$"). From (A.9) and (A.7):
$$
\hat d(\Sigma)=\sum_v\hat d(\Sigma_v)+2N-6L,\qquad L=N-V+1\ \ (\text{independent loops}) .
\tag{A.10}
$$
On trees ($L=0$) this is R39's additivity with $\hat d_B(\text{cylinder})=+2$ per node; each loop costs $-6$, which is 036 (1.5) ($\hat d_B=n_b-n_c-6g$) with (1.7) ($g$ = loops of the dual graph for genus-0 pieces).

\subsection*{Commentary}
(A.8) is the only sewing input, and it is topology, not a moduli-space statement; (A.9) then follows from RR alone. Zwiebach 1993 (hep-th/9206084) is not cached, so no sewing theorem is cited: (A.9) is derived here. For a disconnected configuration (forms on several components, e.g. $G_2=\omega_1^{\otimes2}$), the same formula holds with $L=N-V+1$ allowed negative: a disjoint union of two spheres has $L=-1$ and $\hat d=\sum\hat d_v+6$, which is $S-6$ with $S=\sum_v(\hat d_v+6)$ — the anomaly is 6 per component.
*What the closure relations of 035 C1.3 test.* $2-2+2$, $2-2+4-2+2$, $4-2+4$ involve the vertices $n=4,5$ (stable) and $p_{2,2}^{-1}$, whose $-2$ is the inverse lemma applied to the stable $\Sigma_{0,4}$; they test (A.10) at stable $n$ and the inverse lemma, **not** the unstable values. The relations that do test PH20 are those with a node or a $Q_B$: $\hat d(\mathcal V_3\Delta\mathcal V_3)=0+2+0=2=2\,\mathrm{vd}_\C(0,4)$ (a tree with one node), $\hat d(Q_B)+\hat d_B(\Delta)=0$, and the table rows 1–9, 10 ($g\ge1$), 13, 15 of 030 §1.3 (A.4 below). The ledger text of PH20 ("additivity then closes on … $2-2+2$, $2-2+4-2+2$, $4-2+4$") overstates what those three relations test.

\subsection*{Result}
$$
\boxed{\ \mathrm{vd}_\C\Big(\bigcup_v\Sigma_v\Big/\text{$N$ nodes}\Big)=\sum_v\mathrm{vd}_\C(\Sigma_v)+N,\qquad \hat d=\sum_v\hat d_v+2N-6L\ }
$$
equivalent to the additivity of $\chi_{\rm top}=\deg T(-D)$ under sewing (A.8).
