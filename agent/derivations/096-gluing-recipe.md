## Task and objective

**Literal task.** Given an explicit quartic vertex – a section of local coordinates $f_i(Z)$, $i=1,\dots,4$, over $\mathcal V_{0,4}$ – give a construction, valid off shell, of (a) an asymmetric cubic vertex (the half) and (b) a plumbing operator over $Q_V$ with its antighost insertions, such that gluing two halves with the operator reproduces $\mathcal V_4$ on its vertex region off shell, i.e. the actual $p_{2,2}$.

**Motivating objective.** A paragraph of the paper's worldsheet section explaining, at its true strength, how one would do this for a given explicit vertex, with the split between "cubic vertex" and "plumbing operator" and the freedom in it.

**Known inputs (reused, not re-derived).** R80 (fixed-halves gluing exact on shell over $Q_V$; the region data independent of the halves' parameters), R91 (rigid halves not seam-consistent off shell), R86 (the circle states move with $q$; the dressing equation `eq:dressing`; the law for Möbius germs), R92 (Möbius case: at each $q$ the circle state is the rigid $\gamma'=\beta$ state at $(\lambda(q),\overline{\lambda(q)})$ up to $|\kappa(q)|^{L_0^+}$), R2 (special coordinate, $Z(q)$).

## S1 — Conventions; forced versus chosen

\subsection*{Setup}
A1–A3. The quartic vertex is given as a section: for each $Z\in\mathcal V_{0,4}$ four local coordinates $f_i(\cdot\,;Z)$, germs of injective maps from a disc $|W|<1$ into the sphere with coordinate $\zeta$ and punctures at $(0,1,\infty,Z)$, $f_i(0)=$ puncture $i$. The quartic form on four inputs is $\mathcal V_4(A_1,\dots,A_4)=\int_{\mathcal V_{0,4}}\langle\,B\,B\,\prod_if_i\circ A_i(0)\rangle$ with the two Schiffer antighosts of the section's tangent vectors. The asymmetric cubic vertex is R2's $\mathcal V_3'$: a three-punctured sphere with two ordinary punctures carrying coordinates $h_1,h_2$ and a special puncture carrying the Möbius coordinate $w$ ($u=1/w$), in whose frame the ordinary punctures sit at $u_{1,2}=\pm z$.

\subsection*{Derivation}
*Step 1 – the frame.* The two halves glued by $w_Aw_B=q$ give a four-punctured sphere with global coordinate $u=1/w_A$ and punctures $u_{1,2}=\pm z$, $u_{3,4}=\pm2\beta/q$; its cross-ratio is $Z(q)$ (R2). The map $N_q:u\mapsto\zeta$ with $N_q(u_1,u_2,u_3)=(0,1,\infty)$ (089 (1.2)) identifies the glued sphere with the section's sphere at $Z(q)$, and $N_q(u_4)=Z(q)$ holds (089 (1.3)). Both frames are fixed: the section's by its three normalised punctures, the glued one by the half's frame (special puncture at $u=\infty$, $u_{1,2}=\pm z$). Hence the local coordinates of $\mathcal V_4$ at the four external punctures, read in the glued frame, are
$$
F_i^{(u)}(W;q):=N_q^{-1}\circ f_i\big(W;Z(q)\big),\qquad F_i^{(u)}(0;q)=u_i,\qquad q\in Q_V, \tag{1.1}
$$
with no freedom: $Q_V\ni q\mapsto Z(q)$ is a bijection onto $\mathcal V_{0,4}$ up to measure zero (R80 C1.2), and $N_q$ is the unique Möbius identification. The only freedom that exists at this stage is the half's own: its special coordinate $w$ (a Möbius function determined by its 2-jet at the special puncture, R82), which fixes the circle $|w|=\mathrm{const}$ and hence $u$; a change of it is a reparametrisation of $q$ together with a Möbius change of $u$, and is fixed once and for all by taking R2's half. We do not vary it.

*Step 2 – the split.* Choose for each ordinary puncture a $q$-independent coordinate $h_i$ with $h_i(0)=u_i$ (the half's coordinate at that puncture) and define
$$
g_i(q):=h_i^{-1}\circ F_i^{(u)}(\cdot\,;q),\qquad g_i(0;q)=0,\qquad F_i^{(u)}\circ A_i=h_i\circ\big(U_{g_i(q)}A_i\big), \tag{1.2}
$$
the last equality by A3. The state on the special leg of half $A$ at $q$,
$$
\Phi_2(A_1,A_2;q)=\Phi_2^{h}\big(U_{g_1(q)}A_1,\,U_{g_2(q)}A_2\big), \tag{1.3}
$$
is the circle state of R86/R92 written through the fixed half $h=(h_1,h_2)$ and two input operators. (1.2)–(1.3) hold for every admissible $h_i$: the split is exact and not unique.

*Step 3 – forced versus chosen.* Forced by the inputs: the surface $\Sigma(q)$ and its region $Q_V$ (R80, independent of the halves' ordinary coordinates); the frame and the positions (Step 1); the germs (1.1); the measure $\mathrm ds\,\mathrm d\theta$ with its normalisation (A2); the total antighost insertion of each tangent vector $\partial_s,\partial_\theta$ of the section, as a contour integral on $\Sigma(q)$. Chosen: (i) the half coordinates $h_i$ (C2.3 gives the canonical choice); (ii) consequently the input operators $U_{g_i(q)}$; (iii) where the inner antighost contours are placed – on the inputs (their native place), or traded for insertions on the special leg by the half's conservation laws (C3.3, level by level only). What is *not* a choice: whether the dressing exists. On $Q_V$ the germs (1.1) are $q$-dependent (R86: $\mu_i(q)$ non-constant; R91: not the halves' own) and no choice of $h_i$ makes $g_i(q)$ constant in $q$.

\subsection*{Commentary}
(1.1): the construction uses only that the section is a section, not its Möbius form; A4 enters in S2. (1.2): $h_i^{-1}\circ F_i^{(u)}$ is a germ at $0$ because both maps send $0$ to $u_i$ – this is where fixing the positions at $\pm z$ (the frame of Step 1) matters: with positions not matching, $g_i$ would not fix $0$ and $U_{g_i}$ would contain $L_{-1}$ (a translation), see C3.3.

\subsection*{Result}
The forced data are the frame, the germs (1.1) and the total antighost insertions; the cubic-vertex/plumbing split is the choice of $h_i$ in (1.2), exact for every choice. [C1]

\subsection*{Verification}
Structural (definitions and R2/R80 geometry); the geometry $N_q(u_4)=Z(q)$ is record 089's check `C1-q-inverse`, not repeated. The transport (1.1) is exercised by every check of S2.

## S2 — The halves

\subsection*{Setup}
A1, A3, A4, C1. $d_\kappa(W):=\kappa W$, $m_\mu(W):=W/(1+\mu W)$; $U_{d_\kappa}=\kappa^{L_0}\bar\kappa^{\bar L_0}$, $U_{m_\mu}=\mathrm e^{-\mu L_1-\bar\mu\bar L_1}$ (A3: $m_\mu$ is the time-one flow of $-\mu W^2\partial_W$, so $v_1=-\mu$; the sign is checked in `C3.2-U-sign` on the first descendant of a primary, which is the only place it is visible at this order: geometric value against the matrix exponential in a truncated $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ module, with $v_1$ read off the Taylor series of $m_\mu$). The engine's half map satisfies $h_1^{(\lambda)}=h_1^{(0)}\circ m_{\lambda/\gamma'}$ (094 (iii.5)).

\subsection*{Derivation}
*Möbius section (C2.1).* Each $F_i^{(u)}(\cdot\,;q)$ is Möbius, fixed by $F(0)=u_i$, $F'(0)=:a_i$, $F(\infty)=:R_i$. With $h_i=h_i^{(\lambda)}$ at $\gamma'$, the equation $F_i^{(u)}=h_i^{(\lambda)}\circ d_\kappa$ has the unique solution (094 (iii.1), the cross-ratio form)
$$
\lambda_i(q)=\frac{R_i-u_j}{R_i-u_i}\quad(j\ne i\text{ the partner}),\qquad \kappa_i(q)=\beta\gamma'\,a_i(q), \tag{2.1}
$$
and then, by $h^{(\lambda)}=h^{(0)}\circ m_{\lambda/\gamma'}$ and $m_\mu\circ d_\kappa=d_\kappa\circ m_{\kappa\mu}$ (exact, `C3.2-vector-field`),
$$
g_i(q)=m_{\mu}\circ d_{\kappa}=d_\kappa\circ m_{\kappa\mu},\qquad U_{g_i(q)}=\mathrm e^{-\mu L_1-\bar\mu\bar L_1}\,\kappa^{L_0}\bar\kappa^{\bar L_0}=\kappa^{L_0}\bar\kappa^{\bar L_0}\,\mathrm e^{-\kappa\mu L_1-\bar\kappa\bar\mu\bar L_1},\qquad \mu=\lambda/\gamma', \tag{2.2}
$$
relative to the $\lambda=0$ half; relative to the half $h^{(\lambda(q))}$ itself the input operator is $U_{d_\kappa}$ alone. Two readings of the same identity: (a) *fixed half, two-parameter input operator* – $h_i=h_i^{(\lambda_0)}$ for a fixed $\lambda_0$ (the theory's $\tfrac12$, C2.3), $g_i(q)=m_{\mu(q)-\mu_0}\circ d_{\kappa(q)}$ up to the composition order, with $U_g$ generated by $L_0,L_1$ only; (b) *moving half, one-parameter input operator* – the half at $q$ is the $\gamma'=\beta$ member $\lambda(q)$ of R2's family and the input operator is the rescaling $U_{d_{\kappa(q)}}$, R92's statement. The parameter $\gamma'$ is a redundancy of the split (2.2), absorbed in $\kappa$ ($\lambda$ does not depend on it and $\kappa\propto\gamma'$, `C2.1-factor`; exactly $h^{(\alpha,\gamma')}=h^{(\alpha,\gamma'_0)}\circ d_{\gamma'_0/\gamma'}$, so it rescales the local coordinate at the ordinary punctures and is not a redundancy of the vertex off shell, $(\gamma'_0/\gamma')^{L_0^+}\ne1$ on $\mathcal H_0$); it is fixed together with $\lambda_0$ by the canonical choice $\gamma'=\beta$ of C2.3.

*The four punctures.* For $i=2$, $F_2^{(u)}=-F_1^{(u)}$ (094 (iii.2), Klein $(12)(34)$ realised by $u\mapsto-u$), so $\lambda_2=\lambda_1$, $\kappa_2=\kappa_1$. For half $B$: the Klein element $(13)(24)$ is realised in the circle frame by $u\mapsto1/(qu)$, which is the exchange $w_A\leftrightarrow w_B$ ($u=1/w_A$, $1/(qu)=w_A/q=1/w_B$); so $F_3$ read in half $B$'s own circle frame $u'=1/w_B$ is $F_1^{(u)}$ as a map, and $\lambda_3=\lambda_1$, $\kappa_3=\kappa_1$, $F_4^{(u')}=F_2^{(u)}$ (`C2.1-halfB`, $4.5\times10^{-16}$ at 24 $q$ across the tiles; this uses the section's $S_4$ symmetry, A4, through the section's Klein maps and their transport). Hence one pair $(\lambda(q),\kappa(q))$ serves all four legs: the two halves at $q$ are the *same* member of the family, each with the same input rescaling. [C2.1]

*Non-Möbius section (C2.2).* For a general section (minimal area, or any section given numerically), $F_i^{(u)}$ is a germ with 2-jet $(u_i,a_i)$ and higher coefficients $c_i^{(k)}$; $g_i(q)=h_i^{-1}\circ F_i^{(u)}$ is a germ with $g(0)=0$, $g'(0)=\kappa_i=a_i/h_i'(0)$ and $U_{g_i}=\kappa^{L_0}\exp\big(\sum_{n\ge1}v_nL_n\big)\otimes\overline{(\cdots)}$, where the $v_n$ are determined order by order from the Taylor coefficients of $g_i/\kappa_i$ (the ordered-product form $U_g=\kappa^{L_0}\cdots\mathrm e^{\tilde v_2L_2}\mathrm e^{\tilde v_1L_1}$ of A3, whose coefficients $\tilde v_n$ are read off the jet of $g/\kappa$ order by order; see e.g. LeClair–Peskin–Preitschopf 1989 and Rastelli–Zwiebach 2001, who give the first three coefficients of the single-exponential form $\exp(\sum_nv_nL_n)$, the ordered-product coefficients following order by order and coinciding with them through $n=2$; $\tilde v_1=\tfrac12(g''/g')(0)$, …). On inputs of level $\le L$ only $L_n$ with $n\le L$ act (they lower the level by $n$), so the $(L+1)$-jet of $F_i^{(u)}$ at $q$ determines $U_{g_i}$ exactly on such inputs; this is what a numerical section has to supply, at each $q$ and – for the antighosts of S3 – with its first derivatives in $s$ and $\theta$. The half $(h_1\circ g_1,h_2\circ g_2,w)$ at $q$ is a three-punctured sphere surface state, i.e. an asymmetric cubic vertex in R82's sense, but it is not in R2's Möbius family (its germs are not Möbius), so reading (b) within that family is unavailable and reading (a) is the only split with a fixed half in R2's family: a fixed half and an input operator with infinitely many parameters, truncated by the level (S1 Step 2: any $h_i$ gives an exact split). What distinguishes the Möbius case is exactly that $U_g$ closes on $\{L_0,L_1\}$. [C2.2]

*The canonical half (C2.3).* On $|q|=1$ approached from $Q_V$ the section's germs are those of two glued theory halves (R86's $|q|=1$ clause; 094 C1: $\lambda\to\tfrac12$ as $O(\varepsilon^2)$, $|\kappa|\to1$; `C4.3-boundary`: deviations of $\lambda$ from $\tfrac12$ at $\varepsilon=10^{-2,-3,-4}$ (companion value, not reproduced here), rate $2.01$). With $h_i=h_i^{(1/2)}$ at $\gamma'=\beta$, $g_i(q)\to d_{\kappa}$ with $|\kappa|=1$ on the seam, which is $\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\arg\kappa L_0^-}=1$ on $\mathcal H_0$ (094 §(ii)); so the input operators are the identity on $\mathcal H_0$ at the seam and the surface states join the $s$-channel Feynman surface states continuously; the dressed antighosts join the undressed ones iff $\partial\ln|\kappa|,\ \partial\mu\to0$ there, which holds for the Möbius section (`C4.3-boundary`: $O(\varepsilon^2)$, radial direction, 7 angles). The statement is general: for any section matching the Feynman germs at $|q|=1$, $U_{g_i}\to1$ on $\mathcal H_0$ at the seam iff $h_i=h_i^{\rm th}$ up to a phase rotation (it uses only the seam clause and the exactness of the split, not the Möbius form). The canonical choice is canonical, not forced: the surface state $\langle\Sigma(q)|$ with germs $F_i^{(u)}$ is the same for every split $F_i=h_i\circ g_i$ (S1 Step 2), hence continuous at the seam for every $h_i$. With $h_i=h_i^{\rm th}$ the input operators are trivial at the seam, so the half of the $\Sigma$-exchange is the action's $\mathcal V_3$ and the plumbing operator reduces there to the undressed $b_0^+b_0^-\mathcal S(q)$; any other $h_i$ describes the same surface states with a $q$-independent factor $h_i^{-1}\circ h_i^{\rm th}$ moved into the plumbing operator at $|q|=1^+$ and nowhere at $|q|=1^-$: bookkeeping, not an object of the theory. [C2.3]

\subsection*{Commentary}
(2.1): the pole position $R_i$ is the one datum of a Möbius germ beyond its 2-jet; $\lambda$ is the cross-ratio of $(R_i;u_j,u_i,\ldots)$ in the form of 094 (iii.1). (2.2): the ordering matters for the mode content – $L_1$ before or after $\kappa^{L_0}$ changes $\mu$ by the factor $\kappa$, not the operator. Reading (b) is the one the paper's current text announces ("at each $\zeta$ the coordinates are those of a rigid half with a parameter that varies with $\zeta$, up to a rescaling of the inputs"); reading (a) is the one that generalises to non-Möbius sections.

\subsection*{Result}

## S3 — The plumbing operator

\subsection*{Setup}
A2, A3, A5, C1, C2. $\Phi_2^h(A_1,A_2)$ the circle state of the fixed half $h$ on its special leg; $b^{(i)}[v]$ the antighost contour of a vector field $v$ in the local frame of input $i$ (A3); $\partial_s$, $\partial_\theta$ the real derivatives along $q=\mathrm e^{-s+\mathrm i\theta}$.

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(3.1) The form.* The quartic form restricted to its vertex region, in the coordinate $q$ of $Q_V$, is by definition the integral of the section's form on the tangent vectors $\partial_s,\partial_\theta$: the correlator on $\Sigma(q)$ with the four inputs inserted through $F_i^{(u)}(\cdot\,;q)$ and the two Schiffer antighosts of those tangent vectors. A tangent vector to the section is the vector field of the gluing (the dilation/rotation of the circle) together with the variation of the four coordinate germs; so (R86, `eq:dressing`)
$$
B[\partial_s]=-b_0^+ +\sum_{i=1}^4B_i[\partial_sf_i],\qquad B[\partial_\theta]=\mathrm i\,b_0^-+\sum_{i=1}^4B_i[\partial_\theta f_i], \tag{3.1}
$$
the zero modes acting on the neck ($\mathcal S(q)$) and $B_i$ inside disc $i$. With (1.3) the operator form is
$$
p_{2,2}(A_1\odot A_2,A_3\odot A_4)=\int_{Q_V}\mathrm ds\,\mathrm d\theta\;\Big\langle\Phi_2^h\big(U_{g_3}A_3,U_{g_4}A_4\big)\Big|\,B[\partial_s]\,B[\partial_\theta]\,\mathcal S(q)\,\Big|\Phi_2^h\big(U_{g_1}A_1,U_{g_2}A_2\big)\Big\rangle, \tag{3.2}
$$
a graded product in the order of (3.1), with A2's normalisation, the $B_i$ understood as acting on the inputs as in (3.3).

*(3.3) Where $B_i$ acts.* For a one-parameter family $g(s)$ of germs fixing $0$, $g(s)^{-1}\circ g(s+\mathrm ds)=\mathrm{id}+\mathrm ds\,v^W$ with
$$
v^W(W)=\frac{\partial_sg(W)}{g'(W)},\qquad \partial_sU_{g(s)}=U_{g(s)}\,T[v^W],\qquad B_i[\partial_sf_i]\ \text{acts as}\ A_i\mapsto U_{g_i}\,b^{(i)}[v_i^{s}]\,A_i , \tag{3.3}
$$
i.e. the antighost contour is taken in the input's own frame $W$ and $U_{g_i}$ is applied afterwards (equivalently $b^{(i)}[g_{i*}v_i^s]U_{g_i}$, the same contour in the half's frame, by the invariance of $b[v]$). Since $v^W(0)=0$, $b^{(i)}[v_i^s]=\sum_{k\ge0}v_kb_k^{(i)}$ contains annihilation-type modes only, and $v_k$ for $k>L$ act trivially on level-$\le L$ inputs ($b_L$ and $L_L$ act non-trivially on a level-$L$ input). The same with $\partial_\theta$.

*(3.4) Möbius case (C3.2).* With $g=d_\kappa\circ m_{\kappa\mu}$ (2.2), exactly (`C3.2-vector-field`, sympy with $\kappa(s),\mu(s)$ arbitrary functions)
$$
v^W=(\partial_s\ln\kappa)\,W-\kappa\,(\partial_s\mu)\,W^2,\qquad
B_i[\partial_sf_i]=(\partial_s\ln\kappa)\,b_0^{(i)}+(\partial_s\ln\bar\kappa)\,\bar b_0^{(i)}-\kappa\,\partial_s\mu\;b_1^{(i)}-\bar\kappa\,\partial_s\bar\mu\;\bar b_1^{(i)}, \tag{3.4}
$$
independent of the reference $\mu_0$ of the fixed half (the deformation does not know the reference). On $A_i\in\mathcal H_0$ ($b_0^-A_i=0$) the zero-mode part is $(\partial_s\ln|\kappa|)\,b_0^{+(i)}$; the $\theta$-antighost is (3.4) with $\partial_\theta$. The dressed antighosts thus contain, besides the neck zero modes, $b_0^{+(i)}$ and $b_1^{(i)},\bar b_1^{(i)}$ on each input, with the coefficients $\partial\ln|\kappa|$, $\kappa\partial\mu$ – real-analytic functions of $q$ on each tile (PH34 for the section's regularity; the derivatives of $\lambda$ in (iii.7) are explicit on the FD). This is the "destroys the zero-mode form" of R80 made explicit: the extra modes are $b_1$ and $\bar b_1$, the ones of R86's two-term law. [C3.2]

*(3.5)–(3.7) Absorption into the neck (C3.3).* Question: is there an operator $X(q)$ on the special leg with $\langle V^h|(U_{g_1}\otimes U_{g_2}\otimes1)=\langle V^h|(1\otimes1\otimes X)$, and likewise for the $B_i$?
(3.5) *Exactly, as a coordinate change: no.* If $X=U_x$ for a germ $x$ at the special puncture, both sides are surface states of the three-punctured sphere with the same punctures and coordinates $(h_1\circ g_1,h_2\circ g_2,w)$ versus $(h_1,h_2,w\circ x)$; they coincide iff a Möbius automorphism of the sphere maps one coordinate triple to the other; it fixes the three punctures, so it is the identity, and then $g_1=g_2=\mathrm{id}$. This is the statement for the functional on all of $\mathcal H^{\otimes3}$; on $\mathcal H_0\otimes\mathcal H_0\otimes\mathcal H$ the triples are determined up to phase rotations of $h_{1,2}$, which act trivially ($\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\phi L_0^-}=1$ on $\mathcal H_0$), so the conclusion stands. "No moduli" works against absorption here: there is no deformation of the surface to trade against.
(3.6) *Level by level: yes, explicitly.* The conservation law of the half, $\langle V^h|\sum_{a=1}^3b^{(a)}[v|_a]=0$ for every vector field $v$ holomorphic on the sphere minus the three punctures (contour deformation; same for $T$ with the central terms cancelling at $c_{\rm tot}=0$), applied to a polynomial field $v(u)\partial_u$ – holomorphic except for a pole at the special puncture $u=\infty$ – whose $(L+1)$-jets at $u_1$, $u_2$ match those of $v_1^s$, $v_2^s$ (Hermite interpolation: the value and $L+1$ derivatives at each puncture, matching the $(L+1)$-jets of $v_1^s,v_2^s$, $v(u_i)=0$; $2L+4$ conditions, degree $2L+3$, pole of order $2L+1$ at $u=\infty$: with $w=1/u$, $v(u)\partial_u=-w^2v(1/w)\partial_w$, leading $w^{-(2L+1)}$), gives on inputs of level $\le L$
$$
\langle V^h|\big(b^{(1)}[v_1^s]+b^{(2)}[v_2^s]\big)=-\langle V^h|\,b^{(3)}[v|_3],\qquad v|_3=\sum_{n\le 2L+2}\beta_n(q)\,w^{1-n}\partial_w\ \to\ \sum_n\beta_n(q)\,b_{-n}^{(3)}, \tag{3.6}
$$
and the exponentiated version for $U_{g_1}\otimes U_{g_2}$ (the operators on legs 1,2 preserve the level bound, so the identity iterates). The neck operator so obtained carries creation modes $b_{-n}$, $L_{-n}$ in the special coordinate, of order $n\le2L+2$, growing linearly with the input level, with $q$-dependent coefficients: no level-uniform operator of this kind exists (3.5), and whether a level-uniform operator of another form exists is not decided here (it is the question of R80's 2026-10-05 annotation, a closed form of another shape).
(3.7) *Möbius case.* $v_i^s$ pushed to the sphere is $(\partial_sF_i\circ F_i^{-1})(u)\partial_u$, a *global quadratic* field vanishing at $u_i$ (the derivative of a Möbius family lies in $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$), and $v_2^s=\sigma_*v_1^s$ with $\sigma(u)=-u$ (Klein). Exact absorption of both would need one global field equal to $v_1^s$ near $u_1$ and to $v_2^s$ near $u_2$, hence $v_1^s=v_2^s$ as quadratic fields, hence $(u-z)(\alpha+\beta u)=(u+z)(\alpha-\beta u)$ for all $u$, hence $\alpha=\beta=0$: only the trivial deformation is absorbable, as (3.5) says. What the quadratic form buys is the two-term trade $\langle V^h|b^{(1)}[v_1^s]=-\langle V^h|\big(b^{(2)}[v_1^s|_2]+b^{(3)}[v_1^s|_3]\big)$ with $b^{(2)}[v_1^s|_2]\ni b_{-1}^{(2)}$ ($v_1^s(u_2)\ne0$) and $b^{(3)}[v_1^s|_3]$ a combination of $b_{\pm1}^{(3)},b_0^{(3)}$ – R86's two-term law read in reverse: a neck insertion plus a partner insertion, never a neck insertion alone (the two-term trade is machine-checked in R86's script, `C2-law-mu-nonzero` of `codes/verify-dressed-ckv.py`). [C3.3]

*(3.8) Primary inputs (C3.4).* For $A_i=c_1\bar c_1V_i|k_i\rangle$, $V_i$ a matter primary (any $k_i$): $b_k^{(i)}A_i=0$ for all $k\ge0$ ($\{b_k,c_1\}=0$ for $k\ne-1$, $b_k|0\rangle=0$) and $L_1A_i=0$ ($[L_1,c_1]=-3c_2$, $c_2|0\rangle=0$; $V_i$ primary). So every $B_i$ in (3.1) annihilates such inputs and $U_{g_i}A_i=\kappa^{h_i}\bar\kappa^{h_i}A_i=|\kappa_i(q)|^{2h_i}A_i$, with $h_i$ the $L_0$ ($=\bar L_0$) eigenvalue of the input, $h_i=\alpha'k_i^2/4-1$ for the tachyon. With C2.1 ($\kappa_i=\kappa$ for all four legs) and $\Phi_2^{(\lambda)}=\Phi_2^{(0)}$ on primaries,
$$
p_{2,2}\big(c_1\bar c_1V_1\odot c_1\bar c_1V_2,\;c_1\bar c_1V_3\odot c_1\bar c_1V_4\big)=\int_{Q_V}\mathrm ds\,\mathrm d\theta\;|\kappa(q)|^{2\sum_ih_i}\;\mathcal I^{\rm circ}(q), \tag{3.8}
$$
with $\mathcal I^{\rm circ}(q)$ the fixed-halves integrand of R80 (any $\lambda$). The whole off-shell content of the actual vertex on four matter primaries is the weight $|\kappa(q)|^{2\sum h_i}=\prod_i|F_i^{(u)\prime}(0)|^{2h_i}/|h'(0)|^{2\sum h_i}$, the mapping radii of the section relative to the rigid half. On shell ($h_i=0$) it is $1$: R80's exactness, here without a separate measure argument, since the antighosts of the section's own tangent vectors were used. [C3.4]

\subsection*{Commentary}
(3.2) is nothing but the definition of $\int_{\mathcal V_{0,4}}\Omega$ in the coordinate $q$, with the Schiffer antighosts of the section's tangent vectors; the "plumbing operator" is $B[\partial_s]B[\partial_\theta]\mathcal S(q)$ with the dressings (3.3) attached to the inputs. (3.6): the degree count is for the $b$-insertion; $U_g$'s $T$-version has the same structure. (3.7): the $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ form is special to Möbius sections; for a general section the pushed field is a germ and (3.6) is all there is. (3.8) is the formula a reader can evaluate for a given explicit section: $\kappa(q)=\beta\gamma'\,a_1(q)$ from the 2-jet of $f_1$ at $Z(q)$, transported by $N_q^{-1}$.

\subsection*{Result}
The plumbing operator off shell is $B[\partial_s]B[\partial_\theta]\mathcal S(q)$ over $Q_V$ with the dressed antighosts (3.1), the dressings acting on the inputs by (3.3) – in the Möbius case the explicit modes (3.4). The dressing cannot be moved to the neck as a coordinate change; it can level by level, at the price of creation modes of order $\le2L+2$ on level-$\le L$ inputs (3.6). On four matter primaries the recipe gives (3.8). [C3]

## S4 — Limiting cases and falsifier

*On shell (C4.1).* (3.8) at $h_i=0$: every input operator is the identity, every $B_i$ vanishes, the integrand is R80's; the recipe reduces to the fixed-halves gluing, exact (R80 C1.4). More generally on any inputs with $L_0^+A_i=0$, $L_1A_i=0$, $b_kA_i=0$ ($k\ge0$) – not only tachyons.

*Rigid halves (C4.2).* Setting $g_i\equiv\mathrm{id}$ over $Q_V$ is the model $p^{\rm circ}_{2,2}$: it drops the $q$-dependence of the germs, which R91 shows is incompatible with the cubic exchanges at the outer seam off shell (first-order mismatch $\ln\frac{4\beta^2-1}{4\beta}$ at $\theta=\pi$).
