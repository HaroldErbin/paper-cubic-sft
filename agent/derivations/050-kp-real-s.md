*Why the stub's falsifier does not decide.* $K_P(s)$ is Hilbert–Schmidt (049 C1.2), so its eigenvalues accumulate at $0$ — geometrically here (ratios (companion value, not reproduced here), C1.6, C2). A criterion must separate an *exact zero* of the spectrum from the *accumulation point* $0$.

*Candidates weighed (cost-to-information order).*

1. **Positivity of the kernel (analytic).** Each of the six $S_3$ terms of R48's $F$ is an integral over the region $R$ with a positive weight of $\phi_Z(\ell)\phi_Z(\ell')\,e^{c(Z)\,\ell\cdot\ell'}$ — the exponents of $|Z|,|1-Z|$ are linear in $s$, $\ell^2+\ell'^2$ and $\ell\cdot\ell'$ — and $e^{c\,\ell\cdot\ell'}$ restricted to even functions is $\sum_m c^{2m}(\ell\cdot\ell')^{2m}/(2m)!$, a sum of squares. If this goes through, $K_P(s)$ at $C=+1$ is positive semi-definite on $L^2_{\rm even}$ at every real $s$ and $\ker K_P=0$ reduces to a moment-determinacy argument (all moments of $\beta^{-\alpha'\rho^2}f$ vanish $\Rightarrow$ its Fourier transform, entire by the Gaussian factor, vanishes). Cost: one page of algebra plus a spot-check; information: settles (β) at *all* real $s$, all waves, beyond any discretisation, and is insensitive to a residual weight common to the four punctures (R48's invisible factor; a separable conjugation $DK_PD$). Chosen first; the numerics below become its spot-check and its break leg.
2. **Branch signs in $s$.** A zero of $\mu_{n,j}(s)$ at real $s_*$ is a sign change on a grid in $s$; decides only for the resolved eigenvalues (above the floor $\sim10^{-14}\mu_{0,0}$) and says nothing about the tail. Kept as the numerical spot-check of 1 (all resolved eigenvalues of all waves positive at the three $s$ and on a grid between them), which is exactly what a positive-definite kernel predicts and what an indefinite one would violate.
3. **Ladder regularity.** An eigenvalue out of the geometric ladder that decreases under refinement while its neighbours converge. Cheap once the full spectra are in hand; weakest information (a null vector inside the resolved range only). Reported from the same spectra.

*What is decidable in double precision.* Route 1 decides (β) exactly. Routes 2–3 decide it only on the resolved part of each wave's spectrum: eigenvalues above $\sim10^{-14}\mu_{0,0}$ absolute (C2.8), i.e. the top (companion value, not reproduced here) of the $n=0$ ladder and fewer per higher wave; below that, zero and $10^{-15}\mu_{0,0}$ are indistinguishable. A numerical verdict alone would read "no non-positive resolved eigenvalue", never "$\ker K_P=0$".

\subsection*{Derivation}

*(4.1) The six terms as separable kernels.* By R48 and 049 (1.1), at $C=+1$ and with $J(p,q)=\int_R\lvert Z\rvert^p\lvert1-Z\rvert^q\,d^2Z$ (R50, 045 (3.0); $R=\mathrm{FD}\cap\mathcal V_{0,4}$ a fixed bounded region at positive distance from $Z=0,1,\infty$, 045 C1),
$$K_P(\ell,\ell')=2\beta^{8-\alpha's/2}\,\beta^{-\alpha'(\rho^2+\lambda^2)}\sum_{(x,y,z)\in S_3(s,t,u)}\int_R\lvert Z\rvert^{\alpha'(x-y-z)/4}\lvert1-Z\rvert^{\alpha'(y-x-z)/4}\,d^2Z,\qquad t+u=2(\rho^2+\lambda^2),\quad t-u=4\,\ell\cdot\ell'.\tag{4.1}$$
Substituting $t,u$ in each of the six exponents gives, term by term, an integrand of the form $w_i(Z)\,\phi_{i,Z}(\ell)\,\phi_{i,Z}(\ell')\,e^{c_i(Z)\,\ell\cdot\ell'}$ with

| $(x,y,z)$ | $w_i(Z)/[2\beta^{8-\alpha's/2}]$ | $\phi_{i,Z}(\ell)$ | $c_i(Z)$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $(s,t,u)$ | $\lvert Z\rvert^{\alpha's/4}\lvert1-Z\rvert^{-\alpha's/4}$ | $(\beta^2\lvert Z\rvert)^{-\alpha'\rho^2/2}$ | $+\alpha'\ln\lvert1-Z\rvert$ |
| $(s,u,t)$ | same | same | $-\alpha'\ln\lvert1-Z\rvert$ |
| $(t,s,u)$ | $\lvert Z\rvert^{-\alpha's/4}\lvert1-Z\rvert^{\alpha's/4}$ | $(\beta^2\lvert1-Z\rvert)^{-\alpha'\rho^2/2}$ | $+\alpha'\ln\lvert Z\rvert$ |
| $(u,s,t)$ | same | same | $-\alpha'\ln\lvert Z\rvert$ |
| $(t,u,s)$ | $\lvert Z(1-Z)\rvert^{-\alpha's/4}$ | $\beta^{-\alpha'\rho^2}$ | $+\alpha'\ln\lvert Z/(1-Z)\rvert$ |
| $(u,t,s)$ | same | same | $-\alpha'\ln\lvert Z/(1-Z)\rvert$ |

(e.g. $(s,t,u)$: $\alpha'(s-t-u)/4=\alpha's/4-\alpha'(\rho^2+\lambda^2)/2$, $\alpha'(t-s-u)/4=\alpha'\ell\cdot\ell'-\alpha's/4$; $(t,u,s)$: $\alpha'(t-u-s)/4=\alpha'\ell\cdot\ell'-\alpha's/4$, $\alpha'(u-t-s)/4=-\alpha'\ell\cdot\ell'-\alpha's/4$). Every $w_i>0$ on $R$ for every real $s$; every $\phi_{i,Z}>0$; every $c_i$ is real. In particular $K_P(\ell,\ell')>0$ pointwise at $C=+1$.

*(4.2) Lemma (the exponential kernel on even functions).* Let $g=e^{-a\rho^2}f$ with $f\in L^2(\mathbb R^D)$ real and even, $a>0$, and $c\in\mathbb R$ with $\lvert c\rvert<2a$. Then
$$Q_c[g]:=\iint g(\ell)\,g(\ell')\,e^{c\,\ell\cdot\ell'}\,d^D\ell\,d^D\ell'=\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{c^{2m}}{(2m)!}\sum_{i_1,\dots,i_{2m}}\Bigl(\int g(\ell)\,\ell_{i_1}\cdots\ell_{i_{2m}}\,d^D\ell\Bigr)^2\;\ge0,\tag{4.2}$$
and if $c\ne0$, $Q_c[g]=0$ iff $g=0$. Proof: expand $e^{c\ell\cdot\ell'}=\sum_m c^m(\ell\cdot\ell')^m/m!$; the sum and the double integral commute by dominated convergence, the dominant being $\lvert g(\ell)\rvert\lvert g(\ell')\rvert e^{\lvert c\rvert\rho\lambda}$, whose integral is $\le\|f\|_2^2\,\|e^{-a(\rho^2+\lambda^2)+\lvert c\rvert\rho\lambda}\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^{2D})}$ by Cauchy–Schwarz, finite iff the quadratic form $a(\rho^2+\lambda^2)-\lvert c\rvert\rho\lambda$ is positive definite, i.e. $2a>\lvert c\rvert$; odd $m$ vanish by $\ell\to-\ell$; $(\ell\cdot\ell')^{2m}=\sum_I\ell^I\ell'^I$ over multi-indices $I$ of length $2m$ makes each even term a sum of squares. If $Q_c[g]=0$ with $c\ne0$, every moment $\int g\,\ell^I$ vanishes (even $\lvert I\rvert$ from (4.2), odd $\lvert I\rvert$ by parity); $\hat g(k)=\int g\,e^{-ik\cdot\ell}$ is entire on $\mathbb C^D$ ($\int\lvert g\rvert e^{b\lvert\ell\rvert}<\infty$ for every $b$, by the same Cauchy–Schwarz), with Taylor coefficients at $k=0$ the moments, so $\hat g\equiv0$ and $g=0$ a.e. $\square$

*(4.3) Theorem (definiteness of $K_P(s)$ at real $s$).* For real $s$, $\beta>\sqrt2$ (049 C1.2: $K_P$ Hilbert–Schmidt), $C\ne0$, and $f\in L^2_{\rm even}(\mathbb R^{26})$ real,
$$\langle f,K_Pf\rangle=\int_R d^2Z\sum_{i=1}^6w_i(Z)\,Q_{c_i(Z)}\bigl[\phi_{i,Z}f\bigr]\;\;\ge0\ \ (C=+1),\qquad\text{and}\qquad\langle f,K_Pf\rangle=0\iff f=0.\tag{4.3}$$
Proof: the interchange of $\iint d\ell\,d\ell'$ with $\int_R d^2Z$ is Tonelli, the integrand being $\lvert f\rvert\lvert f'\rvert$ times a positive function whose $Z$-integral is $K_P$ itself, and $\iint\lvert f\rvert K_P\lvert f\rvert\le\|f\|^2\|K_P\|_{HS}<\infty$. Each $g_{i,Z}=\phi_{i,Z}f$ is of the form of (4.2) with $2a_i(Z)-\lvert c_i(Z)\rvert$ equal to $\alpha'[2\ln\beta+\ln\lvert Z\rvert-\ln\lvert1-Z\rvert]$ (terms 1–2), $\alpha'[2\ln\beta+\ln\lvert1-Z\rvert-\ln\lvert Z\rvert]$ (3–4), $\alpha'[2\ln\beta-\ln\lvert(1-Z)/Z\rvert]$ (5–6). On $R$ one has $1\le\lvert Z\rvert\le\lvert1-Z\rvert\le2\lvert Z\rvert$: $\ln\lvert Z\rvert$, $\ln\lvert1-Z\rvert-\ln\lvert Z\rvert$ and $\ln\lvert1-Z\rvert-\ln\lvert Z\rvert-\ln2$ are harmonic on the bounded region $R$ (no singularity inside, 045 C1), so their extrema sit on $\partial R$, where the three inequalities are read off the four arcs of R50 (real segment $Z\in[-1,\dots]$ with $\lvert1-Z\rvert/\lvert Z\rvert\le2$ and equality at the corner $Z=-1$; unit circle $\lvert Z\rvert=1$; vertical segment $\operatorname{Re}Z=\tfrac12$ with $\lvert1-Z\rvert=\lvert Z\rvert$; ellipse arc) — verified numerically on the arc data of the evaluator, check C3.0-region. Hence all six $2a_i-\lvert c_i\rvert\ge\alpha'(2\ln\beta-\ln2)>0$ for $\beta>\sqrt2$, the worst case being term 6 at the corner $Z=-1$ — the same rate as (1.2). So each $Q\ge0$, each $w_i>0$, and the form is $\ge0$. If it vanishes, then for a.e. $Z\in R$ all six $Q_{c_i(Z)}[\phi_{i,Z}f]=0$; the fifth term has $c_5(Z)=\alpha'\ln\lvert Z/(1-Z)\rvert\ne0$ off the line $\operatorname{Re}Z=\tfrac12$, which has measure zero in the two-dimensional region $R$, so by the lemma $\beta^{-\alpha'\rho^2}f=0$, i.e. $f=0$. Since $K_P$ is bounded self-adjoint and $\ge0$, $K_Pf=0\iff\|K_P^{1/2}f\|^2=\langle f,K_Pf\rangle=0$, hence
$$\boxed{\ \ker K_P(s)=\{0\}\ \text{ on }L^2_{\rm even}(\mathbb R^{26})\ \text{ for every real }s,\ \beta>\sqrt2,\ C\ne0;\quad K_P(s)>0\ (C>0),\ <0\ (C<0).\ }\tag{4.4}$$
$\square$ The argument uses: R48's collapse (PH26, PH-044A4, PH-044A5), the pointwise positivity of the integrand of each $J$, the linearity of the exponents in $\ell^2+\ell'^2$ and $\ell\cdot\ell'$, and evenness. It does not use $D=26$, the value of $s$ beyond its reality, or any property of $R$ beyond positive measure off $\operatorname{Re}Z=\tfrac12$.

*(4.5) Corollaries.* (i) Every eigenvalue $\mu_{n,j}(s)$ of every partial wave is strictly positive at $C=+1$ for every real $s$ — the sign observations of 049 C3, 050 C1.4, C2.7 are instances. (ii) No eigenvalue branch $\mu_{n,j}(s)$ has a real zero: the zeros searched by 18c.6 (PH18) are strictly complex, and the "sign change in $s$" test of 049 Part 2 item 3 can never fire. (iii) A residual weight common to the four punctures (R48's invisible factor) $\mu^{\alpha'(\rho^2+\lambda^2)}$ multiplies each $\phi_{i,Z}$ by $\mu^{\alpha'\rho^2}$ and changes nothing in (4.3) as long as the kernel stays Hilbert–Schmidt ($\mu<\beta/\sqrt2$, 049 Part 2 item 8): the decay question of item 8 is moot, there being no null vector. (iv) $C<0$ (R47): $V_4$ on the level-0 block is negative definite; $m_2=G_2^{-1}V_4$ (049 (1.4)) is injective with dense range, so $m_2m_2^{-1}=\mathrm{id}$ on $\operatorname{ran}m_2$ (and $m_2^{-1}m_2=\mathrm{id}$ on $L^2_{\rm even}$), which is what (c2) of 049 Block 2 needs at level 0. (v) At complex $s$ the weights $w_i$ are complex and nothing of (4.3) survives, consistently with PH18.

\subsection*{Commentary}

- *(4.1).* The only input beyond R48 is the bookkeeping $t+u=2(\rho^2+\lambda^2)$, $t-u=4\ell\cdot\ell'$ (049 (1.1)). Each exponent $\alpha'(x-y-z)/4$ is linear in $(s,t,u)$, hence in $s$, $\rho^2+\lambda^2$ and $\ell\cdot\ell'$; the coefficient of $\rho^2+\lambda^2$ is the same for $\rho^2$ and $\lambda^2$, which is what makes $\phi_{i,Z}(\ell)\phi_{i,Z}(\ell')$ a product of the *same* function (crossing symmetry $t\leftrightarrow u$ is not needed for it, only $t+u$ being symmetric in $\ell\leftrightarrow\ell'$); the coefficient of $\ell\cdot\ell'$ is what remains. 049 C1.2's remark "positivity, which does not hold" was a caution, not a result; (4.3) shows it does hold at $C=+1$.
- *(4.2).* The lemma is the classical fact that $e^{c\,\ell\cdot\ell'}$ is a positive-definite kernel for $c>0$ (Schur product of the positive-definite $\ell\cdot\ell'$), sharpened in two ways: on even functions the sign of $c$ is irrelevant (odd powers drop out), and the equality case is decided by moment determinacy — $g$ has a Gaussian factor, so its Fourier transform is entire and is fixed by its Taylor coefficients, the moments. The condition $\lvert c\rvert<2a$ is only the absolute convergence of the double integral; it is what ties the lemma to $\beta>\sqrt2$ through C3.1.
- *(4.3).* Positivity of a sum of positive terms is trivial; the content is the *strict* positivity, which needs one term whose $c_i(Z)$ is non-zero on a set of positive measure — the fifth (or sixth), $c=\pm\alpha'\ln\lvert Z/(1-Z)\rvert$, vanishing only on the vertical segment $\operatorname{Re}Z=\tfrac12$, a boundary arc of $R$, of measure zero. (Terms 1–4 would do as well: $\ln\lvert1-Z\rvert>0$ on $R$ since $\lvert1-Z\rvert\ge\lvert Z\rvert\ge1$ with $\lvert1-Z\rvert=1$ only at $Z=0,2$, outside $R$; $\ln\lvert Z\rvert=0$ only on the unit-circle arc.) The step "$K\ge0$, $\langle f,Kf\rangle=0\Rightarrow Kf=0$" is $\|K^{1/2}f\|=0$; it is where reality of $s$ enters (at complex $s$ the form is not a norm).
- *Scope.* The theorem is about the Wick-rotated pair block (PH25) under R48's collapse (PH26, PH-044A4/A5); it holds for every $D$ and every real $s$, both signs of $C$, both declared $\beta$; it says nothing at complex $s$ (PH18, 18c.6), nothing about the level-$(0,1)$ or higher blocks (PH19, 18c.7), and nothing about the rate at which the eigenvalues accumulate at $0$ (PH3, 18c.8). It closes 049 Part 2 item 3 and makes item 8 (decay of a candidate null vector) empty.
- *What the numerics add.* Nothing to the proof; they are the spot-check the brief asks for and a test of the code path (a sign error in a tile or a negative Funk–Hecke weight would produce a negative resolved eigenvalue somewhere in the $3\times2\times16+8\times9$ spectra). The refinement table of the stub's original deliverable — the smallest $\lvert\mu_{n,j}\rvert/\mu_{n,0}$ per design — is reported for the record, with its reading fixed by the Setup: it sits at the rounding floor and moves with the rule, as the discretised tail of a Hilbert–Schmidt spectrum must.

## Section 18c.8 — PH3: saturation drift of the eigen-expansion (C4)

\subsection*{Setup — the criterion (first checkpoint, 2026-09-25 12:38)}

049 A1–A4, PH25, PH26, PH-044A4, PH-044A5; the discretisation of 18c.2–18c.3; $\beta=2$, $\alpha'P^2=16/3$ ($4$ only if time allows), $C=+1$. *Why the stub's falsifier does not decide by itself.* $N_\epsilon(n_r,\Lambda)$ "bounded on the designs tried" is a statement about three Nyström matrices, not about the operator: a kernel whose expansion converges but not absolutely (or converges only in $L^2$) shows its drift only once the discretisation resolves the offending part of the spectrum, which may sit beyond $n_r=80$. Conversely PH3 is a statement about an infinite sum at a point. A criterion must turn "absolutely convergent at $(\ell_0,\ell_0')$" into something either provable or decidable on finitely many designs, and the wording of the verdict must follow which of the two was achieved.

*Candidates weighed (cost-to-information order).*

1. **Mercer route (analytic).** C3 (18c.4) gives $K_P(s)\ge0$ on $L^2_{\rm even}$ at real $s$, $C=+1$; $K_P$ is even in each argument separately (the $S_3$ sum is symmetric under $t\leftrightarrow u$, i.e. $\ell'\to-\ell'$), so it annihilates the odd sector and is positive semi-definite on all of $L^2(\mathbb R^{26})$; it is continuous (entire in $s,t,u$, 049 Block 1) and its diagonal $K_P(\ell,\ell)$ has the Gaussian bound of 049 (1.2) with rate $\alpha'(2\ln\beta-\ln2)>0$ (the worst term of C3.3), so $\int K_P(\ell,\ell)\,d^{26}\ell<\infty$. These are the hypotheses of Mercer's theorem on a non-compact domain — the statement used: for a continuous, symmetric, positive semi-definite kernel with integrable diagonal on a σ-finite measure space of full support, the eigen-expansion converges absolutely and uniformly on compacts and the trace equals the diagonal integral (Steinwart–Scovel 2012, *Constr. Approx.* 35, 363–417, "Mercer's theorem on general domains"; see also Sun 2005, *J. Complexity* 21, 337–349, and Ferreira–Menegatto 2009, *Integr. Equ. Oper. Theory* 64, 61–81, cited without theorem numbers): the eigen-expansion $K_P(\ell,\ell')=\sum_k\mu_k\psi_k(\ell)\psi_k(\ell')$ converges *absolutely* at every pair of points and uniformly on compacts, $K_P$ is trace class and $\sum_k\mu_k=\int K_P(\ell,\ell)\,d^{26}\ell$. Cost: half a page — the hypotheses of the theorem checked one by one against 049 (1.2), (1.5) and C3. Information: settles PH3 at real $s$ at *every* external point, in the wording R51 asks for, beyond any discretisation; conditional on C3 exactly as the record states C3 (C3's node check is in progress in parallel; C4 cites C3). Chosen first. The numerics of 2–3 become its spot-checks and carry the break legs.
2. **Trace identity (numerical, interpolation-free).** Partial sums $T(n_{\max})=\sum_{n\le n_{\max}\ \rm even}\dim\mathcal Y_n\sum_j\mu_{n,j}$ against $\int K_P(\ell,\ell)\,d^{26}\ell=\lvert S^{25}\rvert\int_0^\infty F(s,4\rho^2,0)\,\rho^{25}\,d\rho$ by direct quadrature. Tests the addition-theorem resummation of the diagonal, $\sum_n\dim\mathcal Y_nk_n(\rho,\rho)/\lvert S^{25}\rvert=F(s,4\rho^2,0)$ (projector normalisation, multiplicities, the $n$-tail of the trace), and the $n_r$-node radial rule against the 400-node one — not the spectrum: $\sum_j\mu_{n,j}=\operatorname{tr}M_n$ is exact for the finite matrix, so a wrong eigenvalue would not be seen; the trace-class statement of 1 is established by the proof. Its convergence in $n_{\max}$ is the multiplicity-weighted tail of the trace, C2.8 (peak, ratio and $10^{-6}$ position: (companion value, not reproduced here)); needs $n_\theta\ge n_{\max}+1$ (use $64$). Break legs: a wrong multiplicity ($\dim\mathcal Y_n\to\dim\mathcal Y_{n+2}$) or a dropped wave must fail it. Second, cheap once the spectra of 3 are in hand.
3. **Pointwise saturation (the stub's deliverable).** $N_\epsilon$ at the three points on $(n_r,\Lambda)\in\{(32,8),(56,8),(80,10)\}$, $n_\theta=64$, terms ordered by $\lvert\mu\rvert$ over all $(n,j)$ with $n\le n_{\max}$, counted with multiplicity $\dim\mathcal Y_n$, angular part $\dim\mathcal Y_n P_n(x)/\lvert S^{25}\rvert$ by the addition theorem, eigenfunctions Nyström-interpolated in the symmetrised basis of 049 (1.3), $\psi(\rho)=\mu^{-1}\sum_jw_j\rho_j^{25}k_n(\rho,\rho_j)\psi(\rho_j)$, reference $K_P(\ell_0,\ell_0')$ by the direct evaluator. Decides only "bounded on the designs tried" and is worded so; its value is as the concrete instance of 1 at the external point and as the carrier of the break legs (a step kernel $\mathbb 1[\rho\lambda\le1]$ at a point of its discontinuity, whose $N_\epsilon$ must grow with $n_r$; the tile-flipped kernel of `c4`). Limiting case: $N_\epsilon$ at $x$ near $0$ far below the $x=1$ value.

\subsection*{Derivation}

*(8.1) The hypotheses of Mercer's theorem, checked against the record.* Let $X=\mathbb R^{26}$ with Lebesgue measure (σ-finite, every open set of positive measure), and $K=K_P(s)$ at real $s$, $\beta>\sqrt2$, $C=+1$.

(a) *Continuity.* $F(s,t,u)$ is entire in $(s,t,u)$ (049 (1.5): each $J(p,q)$ is entire of exponential type, 045 C1), and $(\ell,\ell')\mapsto(t,u)$ is polynomial, so $K$ is continuous on $\mathbb R^{52}$.

(b) *Symmetry and positive semi-definiteness on $L^2(\mathbb R^{26})$.* $K(\ell,\ell')=K(\ell',\ell)$ (049 (1.3)). By C3 (eq. (4.4)), $\langle f,Kf\rangle>0$ for $0\ne f\in L^2_{\rm even}$. $K$ is even in $\ell'$ separately (049 (1.1): $\ell'\to-\ell'$ is $t\leftrightarrow u$, and $F$ is $S_3$-symmetric), hence $Kf=0$ for odd $f$ and $K=K|_{\rm even}\oplus0$ on $L^2=L^2_{\rm even}\oplus L^2_{\rm odd}$: $\langle f,Kf\rangle=\langle f_e,Kf_e\rangle\ge0$ for every $f\in L^2$. For a continuous kernel and a measure of full support this operator statement is equivalent to the pointwise one, $\sum_{a,b}c_ac_bK(\ell_a,\ell_b)\ge0$ for all finite families (test functions concentrating at the $\ell_a$).

(c) *Integrable diagonal.* At $\ell=\ell'$: $\rho=\lambda$, $x=1$, $t=4\rho^2$, $u=0$, and 049 (1.2) gives $0<K(\ell,\ell)\le Cc_s\,e^{-2\alpha'\rho^2(\ln\beta-\frac12\ln2)}$, so
$$\operatorname{Tr}K:=\int K(\ell,\ell)\,d^{26}\ell=\lvert S^{25}\rvert\int_0^\infty F(s,4\rho^2,0)\,\rho^{25}\,d\rho<\infty\qquad(\beta>\sqrt2).\tag{8.1}$$
(Positivity of $K(\ell,\ell)$ pointwise is (4.1): every integrand is positive.)

(d) *Hilbert–Schmidt.* 049 C1.2. So $K$ is compact self-adjoint with eigenvalues $\mu_k\ge0$ (C3: $>0$ on the even sector, $=0$ on the odd one), orthonormal eigenfunctions $\psi_k$, and $\mu_k\psi_k(\ell)=\int K(\ell,\ell')\psi_k(\ell')d^{26}\ell'$; each $\psi_k$ with $\mu_k>0$ is continuous (dominated convergence under the Gaussian bound (1.2), $\psi_k\in L^2$).

*(8.2) Mercer's theorem on $\mathbb R^{26}$.* Under (a)–(d) — a continuous, symmetric, positive semi-definite kernel with integrable diagonal on a σ-finite measure space of full support — Mercer's theorem in its non-compact form (Steinwart–Scovel 2012, *Constr. Approx.* 35, 363–417; see also Sun 2005 and Ferreira–Menegatto 2009, as in the Setup) states
$$K(\ell,\ell')=\sum_k\mu_k\,\psi_k(\ell)\,\psi_k(\ell')\quad\text{absolutely at every }(\ell,\ell'),\ \text{uniformly on compacts},\qquad\sum_k\mu_k=\operatorname{Tr}K<\infty.\tag{8.2}$$
The two steps that carry the conclusion, so that the record does not rest on the citation alone: (i) for every $N$ the remainder $R_N=K-\sum_{k\le N}\mu_k\psi_k\otimes\psi_k$ is a continuous positive semi-definite kernel (its operator is $K$ restricted to the orthogonal complement of $\psi_1,\dots,\psi_N$), so its diagonal is $\ge0$ by (b): $\sum_{k\le N}\mu_k\psi_k(\ell)^2\le K(\ell,\ell)$ for every $\ell$ and $N$, hence $\sum_k\mu_k\psi_k(\ell)^2$ converges, and by Cauchy–Schwarz
$$\sum_k\mu_k\,\lvert\psi_k(\ell)\psi_k(\ell')\rvert\le\bigl(K(\ell,\ell)\,K(\ell',\ell')\bigr)^{1/2}<\infty\tag{8.3}$$
— absolute convergence at every pair of points, the statement of PH3; (ii) (Riesz–Nagy §98 order) for fixed $\ell$, $K(\ell,\cdot)\in L^2(\mathbb R^{26})$ is orthogonal to $\ker K$, since $\int K(\ell,\ell')f(\ell')\,d^{26}\ell'=(Kf)(\ell)=0$ for $f\in\ker K$, so $K(\ell,\cdot)=\sum_k\mu_k\psi_k(\ell)\psi_k(\cdot)$ in $L^2(d\ell')$; by (8.3)'s Cauchy–Schwarz with $\sum_{k>N}\mu_k\psi_k(\ell)^2\to0$, the series converges uniformly in $\ell'$ on compacts, so its sum is continuous in $\ell'$, equals $K(\ell,\ell')$ a.e. in $\ell'$, hence everywhere (full support, both sides continuous); in particular $\sum_k\mu_k\psi_k(\ell)^2=K(\ell,\ell)$ for every $\ell$. Only *then* Dini's theorem (a monotone series of continuous functions with a continuous limit) gives uniform convergence of the diagonal series on compacts, and the trace formula is monotone convergence in $\int\sum_k\mu_k\psi_k(\ell)^2\,d^{26}\ell$ with $\|\psi_k\|=1$. In particular $K$ is trace class (positive with finite trace), which strengthens 049 C1.2's Hilbert–Schmidt.

*(8.3) The expansion at the external points, in partial waves.* By 049 (1.6) the eigenfunctions are $\psi_{n,j}(\rho)Y(\hat\ell)$, $Y\in\mathcal Y_n$ (even $n$), and by the addition theorem $\sum_{Y\in\mathcal Y_n}Y(\hat\ell)Y(\hat\ell')=\dim\mathcal Y_n\,P_n(x)/\lvert S^{25}\rvert$, so (8.2) reads
$$K_P(\ell,\ell')=\sum_{n\ \rm even}\frac{\dim\mathcal Y_n\,P_n(x)}{\lvert S^{25}\rvert}\sum_j\mu_{n,j}\,\psi_{n,j}(\rho)\,\psi_{n,j}(\lambda),\qquad\operatorname{Tr}K_P=\sum_{n\ \rm even}\dim\mathcal Y_n\sum_j\mu_{n,j},\tag{8.4}$$
both absolutely convergent. At the on-shell point $\ell'=\ell$ ($x=1$, $P_n(1)=1$) every term of (8.4) is positive: absolute and plain convergence coincide there, and (8.3) is saturated, $\sum_k\mu_k\psi_k(\ell_0)^2=K_P(\ell_0,\ell_0)$ — the on-shell point is the worst point for the rate of the expansion and the best for its verification. At generic $x$ the $P_n(x)$ alternate and are $O(n^{-12})$ for $\lvert x\rvert<1$ (DLMF 18.15.10: $C^{(\lambda)}_n(\cos\theta)=O(n^{\lambda-1})$ against $C^{(\lambda)}_n(1)\sim n^{2\lambda-1}$, $\lambda=12$), so the $n$-sum converges faster than at $x=1$.

*(8.5) Numerical spot-checks of (8.4).* Script group `c8` (`codes/verify-kp-real-s.py --only c8`; every number below is from run 3, `agent/scratch/c8-run3.log`, and the orchestrator re-run `agent/scratch/c8-rerun-orchestrator-2026-09-25.log`, which agree; run 4 of 2026-09-26, `c8-run4.log`, adds the restricted $x=1$ check): designs $(n_r,\Lambda,n_\theta)\in\{(32,8,64),(56,8,64),(80,10,64)\}$, even $n\le50$, $\beta=2$, $\alpha'P^2\in\{16/3,4\}$; points: on-shell ($\rho_0=\lambda_0=\sqrt{4-\alpha's/4}$, i.e. $\alpha'\rho_0^2=8/3$, $3$; $x=1$), genericA ($x$ seeded in $[0.35,0.75]$), genericB ($\lvert x\rvert<0.08$), radii seeded in $[1,2.6]$ (seed 20260923). *Trace identity*: partial sums $\sum_{n\le n_{\max}}\dim\mathcal Y_n\operatorname{tr}M_n$ against (8.1) by 400-node Gauss–Legendre on $[0,14]$ (stable to $3\times10^{-16}$ against $[0,10]$). *Angular reconstruction*: $\sum_n\dim\mathcal Y_nP_n(x)k_n(\rho,\lambda)/\lvert S^{25}\rvert$ with $k_n(\rho,\lambda)$ by direct projection (no eigenvectors) against $F$ at the point — separates the $n$-truncation from the radial eigen-expansion. *Pointwise saturation*: $N_\epsilon$ = number of terms (ordered by $\lvert\mu_{n,j}\rvert$ over all $(n,j)$, counted with multiplicity $\dim\mathcal Y_n$; the count of distinct $(n,j)$ pairs alongside) after which every partial sum of (8.4) with Nyström-interpolated $\psi_{n,j}$ stays within $\epsilon$ of the direct value, plus the floor (best relative error over all partial sums); "bounded" = the distinct-term count varies by $\le2$ across the three designs. *Break legs*: $\dim\mathcal Y_n\to\dim\mathcal Y_{n+2}$ and the dropped peak wave $n=8$ on the trace identity; the step kernel $\mathbb 1[\rho\lambda\le1]$ on $[0,2]$ (discontinuous, hence outside Mercer) at a grid pair on its discontinuity, whose $N_{10^{-3}}$ must grow with $n_r\in\{32,64,128,256\}$, with a Gaussian kernel as control (saturates). \subsection*{Commentary}

- *(8.1) (b).* The only step beyond C3 is the extension from $L^2_{\rm even}$ to $L^2$: it is not a strengthening of C3 but the observation that $K_P$ annihilates the odd sector, so "positive on even functions" is "positive semi-definite on all functions", which is what Mercer needs. The zero eigenvalue on the odd sector contributes nothing to (8.2); the $\psi_k$ there are the even eigenfunctions.
- *(8.1) (c).* The integrability of the diagonal is the same Gaussian rate as C3.3's worst term, $\alpha'(2\ln\beta-\ln2)$ — at $\ell=\ell'$ the bound (1.2) is attained by the $x=1$ growth $2^{\alpha'\rho\lambda}$ of 045 C3.7, so $\beta>\sqrt2$ is exactly the condition for (8.1), as it was for Hilbert–Schmidt. Nothing new is asked of the kernel.
- *(8.2).* 049 C1.2 wrote "trace class would need a derivative bound or positivity, which does not hold"; with C3 positivity does hold, and (8.2) is the trace-class statement it was missing. The expansion (8.3) is stronger than PH3 asks (PH3 is absolute convergence at the external points; (8.3) is absolute convergence everywhere with a uniform bound on compacts). The version of Mercer's theorem used is the one for a σ-finite measure of full support on a metric space, which is why (b) is stated in its pointwise form as well; on a compact domain the classical statement (Riesz–Nagy §98) would do, and the two steps (i)–(ii) are its proof transcribed, so that a reader who does not have Steinwart–Scovel 2012 at hand can check the argument on (a)–(d) directly.
- *(8.4).* The multiplicity $\dim\mathcal Y_n$ grows like $n^{24}$ and the eigenvalues fall geometrically (C2, ratio (companion value, not reproduced here) per step of two in $n$ for $\mu_{n,0}$); the product peaks at (companion value, not reproduced here) and then decays with ratio (companion value, not reproduced here) (C2.8). This is the concrete face of absolute convergence at $x=1$: a convergent geometric tail after a polynomial prefactor. It is also why the numerics need $n\le50$ and $n_\theta=64$ for $10^{-6}$ at $x=1$ while a few waves suffice at $x\approx0$.
- *Why the numerics are spot-checks and not the verdict.* A Nyström matrix of size $n_r$ has $n_r$ eigen-terms per wave, and its pointwise expansion at a grid pair reconstructs the matrix entry exactly with all terms; "bounded in $n_r$" for $N_\epsilon$ therefore only shows that the resolved part of the spectrum saturates on the designs tried — the reading the stub asked for, which the break leg (step kernel) shows is falsifiable, but which by itself cannot exclude a slow tail beyond the resolution. The verdict rests on (8.2)–(8.3); the numerics confirm the addition-theorem resummation of the diagonal in (8.1)=(8.4) (with the radial rule and the multiplicity-weighted $n$-tail; the spectral content of the trace formula, $\sum_k\mu_k<\infty$ for the operator, is the proof's, not the check's) and the saturation at the external points.
- *The tile-flipped kernel as a break leg.* The brief offered `f_signed` (sign-indefinite); it is not a counterexample to absolute convergence — it is analytic with a Gaussian bound, and its expansion converges absolutely for the same reason as (8.3) applied to its positive and negative parts (indefinite analytic kernels are trace class too, by the derivative-bound route). A break leg for saturation must leave Mercer's hypotheses: the step kernel does (discontinuous, eigenvalues $O(1/k)$, expansion not absolutely convergent at the discontinuity), and drifts as required.

\subsection*{Result}

$$\boxed{\ \text{PH3 holds at level 0 at every real }s\text{ (}\beta>\sqrt2,\ C\ne0\text{): the eigen-expansion of }K_P(s)\text{ converges absolutely at every }(\ell,\ell'),\ \text{the external points included, and }K_P(s)\text{ is trace class with }\operatorname{Tr}K_P=\sum_{n\ \rm even}\dim\mathcal Y_n\sum_j\mu_{n,j}=\lvert S^{25}\rvert\int_0^\infty F(s,4\rho^2,0)\rho^{25}d\rho.\ }\tag{8.6}$$
Proven by (8.1)–(8.3) from C3 (positivity), 049 (1.2) (Gaussian bound) and 049 (1.5) (continuity, Hilbert–Schmidt); conditional on C3 (node check of 2026-09-25: all nodes hold) and, through C3, on PH25, PH26, PH-044A4, PH-044A5. Not a discretisation statement: the numerics below are spot-checks. Qualifier for the ledger: PH3 at level 0, in the eigenbasis form (iii) of R51 (sufficient, not equivalent), at every real $s$, by proof (Mercer from C3); the sum over strand levels and levels $>0$ open.

*What (8.6) says about PH3, and what it does not.* PH3 as the Hypotheses table states it is completeness and convergence, on the vectors $m_2P$, of the sum over the two strands' oscillator levels *and* the pair's relative momentum, in the form interchangeable with $m_2^{-1}$ at the collapse: $s_N(P,Q)=\sum_{I\le N}V_4(P,m_2^{-1}\lambda_I)\langle\lambda_I,m_2Q\rangle\to V_4(P,Q)$. (1) At level 0, $G_2=2g_T^2$ is constant on the block (049 (1.4)), so in the eigenbasis of $K_P$, $m_2^{-1}\lambda_I=(2g_T^2/\mu_I)\lambda_I$ and each term of $s_N$ is $\mu_I\langle P,\lambda_I\rangle\langle\lambda_I,Q\rangle$: the interchange with $m_2^{-1}$ is term-by-term exact, and for the external tachyon momentum eigenstates $s_N$ is the partial sum $\sum_{I\le N}\mu_I\lambda_I(\ell_0)\lambda_I(\ell_0')$ of (8.2), which (8.3) makes absolutely convergent and (8.2) makes equal to $K_P(\ell_0,\ell_0')=V_4$ — the ordering by $\lvert\mu_I\rvert$ that 049 (c4) needed is irrelevant. (2) *Completeness* is C3's contribution: $\ker K_P=0$ on $L^2_{\rm even}$ means the eigenfunctions with $\mu>0$ span the level-0 pair space, so the resolution of the identity inserted at the collapse is the spectral theorem with nothing missing. (3) *Basis.* The row's partial sums are written in the momentum basis, where they are the integral $\int d^{26}\ell\,K_P(\ell_0,\ell)(\cdots)$, absolutely convergent at level 0 by the Gaussian bound 049 (1.2) under PH25 — which also disposes of the row's momentum-part falsifier (a divergence of $\int d^Dk_a$ at fixed external momenta) at level 0; the eigenbasis form is R51's stand-in (iii), in which "partial sum" is basis-independent, and it is *sufficient* for the row, not equivalent to it. (4) *What stays open.* Levels $>0$: the oscillator sum is finite at each level, but $G_2$ is indefinite there and the sum over levels is the level-truncation limit (PH19's territory) — the row's falsifier "saturation drift of the partial level sums" is untouched; and complex $s$ (PH18, 18c.6). Hence the qualifier above, and the Hypotheses table keeps the level sum open.

### (1) The object and its three representations

\subsection*{Setup}

Level-0 tachyon-pair block, 049 (1.1)–(1.4): at fixed total momentum $P$ of the $s$-channel pair, $V_4$ restricted to the block is the kernel $K_P(s)(\ell,\ell')=C\,F(s,(\ell+\ell')^2,(\ell-\ell')^2)$ on $L^2_{\rm even}(\mathbb R^{26})$, $s=P^2$ in the record's sign convention; $G_2=2g_T^2\,\mathbb 1$ on the block, so $m_2=G_2^{-1}V_4=(2g_T^2)^{-1}K_P$. Eigenpairs $(\mu_I(s),\lambda_I)$, $I=(n,m,j)$, real orthonormal, $\mu_I>0$ at $C=+1$ (R53). **On-shell external data** (050 (8.5), under PH25): the four tachyons on shell fix the relative momenta of the two external pairs to $\lvert\ell_0\rvert=\lvert\ell_0'\rvert=\rho_0(s)$ with $\alpha'\rho_0^2=4-\alpha's/4$, and $x=\hat\ell_0\cdot\hat\ell_0'\in[-1,1]$ free; then $t=2\rho_0^2(1+x)$, $u=2\rho_0^2(1-x)$ and $s+t+u=16/\alpha'$ identically. The symmetric point is $\alpha's=16/3$, $x=0$ ($s=t=u$); 050's "on-shell point" is $x=1$ ($u=0$), the slowest point of the expansion (050 (8.4)). The on-shell region used below is $\mathcal O=\{(s,x):\alpha's<16,\ x\in[-1,1]\}$, where $\rho_0$ is real.

\subsection*{Derivation}

With $a_I(s)=\lambda_I(\ell_0)$, $b_I(s)=\lambda_I(\ell_0')$ (continuous eigenfunctions, 050 (8.1)(d)), the $\Sigma$-exchange at level 0 is the vertex–propagator–vertex chain $V_4(P,\cdot)\,m_2^{-1}\,V_4(\cdot,Q)$ (R29, 023 (3.1)–(3.4)). In the eigenbasis of $K_P(s)$ the three objects of 049 (c3) D1 read
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{(R-a) vertex:}\quad &V_4(\ell_0,\ell_0')=K_P(\ell_0,\ell_0')=\sum_I\mu_I\,a_Ib_I,\\
\text{(R-b) exchange:}\quad &E=\sum_I\underbrace{V_4(\ell_0,m_2^{-1}\lambda_I)}_{2g_T^2\,a_I}\;\underbrace{\langle\lambda_I,m_2\,\delta_{\ell_0'}\rangle}_{(2g_T^2)^{-1}\mu_I\,b_I}=\sum_I\mu_I^{\,}\,\mu_I^{-1}\,\mu_I\;a_Ib_I=\sum_I\mu_I\,a_Ib_I,\\
\text{(R-c) propagator:}\quad &m_2^{-1}=2g_T^2\sum_I\mu_I^{-1}\,\lambda_I\otimes\lambda_I .
\end{aligned}\tag{A.1}$$

\subsection*{Commentary}

- (R-a) carries $\mu_I$ and no inverse. (R-c) carries $\mu_I^{-1}$: it is the $\Sigma$-propagator itself, an unbounded operator on $L^2_{\rm even}$ since $\mu_I\to0$ (defined on the dense range of $K_P$, R53), whose matrix elements between momentum eigenstates do not exist; it never appears alone in the four-point amplitude. This is where a branch zero would produce a pole (PH18, complex $s$; 049 D1).
- (R-b) is the exchange diagram. Written as the chain it contains one $\mu_I^{-1}$ (from $m_2^{-1}\lambda_I$) between two vertex factors each carrying $\mu_I$; the cancellation $\mu_I\mu_I^{-1}\mu_I=\mu_I$ is legitimate term by term at every $s$ where $\mu_I(s)\ne0$, i.e. at every real $s$ by R53. After it, (R-b) coincides term by term with (R-a): this is the collapse $V_4(P,m_2^{-1}m_2Q)=V_4(P,Q)$ at level 0, in the eigenbasis form (iii) of R51 (049 (2.1)).
- **The on-shell limit concerns (R-b)**: the limit $s\to s_*$ of the exchange contribution, with the collapse performed at each $s$ of a neighbourhood of $s_*$ (not only at $s_*$). The two things it needs are (i) that (R-b) is defined at every real $s$ near $s_*$ — R53 ($\mu_I\ne0$, so $m_2^{-1}$ exists on the range, the collapse holds, and the eigenfunctions are complete) — and (ii) that its sum and its limit can be exchanged — R55 and item (2).

### (2) Analyticity of the on-shell limit and the interchange with the truncation

\subsection*{Setup}

As in (1); A1–A4 of 049; $K(s):=K_P(s)$ at $C=+1$. Inputs: R51 ($s\mapsto K(s)$ entire as a Hilbert–Schmidt-valued function, hence in operator norm, with the Gaussian bound 049 (1.2) uniform on compacts of complex $s$ up to $e^{O(\lvert\operatorname{Im}s\rvert)}$, 049 (1.5)); R53 ($K(s)>0$ on $L^2_{\rm even}$ at every real $s$); R55 (Mercer: (8.2)–(8.3) at every real $s$). Truncation by threshold: $E_\delta(s,x)=\sum_{I:\,\mu_I(s)>\delta}\mu_I\,a_Ib_I$, a finite sum (finitely many eigenvalues of a compact operator exceed $\delta$, multiplicities included). Tail on the diagonal: $\tau_\delta(s)=\sum_{I:\,\mu_I(s)\le\delta}\mu_I\,\lambda_I(\ell_0)^2$, which depends on $\ell_0$ only through $\rho_0(s)$ (the $m$-sum is the addition theorem, 050 (8.4)).

\subsection*{Derivation}

*(A.2) The full sum (C5.2).* For every real $s<16/\alpha'$ and $x\in[-1,1]$, by (A.1) and R55 ((8.2) holds at every pair of points, (8.3) makes it absolute),
$$E(s,x)=\sum_I\mu_I\,a_Ib_I=K(s)(\ell_0,\ell_0')=C\,F\Bigl(s,\ \tfrac{16-\alpha's}{2\alpha'}(1+x),\ \tfrac{16-\alpha's}{2\alpha'}(1-x)\Bigr),\tag{A.2}$$
using $2\rho_0^2=(16-\alpha's)/(2\alpha')$. The same holds at every real pair $(\ell_0,\ell_0')$, off shell included (R55 is pointwise at every point and 049 (1.1) is the off-shell kernel), so $E=C\,F(s,(\ell_0+\ell_0')^2,(\ell_0-\ell_0')^2)$ is jointly continuous and the approach to the shell from off-shell external data also gives the on-shell value. $F$ is entire in $(s,t,u)$ (045 C1; 049 (1.5)) and the arguments are polynomial in $(s,x)$, so $E$ is the restriction to $\mathcal O$ of an entire function of $(s,x)$: $\lim_{s\to s_*}E(s,x)=E(s_*,x)$, uniformly in $x$, at every $s_*<16/\alpha'$, $s_*=16/3$ included.

*(A.3) Analyticity of the truncations (C5.3 (i)).* Let $\delta>0$, $\delta\notin\operatorname{spec}K(s_*)$, and $\Gamma$ a closed contour in the resolvent set of $K(s_*)$ crossing the real axis at $\delta$ and at $\lVert K(s_*)\rVert+1$, so that it encloses exactly the eigenvalues $>\delta$ and not $0$. For $\lvert s-s_*\rvert<r$ ($r>0$ fixed by $\lVert K(s)-K(s_*)\rVert<\min_{z\in\Gamma}\lVert(K(s_*)-z)^{-1}\rVert^{-1}$, Neumann series) $\Gamma$ stays in the resolvent set of $K(s)$, and
$$B_\delta(s)=\frac1{2\pi\I}\oint_\Gamma z^{-1}\,(z-K(s))^{-1}\,dz\ \overset{s\ \rm real}{=}\ \sum_{\mu_I(s)>\delta}\mu_I^{-1}\,\lambda_I\otimes\lambda_I\tag{A.3}$$
is holomorphic in $s$ (the resolvent of a holomorphic family is holomorphic in $(s,z)$ where it exists; Kato, *Perturbation theory for linear operators*, VII §1.1–1.3, cited by section). With $k_{s,\ell}=K(s)(\ell,\cdot)\in L^2$ and the bilinear pairing of 049 (1.4), $\langle k_{s,\ell},\lambda_I\rangle=\mu_I\lambda_I(\ell)$, so
$$E_\delta(s,x)=\bigl\langle k_{s,\ell_0},\,B_\delta(s)\,k_{s,\ell_0'}\bigr\rangle=\sum_{n\ \rm even}\frac{\dim\mathcal Y_nP_n(x)}{\lvert S^{25}\rvert}\,g_{n,\delta}(s),\qquad g_{n,\delta}(s)=\sum_{j:\,\mu_{n,j}>\delta}\mu_{n,j}\,\psi_{n,j}(\rho_0(s))^2,\tag{A.4}$$
with finitely many $n$ contributing; $g_{n,\delta}$ is the same pairing for the wave-$n$ radial operator, and $\rho\mapsto k^{(n)}_{s,\rho}\in L^2(\lambda^{25}d\lambda)$ is holomorphic in $(s,\rho)$ (entire $F$, dominated convergence under the uniform Gaussian bound), $\rho_0(s)$ analytic for $\alpha's<16$. Hence **$E_\delta$ is real-analytic in $(s,x)$ on $\lvert s-s_*\rvert<r$**, a polynomial in $x$. The $\mu_I^{-1}$ of (R-b) sits in $B_\delta$ and is harmless because $\Gamma$ avoids $0$.

*Rellich, for individual branches.* Rellich's theorem (Kato II §6.2, finite dimension): a real-analytic family of Hermitian matrices on a real interval admits real-analytic eigenvalues and a real-analytic orthonormal eigenbasis on the whole interval, through crossings. Applied to the reduced family $K(s)$ on $\operatorname{Ran}E_\Gamma(s)$ (a finite-rank real-analytic family, transported to a fixed space by Kato's transformation function, Kato II §4.2, VII §1.3), it splits $g_{n,\delta}$ into real-analytic branches $\mu_{n,j}(s)\psi_{n,j}(\rho_0(s))^2$ near $s_*$, so every fixed finite set of branches gives a real-analytic partial sum. Not needed for (A.4)–(A.6), which use the group projection only. *Bounded away from zero (side remark):* the ordered eigenvalues $\mu_k(s)$ ($k$-th largest with multiplicity) satisfy $\lvert\mu_k(s)-\mu_k(s')\rvert\le\lVert K(s)-K(s')\rVert$ (Courant–Fischer min-max for a positive compact operator; Weyl's inequality), hence are continuous; by R53 each is $>0$ at every real $s$, so $\min_{s\in J}\mu_k(s)>0$ on every compact $J$. This is the form in which "every branch is bounded away from zero" holds (side remark; not used in (A.4)–(A.7)); the caveat of R53's critic (a labelled branch tending to $0$ with degenerating eigenvectors) concerns a branch followed through infinitely many crossings, which nothing here uses.

*(A.5) Uniform convergence of the truncations (C5.3 (ii)).* Cauchy–Schwarz on the tail, as in (8.3), with $\lvert\ell_0\rvert=\lvert\ell_0'\rvert=\rho_0(s)$:
$$\lvert E(s,x)-E_\delta(s,x)\rvert\le\bigl(\tau_\delta(s)\,\tau_\delta(s)\bigr)^{1/2}=\tau_\delta(s)\qquad\text{for all }x\in[-1,1].\tag{A.5}$$
Claim: $\sup_{s\in J}\tau_\delta(s)\to0$ as $\delta\downarrow0$, for every compact $J\subset(-\infty,16/\alpha')$. Write $\tau_\delta(s)=K(s)(\ell_0,\ell_0)-g_\delta(s)$, $g_\delta(s)=\sum_{\mu_I>\delta}\mu_I\lambda_I(\ell_0)^2$. (a) $K(s)(\ell_0,\ell_0)=C\,F(s,4\rho_0^2,0)$ is continuous in $s$. (b) $g_\delta$ is lower semicontinuous: at $s_1$, pick $\delta'\in(\delta,\ \min\{\mu\in\operatorname{spec}K(s_1):\mu>\delta\})$ (if no eigenvalue exceeds $\delta$ at $s_1$, $g_\delta(s_1)=0\le g_\delta$); then $\delta'\notin\operatorname{spec}K(s_1)$, $g_{\delta'}$ is continuous near $s_1$ by (A.3)–(A.4), $g_{\delta'}(s_1)=g_\delta(s_1)$, and $g_\delta\ge g_{\delta'}$ everywhere because **every term is $\ge0$ (R53)**; so $\liminf_{s\to s_1}g_\delta(s)\ge g_\delta(s_1)$. (c) Hence $\tau_\delta$ is upper semicontinuous, $\ge0$, non-increasing as $\delta\downarrow0$, and $\to0$ pointwise on $J$ (R55: $\sum_I\mu_I\lambda_I(\ell)^2=K(\ell,\ell)$ at every real $s$). (d) Dini for upper semicontinuous functions: for $\epsilon>0$ and $\delta_m\downarrow0$ the sets $A_m=\{s\in J:\tau_{\delta_m}(s)\ge\epsilon\}$ are closed, nested and have empty intersection, so one is empty; so
$$\sup_{(s,x)\in J\times[-1,1]}\lvert E(s,x)-E_\delta(s,x)\rvert\le\sup_{s\in J}\tau_\delta(s)\xrightarrow[\delta\to0]{}0.\tag{A.6}$$

*Consequence.* Choose $\delta_m\downarrow0$ outside the countable set $\operatorname{spec}K(s_*)$. By (A.4) $E_{\delta_m}$ is continuous at $s_*$, and by (A.6) the convergence is uniform near $s_*$, so
$$\lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{s\to s_*}E_{\delta_m}(s,x)=\lim_{s\to s_*}\lim_{m\to\infty}E_{\delta_m}(s,x)=E(s_*,x)=C\,F\bigl(s_*,t_*,u_*\bigr),\tag{A.7}$$
at $s_*=16/3$ with $t_*=u_*=16/3$ at the symmetric point $x=0$, and on the whole on-shell region $\mathcal O$. (A.7) as a double limit needs only continuity of $E_{\delta_m}$ at $s_*$ and pointwise Mercer at $s_*$; what (A.6) adds is the joint limit $E_{\delta_m}(s_m)\to E(s_*)$ for any $s_m\to s_*$, $\delta_m\downarrow0$, thresholds on the spectrum at $s_m\ne s_*$ included — the form a truncated numerical evaluation needs.

\subsection*{Commentary}

- *(A.2)* is the limit statement, and at level 0 it is short: once the collapse holds at every real $s$ (R53 for $m_2^{-1}$ and completeness, R55 for the pointwise sum), the exchange **is** the contact kernel at the external points, and the contact kernel is entire. No property of the spectrum near $s_*$ is used. The on-shell data enter only through $\rho_0^2$, linear in $s$, so no square root of the mass shell appears.
- *(A.3)–(A.7)* carry the part that is not automatic: a computation that truncates first (a finite set of eigenmodes, as any Nyström or level-truncated evaluation does), goes on shell, and then removes the truncation, gets the same number. This is a double limit, not a single one: (A.7)'s repeated limits alone need only continuity of $E_{\delta_m}$ at $s_*$ (threshold off the spectrum there) and pointwise Mercer at $s_*$, which is little more than (A.2); the joint limit that a truncated numerical evaluation actually performs — $s_m\to s_*$ and $\delta_m\downarrow0$ together, thresholds crossing the spectrum at $s_m\ne s_*$ allowed — is what needs the full (A.3)–(A.6): the threshold must avoid the spectrum at $s_*$ (otherwise $E_\delta$ jumps by a whole term as an eigenvalue crosses $\delta$ — the break leg of the check), and the tail must be uniformly small near $s_*$ — Dini, which needs the positivity of every term (R53) and the pointwise Mercer identity (R55).
- *Where each input enters.* R51: operator-norm analyticity (A.3), continuity (A.5)(a). R53: $\mu_I\ne0$ so (R-b) is defined and collapses at every $s$ near $s_*$ ((1)); every tail term $\ge0$ ((A.5)(b)–(c)) (side remark; not used in (A.4)–(A.7): ordered eigenvalues bounded below on compacts). R55: the pointwise identity (A.2) and the pointwise limit in (A.5)(c).
- *Limiting case.* At $x=1$ ($u=0$), (A.5) is an equality in the $\ell_0'=\ell_0$ sense: the tail is exactly $\tau_\delta$, the slowest point (050 (8.4)); at $x=0$ the partial-wave alternation makes the actual tail far smaller than the bound, which is why the check runs there with $n\le22$.
