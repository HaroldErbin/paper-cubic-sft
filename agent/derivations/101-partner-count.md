### Block F – The partner presentation is the multiplier form in rotated variables (101b #1)

\subsection*{Setup}

A1–A3. Let $S[\Psi,\Sigma_2,\Sigma_{\ge3}]$ be the elementary action without partner (any order, any partitions, any $c_6$): $S=S_{\rm kin}(\Psi)+\tfrac{g^2}{4!}\big[-V_4(\Sigma_2^2)+2V_4(\Sigma_2,\Psi^2)\big]+S_{\rm c}[\Psi,\Sigma_2,\Sigma_{\ge3}]$, where $S_{\rm c}$ collects every other coupling (all cubic, A1). The **rule** builds the partner action from $S$: (a) every coupling of $S_{\rm c}$ with a $\Sigma_2$ leg is taken with $\Sigma_2\to\Sigma_2+\Upsilon_2$ in that leg, i.e. $S_{\rm c}\to S_{\rm c}[\Psi,\Sigma_2+\Upsilon_2,\Sigma_{\ge3}]$; (b) the quadratic terms stay, with $+\tfrac{g^2}{4!}V_4(\Upsilon_2^2)$ added and no $V_4(\Upsilon_2,\Psi^2)$:
$$S^\Upsilon[\Psi,\Sigma_2,\Upsilon_2,\Sigma_{\ge3}]:=S_{\rm kin}+\tfrac{g^2}{4!}\big[-V_4(\Sigma_2^2)+2V_4(\Sigma_2,\Psi^2)+V_4(\Upsilon_2^2)\big]+S_{\rm c}[\Psi,\Sigma_2+\Upsilon_2,\Sigma_{\ge3}]. \tag{F.1}$$

\subsection*{Derivation}

With $\Sigma_2':=\Sigma_2+\Upsilon_2$, bilinearity and symmetry of $V_4$ give
$$\begin{aligned}
&-V_4(\Sigma_2'^2)+2V_4(\Sigma_2',\Psi^2)+2V_4(\Upsilon_2,\Sigma_2'-\Psi^2)\\
&=-V_4(\Sigma_2^2)-2V_4(\Sigma_2,\Upsilon_2)-V_4(\Upsilon_2^2)+2V_4(\Sigma_2,\Psi^2)+2V_4(\Upsilon_2,\Psi^2)+2V_4(\Upsilon_2,\Sigma_2)+2V_4(\Upsilon_2^2)-2V_4(\Upsilon_2,\Psi^2)\\
&=-V_4(\Sigma_2^2)+2V_4(\Sigma_2,\Psi^2)+V_4(\Upsilon_2^2),
\end{aligned}\tag{F.2}$$
hence, exactly and at every order,
$$\boxed{\;S^\Upsilon[\Psi,\Sigma_2,\Upsilon_2,\Sigma_{\ge3}]=S[\Psi,\Sigma_2',\Sigma_{\ge3}]+\frac{2g^2}{4!}\,V_4\big(\Upsilon_2,\ \Sigma_2'-\Psi^2\big),\qquad \Sigma_2'=\Sigma_2+\Upsilon_2.\;}\tag{F.3}$$
The change of variables $(\Sigma_2,\Upsilon_2)\to(\Sigma_2',\Upsilon_2)$ is linear and invertible, so the joint classical point is the same in both sets. In (F.3) $\Upsilon_2$ appears only linearly:
$$\partial_{\Upsilon_2}:\ p_{2,2}(\Sigma_2'-\Psi^2)=0\ \Rightarrow\ \Sigma_2'=\Psi^2\ \text{(on the quotient, A3)};\qquad \partial_{\Sigma_2'}:\ \Upsilon_2^*=-\frac{4!}{2g^2}\,p_{2,2}^{-1}\,\partial_{\Sigma_2}S\big|_{\Sigma_2=\Psi^2};\qquad \partial_{\Sigma_{\ge3}}S[\Psi,\Psi^2,\Sigma_{\ge3}]=0. \tag{F.4}$$
The linear term vanishes at the solution, so
$$S^\Upsilon(\text{classical})=S[\Psi,\Psi^2,\Sigma_{\ge3}^*]:\qquad \Delta^\Upsilon(\Psi)=\Big[S[\Psi,\Psi^2,\Sigma^*_{\ge3}]-\sum_n\tfrac{g^{n-2}}{n!}V_n(\Psi^n)\Big]. \tag{F.5}$$
The deficit of the rule's partner presentation is the exchange content of the $\Sigma_{k\ge3}$ sector with $\Sigma_2$ frozen at $\Psi^2$: it contains no $p_{2,2}^{-1}$ line, and it is independent of every coupling with an $\Upsilon_2$ leg. Every structure with a $p_{2,2}^{-1}$ line therefore vanishes identically under the rule, at every order and for every $c_6$ and partition choice; what is left is the $\Sigma_{\ge3}$ sector, where the completed squares of block C act.

**The rule in the record's parameters.** A coupling $\kappa V_n(\Sigma_2^m,R)$ gives $\binom mj\kappa\,V_n(\Upsilon_2^j\Sigma_2^{m-j},R)$:
$$\begin{aligned}
&V_5:\ b=2,\ c=1;\qquad V_6:\ d=3c_6,\ d'=2(1-c_6),\ f_6=3c_6,\ f_{6b}=c_6\ (d+d'=2+c_6\ \text{as A5});\\
&V_7:\ f_7=2b_7,\ f_{7b}=b_7;\qquad V_8:\ f_8=\delta,\ f_8'=2\gamma,\ f_{8c}=\gamma;\qquad V_9:\ f_9=\mu;\qquad V_{10}:\ \rho\,V_{10}(\Upsilon_2,\Sigma_4^2)\ (\text{order }9).
\end{aligned}\tag{F.6}$$
Against the model's closure families: at $c_6=0$, $d=0$, $d'=2$, $f_6=f_{6b}=0$ ((E.7) and then (E.3), (E.5)); at $c_6=\tfrac13$, $d=1$ and $f_6=-\tfrac38+\tfrac{11}4-\tfrac{11}8=1=3c_6$ ((D.4)); $f_7=2b_7=4$, $f_{7b}=b_7=2=\tfrac{d^2}4+2$ at $d=0$; $f_8+f_8'=\delta+2\gamma=4$ at $(\delta,\gamma)=(0,2)$ ((E.5)); $f_{8c}=\gamma=2=-\tfrac{16}4+12-6$ at $f_8'=4$ ((E.7)); $f_9=\mu=-2\lambda$ ((E.7)); Step 2 without $\Sigma_4$ ($e_8=\delta=1$): $f_8=1=d+1$ at $d=0$ ((D.6)). Every closure value of steps 2–3 at $d=0$ is the rule's value. The model's families are wider than the rule ($d$ free through order 7, $f_8'$ free at order 8): a departure from the rule at order $m$ is a change of the $\Upsilon_2$ couplings that no structure of orders $m$, $m+1$ sees, and the model shows it seen at $m+2$ ($d$ at order 8, through $\delta_{27}\propto d^3$, $\delta_{29}\propto d^2$). Its assumptions: strand conservation (ghost saturation, R23), normalisation $g_s^{n-2}/n!$, at most cubic, and the saddle orders $o$ ($o=0$ for a field whose kinetic term and leading source sit at the same order, $o=1$ for a source-deprived partner).

$$D=\sum_j n_j-2\sum_\ell s_\ell=\#\{\text{external }\Psi\},\qquad \mathrm{ord}=\sum_j(n_j-2)-\sum_\ell(2s_\ell-2)=D-2. \tag{A.2}$$
Node admissibility (the elimination can only produce nodes realised by couplings of the admissible action):

- N1 $n\ge3$.
- N2 collapses: a node with one line of strand $s$ and $e=s$ externals is $p_{s,s}^{-1}\circ V_{2s}(\cdot,\Psi^s)=\Psi^s$ (Veronese); a node with two lines of equal strand $s$ and $e=0$ is the kinetic form, $p_{s,s}^{-1}V_{2s}p_{s,s}^{-1}=p_{s,s}^{-1}$. Both are removed.

The statement "no $\Upsilon_2$ coupling reaches a $p_{2,2}^{-1}$-free tree" (lemma B.1) holds within the class of cubic elementary presentations whose auxiliaries are eliminated by Gaussian integrals at tree level (A1–A3); the physical reason for the class is the paper's (every vertex elementary).
