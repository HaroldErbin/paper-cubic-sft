## 2. Part A.2 — Truncation lemma (derived)

\subsection*{Setup}
A1–A3. Write the unrestricted-ghost-number action as $S=\sum_{n\ge2}g^{n-2}S_n$, $S_n(\Psi)=\tfrac1{n!}\{\Psi^n\}_0$ ($S_2=\tfrac12\langle\Psi,Q\Psi\rangle$), each $S_n$ even and homogeneous of degree $n$ in the field/antifield coordinates. The truncation at order $k$ is $S^{(k)}=\sum_{n=2}^{k+2}g^{n-2}S_n$. The antibracket (C2) is bilinear and, on even functionals, symmetric: $(S_a,S_b)=(S_b,S_a)$.

\subsection*{Derivation}
$$\begin{aligned}
(S,S)&=\sum_{m\ge0}g^m\,\mathcal J_m,\qquad \mathcal J_m:=\sum_{\substack{a+b=m+4\\a,b\ge2}}(S_a,S_b), \qquad (2.1)\\
(S,S)=0&\iff \mathcal J_m=0\ \ \forall m, \qquad (2.2)\\
(S^{(k)},S^{(k)})&=\sum_{m\ge0}g^m\sum_{\substack{a+b=m+4\\2\le a,b\le k+2}}(S_a,S_b), \qquad (2.3)\\
[g^m](S^{(k)},S^{(k)})&=\mathcal J_m=0\qquad (m\le k), \qquad (2.4)\\
[g^{k+1}](S^{(k)},S^{(k)})&=\mathcal J_{k+1}-2(S_2,S_{k+3})=-2(S_2,S_{k+3}). \qquad (2.5)
\end{aligned}$$
With $\partial_rS_n/\partial\psi^s=\tfrac1{(n-1)!}\{\Psi^{n-1},\Phi_s\}_0$ and $\partial_lS_n/\partial\psi^*_s\propto\{\Psi^{n-1},\tilde\Phi_s\}_0$ (Zwiebach (4.73)), the completeness relation of the field/antifield basis (Zwiebach (4.34), (4.77)–(4.80) at $g=0$) gives
$$
(S_a,S_b)+(S_b,S_a)=-\frac{1}{(a-1)!\,(b-1)!}\Big(\{\Psi^{a-1},[\Psi^{b-1}]_0\}_0+\{\Psi^{b-1},[\Psi^{a-1}]_0\}_0\Big)\cdot c, \qquad (2.6)
$$
with a convention-dependent nonzero constant $c$ common to all $(a,b)$ (Zwiebach's normalisation gives (4.80) at $g_1=g_2=0$). Hence
$$
[g^{k+1}](S^{(k)},S^{(k)})=-2(S_2,S_{k+3})\ \propto\ \frac{1}{(k+2)!}\Big(\langle\Psi,Q[\Psi^{k+2}]_0\rangle+(k+2)\langle\Psi,[\Psi^{k+1},Q\Psi]_0\rangle\Big), \qquad (2.7)
$$
the $\Psi$-contraction of the two terms of the arity-$(k+2)$ $L_\infty$ relation that contain the dropped bracket $\ell_{k+2}=[\cdot^{k+2}]_0$ (dual to $V_{k+3}$), equivalently minus the sum of its terms built from kept brackets only:
$$
-2(S_2,S_{k+3})=\sum_{\substack{a+b=k+5\\3\le a,b\le k+2}}(S_a,S_b). \qquad (2.8)
$$

\subsection*{Commentary}
(2.1): bilinearity and $\deg_g S_n=n-2$. (2.2): $(S,S)$ is a formal power series in $g$ whose coefficients are independent functionals. (2.3)–(2.4): for $m\le k$ every pair with $a+b=m+4\le k+4$ and $a,b\ge2$ has $a,b\le k+2$, so the truncated and full coefficients coincide. (2.5): at $m=k+1$, $a+b=k+5$, the only pairs with an index $>k+2$ are $(2,k+3)$ and $(k+3,2)$, equal by symmetry. (2.6)–(2.7) are heuristic and not load-bearing (a sum of two brackets, an unnamed constant $c$, "$\propto$"): they only indicate that the remainder is the $\Psi$-contraction of the $\ell_{k+2}$ terms of the arity-$(k+2)$ relation; the lemma is (2.5) and (2.8), which do not use them. (2.8) is (2.5) rewritten with $\mathcal J_{k+1}=0$; it is the form that is computable from the kept vertices alone. Orders $m\ge k+2$ of the truncated bracket involve further dropped vertices and are not controlled; nothing is claimed about them.

\subsection*{Result}
$$\boxed{(S^{(k)},S^{(k)})=g^{k+1}\Big(-2(S_2,S_{k+3})\Big)+O(g^{k+2}),\qquad -2(S_2,S_{k+3})=\sum_{\substack{a+b=k+5\\3\le a,b\le k+2}}(S_a,S_b)}$$

- $k=2$ (R1, vertices through $V_4$): $(S^{(2)},S^{(2)})=g^3\,[2(S_3,S_4)]+O(g^4)=-2g^3(S_2,S_5)+O(g^4)$.
- $k=3$ (R17, through $V_5$): $(S^{(3)},S^{(3)})=g^4\,[2(S_3,S_5)+(S_4,S_4)]+O(g^5)=-2g^4(S_2,S_6)+O(g^5)$.

The remainder is generically nonzero (it vanishes iff $V_{k+3}$ is $Q$-closed, i.e. iff $\ell_{k+2}$ could be dropped consistently); this is what Part B's `B2-rem` tests.

**(d) Properness (C3.4).** Properness in the sense of Henneaux–Teitelboim (ch. 17, paraphrased; section number not verified; also Gomis–París–Samuel 1995 §3, from memory): on the stationary surface $\Sigma=\{\partial S/\partial z=0\}$ of the field–antifield space, the Hessian $H_{AB}=\partial_A\partial_BS$ has rank $N=\tfrac12\dim$. For a field theory, rank means rank at generic momentum, i.e. of $H$ as an operator on field configurations. In free Maxwell theory the pointwise rank drops on the light cone, which reflects propagating modes, not missing gauge generators. The argument below replaces the earlier citation of Zwiebach (3.36)–(3.42), which proves that the kinetic term solves the CME and says nothing about rank.

(i) *$g_s=0$.* $Q$ commutes with $L_0^\pm$ and with the momentum, so work in a block $\mathcal H'_{k,\ell}$ of fixed momentum $k$ and level $\ell$ of the unrestricted, level-matched, $b_0^-$-annihilated space (finite-dimensional). BPZ pairs $G$ with $5-G$, so fields ($G\le2$) and antifields ($G\ge3$) are halves: $N=\tfrac12\dim\mathcal H'_{k,\ell}$. The Hessian of $S_2$ is $H_0=\omega(\cdot,Q\cdot)$, of rank $\operatorname{rank}Q$. At generic momentum $L_0^+\neq0$ on the block, and $\{Q,b_0^+\}=L_0^+$ makes $b_0^+/L_0^+$ a contracting homotopy (it preserves $b_0^-=0$ and $L_0^-=0$), so $\ker Q=\operatorname{im}Q$ and
$$\operatorname{rank}H_0=\dim\operatorname{im}Q=\tfrac12\dim\mathcal H'_{k,\ell}=N. \qquad (3.4)$$
(ii) *Upper bound from the CME.* Differentiating twice, $\partial_A\partial_B(S,S)=2(HPH)_{AB}\pm2\,\partial_CS\,P^{CD}\partial_D\partial_A\partial_BS$ (signs as in convention C2, $P$ the non-degenerate antibracket bivector). On $\Sigma$ the second term vanishes:
$$HPH\big|_\Sigma=\tfrac12\partial\partial(S,S)\big|_\Sigma=O(g_s^{k+1})\quad\text{for }S=S^{(k)}_{\rm BV}\ \text{(C3.3)}. \qquad (3.5)$$
(iii) *Rank $=N$ order by order.* In a basis adapted to $H_0$, $H_0=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$ with $A$ invertible of size $N$. $A$ is $Q:C\to I$, and $A^{-1}$ is the propagator $b_0^+/L_0^+$ at generic momentum. At $g_s=0$ the CME is exact ($Q^2=0$), so $H_0PH_0=0$ gives $P_{11}=0$, hence $P_{12}$ is invertible because $P$ is non-degenerate. On the formal neighbourhood of the origin in $\Sigma$, $H=\begin{pmatrix}A'&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}=H_0+O(g_s,z)$ with $A'=A+O(g_s,z)$ invertible (the lower-semicontinuity step). With $L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-cA'^{-1}&1\end{pmatrix}$, $R=\begin{pmatrix}1&-A'^{-1}b\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$,
$$LHR=\operatorname{diag}(A',D'),\quad D'=d-cA'^{-1}b,\qquad L(HPH)R=\operatorname{diag}(A',D')\,P'\,\operatorname{diag}(A',D'),\quad P'=R^{-1}PL^{-1}=P+O(g_s,z). \qquad (3.6)$$
The $(1,2)$ block is $A'P'_{12}D'=O(g_s^{k+1})$ by (3.5), with $A'$ and $P'_{12}$ invertible, so
$$D'=O(g_s^{k+1}),\qquad \operatorname{rank}H=N+\operatorname{rank}D'=N\ \text{ through order }g_s^k. \qquad (3.7)$$
(iv) *The $(\hat Y,\hat Y^+)$ block.* The Hessian is $\operatorname{diag}(-\tfrac{g_s^2}{12}p,0)$, block-diagonal with the $\Psi$ sector (056 §3(e)). Its rank is $\dim\hat Y$, half the block, for $g_s\neq0$ and $p$ invertible on the quotient (PH1, PH5); order by order it is the rank after the rescaling $\hat Y'=g_s\hat Y$, $\hat Y'^+=\hat Y^+/g_s$ (canonical for $g_s\neq0$), which makes the block $-\tfrac1{12}p$. So
$$\operatorname{rank}H_{S_{\rm BV}}=N_\Psi+\dim\hat Y\quad\text{through order }g_s^k,\ \text{generic momentum}. \qquad (3.8)$$
(v) *On-shell momenta.* Where $L_0^+$ has a zero eigenvalue on the block (the mass shells at every level, massless states included, and $k=0$), $H_Q\neq0$ and $\operatorname{rank}H_0=N-\tfrac12\dim H_Q$ there. These are the physical and zero-momentum states of the free theory, a measure-zero set of momenta that does not count against properness in the generic-rank sense. The Schur step needs the propagator $A^{-1}$ and is not made there. In the interacting theory the vertices couple momenta, so (3.7) holds with all momenta off the shells; the on-shell sector (massless tadpoles, dilaton, zero-momentum states) is excluded, not treated. Literature precedent for this argument, for the open-string master action: Bochicchio 1987, Thorn 1989 (from memory, bibliographic details unverified).

(vi) *What this does and does not close in 056.* It closes 056 C2.4 and the properness clause of C2.3 to the truncation order. It does **not** upgrade 056's elimination theorem (§3(f)), which stays "paraphrased, not checked against the source": C3 supplies its premise, not its statement. The reduced action itself ($\hat Y=0$ gives $S^\Psi_{\rm BV}$) needs no theorem (056 §3(f)).
