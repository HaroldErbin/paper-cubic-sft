\subsection*{Derivation}
*Exact $\Sigma$-equation (C1.1).* With $\Gamma[\Sigma]$ as in A3 and $\partial_\Sigma$ the derivative along $\delta\Sigma\in\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H$,
$$
\partial_\Sigma\Gamma[\Sigma]\cdot\delta\Sigma=\frac{\int\mathcal D\Psi\,(\partial_\Sigma S\cdot\delta\Sigma)\,e^{S/\hbar}}{\int\mathcal D\Psi\,e^{S/\hbar}}=\big\langle\partial_\Sigma S\cdot\delta\Sigma\big\rangle_\Sigma,\qquad
\partial_\Sigma S_2\cdot\delta\Sigma=-\tfrac{g^2}{12}V_4(\Sigma,\delta\Sigma)+\tfrac{g^2}{12}V_4(\Psi^{\otimes2},\delta\Sigma). \tag{1.1}
$$
Hence
$$
\partial_\Sigma\Gamma\cdot\delta\Sigma=\tfrac{g^2}{12}\,V_4\big(\langle\Psi^{\otimes2}\rangle_\Sigma-\Sigma,\ \delta\Sigma\big), \tag{1.2}
$$
and the stationarity of $\Gamma$ (equivalently of $\mathcal V_{\rm eff}$) is
$$
\boxed{\;V_4\big(\Sigma-\langle\Psi^{\otimes2}\rangle_\Sigma,\ \cdot\,\big)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Sigma=\langle\Psi\otimes\Psi\rangle_\Sigma\ \text{on }\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H/\ker V_4\;} \tag{1.3}
$$
where $\langle\cdot\rangle_\Sigma$ is the full $\Psi$-average at fixed $\Sigma$ ($V_3$ and all loops included). (1.3) is a formal identity of the path integral (order by order in $\hbar$, or under A3; Conventions) and the quantum form of R1's $\Sigma=\Psi^{\otimes2}$; at $S_3$ the R17 term adds $\tfrac{g^3}{60}V_5(\Sigma,\langle\Psi\rangle_\Sigma,\delta\Sigma)$ to (1.2).

*Mean field = Gaussian evaluation (C1.2).* Drop $V_3$ (and $V_5$). $S_2$ is then quadratic in $\Psi$, $S_2=-\tfrac{g^2}{24}V_4(\Sigma,\Sigma)+\tfrac12\omega(\Psi,Q_\Sigma\Psi)$ with $Q_\Sigma=Q+\tfrac{g^2}6\ell_3(\Sigma,\cdot)$ (R69 C1.1: the $\tfrac{2g^2}{4!}V_4(\Sigma,\Psi^{\otimes2})$ term has Hessian $\tfrac{g^2}6V_4(\Sigma,\cdot,\cdot)$). The Gaussian $\int\mathcal D\Psi\,e^{\frac1{2\hbar}\omega(\Psi,Q_\Sigma\Psi)}=\det\!\big(-\omega Q_\Sigma/2\pi\hbar\big)^{-1/2}$ (A3 for the indefinite directions) gives
$$
\boxed{\;\mathcal V_{\rm eff}[\Sigma]=\tfrac{g^2}{24}V_4(\Sigma,\Sigma)+\tfrac\hbar2\operatorname{Tr}\log(-\omega Q_\Sigma)+\text{const}+O(\hbar^2,\ \hbar gV_3)\;} \tag{1.4}
$$
the transcription of (21.6.69); $\Gamma=-\mathcal V_{\rm eff}$, so on the action side the one-loop term is $-\tfrac\hbar2\operatorname{Tr}\log(-\omega Q_\Sigma)$ (the brief's "$+\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}\log Q_\Sigma$" is the potential-side sign, C5).

*The $O(\Sigma)$ term (C1.3).* With $M_0=-\omega Q$, $\delta M=-\tfrac{g^2}6V_4(\Sigma,\cdot,\cdot)$ and $G:=M_0^{-1}=(-\omega Q)^{-1}\in\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H$ (the Siegel-gauge propagator $-b_0^+/L_0^+$ with the sign of C5),
$$
\tfrac\hbar2\operatorname{Tr}\log(M_0+\delta M)=\tfrac\hbar2\operatorname{Tr}\log M_0-\tfrac{\hbar g^2}{12}\,V_4(\Sigma,G)-\tfrac{\hbar g^4}{144}\operatorname{Tr}\big[GV_4(\Sigma)\,GV_4(\Sigma)\big]+O(\Sigma^3), \tag{1.5}
$$
where $V_4(\Sigma,G)=\sum_{ij}G^{ij}V_4(\Sigma,e_i,e_j)$ is the quartic vertex with two legs closed by the propagator, evaluated on $\Sigma$. In the $\Psi$-picture ($\Sigma\to\Psi^{\otimes2}$) this is $-\tfrac{\hbar g^2}{12}V_4(\Psi,\Psi,G)$, the self-energy $\tfrac{\hbar g^2}6\ell_3(G,\cdot)$ read on the potential side. This is *one third* of the Hartree self-energy $\Pi_{\rm H}=\tfrac{\hbar g^2}2\ell_3(G,\cdot)$ of the quartic theory $\tfrac12\omega(\Psi,Q\Psi)+\tfrac{g^2}{24}V_4(\Psi^4)$ (the Hessian of $\tfrac{g^2}{24}V_4(\Psi^4)$ is $\tfrac{g^2}2V_4(P_0,\cdot,\cdot)$, C4): the direct channel of R66. The other two thirds, in the cubic theory, come from the $\Sigma$ exchange with one $\Psi$ contraction ($\tfrac{\hbar g^2}3$), which the Gaussian evaluation at fixed $\Sigma$ does not contain (check `C2.7-third`). It is a part of the quartic-vertex contribution to the genus-one two-point function (the one-loop mass shift), *not* the one-point tadpole (which is $\tfrac{\hbar g}2V_3(\Psi,G)$ and needs $V_3$, O4). The $O(\Sigma^2)$ term is the $\Sigma$-exchange bubble: two $V_4$ vertices, two propagators, the one-loop correction to the $\Sigma$ kinetic term. At $\hbar\to0$, (1.4) is $\tfrac{g^2}{24}V_4(\Sigma,\Sigma)$, R69's trivial-branch $\Gamma_0$ with the potential-side sign.

*Gap equation and its perturbative root (C1.4).* $\partial_\Sigma$ of (1.4), using $\partial_\Sigma\operatorname{Tr}\log M_\Sigma\cdot\delta\Sigma=\operatorname{Tr}[M_\Sigma^{-1}\partial_\Sigma M_\Sigma\cdot\delta\Sigma]=-\tfrac{g^2}6V_4(\delta\Sigma,G_\Sigma)$:
$$
\boxed{\;\tfrac{g^2}{12}V_4(\Sigma,\delta\Sigma)=\tfrac{\hbar g^2}{12}V_4(G_\Sigma,\delta\Sigma)\ \ \forall\delta\Sigma,\qquad G_\Sigma:=(-\omega Q_\Sigma)^{-1};\quad\text{i.e.}\quad \Sigma=\hbar\,G_\Sigma\ \text{on }\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H/\ker V_4\;} \tag{1.6}
$$
This is (1.3) with $\langle\Psi\otimes\Psi\rangle_\Sigma$ replaced by its Gaussian value $\hbar G_\Sigma$: the mean field is the self-consistent dressed propagator, the transcription of (21.6.70) ($\Delta_0\propto\int V\Delta_0/\sqrt{E^2+\Delta_0^2}$, whose right-hand side is the anomalous propagator in the gapped Gaussian). By R69 C1.2, $S=g^{-2}\hat S(g\Psi,g^2\Sigma)$, so $e^{S/\hbar}=e^{\hat S/\hbar g^2}$ and (1.6) in scaled variables reads $\hat\Sigma=\hbar g^2\,\hat G_{\hat\Sigma}$: the loop parameter is $\hbar g^2$. For $Q$ invertible ($G$ finite) the implicit function theorem gives a unique root near the origin,
$$
\Sigma_*=\hbar\,G+\hbar^2\,\tfrac{g^2}{6}\,G\,V_4(G)\,G+O(\hbar^3)\qquad(\text{matrix products in }\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H,\ \omega=\mathbb 1), \tag{1.7}
$$
the perturbative (normal-phase) branch, $\Sigma_*\to0$ as $\hbar\to0$. A BCS-type root, $\hat\Sigma=O(1)$ at $\hbar g^2\ll1$, needs $\hat G_{\hat\Sigma}=O(1/\hbar g^2)$: a near-divergence of the dressed propagator, i.e. $\Sigma$ near R69's hypersurface $D$. In the finite model such roots exist generically: one per wall of $D$, at distance $\hbar g^2$ (in $\hat\Sigma$) from the wall, a maximum of $\mathcal V_{\rm eff}$ along the normal (O3, (2.3a), check `C2.3-nearwall`). They are near-wall roots of an uncontrolled truncation, not condensates. This is where the analogy is decided (O1).

\subsection*{Commentary}
- (1.1): the $\Sigma$-derivative passes through the $\Psi$-integral because $\Sigma$ is an external parameter of the integrand; no Jacobian since $\Sigma$ is not integrated. The same identity at $S_3$ picks up the R17 term.
- (1.4): the Gaussian sign follows C5: on the Euclidean massive sector $\omega(\Psi,Q\Psi)<0$, so $-\omega Q>0$ and $\det(-\omega Q_\Sigma)^{-1/2}$ is the convergent Gaussian; $\log\det=\operatorname{Tr}\log$. The constant collects $\log(2\pi\hbar)$ per mode and is $\Sigma$-independent.
- (1.5): $\operatorname{Tr}[M_0^{-1}\delta M]=-\tfrac{g^2}6\sum_{ij}G^{ij}V_4(\Sigma,e_i,e_j)$ with $G=M_0^{-1}=(-\omega Q)^{-1}$, the propagator with the sign that makes $\langle\Psi\otimes\Psi\rangle_0=\hbar G$ (weight $e^{-\omega(\Psi,M_0\Psi)/2\hbar}$); the factor $\tfrac\hbar2$ gives $-\tfrac{\hbar g^2}{12}$. Its $\Sigma$-derivative, $-\tfrac{\hbar g^2}{12}V_4(G,\delta\Sigma)$, is the $\hbar$-term of (1.6) at $\Sigma=0$: (1.5) and (1.6) carry the same sign, and (1.7) follows from $G_\Sigma=G-G\,\delta M\,G+\dots=G+\tfrac{g^2}6GV_4(\Sigma)G+O(\Sigma^2)$.
- Dimensions and orders against R1: every term of (1.4) is $O(g^2)$ at fixed $\Sigma$ with $\Sigma$ counting two legs, as R1's block; $\hbar$ multiplies exactly one power of $g^2$ per loop ($\hbar g^2$, C1.4), the standard closed-string loop counting.

\subsection*{Result}
Transcribed effective potential (1.4); gap equation (1.6), $\Sigma=\hbar G_\Sigma$ on the quotient with $G_\Sigma=(-\omega Q_\Sigma)^{-1}=\langle\Psi\otimes\Psi\rangle_\Sigma/\hbar$ (the string form on the quotient Conditional(PH1)); Schwinger–Dyson form (1.3), a formal identity; perturbative root (1.7). The gap equation is the direct-channel third of the Hartree equation (R66): $Q_{\rm eff}=Q+\tfrac{\hbar g^2}6\ell_3(G_{\rm eff},\cdot)$ versus Hartree's $Q_{\rm eff}=Q+\tfrac{\hbar g^2}2\ell_3(G_{\rm eff},\cdot)$ (see §2 O7).

## 2. Obstacles (C2)

Each obstacle names the point at which the BCS analogy breaks, with the equation that shows it. None is a no-go on the mean-field question; together they fix what a mean-field statement can be (§4).

**O1 — No Fermi surface (C2.1).** Weinberg's mechanism is the density of states at $E(\mathbf p)=0$: the shell integral $\int d\ell\,[\sqrt{\ell^2v_F^2+\lvert\Delta\rvert^2}-\ell v_F]$ produces the $\lvert\Delta\rvert^2\ln\lvert\Delta\rvert$ of (21.6.74), so $V[\Delta]$ has a minimum at $\Delta\ne0$ for any attractive potential, however weak (QFT II §21.6). Two ingredients: a codimension-one zero locus of the kinetic term, and the Nambu structure $\sqrt{E^2+\Delta^2}$, even in $\ell$, whose $\Delta^2/2\lvert\ell\rvert$ tail integrates to the log. In the string, with the loop momentum inside the trace (A5),
$$
\operatorname{Tr}\log(-\omega Q_\Sigma)=\sum_{n\ge0}\int\!\frac{d^Dk}{(2\pi)^D}\,\operatorname{tr}_n\log\Big[\tfrac{\alpha'}2k^2+n-1+\tfrac{g^2}6v_n(\Sigma;k)\Big]\quad(\text{Siegel gauge, }-\omega Q=L_0^+\text{ on the }b_0^+\text{-gauge-fixed space up to O6}), \tag{2.1}
$$
with $v_n(\Sigma;k)$ the $\Sigma$-dressing on level $n$ (Gaussian in $k$ from the vertex). The zero locus of $L_0^+$: (i) massless, $n=1$: the point $k=0$, of measure zero in $d^Dk$, and $\int d^Dk\log(k^2+v)$ is analytic plus $v^{D/2}\log v$ at small $v$ in even $D$ (the $\Gamma(-D/2)$ pole of dimensional regularisation), $v^{13}\log v$ in $D=26$ — no enhancement; R69's zero-momentum exclusion (A4 there) concerns the classical $\Psi$-branches through $\Sigma=0$ and not the trace. (ii) Tachyon, $n=0$: the sphere $\alpha'k^2/2=1$ in *Euclidean* $k$, codimension one like the Fermi surface — but the bosonic propagator has no Nambu structure: the dressing shifts the pole, $\log(\ell v_F+\delta)$ instead of $\sqrt{\ell^2v_F^2+\Delta^2}$, and the principal-value shell integral $\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}d\ell\log\lvert\ell+\delta\rvert=(\kappa+\delta)\log(\kappa+\delta)+(\kappa-\delta)\log(\kappa-\delta)-2\kappa$ is analytic in $\delta$ (check `C2.1-shell`; the fermionic integral in the same check shows the $\delta^2\log\delta$). What the tachyon shell does produce is an imaginary part $\propto\delta$ (the $i\pi$ of crossing the pole): the bosonic-string IR problem, to be handled by the Pius–Rudra–Sen continuation, not a BCS log. **Consequence:** a nontrivial root of (1.6) at weak coupling $\hbar g^2\ll1$ needs $\hat G_{\hat\Sigma}=O(1/\hbar g^2)$ (C1.4), which neither zero locus of the string trace provides. The walls of $D$ do provide it: in the finite model the gap equation has, generically, one root per wall at distance $\hbar g^2$ (in $\hat\Sigma$) from the wall, a maximum of $\mathcal V_{\rm eff}$ along the normal (O3, (2.3a)) — a near-wall root of an uncontrolled truncation, not a condensate. Any other root sits at $g^2\Sigma=O(1)$, where the Gaussian truncation has no small parameter (R69 §5, the same conclusion in the $\Sigma$ direction).

**O2 — No justification for dropping internal $\Sigma$ lines (C2.2; Argued).** A mean field keeps the $\Psi$ loop at fixed $\Sigma$ and drops every diagram with an internal $\Sigma$ line. Two mechanisms are known to justify that. (a) BCS: Fermi-surface power counting, Weinberg (21.6.43)–(21.6.46) and the paragraph before (21.6.51) ("we must not include any internal $\Psi$ lines at all"; Weinberg's $\Psi$ is the pair field, our $\Sigma$) — it rests on the Fermi surface, O1's mechanism, absent here. (b) Large $N$: in the $O(N)$ model (Coleman, Jackiw, Politzer 1974; review Moshe, Zinn-Justin 2003) the HS field is the singlet $\sigma=\phi^a\phi^a/N$, both terms of $\Gamma[\sigma]$ are $O(N)$ and the $\sigma$ fluctuations are $O(1)$ — absent as argued below. Quantitatively, what is dropped is not small: at $O(\hbar g^2)$ the one-loop $\Psi$ self-energy of the cubic theory is the $\Sigma$-tadpole ($\langle\Sigma\rangle=\hbar G$), $\tfrac{\hbar g^2}6\ell_3(G,\cdot)$, which the mean field keeps, plus the $\Sigma$ exchange with one $\Psi$ contraction (two vertices $\tfrac{g^2}{12\hbar}V_4(\Sigma,\Psi,\Psi)$, $\Sigma$ propagator $\tfrac{12\hbar}{g^2}V_4^{-1}$), $\tfrac{\hbar g^2}3\ell_3(G,\cdot)$, which it drops; the sum is the Hartree self-energy $\tfrac{\hbar g^2}2\ell_3(G,\cdot)$ of the quartic theory. The mean field drops $\tfrac23$ of the one-loop $V_4$ self-energy (R66's factor, check `C2.7-third`). On (b): here $\Sigma$ ranges over all of $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H$ (no singlet projection), and in scaled variables ($S=g^{-2}\hat S$) the $\Sigma$-block, the $\Psi$-loop and the $\Sigma$-loop are all $O(1)$:
$$
\Gamma^{(1)}_{\rm joint}[0,\Sigma]=S[0,\Sigma]-\tfrac\hbar2\operatorname{Tr}\log(-\omega Q_\Sigma)-\tfrac\hbar2\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{Sym}^2}\log\big(\tfrac{g^2}{12}V_4\big)+\text{const}, \tag{2.2}
$$
by R65's congruence at $\Psi_0=0$, where the mixed block $\tfrac{g^2}6V_4(\Psi_0,\cdot,\cdot)$ vanishes and $H_{\rm joint}=\operatorname{diag}(\omega Q_\Sigma,-\tfrac{g^2}{12}V_4)$. Candidates for $N$ and why they fail: the number of Fock states per level (not a symmetry multiplicity: $\Sigma$ is not summed over it), the spacetime dimension $D$ (fixed, and it counts the loop integral, not a field multiplicity), the number of auxiliary fields $\Sigma_k$ (each enters once). The relaxation that would evade it: a vector-like index on $\Psi$; closed strings carry none, and open strings with Chan–Paton factors are adjoint (matrix, not vector), whose large-$N$ is the planar limit, not a Gaussian-dominated one. Not a proof of absence: an enumeration of the scalings the action provides. Note that (2.2) is a statement about the potential as a function of the background $\Sigma$; it does not mean the gap equation captures the one-loop $\Psi$ self-energy (it captures one third of it).

**O3 — The indefinite blocks (C2.3).** At zero momentum the $\Psi$ Gaussian has the tachyon direction ($V_2(T,T)=+1$ in $S$, 064 Table 1.1) and the $\Sigma$ block one $A$-negative direction (R68). With A3, inside a chamber of constant inertia of $-\omega Q_\Sigma$ the phase is constant and
$$
\mathcal V_{\rm eff}[\Sigma]=\tfrac{g^2}{24}V_4(\Sigma,\Sigma)+\tfrac\hbar2\log\lvert\det(-\omega Q_\Sigma)\rvert+\tfrac{i\pi\hbar}{2}\,n_-(\Sigma)+\text{const}, \tag{2.3}
$$
$n_-$ the number of rotated directions, piecewise constant. The chamber walls are exactly R69's $D=\{\det Q_\Sigma=0\}$, where $\mathcal V_{\rm eff}\to-\infty$ logarithmically: the walls attract the mean field. One-dimensional instance ($N=1$, $V_2=-1$, $V_4=v$, $a:=g^2v/6$):
$$
\mathcal V(\sigma)=\tfrac a4\sigma^2+\tfrac\hbar2\log\lvert1-a\sigma\rvert,\qquad \sigma(1-a\sigma)=\hbar,\qquad \sigma_\pm=\tfrac1{2a}\big[1\pm\sqrt{1-4a\hbar}\big],\qquad \mathcal V''(\sigma_+)=\tfrac a2-\tfrac1{2\hbar}+O(a)<0. \tag{2.3a}
$$
$\sigma_-=\hbar+a\hbar^2+\dots$ is (1.7) with $G=1$; $\sigma_+=\sigma_D-\hbar+O(\hbar^2)$, $\sigma_D=1/a$ the wall, i.e. at distance $\hbar g^2$ in $\hat\sigma=g^2\sigma$, and it is a maximum along the normal, $\mathcal V\to-\infty$ at the wall (check `C2.3-nearwall`). In the finite model a non-perturbative root therefore exists generically, one per wall of $D$, and it is not a minimum: it is the root class angle A will find (§3 A). A saddle-point evaluation of the $\Sigma$ integral on top of (2.3) (the next order, O2) uses R68's rotated contour and adds the constant $\Sigma$-block determinant only at $S_2$ (2.2). At $S_3$ the $\Sigma$ block becomes $K_\Psi=V_4-\tfrac g5V_5(\cdot,\cdot,\Psi)$ (R17), still $\Sigma$-independent at $\Psi_0=0$.

**O4 — The cubic term $V_3$ (C2.4).** At $\Psi_0=0$, $\partial_\Psi^2[\tfrac g6V_3(\Psi^3)]=0$: $V_3$ drops out of the one-loop Hessian, and (1.4) is the one-loop 1PI effective potential of the cubic theory on the slice $\Psi_0=0$, $V_3$ included, at $S_2$ (2.2). What $V_3$ spoils is the slice: the $\Psi$-half of the one-loop equations,
$$
\partial_{\Psi_0}\Gamma^{(1)}\big\vert_{\Psi_0=0}=+\tfrac{\hbar g}{2}\,V_3(G_\Sigma,\cdot)+O(\hbar^2)\neq0\quad(\text{action side, as (2.2)}),\qquad \langle\Psi\rangle=G_\Sigma\,\partial_{\Psi_0}\Gamma^{(1)}\big\vert_0=+\tfrac{\hbar g}2\,G_\Sigma V_3(G_\Sigma)+O(\hbar^2), \tag{2.4}
$$
(from $\partial_{\Psi_0}\Gamma^{(1)}=\partial S-\tfrac\hbar2\operatorname{Tr}[H^{-1}\partial H]$ with $H^{-1}=(\omega Q_\Sigma)^{-1}=-G_\Sigma$ and $\partial H\cdot\delta=gV_3(\delta,\cdot,\cdot)$; the second form is the direct Wick contraction of $\tfrac g{6\hbar}V_3(\Psi^3)$, checks `C2.4-tadpole`, `C2.4-tadpole-wick`), the one-loop tadpole (dilaton/tachyon), so $(\Psi_0=0,\Sigma_*)$ is not a stationary point of the one-loop effective action; the exact (1.3) sees this through $\langle\Psi^{\otimes2}\rangle=\hbar G_\Sigma+\langle\Psi\rangle^{\otimes2}+O(\hbar^2)$ with $\langle\Psi\rangle=O(\hbar g)$. At $S_3$ the R17 term adds the classical tadpole $\tfrac{g^3}{5!}V_5(\Sigma,\Sigma,\cdot)$ and R69's $O(g^6)$ exchange term to $\Gamma_0$ (R69 C4.2): the mean-field equation at $S_3$ has a classical $\Sigma^3$ term beside the trace. BCS has none of this: the fermion action is exactly quadratic, and $\langle\psi\rangle=0$ by statistics.

**O5 — The zero-momentum five-field model as a "loop" (C2.5).** Angle A's natural instance (level (1,1), zero momentum, no loop momentum) has $V_2=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ on $(T,D)$ (064 Table 1.1: $V_2(D,D)=0$, the dilaton zero mode), so with the Hankel dressing $V_4(e_i,X_a,X_b)=h_{i-1+a+b}$,
$$
\det\big(\omega Q_\sigma\big)=\tfrac{g^2}6\,(h_2\sigma_1+h_3\sigma_2+h_4\sigma_3)+O(\sigma^2),\qquad \tfrac\hbar2\log\lvert\det\omega Q_\sigma\rvert\sim\tfrac\hbar2\log\lvert\sigma\rvert\ \text{at }\sigma\to0 \tag{2.5}
$$
(check `C2.5-level11-det`; break $V_2(D,D)=1$). The origin is on a chamber wall. Along a ray $\sigma=s\,n$, with $A$ R68's $3\times3$ block,
$$
\mathcal V_{\rm eff}\approx\tfrac{g^2}{24}(n^\top An)\,s^2+\tfrac\hbar2\log\lvert s\rvert,\qquad \partial_s\mathcal V_{\rm eff}=0\ \Leftrightarrow\ s^2=-\frac{6\hbar}{g^2\,n^\top An}, \tag{2.5a}
$$
so a real root exists only for $n^\top An<0$ (R68's $A$-negative cone, $\approx TD$), at the scale $g^2\sigma\sim\sqrt{\hbar g^2}$ (i.e. $\sigma\sim\sqrt\hbar/g$). Along $A$-positive directions (e.g. $DD$, $h_4>0$) there is no root and $\mathcal V_{\rm eff}$ decreases without bound into the origin, the wall through $\sigma=0$: the attraction is to the wall, not to $\sigma\neq0$. By O1 (i) the whole structure is the artifact of dropping $\int d^Dk$: with the loop momentum the massless point is measure zero. Any zero-momentum five-field gap equation must be read with this caveat: its root near the origin (in the $A$-negative cone only) is the zero mode, not a condensate.

**O6 — The ghost sector (C2.6).** (1.4) as written traces over the classical sector $N_{\rm gh}=2$. The gauge-invariant one-loop determinant is the Siegel-gauge supertrace over the full $b_0^+$-gauge-fixed Fock space with $(-1)^{N_{\rm gh}}$ (the torus partition function with the $b_0^-c_0^-$ insertion), and $\Sigma$ enters it through the BV-extended $V_4(\Sigma,\cdot,\cdot)$ on all ghost numbers (record 016 / R22 for the BV structure of the cubic theory). The $\Sigma$-dependence of the ghost determinant is a computation nobody has done; at zero momentum on the classical five-field block it is absent by construction. No BCS counterpart.

**O7 — Gauge invariance: the mean field is a region of $\mathcal M_{1,2}$ (C2.7).** The gap equation (1.6) at $\Sigma=\hbar G_\Sigma$ reads
$$
Q_{\rm eff}=Q+\tfrac{\hbar g^2}6\,\ell_3(G_{\rm eff},\cdot),\qquad G_{\rm eff}=(-\omega Q_{\rm eff})^{-1}, \tag{2.6}
$$
the direct-channel third of the Hartree equation (R66). The Hartree (leading 2PI, Cornwall–Jackiw–Tomboulis 1974) equation of $\tfrac12\omega(\Psi,Q\Psi)+\tfrac{g^2}{24}V_4(\Psi^4)$ is $Q_{\rm eff}=Q+\tfrac{\hbar g^2}2\ell_3(G_{\rm eff},\cdot)$: the Hessian of $\tfrac{g^2}{24}V_4(\Psi^4)$ is $\tfrac{g^2}2V_4(P_0,\cdot,\cdot)$ (C4), and $\Pi=2\,\delta\Gamma_2/\delta G$ with $\Gamma_2=\tfrac{\hbar^2g^2}8V_4(G,G)$. The factor $\tfrac13$ is R66's: the classical auxiliary carries the direct channel; the two thirds carried by the $\delta\Sigma$ exchange are the internal-$\Sigma$-line diagrams that a mean field drops (O2; check `C2.7-third`: the one-loop $\Psi$ self-energy of the quartic theory, by quadrature, is three times the mean-field dressing). In closed SFT the full $V_4$ bubble is one piece of the genus-one two-point function; the others at the same order $\hbar g^2$ are the $V_3$–$V_3$ bubble, the fundamental $\mathcal V_{1,2}$ vertex, the tadpole-attached diagram (the $\mathcal V_{0,3}$–$\mathcal V_{0,3}$ diagram of (2.4)), and the $\mathcal V_{1,1}$–propagator–$\mathcal V_{0,3}$ diagram (the one-loop tadpole vertex attached to the external line): five regions. The sum covers $\mathcal M_{1,2}$ once; the $V_4$ bubble alone covers the region reached by sewing two legs of $\mathcal V_{0,4}$, and the mean field resums one third of it. A mean-field statement is therefore a statement about one third of one region of moduli space — gauge-variant by itself, like any single Feynman region — and the HS structure does not change this: R14's telescoping reproduces the contact $V_4$ at tree level only, and the $\Psi$-loop with $V_3$ is not an HS object. This is the obstacle that decides the verdict (§4): even a nontrivial root of (1.6) is a statement about the resummation of one third of one region, not about the theory. (Argued: the region decomposition is standard, Zwiebach 1993 §5; the closed-SFT citations are to be confirmed, §3 C.)

\subsection*{Commentary}
- (2.1): the momentum damping of the Siegel-gauge vertices makes each level's $k$-integral convergent at fixed $\Sigma$; the level sum is level truncation (PH1's regime), no other cutoff is available. The regularisation question of the brief is therefore: convergence of the level sum of $\operatorname{tr}_n[G_nv_n(\Sigma)]$ — vertex suppression against the growth of the number of states — the same convergence level truncation relies on for the classical potential; no new statement here.
- (2.2): R65 gives the congruence at any $(\Psi_0,\Sigma_0)$ with $H_{\rm eff}$ the Schur complement; at $\Psi_0=0$ the mixed block vanishes and the Schur complement is $\omega Q_{\Sigma_0}$ itself, so (1.4) and the joint one-loop potential on the slice coincide up to the constant $\Sigma$-block term. This is what makes O2 sharp: as a function of the background $\Sigma$, nothing is "dropped" by mean field at one loop on the slice; what is dropped is the slice's inconsistency (2.4), the other one-loop regions (O7), and — in the gap equation — two thirds of the one-loop $\Psi$ self-energy (the internal-$\Sigma$-line diagrams, O2). Check `C2.8-joint` confirms the coincidence in the finite model with $V_3$ included (joint Hessian block-diagonal at $\Psi_0=0$; $\Gamma^{(1)}_{\rm joint}+\mathcal V_{\rm eff}$ independent of $\Sigma$).
- (2.5) is the finite-model analogue of R69's "at zero momentum $\Sigma=0\in D$" (065 A4), now with the logarithm attached.
