## Task and objective

**Literal task.** With 064's action and $\Sigma$ block and 059's critical points, assemble the $5\times5$ joint Hessian of R1's cubic action on $(\delta t,\delta d,\delta\sigma_1,\delta\sigma_2,\delta\sigma_3)$ at each critical point, both $\beta$; verify 060's congruence (1.15) against the direct Hessian of the eliminated potential to machine precision; report the inertia per point, per $\beta$, on both sides (Hess $S$, Hess$(-S)$; conventions C5); write out the mixed block; state that the $\Sigma\Sigma$ block is R68's constant.

**Objective.** The level-(1,1) instance of objective 2(b): the first concrete case in which the cubic theory's fluctuation problem around a nontrivial critical point is solved by linear algebra on low vertices only. What this record keeps: which fluctuation directions are the auxiliary's, how the inertia splits, and that the auxiliary adds exactly one constant direction on the potential side. Stability of the points is not decidable from zero-momentum data (R64's scope) and is not claimed. Task 21 (record 066, in progress) states that the joint Hessian is affine in the background; cited as in progress, not used.

**Reading of "both branches".** 059 Table 3.1 lists three regular critical points per $\beta$: the $\tau,\delta>0$ point, the deep $\delta<0$ point and the valley point (a truncation artefact, 059 §3). All three are treated; the first two are the two branches of the brief, the valley point is added at no cost and flagged. The origin (perturbative vacuum) is treated first.

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(a) The congruence as a splitting.* With $L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-J&1\end{pmatrix}$ (§2), $L(0,y)=(0,y)$ and $L(v,Jv)=(v,0)$: the congruence fixes $\mathcal A$ pointwise and maps $\mathcal T$ onto $\mathcal F$. Hence, on either side,
$$
H(\mathcal A,\mathcal T)=0,\qquad H\vert_{\mathcal A}=\pm\tfrac1{12}A,\qquad H\vert_{\mathcal T}=\mathrm{Hess}(\mp S_{\rm eff}),\qquad \mathcal A\oplus\mathcal T=\mathbb R^5, \tag{3.1}
$$
upper sign $\mathrm{Hess}(-S)$. $H(\mathcal A,\mathcal T)=0$ is $H_{sx}+H_{ss}J=\tfrac1{12}AJ-\tfrac1{12}AJ=0$. All four statements are invariant under changes of basis of $\Psi$ and of $\Sigma$ separately (A3). **Answer to the alignment question:** on the potential side the auxiliary's negative directions lie in $\mathcal A$ itself, with no admixture of $\delta\Psi$: in the original variables, not only after a change of variables. $H\vert_{\mathcal A}=\tfrac1{12}A$ has exactly one negative direction ($n_-=1$), the same form at every background, and $\mathcal A$ is $H$-orthogonal to the whole $\Psi$ sector $\mathcal T$. \subsection*{Commentary}
- (3.1) is the geometric content of 060 (1.15) at this instance: the auxiliary's directions are the coordinate subspace $\mathcal A$ itself, and the $\Psi$ directions are the shell tangent, not $\mathcal F$. On the potential side $H\vert_{\mathcal A}=\tfrac1{12}A$ has exactly one negative direction, background-independent; the $TD$ coordinate direction is negative ($A_{22}=h_2<0$), and the Euclidean negative eigenvector $u_-$ is close to it (basis-dependent). What R68 said about the constant block therefore carries over to the fluctuation problem at every critical point.
- (b): the frozen-auxiliary count is Sylvester-invariant under separate linear changes of basis of $\Psi$ and of $\Sigma$, so the mismatch is not an effect of those coordinates. It is not invariant under a $\Psi$-dependent redefinition of the auxiliary: with the HS-shifted $\Sigma'=\Sigma-\Psi^{\otimes2}$, $\delta\Sigma'=\delta\Sigma-J\delta\Psi$, the frozen subspace $\delta\Sigma'=0$ is exactly $\mathcal T$, and the frozen count is right. The miscount is therefore a property of freezing the HS variable $\Sigma$ itself, which is what a mean-field saddle does, and that makes it the relevant case. It is the level-(1,1) instance of 060 C2.1 (direct channel one third).
- (b), the standard reading: this is the mean-field versus RPA distinction of many-body theory. Gaussian fluctuations around a Hubbard–Stratonovich mean-field saddle give the RPA kernel, direct plus exchange through the auxiliary propagator, not the frozen mean-field kernel; the frozen kernel is the fluctuation operator only when the exchange is parametrically suppressed, as at large $N$ (Negele & Orland, *Quantum Many-Particle Systems*, 1988: the HS transformation, and the RPA as the Gaussian fluctuations of the auxiliary field). The distinction bites here because there is no large-$N$ parameter and the exchange is twice the direct channel.
- (b), the precise statement for Task 15. Around a joint critical point $(\Psi_*,\Sigma_*=\Psi_*^{\otimes2})$ the $\Psi$ fluctuation operator is the Schur complement $H_{xx}-H_{xs}H_{ss}^{-1}H_{sx}=\mathrm{Hess}\,S_{\rm eff}$, not the mean-field block $H_{xx}$. The difference is the $\delta\Sigma$ exchange $\tfrac{g^2}3V_4(P_0,\cdot,\cdot)$, twice the direct channel, and no parameter suppresses it at level (1,1). At one loop the joint Gaussian determinant is $\det H=\det(-\tfrac{g^2}{12}A)\det\mathrm{Hess}\,S_{\rm eff}$; the first factor is background-independent, so the joint fluctuation determinant equals the eliminated theory's up to a constant, in modulus and in the number of negative directions (phase). A treatment that freezes $\Sigma$ at its mean-field value and integrates only $\delta\Psi$ uses $\det H_{xx}$: wrong in modulus, and at the three points of (b) wrong in phase ($n_-=2$ instead of $1$ on the potential side). Task 15's mean field must therefore either integrate $\delta\Sigma$ jointly or use the Schur complement for the $\Psi$ fluctuations; freezing $\Sigma$ is legitimate for the saddle equations only.
- (c): the Euclidean negative eigenvectors mix $\Psi$ and $\Sigma$ at order one. Their $\Sigma$ parts are along $u_-$ (overlap close to 1), except at the deep points, where the most negative eigenvector's $\Sigma$ part is along $u_{+,{\rm large}}\approx TT$ and its $\Psi$ part along $v_-$. The large positive eigenvalue ($\approx h_0/12$ plus the mixing) is the $TT$ auxiliary direction. At $\beta=2$, $\tau,\delta>0$, the two negative eigenvectors are nearly separated: one mostly $\Psi$ (along $v_-$), one mostly the $TD$ auxiliary. These numbers depend on the relative normalisation of $\Psi$ and $\Sigma$ coordinates ($g$, the $\tfrac12$ in $e_2$), and carry no invariant meaning beyond what (3.1) states.
- The Euclidean eigenvalues are not $\lambda(\mathrm{Hess}\,\mathcal V_{\rm eff})\cup\lambda(A)/12$: at $\beta=\tfrac32$, $\tau,\delta>0$, the negative pair of $\mathrm{Hess}(-S)$ differs from the negative eigenvalues of $\mathrm{Hess}\,\mathcal V_{\rm eff}$ and of $A/12$ taken together: congruent, not isospectral (R65).
