## §2 Positivity of the tachyon measure on a momentum-independent region (C2, C2′)

\subsection*{Setup}
Uses A1, A3. $R\subset\mathcal V_{0,6}$ a measurable set independent of the momenta; $\Omega_6$ the standard measure, $\Omega_6=\langle\Sigma(m)\vert\,b(v_1)\cdots b(v_6)\,\vert T_1\rangle\cdots\vert T_6\rangle\,d^6m$ with $\langle\Sigma(m)\vert$ the six-punctured surface state with the section's local coordinates $z=f_i(w_i)$, $v_a$ the Schiffer vectors of $\partial_{m_a}$ and $\vert T_i\rangle=c_1\bar c_1\vert0;p_i\rangle$. Global coordinate $z$ with $(z_1,z_2,z_3)=(0,1,\infty)$ and $m=(z_4,z_5,z_6)$. $h_i:=\alpha'p_i^2/4-1$ (zero on shell).

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(2.1) The $b$-modes that survive on a tachyon.* In the local frame $w_i$ of puncture $i$, $b(v_a)\vert_{i}=\oint v_a^{(i)}(w)b(w)\,dw=\sum_nv^{(i)}_{a,n}b_n$ with $v^{(i)}_a(w)=\sum_nv^{(i)}_{a,n}w^{n+1}$; the Schiffer vectors of a puncture variation, as vector fields $v(w)\partial_w$, are regular at the puncture (Commentary (2.2)), so $v(w)=\sum_{n\ge-1}v_nw^{n+1}$ and $n\ge-1$ (the earlier "simple pole" wording, which would give $n\ge-2$, removed after the last review, #1 residue). On $\vert T\rangle=c_1\bar c_1\vert0;p\rangle$, with $\{b_m,c_n\}=\delta_{m+n,0}$ and $b_n\vert0;p\rangle=0$ for $n\ge-1$,
$$
b_n\,c_1\bar c_1\vert0;p\rangle=+c_1\bar c_1\,b_n\vert0;p\rangle=0\quad(n\ge0),\qquad b_{-1}\,c_1\bar c_1\vert0;p\rangle=\bar c_1\vert0;p\rangle ,
\tag{2.1}
$$
(two anticommutations, $\{b_n,c_1\}=\{b_n,\bar c_1\}=0$ for $n\ge0$; the sign was written as $-$ before, the critic's #1, the value being $0$ either way)
and likewise for $\bar b_n$. No step uses $p^2$: (2.1) holds off shell. Hence only the translation components $v^{(i)}_{a,-1}$, $\bar v^{(i)}_{a,-1}$ of the Schiffer vectors act — the parts of $\partial_{m_a}$ that move the puncture, not those that change its local coordinate ($n\ge0$) — and $\prod_ab(v_a)$ strips $c_1\bar c_1$ from exactly three punctures, the choice summed over, each stripping weighted by the $6\times6$ determinant of translation components. [`C2-bmodes`: (2.1) and its break leg in a finite Fock representation.]

*(2.2) Frame factors.* $\vert T\rangle$ is a primary of weight $(h,h)$, so $f_i\circ(c\bar ce^{ipX})(0)=\lvert f_i'(0)\rvert^{2h_i}\,c\bar ce^{ipX}(z_i)$; and $b^{(w)}_{-1}=\oint b(w)dw=\oint(dz/dw)\,b(z)\,dz=f_i'(0)\,b^{(z)}_{-1}+\sum_{n\ge0}(\cdots)b^{(z)}_n$, whose $n\ge0$ part annihilates the $z$-frame state by (2.1); the translation component in the $w$ frame is $v^{(i)}_{a,-1}=\partial_{m_a}z_i/f_i'(0)$. The $f_i'(0)$ cancel: in the chart $m=(z_4,z_5,z_6)$ the determinant of translation components is $1$ and the stripped punctures are $4,5,6$ (the strippings of a fixed puncture carry $\partial_{m_a}z_i=0$).

*(2.3) The correlator.* With $c\bar c$ at $z_1,z_2,z_3$ (bosonic pairs, no ordering sign) and the matter Koba–Nielsen factor (Polchinski (6.2.17), closed string),
$$
\Omega_6=\mathcal N\,\prod_{i=1}^6\lvert f_i'(0)\rvert^{2h_i}\;\lvert z_{12}z_{13}z_{23}\rvert^2\prod_{i<j}\lvert z_{ij}\rvert^{\alpha'p_i\cdot p_j}\;d^2z_4\,d^2z_5\,d^2z_6
\tag{2.2}
$$
(the puncture at $\infty$, the critic's #2: in this chart $\lvert z_{12}z_{13}z_{23}\rvert^2\to\lvert z_{12}\rvert^2\lvert z_3\rvert^4$ and $\prod_{i\ne3}\lvert z_{i3}\rvert^{\alpha'p_i\cdot p_3}\to\lvert z_3\rvert^{\alpha'\sum_{i\ne3}p_i\cdot p_3}=\lvert z_3\rvert^{-\alpha'p_3^2}$, so the $\lvert z_3\rvert$ powers cancel on shell, $\alpha'p_3^2=4$; off shell the residual $\lvert z_3\rvert^{4-\alpha'p_3^2}$ combines with $\lvert f_3'(0)\rvert^{2h_3}$ measured in the $1/z$ chart into a finite factor — (2.2)–(2.5) are to be read with $\ell_{i3}$ and $d\mu_0$ after this cancellation, equivalently through cross-ratios as in §3.5 (ii)), where $\mathcal N$ collects the ghost-vacuum normalisation of the project conventions, the $(2\pi)^{26}\delta(\sum p)$ stripping, and the orientation of $\mathcal M_{0,6}$ against $d^2z_4d^2z_5d^2z_6$: a constant on the connected manifold $\mathcal M_{0,6}$, since the density multiplying it is strictly positive on the interior (every $z_{ij}\ne0$, $\infty$), and the same constant for every region of the same amplitude, $\mathcal V_{0,6}$ included.

*(2.4) On shell.* $h_i=0$: the local coordinates drop out of (2.2) entirely, and
$$
\int_R\Omega_6=\mathcal N\int_Rd\mu_0(m)\,\exp\Big(\sum_{i<j}\ell_{ij}(m)\,p_i\cdot p_j\Big),\qquad d\mu_0=\lvert z_{12}z_{13}z_{23}\rvert^2\,d^6z>0,\quad \ell_{ij}=\alpha'\ln\lvert z_{ij}\rvert .
\tag{2.3}
$$
On $\overline{\mathcal V_{0,6}}$, compact in the interior of $\mathcal M_{0,6}$ (A3), every $\lvert z_{ij}\rvert$ is bounded above and away from zero, so each $\ell_{ij}$ is continuous and bounded there, and $\mu_0(\overline{\mathcal V_{0,6}})<\infty$. The exponent is linear in the invariants; with the six conservation relations $\sum_{j\ne i}p_i\cdot p_j=-p_i^2=-4/\alpha'$ it is an affine function of the nine coordinates $x$ of A1, $\sum_{i<j}\ell_{ij}x_{ij}=\langle\tilde\ell(m),x\rangle+c(m)$ with $c(m)$ absorbed into the measure (still positive):
$$
\int_R\Omega_6=\mathcal N\int_{\mathbb R^9}e^{\langle\lambda,x\rangle}\,d\nu_R(\lambda),\qquad \nu_R:=\tilde\ell_*\big(e^{c}\,\mathbf 1_R\,\mu_0\big)\ge0,\ \ \operatorname{supp}\nu_R\ \text{compact}.
\tag{2.4}
$$
This is C2.

*(2.5) Off shell (C2′).* For external tachyons off shell, $h_i\ne0$ and (2.2) gives
$$
\int_R\Omega_6=\mathcal N\int_Rd\mu_0(m)\prod_i\lvert f_i'(0)\rvert^{-2}\,\exp\Big(\sum_{i<j}\ell_{ij}\,p_i\cdot p_j+\sum_i\ell_{ii}\,p_i^2\Big),\qquad \ell_{ii}(m)=\tfrac{\alpha'}2\ln\lvert f_i'(0)\rvert ,
\tag{2.5}
$$
a positive measure (the section's $f_i'(0)\ne0$ and continuous on $\overline{\mathcal V_{0,6}}$, A3) times the exponential of a function linear in the twenty-one invariants, fifteen of them independent off shell. The plan's risk — a term of uncontrolled sign from the $b$-insertions acting on the state when the local coordinates vary with the moduli — does not arise for tachyons, because those are the $n\ge0$ modes and (2.1) kills them off shell as well. It does arise for excited external states, where $b_{n\ge0}$ act non-trivially and (2.2) is not the form of $\Omega_6$; C2′'s class is therefore "six external tachyons, on or off shell", and H-C3/T3′ are not needed.

\subsection*{Commentary}
(2.1): $b_n\vert0\rangle=0$ for $n\ge-1$ is the SL$(2,\mathbb C)$ invariance of the vacuum for a weight-2 field; the sign of $b_{-1}c_1\bar c_1\vert0\rangle=+\bar c_1\vert0\rangle$ is immaterial for C2, which needs only that a single mode survives per puncture. (2.2): the Schiffer vector of a puncture variation in the chart $(z_4,z_5,z_6)$ is the constant vector field $\partial_{m_a}z_i$ near puncture $i$ plus the variation of the local coordinate, $v^{(i)}_a(w)=-\partial_{m_a}f_i(w)/f_i'(w)$, a Taylor series in $w$ (regular at $w=0$: $\partial_z=(1/f'(w))\partial_w$ is regular there, the earlier "simple pole" wording was wrong, the critic's #1); as a vector field $v(w)\partial_w$ with $v(w)=\sum_nv_nw^{n+1}$ this is $n\ge-1$, the $n=-1$ term being the translation. *Second route* (the critic's #24): R48's four-point prefactor $\beta^{8-\alpha'(s+t+u)/2}$ is $\prod_{i=1}^4\lvert f_i'(0)\rvert^{2h_i}$ for $\lvert f_i'(0)\rvert\propto1/\beta$, since $\sum_i2h_i=\alpha'(s+t+u)/2-8$, and equals $1$ on shell: the project's own four-point confirmation of the frame factor of (2.2)/(2.5). (2.3): the sign of $\mathcal N$ depends on the project's vacuum normalisation and the orientation convention and is not computed here; nothing below uses its value, only that it is common to all regions. (2.4): an on-shell weight-$0$ primary is insensitive to the local coordinate; this is the familiar statement that on-shell amplitudes do not depend on the section, here in the form needed: the moduli-dependence of the integrand is through $\lvert z_{ij}\rvert$ only.

\subsection*{Result}
$$
\boxed{\ \int_R\Omega_6\ =\ \mathcal N\int_{\mathbb R^9}e^{\langle\lambda,x\rangle}\,d\nu_R(\lambda),\qquad\nu_R\ge0\ \text{of compact support, for every momentum-independent }R\subset\mathcal V_{0,6}\ }
$$

### §3.0 Preamble (C3)

(i) *Level truncation.* (a), (b), (c) are statements about the limit $\mathcal C$ of (1.2). The truncation $\mathcal C^{(L)}$ differs from $\mathcal C$ by $\lvert\mathcal C-\mathcal C^{(L)}\rvert$, which is not a "tail of a level sum" (the inverse mixes levels, §1); a region integral has no level structure, so $\mathcal C^{(L)}$ is not a region integral even when $\mathcal C$ is. *Failures:* a failure of any test on $\mathcal C^{(L)}$ refutes (a)/(b) only with a bound $\lvert\mathcal C-\mathcal C^{(L)}\rvert<\varepsilon$ on the tested points smaller than the failure margin (H-C2′ gives convergence, not a rate). *Passes:* a pass of any test on $\mathcal C^{(L)}$ at finitely many points, with the same bound $\varepsilon$, establishes the tested inequality or identity for $\mathcal C$ at those points up to $\varepsilon$ and nothing on an open set; in particular a constant ratio $\mathcal C^{(L)}/\mathcal V_6$ at the sampled points gives $\lvert\mathcal C/\mathcal V_6-\alpha\rvert\lesssim\varepsilon/\lvert\mathcal V_6\rvert$ there, which is consistent with (b) and does not establish it (the critic's #6). R39 and R40 are all at fixed level and do not meet the failure condition (`knowledge/decisions.md` 2026-10-12, third bullet). (ii) *Real points.* T1, T2 are pointwise. T3 and T3⁺ compare values at points and their pairwise midpoints; all of them must lie in $\mathcal D_{\rm reg}$ (where $\mathcal C$ is the diagram's value, C1): no segment condition is needed, since the inequalities below are midpoint inequalities. Once the extension of $\mathcal C$ to an open neighbourhood of $\mathcal D$ (or to all of $\mathbb R^9$) is proven, any real points count. (iii) *What is tested.* T1 tests (b); T2 tests the sign and size under (a)/(a′)/(b); T3, T3⁺, T4 test the Laplace structure under (a)/(a′) and, through $\mathcal C=\alpha\mathcal V_6$, under (b).

### §3.1 T1 (C3.1)

Under (b), $\mathcal C(x)/\mathcal V_6(x)=\alpha$ wherever $\mathcal V_6(x)\ne0$ — and $\mathcal V_6=\mathcal NF_{\mathcal V_{0,6}}$ never vanishes on $\mathbb R^9$ ($F>0$, C2). Conversely: $\mathcal C$ is real-analytic on $\mathcal D^\circ$ (C1, under H-C2′ (iii)) and $\mathcal V_6$ is entire in $x$ (compact region, 035 Prop. 2.1 easy half) and non-vanishing on $\mathbb R^9$; if $\mathcal C/\mathcal V_6=\alpha$ on a non-empty open $U\subset\mathcal D^\circ$, then $\mathcal C-\alpha\mathcal V_6$ is real-analytic and vanishes on $U$, hence on the connected component of $\mathcal D^\circ$ containing $U$ (identity theorem); above level 0 this is vacuous unless $\mathcal D^\circ\ne\emptyset$ (C1's gap). So on tachyons, constancy on an open set gives (b) on that component; (b) as a statement on all states is not reached by a tachyon test, and (b) on the other components of $\mathcal D^\circ$ (if it is disconnected, which is not decided here) needs a test point in each. *As a theorem about the function:* a non-constant ratio refutes (b); constancy on an open set gives (b) on tachyons (on the component). *As a test* (the critic's #6): a run samples finitely many points at finite $L$; a non-constant ratio refutes (b) with the finite-level caveat of §3.0 (i); a constant ratio at the sampled points is consistent with (b) and establishes nothing on an open set. "Decisive" is withdrawn from every restatement. The converse uses C1.1 (the ratio as a function of $x$); the refutation direction does not. ### §3.2′ The forward-configuration sign at level 0 (C3.2′)

*Setup.* One splitting $(123\vert456)$, level-0 pair, forward configuration $p_{3+i}=-p_i$ (so $p_4+p_5+p_6=-P$, consistent), and the Gram matrix of $(p_1,p_2,p_3)$ positive definite — equivalently (Sylvester) there is a Lorentz frame in which $p_1,p_2,p_3$ are purely spatial, $p_i^0=0$; such configurations exist on shell since tachyon momenta are spacelike ($\vec p_i^{\,2}=4/\alpha'$), and $P^2=\vec P^2>0$ there. Such configurations have $\operatorname{rank}G\le3$ for the six-point Gram matrix, so they lie on $\partial\mathcal D$ (generic rank $5$); this is immaterial for a pointwise sign. *Step 1, $w=u$.* $\mathcal V_5$ on tachyons is a region integral of $\prod\lvert z_{ij}\rvert^{\alpha'k_i\cdot k_j}$ times mapping-radius powers of the $k_i^2$ (the five-point (2.2)), hence a function of the ten invariants $k_i\cdot k_j$ and even under $k\to-k$ on all legs at once. So
$$
w_p(\ell')=\mathcal V_5(\tfrac P2+\ell',\tfrac P2-\ell';-p_1,-p_2,-p_3)=\mathcal V_5(-\tfrac P2-\ell',-\tfrac P2+\ell';p_1,p_2,p_3)=u_p(\ell') ,
\tag{3.1}
$$
using also the $S_5$ symmetry of $\mathcal V_5$ in its legs (the pair legs are the last two in both): an $L_\infty$ vertex is graded-symmetric by definition (A3; R80's scope treats the actual $\mathcal V_5$ so), so no caveat about an asymmetric section applies (the critic's #17; the earlier self-review caveat is withdrawn). The same leg swap gives $u_p(-\ell)=u_p(\ell)$: $u\in L^2_{\rm even}(\mathbb R^{26})$, the space on which R53 states positivity (the critic's #8). *Step 2, reality of $u$ on the contour.* In the frame $p_i^0=0$ ($i\le3$) with $\ell=(i\ell^0_E,\vec\ell)$: $\ell^2=\ell_E^2$, $P\cdot\ell=\vec P\cdot\vec\ell$, $p_i\cdot\ell=\vec p_i\cdot\vec\ell$ — every invariant of $u_p(\ell)$ is real, the integrand of the region integral is real, so $u_p(\ell)\in\mathbb R$ for every $\ell$ on the contour. In a general forward configuration (some $p_i^0\ne0$) one has instead $\overline{u_p(\ell)}=u_p(\theta\ell)$, $\theta$ the Euclidean time reflection, so $u=\operatorname{Re}u+i\operatorname{Im}u$ with $\operatorname{Re}u$ $\theta$-even and $\operatorname{Im}u$ $\theta$-odd; since $K_P^{-1}$ commutes with $\theta$ (049 C1.1, $O(26)$ invariance) the cross terms vanish and $\int uK_P^{-1}u=\langle\operatorname{Re}u,K_P^{-1}\operatorname{Re}u\rangle-\langle\operatorname{Im}u,K_P^{-1}\operatorname{Im}u\rangle$, which is **not** signed: the brief's "reality gives $w=\bar u$" holds in the static frame only, where $\operatorname{Im}u=0$, and that is the class this node covers. Coverage beyond the static frame: for a boosted image of a static-class configuration the value is the same number if C1.1 holds (Lorentz invariance), so the sign then covers the whole Lorentz class $\operatorname{Gram}(p_1,p_2,p_3)\succ0$; what is not covered in any case is $\operatorname{Gram}(p_1,p_2,p_3)\not\succ0$. *Step 3, the sign.* With $u$ real and even, (1.1) is the quadratic form $\langle u,K_P^{-1}u\rangle=\sum_k\langle u,\psi_k\rangle^2/\mu_k(P^2)$ over the complete eigenbasis $\{\psi_k\}$ of $L^2_{\rm even}$ ($K_P$ injective with dense range), convergent by H-C2′ (i) and with $\operatorname{sign}(C)\mu_k>0$ for every $k$ at real $P^2$ (R53, Conditional(PH25, PH29, PH30)); $u\not\equiv0$ (a vertex-region integral of a positive integrand). Hence
$$
\operatorname{sign}(C)\;\mathcal C^{(0)}_{(123\vert456)}(p)>0\qquad\text{(forward configuration, Gram}(p_1,p_2,p_3)\succ0\text{, level 0)} .
\tag{3.2}
$$
*What it is and is not.* A sign statement on the $L=0$ truncation $\mathcal C^{(0)}_{(123\vert456)}=u^{(0)}(K^{(0)}_P)^{-1}u^{(0)}$ of one splitting — not "one diagonal term" of a level sum, since the level-0 entry of $(K^{\le L}_P)^{-1}$ for $L>0$ is a Schur complement (§1) and (3.2) says nothing about it; the nine other splittings have $u\ne w$ and complex slices, and the sign of $\mathcal V_6$ (needed for the sign half of T2) is not computed here; $C$'s sign is R47's ($C<0$ in the ledger). *What it does not test* (rewritten after the last review, F4; the first pass's suggestion #25 is withdrawn). (3.2) gives the sign of one splitting's $L=0$ truncation: informative about the sign structure of the exchange, and not a test of (a), (a′) or (b). T2's sign half is a statement about $\mathcal C$, the $L\to\infty$ limit of a sum over ten splittings, and under (a) or (a′) the sign of one summand's $L=0$ truncation is unconstrained; a mismatch between $\operatorname{sign}(C)\,\mathcal C^{(0)}_{(123\vert456)}$ and $\operatorname{sign}\mathcal N$ would refute something only under two further statements, neither declared here: a splitting-wise refinement of (a) (each splitting's term itself a region integral, which R39 (2)(iii)'s $G$-invariant decomposition suggests but does not give), and a bound placing the $L=0$ truncation within $\lvert\mathcal C-\mathcal C^{(0)}\rvert$ of the limit. [Proven under H-C2′ (i) and Conditional(PH25, PH29, PH30) through R53, for the stated class, as a statement about $\mathcal C^{(0)}$; the general forward configuration not covered]

### §3.3 T3: log-convexity (C3.3)

*Statement.* Under (a) on on-shell external tachyons (C2; C2′ is not used here: C1 defines $\mathcal C$ on shell only, and an off-shell $\mathcal C$ would need the off-shell section of $\mathcal V_5$ — C2′ is a statement about $\Omega_6$ with no $\mathcal C$ to act on until that definition exists, the critic's #3), $\mathcal C=\mathcal NF_R$ with $F_R(x)=\int e^{\langle\lambda,x\rangle}d\nu_R(\lambda)$, $\nu_R\ge0$. For any $x,y\in\mathbb R^9$, by Cauchy–Schwarz in $L^2(\nu_R)$,
$$
F_R\big(\tfrac{x+y}2\big)=\int e^{\langle\lambda,x\rangle/2}e^{\langle\lambda,y\rangle/2}d\nu_R\ \le\ F_R(x)^{1/2}F_R(y)^{1/2},
\tag{3.3}
$$
i.e. $\log F_R$ is midpoint-convex, and convex (it is continuous). Under (b), $\lvert\mathcal C\rvert=\lvert\alpha\rvert\,\lvert\mathcal N\rvert F_{\mathcal V_{0,6}}$ and $\log\lvert\mathcal C\rvert=\log\lvert\alpha\mathcal N\rvert+\log F_{\mathcal V_{0,6}}$ is convex for every $\alpha\ne0$ (the brief's "$\alpha>0$" is not needed once the absolute value is taken; the earlier "$\alpha<0$ is caught by T2" is withdrawn after the last review, #7 residue: T2 measures $\alpha$ and does not test (b)). (Equality in (3.3) at $x\ne y$ confines $\operatorname{supp}\nu_R$ to a hyperplane $\langle\lambda,x-y\rangle=\mathrm{const}$, not to a point; the refutation is the strict inequality and no equality-case argument is needed — the critic's #9.) *Coverage.* $R$ is any measurable momentum-independent subset of $\mathcal V_{0,6}$: open or not, connected or not, touching $\partial\mathcal V_{0,6}$ or not (compactness of $\overline{\mathcal V_{0,6}}$ in the interior of $\mathcal M_{0,6}$ is all that is used), with the standard measure. *Not covered.* Regions $R(p)$ depending on the momenta — including the sector-type regions of R40 (4)(ii) (the paper's F-b-56 sentence) and the cusp of 036 §3.6 — and momentum-weighted densities: $\int_{R(p)}d\mu_0e^{\langle\ell,x\rangle}$ is not the transform of a fixed measure and (3.3) does not apply; they are outside the protocol and not pursued (the G1 decision, `knowledge/decisions.md` 2026-10-18 about 10:10). ### §3.5 T3⁺: the converse and exponential convexity (C3.5)

*(i) Injectivity of the transform.* $\nu\mapsto\hat\nu(x)=\int e^{\langle\lambda,x\rangle}d\nu$ is injective on finite signed measures of compact support on $\mathbb R^9$ ($\hat\nu$ is entire and its restriction to $i\mathbb R^9$ is the Fourier transform). *(ii) Injectivity of the moduli map.* With $(z_1,z_2,z_3)=(0,1,\infty)$, the nine Möbius-invariant exponents are $\ln\lvert z_j\rvert,\ln\lvert1-z_j\rvert$ ($j=4,5,6$) and $\ln\lvert z_{45}\rvert,\ln\lvert z_{46}\rvert,\ln\lvert z_{56}\rvert$ (the fifteen $\ln\lvert z_{ij}\rvert$ modulo the six-dimensional gauge $\ell_{ij}\to\ell_{ij}+a_i+a_j$, which is the Möbius covariance and pairs to zero with the conservation relations). $\lvert z_j\rvert,\lvert1-z_j\rvert$ fix $z_j$ up to $z_j\leftrightarrow\bar z_j$ (two circles meet in a conjugate pair); if $\operatorname{Im}z_4\ne0$, the choice of $z_4$ fixes the global conjugation, and $\lvert z_4-z_5\rvert\ne\lvert z_4-\bar z_5\rvert$ (equality iff $\operatorname{Im}z_4=0$) selects $z_5$, likewise $z_6$; if all $z_j$ are real the point is conjugation-fixed and the fibre is one point. ## §5 Calibration on the fixed-halves slab model (C5)

\subsection*{Setup}

(4.3)–(4.4)), the paper's illustration (`06` l. 725–734): level-matched sector $m=0$, constant cutoff $\tau$ (074's $\sigma$), quintic neck regions slabs of widths $a,b$ (074's $\sigma_5,\sigma_5'$), Neumann branch. The interval of calibration is the convergence half-line of the tower, $h>0$; values at $h\le0$ are the continuation and are used only for the $h\to-\infty$ exponent of T4.

\subsection*{Derivation}
*(5.1) Closed form.* With $\hat f_x(h):=(e^{xh}-1)/h$ the transform of $\mathbf 1_{[-x,0]}$ and $\sum_{n\ge1}he^{-n\tau h}=h/(e^{\tau h}-1)$ ($h>0$),
$$
\hat\mu_{\mathcal C}(h)=\hat f_a(h)\,\hat f_b(h)\,\frac{h}{e^{\tau h}-1}=\frac{\hat f_a\hat f_b}{\hat f_\tau},\qquad
\mu_{\mathcal C}=\sum_{n\ge1}\big[\mathbf 1_{[n\tau-a-b,\,n\tau-a]}-\mathbf 1_{[n\tau-b,\,n\tau]}\big] .
\tag{5.1}
$$
*(5.2) Single slab, $a=\tau$.* The sum telescopes to $\mathbf 1_{[-b,0]}$ and $\hat\mu_{\mathcal C}=\hat f_b$ (074 (4.4)(a)): the transform of a positive measure, log-convex and exponentially convex on $\mathbb R$; T4's exponents are $\lim_{h\to+\infty}\tfrac1h\log\hat f_b=b=-\inf\operatorname{supp}$ and $\lim_{h\to-\infty}\tfrac1h\log\hat f_b=0=-\sup\operatorname{supp}$, saturating the bound by the known support $[-b,0]$. The ratio $\hat\mu_{\mathcal C}/\hat f_b=1$ restates (5.1): bookkeeping. *(5.3) Compact support, $a=k\tau$.* $\hat f_{k\tau}/\hat f_\tau=\sum_{j<k}e^{j\tau h}$, so $\hat\mu_{\mathcal C}=\hat f_b\sum_{j<k}e^{j\tau h}$, the transform of $\sum_{j<k}\mathbf 1_{[-b-j\tau,-j\tau]}\ge0$ (074 (4.4)(b)): a positive measure, hence **T3 and T3⁺ pass** — the verdict derived before the run. The model's second case is therefore *not* separated from the first by T3/T3⁺/T4 (it is separated by the support); what the tests separate is positive from signed weight. *The $b\lessgtr\tau$ split* (the critic's #20): the $k$ slabs $[-b-j\tau,-j\tau]$ overlap iff $b>\tau$, giving a density with values up to $\lceil b/\tau\rceil$; for $b\le\tau$ they are disjoint (touching at $b=\tau$) and the density is $\{0,1\}$-valued — a union of $k$ bounded plumbing regions in the final neck, i.e. a sub-region with the standard measure in the model's sense. The paper's sentence (`06` l. 734: "in the other two it is neither a union of Feynman regions nor a sub-region with the standard measure") therefore holds for the compact case only when $b>\tau$, or in a sense of "sub-region" it does not state; flagged for the rewrite (plan Task 5), not edited here. *(5.4) Generic.* $\log\hat\mu_{\mathcal C}=\log\hat f_a+\log\hat f_b-\log\hat f_\tau$ and $(\log\hat f_x)''(h)=\operatorname{Var}_h(S)$ for the tilted uniform law on an interval of length $x$, $=\tfrac1{h^2}-\tfrac{x^2}{4\sinh^2(xh/2)}=\tfrac{x^2}{12}-\tfrac{x^4h^2}{240}+O(h^4)$; hence
$$
(\log\hat\mu_{\mathcal C})''(h)=\frac1{h^2}-\frac{a^2}{4\sinh^2\frac{ah}2}-\frac{b^2}{4\sinh^2\frac{bh}2}+\frac{\tau^2}{4\sinh^2\frac{\tau h}2},\qquad
(\log\hat\mu_{\mathcal C})''(0^+)=\frac{a^2+b^2-\tau^2}{12} .
\tag{5.2}
$$
So log-convexity fails on an interval $(0,h_1)$ of the real axis whenever $a^2+b^2<\tau^2$ — e.g. 074's $(a,b,\tau)=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac34)$, where $(5.2)$ gives $-\tfrac1{192}$ — and the failing triples are explicit (small $x,y$). The reason is the signed weight of the tower (the negative slabs $-\mathbf 1_{[n\tau-b,n\tau]}$ that the positive ones do not cancel when no width is on the lattice $\tau\mathbb N$); the poles of (5.1) at $2\pi ik/\tau$ are a property of the closed form (the positive Dirac comb has the same poles and is log-convex) and are not cited as the reason. For $a^2+b^2\ge\tau^2$ the small-$h$ criterion is silent and the $2\times2$ test may pass on all of $(0,\infty)$ (it does numerically for $(0.7,0.7,0.75)$); but **T3⁺ fails for every generic pair**: by Widder's theorem an exponentially convex function on $(0,\infty)$ is the transform of a positive measure, unique by the uniqueness theorem for bilateral transforms convergent on an interval, and $\mu_{\mathcal C}$ — bounded, supported on $[\tau-a-b,\infty)$, with signed weight at arbitrarily large $S$ (074 (4.4)(c)) — is that measure; so some finite Hankel matrix is indefinite. How visible the failure is depends on the signed weight: for $(0.7,0.7,0.75)$ it is a sequence of adjacent $\pm1$ slabs of width $\lvert a-\tau\rvert=0.05$ (dipoles), and $4\times4$ matrices on $(0,20]$ do not resolve it at $10^{-10}$ relative; for $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac34)$ the $4\times4$ matrix at $h\in\{0.05,0.25,0.45,0.65\}$ has relative minimum eigenvalue $-6\times10^{-5}$ and the random search reaches $-2\times10^{-3}$. *(5.5) Break.* Flipping the sign of the $n=1$ term of the telescoping sum in the single-slab case gives $\mu_{\mathcal C}-2T_1=-\mathbf 1_{[-b,0]}+2\cdot\mathbf 1_{[\tau-b,\tau]}$, transform $\hat f_b(2e^{-\tau h}-1)$ with a real zero at $h=\ln2/\tau$; $\log\lvert\hat\mu\rvert$ is concave near a simple zero and the midpoint test fails there (flipping the whole measure would leave $\lvert\hat\mu\rvert$ unchanged and not fire).

\subsection*{Result}
In the model, T3/T3⁺/T4 separate the positive-measure cases (single slab; compact support) from the signed-density case (generic), and T3⁺ does so for every generic pair of widths while T3 alone does so only when $a^2+b^2<\tau^2$ or at points the $2\times2$ test happens to resolve; T4 reads the support's lower end $\tau-a-b$ in every case.
