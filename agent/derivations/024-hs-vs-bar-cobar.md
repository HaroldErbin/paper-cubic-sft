### 1.2 The dg Lie algebra and its MC equation, symmetric version, lengths $\le3$

$\Omega B_\infty(A)=\bigl(L(s^{-1}C),\,D=B+\Delta_c,\,[\cdot,\cdot]\bigr)$, $D^2=0$, MC equation for $\Phi\in L(s^{-1}C)_{-1}$:
$$D\Phi+\tfrac12[\Phi,\Phi]=0.\tag{1.2}$$
Write $\Phi=\Phi_1+\Phi_2+\dots$ by factor number and $\Phi_1=\sum_\ell s^{-1}\phi_{1,\ell}$, $\phi_{1,\ell}\in\mathrm{Sym}^\ell\mathcal H$ at $N_{\rm gh}=2\ell$. Since $B$ preserves $f$ while $\Delta_c$ and the bracket raise $f$ by one, (1.2) splits by factor number. For a **type-I ansatz** ($\Phi_f=0$, $f\ge2$) it closes exactly in two pieces — this is why canonical solutions exist:
$$\text{(a)}\ \ B\Phi_1=0\ (f=1),\qquad \text{(b)}\ \ \Delta_c\Phi_1+\Phi_1\otimes'\Phi_1=0\ (f=2),\qquad f\ge3:\ \text{trivially }0.\tag{1.3}$$

**(a) by length**, using $\bar B_k(x_1\wedge\dots\wedge x_\ell)=\sum_{\rm unsh}\pm\, b_k(x_{\sigma(1)},..,x_{\sigma(k)})\wedge x_{\sigma(k+1)}\wedge\dots$:
$$\begin{aligned}
L=1:&\quad b_1\phi_{1,1}+b_2\phi_{1,2}+b_3\phi_{1,3}+\dots=0,\\
L=2:&\quad \bar B_1\phi_{1,2}+\bar B_2\phi_{1,3}+\bar B_3\phi_{1,4}+\dots=0,\\
L=3:&\quad \bar B_1\phi_{1,3}+\bar B_2\phi_{1,4}+\dots=0 .
\end{aligned}\tag{1.4}$$
In project language: $b_k\phi_{1,k}=\ell_k$ contracted with the $k$-strand state $\sigma_k$ (up to the $s$-signs, trivial for even inputs); the $L\ge2$ rows apply $\ell_k$ to $k$ strands of $\sigma_{L+k-1}$ and leave $L-1$ strands untouched.

**(b) by total length $n$**. With $\deg\Phi_1=-1$ odd, $\tfrac12[\Phi_1,\Phi_1]=\Phi_1\otimes'\Phi_1$. On even inputs ($N_{\rm gh}$ even) the Koszul signs of $\bar\Delta_c$ are $+1$, and (b) reads, component $(i,n-i)$ in $s^{-1}\mathrm{Sym}^i\otimes' s^{-1}\mathrm{Sym}^{n-i}$:
$$\bar\Delta_c^{(i,n-i)}\phi_{1,n}=\phi_{1,i}\otimes\phi_{1,n-i},\qquad 1\le i\le n-1,\tag{1.5}$$
where $\bar\Delta_c^{(i,n-i)}:\mathrm{Sym}^n\to\mathrm{Sym}^i\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{n-i}$ is the $\binom ni$-term unshuffle. Because $\tau\Delta_c=-\Delta_c$ and the bracket is $\tau$-odd, the $(i,n-i)$ and $(n-i,i)$ components are one equation: **one equation per unordered split $\{i,n-i\}$**. Explicitly:
$$\begin{aligned}
n=2:&\quad \bar\Delta_c\phi_{1,2}=\phi_{1,1}\otimes\phi_{1,1}&&\text{(one split)},\\
n=3:&\quad \bar\Delta_c^{(2,1)}\phi_{1,3}=\phi_{1,2}\otimes\phi_{1,1}&&\text{(one split)},\\
n=4:&\quad \bar\Delta_c^{(3,1)}\phi_{1,4}=\phi_{1,3}\otimes\phi_{1,1},\quad \bar\Delta_c^{(2,2)}\phi_{1,4}=\phi_{1,2}\otimes\phi_{1,2}&&\text{(two splits)}.
\end{aligned}\tag{1.6}$$
Note $\bar\Delta_c^{(i,n-i)}$ is *injective* with image the totally symmetric tensors inside $\mathrm{Sym}^i\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{n-i}$, a strictly smaller space than the target; so (1.5) has more components than $\dim\mathrm{Sym}^n\mathcal H$ — it also constrains the right-hand side to be totally symmetric. This is used in §2.

**Canonical solution and normalisation.** The group-like element of $S^c$ is $e^{\wedge\varphi}=\sum_n\varphi^{\wedge n}/n!$, and $\bar B(e^{\wedge\varphi})=\bigl(\sum_k\tfrac1{k!}b_k(\varphi^{k})\bigr)\wedge e^{\wedge\varphi}$. Hence in the symmetric case the canonical solution is
$$\phi_{1,n}=\frac{\varphi^{\wedge n}}{n!}\quad(\text{not }\varphi^{\otimes n}\text{ as in the }A_\infty\text{ text}),\tag{1.7}$$
for which (1.5) holds for every split (since $\bar\Delta_c^{(i,n-i)}\varphi^{n}/n!=\varphi^i/i!\otimes\varphi^{n-i}/(n-i)!$), and (1.4) collapses to its $L=1$ row, the $L_\infty$ MC equation $\sum_k\frac1{k!}b_k(\varphi^k)=0$, the $L\ge2$ rows being $\text{MC}\wedge\varphi^{L-1}/(L-1)!$. **Their canonical sector** = type-II $=0$, type-I $=(1.7)$.

## §2 — Claim (1): the cotangent construction

### 2.1 The cotangent (doubled) action of a non-cyclic dg Lie algebra — general form

Let $(L,D,[\cdot,\cdot])$ be a dg Lie algebra in CMZ's conventions (fields at degree $-1$, $D$ of degree $-1$), with MC map $F(\Phi)=D\Phi+\tfrac12[\Phi,\Phi]$ of degree $-2$. The standard way to supply a cyclic structure is $L_{\rm dbl}=L\oplus L^\vee$, $L^\vee$ the (shifted) dual, with the canonical pairing $\langle\Lambda,X\rangle$ nonzero iff $\deg\Lambda+\deg X=-3$ (so that the cyclic structure has CMZ degree "three"), $D$ extended by its adjoint and the bracket by the coadjoint action. The doubled MC equation and action are
$$F_{\rm dbl}(\Phi,\Lambda)=\bigl(F(\Phi),\ D^\vee\Lambda+[\Phi,\Lambda]^\vee\bigr)=0,\qquad S_{\rm dbl}=\langle\Lambda,F(\Phi)\rangle=\langle\Lambda,D\Phi\rangle+\tfrac12\langle\Lambda,[\Phi,\Phi]\rangle.\tag{2.1}$$
Degrees: the multiplier $\Lambda$ is a classical field (degree $-1$ in $L_{\rm dbl}$) dual to the degree-$(-2)$ part of $L$, the part where $F(\Phi)$ lives. Euler–Lagrange: $\delta\Lambda$ gives $F(\Phi)=0$ — *all* of the original MC equations; $\delta\Phi$ gives the adjoint equations, homogeneous linear in $\Lambda$, solved by $\Lambda=0$. So the MC set of $L_{\rm dbl}$ at $\Lambda=0$ is that of $L$. **Applied to the whole cobar algebra**, (2.1) is a cubic action (each term $\le3$ fields, since $F$ is quadratic) whose EL equations contain CMZ's full quadratic equations (type-I and type-II, all splits, all lengths). This is elementary and answers their open question 1 in the cheapest way — at the price of doubling *everything*. It is not Route C. It is recorded here because it is the yardstick against which Route C must be measured, and because CMZ's two obstructions (§7 of the paper) concern a pairing *within* $L$, which the cotangent trick sidesteps by construction (5c, claim (4)).

### 2.2 Route C is not (2.1) for the type-I sector — term-by-term comparison

The "type-I sector" of the cobar algebra is $s^{-1}C$ with $B$; $\Delta_c$ and $[\cdot,\cdot]$ leave it. So the doubled action of the type-I sector *as a dg Lie algebra* would be $\langle\Lambda_1,B\Phi_1\rangle$: EL equations $B\Phi_1=0$ and $B^\vee\Lambda_1=0$ — it produces (1.4) and **no constraint** (1.5). Route C's constraints come from the factor-2 equation, which is not an endomorphism of type-I. Claim (1) as literally posed ("cotangent action of the type-I sector") therefore fails at the first term: **the constraint $\sigma_k=\sigma_{k-1}\wedge\Psi$ has no source in the type-I dg Lie algebra**; it is the $f=2$ component of the MC equation, projected as in 2.3.

Instead compare Route C with the *canonical-sector* system (1.3), equation by equation. Route C's action (normalisation (1.7), $c_k=1/k$ written explicitly in the constraint):
$$S_C=\tfrac12\omega_1(\Psi,Q\Psi)+\sum_{n\ge3}g_s^{n-2}\langle V_n|\sigma_n\rangle+\sum_{k\ge2}\langle\lambda_k,\ \sigma_k-\tfrac1k\,\sigma_{k-1}\wedge\Psi\rangle_{\omega_1^{\otimes k}},\qquad\sigma_1\equiv\Psi.\tag{2.2}$$
EL equations:
$$\begin{aligned}
(\lambda_k):&\quad \sigma_k=\tfrac1k\,\sigma_{k-1}\wedge\Psi\quad(k\ge2)\ \Rightarrow\ \sigma_k=\Psi^{\wedge k}/k!,\\
(\sigma_n):&\quad g_s^{n-2}\,\langle V_n|=\ -\langle\lambda_n,\cdot\rangle+\langle\lambda_{n+1},\ \cdot\wedge\Psi\rangle\quad(n\ge2;\ V_2\equiv0),\\
(\Psi):&\quad Q\Psi\ -\ \sum_{k\ge2}\lambda_k\,\lrcorner\,\sigma_{k-1}=0,
\end{aligned}\tag{2.3}$$
where $\lambda_k\lrcorner\sigma_{k-1}\in\mathcal H$ denotes $\omega_1^{\otimes(k-1)}$-contraction of $k-1$ strands of $\lambda_k$ with $\sigma_{k-1}$, the free strand converted to a vector by $\omega_1^{-1}$. Eliminating $\lambda$ from the $(\sigma_n)$ tower (finite truncation at $n=N$, top down) and inserting into $(\Psi)$ gives, with the constraints, $Q\Psi+\sum_{n\ge3}g_s^{n-2}\ell_{n-1}(\Psi^{n-1})/(n-1)!=0$ — the $L_\infty$ MC equation, i.e. row $L=1$ of (1.4). (This is R13's back-substitution statement, not re-derived; the toy check below tests it.)

**Why the solution sets nevertheless agree.** The chain of projected constraints $(\lambda_k)$ is triangular and forces $\sigma_k=\Psi^{\wedge k}/k!$, which is (1.7); (1.7) satisfies *every* split of (1.5) and makes rows $L\ge2$ of (1.4) consequences of row $L=1$ (§1.2). So on Route C's constraint surface all dropped equations hold identically, and the surviving $(\Psi)$ equation is row $L=1$. Conversely a canonical solution gives $(\sigma,\Psi)$ satisfying $(\lambda_k)$ and $(\Psi)$, and, for the tower truncated at a finite $n=N$ (as in (2.3) and the toy), the $(\sigma_n)$ tower then determines $\lambda$ uniquely (top down from $\lambda_N$, $\omega_1$ non-degenerate; an untruncated tower has a free top). Hence, for the $N$-truncated theory: $\{$EL solutions of $S_C\}/\{\lambda\}$ $\leftrightarrow$ $\{$CMZ canonical solutions truncated at length $N\}$, bijectively. The dropped equations are *redundant on the chain*, not independent.

**Structural reading.** Route C is the cotangent action (2.1) applied to the **universal** part of the canonical sector only — the *chain* subsystem $\{\text{split }(k-1,1),\ \mu\text{-projected}\}$ of $\Delta_c\Phi_1+\Phi_1\otimes'\Phi_1=0$, i.e. to the quadratic map $F_{\rm chain}(\Psi,\sigma)_k=\sigma_k-\sigma_{k-1}\wedge\Psi$ on $\bigoplus_{k\ge2}\mathrm{Sym}^k\mathcal H$ — while the **theory-dependent** operator $B$ is not doubled at all: its length-$n\to1$ action is kept as the cyclic $\Psi$-kinetic term (row $L=1$, $n=1$), and its length-$n\to2$ actions are closed with $\omega_1$ into the linear sources $\langle V_n|\sigma_n\rangle$. This mirrors CMZ's own split "universal quadratic term / theory in the linear term": in Route C the universal part is the multiplier constraint and the theory sits in a term *linear* in the auxiliary fields. The dual tower supplies the pairing exactly where CMZ lack one (between $\sigma_k$ at $N_{\rm gh}=2k$ and its dual), and nowhere else.

### 2.3 $N_{\rm gh}(\lambda_k)$ from the cotangent degree (R13's open item)

In (2.1) $\lambda_k$ is dual to the space where the constraint lives. Route C realises the dual of $\mathrm{Sym}^k\mathcal H$ through $\omega_1^{\otimes k}$ (R13's own choice of pairing), which saturates at $N_{\rm gh}$-sum $5k$. The constraint $\sigma_k-\sigma_{k-1}\wedge\Psi$ has $N_{\rm gh}=2k$, hence
$$\boxed{N_{\rm gh}(\lambda_k)=5k-2k=3k},\qquad\text{Grassmann parity of }\lambda_k=k\bmod2.\tag{2.4}$$

## §4 — Claim (3): gauge symmetry (5b)

**Literal task.** Their $\delta_\Lambda\Phi=D\Lambda+[\Phi,\Lambda]$ (cache line 2145, `infgaugbbb`) restricted to type-I $\Phi$ and parameters of length $\le2$; project onto the chain; compare with R11's composite law $\delta\Sigma_2=\delta\Psi\wedge\Psi$ and R26(5)'s homogeneous rotations; count parameters; gauge-fixing condition or obstruction. **Objective.** Whether their enlarged gauge symmetry is the theory of which the HS action is a gauge-fixed form (R18, T6-bis).

### 4.1 Their transformation restricted to type-I, length $\le2$

CMZ (cache 2145, 2453–2520): $\delta\Phi=D\Lambda+[\Phi,\Lambda]$, $D=B+\Delta_c$. For type-I $\Phi=\Phi_1$ and a factor-1 parameter $\Lambda=\Lambda_1=\sum_\ell s^{-1}\Lambda_{1,\ell}$ (their subclass $\Lambda_{f\ge2}=0$), filtering by factor number:
$$\delta\Phi_1=B\Lambda_1\quad(f=1),\qquad \delta\Phi_2=\Delta_c\Lambda_1+[\Phi_1,\Lambda_1]=:\Delta_{\Phi_1}\Lambda_1\quad(f=2).\tag{4.1}$$
The transformed field stays type-I (canonical) iff $\Delta_{\Phi_1}\Lambda_1=0$. Their result (2480–2500, $A_\infty$ text; symmetric version by $\otimes\to\wedge$, $\psi^n\to\varphi^{\wedge n}/n!$ as in (1.7)): a factor-1 element cannot be $\Delta_{\Phi_1}$-exact, and the closed ones form the class
$$\Lambda_{1,\ell}=\lambda\wedge\frac{\Psi^{\wedge(\ell-1)}}{(\ell-1)!},\qquad \lambda:=\Lambda_{1,1}\in\mathcal H,\ N_{\rm gh}(\lambda)=1 .\tag{4.2}$$
Ghost check (table §1.1): $N^{\rm tot}_{\rm gh}(\Lambda_{1,\ell})=1+2(\ell-1)=2\ell-1=2\ell-f$ with $f=1$ ✓. So within the canonical sector **the length-2 parameter is not independent**: $\Lambda_{1,2}=\lambda\wedge\Psi$ is fixed by $\lambda$. An *independent* $\Lambda_{1,2}'\in\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H$ at $N_{\rm gh}=3$ has $\Delta_{\Phi_1}\Lambda'_{1,2}=\Delta_c\Lambda'_{1,2}+[\Psi,\Lambda'_{1,2}]\ne0$ (the unshuffle is injective, §1.2): it generates type-II components and leaves the canonical sector.

### 4.2 Projection onto the chain vs R11

Insert (4.2) into $\delta\Phi_1=B\Lambda_1$, length by length ($\bar B_k$: $b_k$ on $k$ strands, $g_s^{k-1}$ weights):

Length 1: $\delta\Psi=\sum_k g_s^{k-1}b_k(\Lambda_{1,k})=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{g_s^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\ell_k(\lambda,\Psi^{k-1})=Q\lambda+g_s\ell_2(\Psi,\lambda)+\tfrac{g_s^2}2\ell_3(\Psi^2,\lambda)+\dots$ — R11's $L_\infty$ gauge law (their eq. at 2515, symmetric form). 

Length 2: $\delta\sigma_2=\sum_k g_s^{k-1}\bar B_k\Lambda_{1,k+1}$. In $\bar B_k(\lambda\wedge\Psi^k/k!)$ the $k$ strands hit by $b_k$ either include $\lambda$ (leaving one $\Psi$) or are all $\Psi$ (leaving $\lambda$):
$$\delta\sigma_2=\Bigl(\sum_k\frac{g_s^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\ell_k(\lambda,\Psi^{k-1})\Bigr)\wedge\Psi+\Bigl(\sum_k\frac{g_s^{k-1}}{k!}\ell_k(\Psi^k)\Bigr)\wedge\lambda=\boxed{\delta\Psi\wedge\Psi+\mathrm{MC}(\Psi)\wedge\lambda}.\tag{4.3}$$
Since $\sigma_2=\Psi^{\wedge2}/2$ has $\delta(\Psi^2/2)=\delta\Psi\wedge\Psi$, (4.3) is R11's composite law **plus an EOM-proportional term** $\mathrm{MC}(\Psi)\wedge\lambda$, $\mathrm{MC}=Q\Psi+\sum g_s^{k-1}\ell_k(\Psi^k)/k!$. Consistently, $\delta X=\delta(\sigma_2-\Psi^2/2)=\mathrm{MC}\wedge\lambda$: their transformation preserves the chain **on shell only** — as it must, being a symmetry of the equations, not of an action; R11's law is its off-shell completion (the difference is an EOM-trivial symmetry, R11's "residual freedom"). Same pattern at length $\ell$: $\delta\sigma_\ell=\delta\Psi\wedge\Psi^{\ell-1}/(\ell-1)!+\mathrm{MC}\wedge\lambda\wedge\Psi^{\ell-2}/(\ell-2)!$. Toy: script part 3, G1 (length 1), G2 ((4.3) off-shell, both terms), G3 (break: without the MC term (4.3) fails off-shell by $|\mathrm{MC}\wedge\lambda|$).

R26(5)'s homogeneous kernel rotations $\delta Y_k=-\tfrac12\hat H_k^{-1}(\delta\hat H_k)Y_k$ are of the *same* type: proportional to $Y_k$, i.e. to the deviation from the chain, hence vanishing on the saddle; they are the HS-action counterpart of CMZ's freedom to move within the class (4.2) at fixed $\lambda$ (here: no freedom, the class is a point) plus EOM-trivial pieces. ### 4.3 Parameter count and gauge fixing

- **Full CMZ theory**: parameters $\Lambda\in L(s^{-1}C)_0$: one for each $(f,\ell)$, $N^{\rm tot}_{\rm gh}=2\ell-f$ — infinitely many multi-string parameters; $(1,2)$ is the $N_{\rm gh}=3$ element of $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H$, i.e. exactly R18(3)'s Stückelberg parameter $B$ / T6-bis's $\Lambda_1$ ($\delta\Sigma\ni\hat Q\Lambda_{1,2}$ from $\bar B_1\Lambda_{1,2}$, plus $\delta\Psi\ni g_s\ell_2(\Lambda_{1,2})$ from $b_2\Lambda_{1,2}$, plus type-II generation).

## §5 — Claim (4): the two action obstructions (5c)

**Literal task.** For each of CMZ's two obstructions to an action (Discussion, open question 1, cache lines 2708–2716): state it, translate it into the project's ghost-number grading, say by which structure Route C and the HS action evade it, and check honestly whether either obstruction bites somewhere in the project (Route C cyclicity, R7's shifted pairing, R26's tower). **Objective.** Row (4) of §0; the positioning paragraph must not claim more than this section establishes.

### 5.1 Obstruction 1 — no nondegenerate degree-3 pairing on the cobar space

**Their statement** (cache 2708–2710). "The bar-cobar construction has a state space that does not allow for a nondegenerate, degree three bilinear form. This is the case, for example, in the scalar field theory example, where the bar-cobar vector space $T(s^{-1}T^c(V))$ has vectors of all negative degrees … Since there are no vectors of zero and higher degrees, a nondegenerate bilinear form cannot exist in that complex." A cyclic structure is for them a nondegenerate form $\omega:A\otimes A\to\mathbb R$ of degree $3$ with $S=\sum\frac1{n+1}\omega(m_n(\varphi^n),\varphi)$; in the cobar space the field $\Phi$ has degree $-1$ and the equation $\mathcal E=D\Phi+\Phi\otimes'\Phi$ degree $-2$, so the form needed is $\langle\Phi,\mathcal E\rangle$ pairing degrees $-1$ and $-2$ (their "degree three" up to the sign convention of homological vs cohomological degree; in this record: degree $-3$, i.e. $\deg x+\deg y=-3$).

**Translation to the project's grading.** A type-I element of length $\ell$ and total ghost number $N$ has cobar degree $\deg=2\ell-1-N$ (§1.1: $\deg_V=2-N_{\rm gh}$ per strand, $-1$ for $s^{-1}$). The pairing the theory supplies on the length-$\ell$ component is $\omega_1^{\otimes\ell}$, saturating at $N$-sum $5\ell$, hence of cobar degree
$$\deg\bigl(\omega_1^{\otimes\ell}\bigr)=2(2\ell-1)-5\ell=-\ell-2 ,\tag{5.1}$$
which equals $-3$ only at $\ell=1$. In the string case the degree argument of their scalar example does not apply literally ($\mathcal H$ carries all ghost numbers, so every cobar degree is populated at every length), but the sharper form of the obstruction survives: *the natural length-diagonal form has the wrong degree at every $\ell\ge2$*, and the mismatch $\ell-1$ is exactly the shift of R7 ($\omega_2^{\rm symp}=\omega_1^{\otimes2}\circ(s\otimes s)$ on $(\mathcal H^{\otimes2})[1]$, one unit at $\ell=2$). For even $\ell$ the mismatch is also a parity mismatch — $\omega_1^{\otimes\ell}$ is Grassmann-even, an odd-degree form must be odd — which is R7's "graded symmetric, not an odd symplectic form". So obstruction 1, in the project's words: **a $\langle\Phi,\mathcal E\rangle$-type action on the type-I space would need a length-dependent shift of $\omega_1^{\otimes\ell}$, and the natural length-diagonal forms $\omega_1^{\otimes\ell}$ have length-dependent degree** (a statement about these forms, not a proof that no other form exists).

**How Route C evades it.** Route C does not put a form on the type-I space. It pairs the length-$\ell$ *equation* (the factor-2 constraint $\sigma_\ell-\sigma_{\ell-1}\wedge\Psi/\ell$, degree $-2$ in CMZ's counting, ghost number $2\ell$) with a *new* field $\lambda_\ell$ living in the dual, through $\omega_1^{\otimes\ell}$ used as the duality pairing — with whatever degree (5.1) it has. Saturation fixes $N_{\rm gh}(\lambda_\ell)=3\ell$ (2.4) and Grassmann parity $\ell$, so that each term $\langle\lambda_\ell,\cdot\rangle$ is even, as an action term must be. The multipliers are not elements of the cobar space ($\deg\lambda_\ell=-\ell-1\ne-1$, §2.3); the cotangent construction asks nothing of the degree of the pairing, only that it be nondegenerate — and $\omega_1^{\otimes\ell}$ is (R7: $\det\omega_2=(\det\omega_1)^{2\dim\mathcal H}$; in general because $\omega_1$ is). The single form of degree $-3$ that Route C does use is $\omega_1$ itself, on the one field that is *not* doubled, $\Psi$. What is *not* claimed: a BV-level statement. The total pairing $\omega_1\oplus\bigoplus_\ell\bigl(\omega_1^{\otimes\ell}$ between $\sigma_\ell,\lambda_\ell\bigr)$ has sector-dependent degree, so whether Route C is the classical part of a BV action with one odd symplectic form of uniform degree is R18 ST5, open and untouched here.

**How the HS action evades it.** Differently, and more economically: the HS action needs no odd pairing at all on the auxiliary sector, because $\Sigma_2$ is an auxiliary field without kinetic operator. Its quadratic term $-\tfrac{g_s^2}{4!}V_4(\Sigma_2,\Sigma_2)$ is a Grassmann-*even*, *symmetric* form of ghost-number saturation $4+4=8=2\cdot4$ (cobar degree $2\cdot3-8=-2$), supplied by the theory's own quartic vertex; nondegenerate on $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H_0/\ker V_4$ (R13's quotient). Identity (3.2) is the one-line bridge: the composite multiplier $\lambda_2=-\tfrac{g_s^2}3\,\omega_1^{-\otimes2}V_4(\sigma_2,\cdot)$ converts Route C's duality pairing into the HS mass term. At level $k$ the same holds with $V_{2k}$ (R26's diagonal blocks $\hat M_k$) — see §5.3(c) for the degeneracy question.

### 5.2 Obstruction 2 — cyclicity and self-adjointness across factor numbers

**Their statement** (cache 2712–2714). Even granting a form coupling degree $-2$ to degree $-1$ states, "if the bilinear form was diagonal in factor number and length, cyclicity seems unlikely to hold: $\langle\Phi_2,\Phi_1\otimes'\Phi_1\rangle$ could be nonzero, but the cyclically permuted $\langle\Phi_1,\Phi_2\otimes'\Phi_1\rangle$ would be zero. Nor would it seem possible to have a self-adjoint $\Delta$ operator as $\langle\Phi_f,\Delta\Phi_{f-1}\rangle$ could be nonzero, but … $\langle\Delta\Phi_f,\Phi_{f-1}\rangle$ … is zero."

**Translation.** $\Delta$ and $\otimes'$ raise the factor number by one; the equation $\mathcal E_f$ at factor $f$ involves fields at factors $f-1$ and $f$ but not $f+1$, so the "Jacobian" of the equation map is block-triangular in factor number and cannot be self-adjoint for any factor-diagonal form. This is the *Helmholtz condition* of the inverse variational problem: a system $\mathcal E(x)=0$ derives from an action through a constant nondegenerate form $\Omega$ iff $\Omega\,\partial\mathcal E/\partial x$ is symmetric. In the canonical sector the statement is already visible at the lowest level: the pair $\bigl(Q\Psi+g_s\ell_2(\sigma_2)+\dots,\ \sigma_2-\Psi\wedge\Psi/2\bigr)$ has Jacobian $\begin{pmatrix}Q&g_s\ell_2\\-\Psi\wedge\cdot&1\end{pmatrix}$ whose two off-diagonal blocks are unrelated ($g_s\ell_2$ is $\Psi$-independent, $-\Psi\wedge\cdot$ is not), so no constant $\Omega$ symmetrises it (script part 4, Q2: the Helmholtz nullspace on the toy contains only degenerate forms).

**How Route C evades it.** By changing the *system*, not by finding a form for theirs: the cotangent doubling adds the multiplier equations and makes every constraint the $\lambda$-gradient of a term $\langle\lambda_\ell,\mathcal E_\ell\rangle$; the doubled system is a gradient by construction and its Jacobian is self-adjoint for the cotangent form $\Omega_{\rm cot}=\omega_1\oplus\bigoplus_\ell\begin{pmatrix}0&\omega_1^{\otimes\ell}\\ \omega_1^{\otimes\ell}&0\end{pmatrix}$ (script Q1, on the *hand-written* EL system (2.3), not on `action_C`). The form is off-diagonal between $\sigma_\ell$ and $\lambda_\ell$, which is exactly what the block-triangularity requires: the factor-2 equation is paired with a field that is not in the type-I space. There are no type-II fields, so no cross-factor cyclicity is ever needed. Their $\otimes'$ product survives only as the constraint's bilinear term $\sigma_{\ell-1}\wedge\Psi$ — symmetric by construction of $\mathrm{Sym}^\ell$, which is why the vertices $\langle V_n|\sigma_n\rangle$ are cyclic with no further condition.

**How the HS action evades it.** Same mechanism, form changed: the pair $(\Psi,\Sigma_2)$ with $S_{\rm HS}$'s gradients is self-adjoint for $\omega_1\oplus\omega_1^{\otimes2}$ (script Q3), and the block-triangularity is broken because the $\Psi$-equation now contains $V_4(\Sigma_2,\Psi,\cdot)$ — $\Psi$-dependent — and the $\Sigma_2$-equation $V_4(\Sigma_2-\Psi^2/2,\cdot)$: the two off-diagonal blocks are both $g_s^2V_4(\cdot,\Psi,\cdot)$ up to the factor forced in (3.2). The price, as in §3, is that the $\Sigma_2$-equation is the chain only modulo $\ker V_4$.

### 5.3 Where an obstruction could bite in the project

**(a) Cyclicity of Route C with the multipliers.** Not a hollow question, because cyclicity of an *action* is automatic — the test must be on the *equations*. Script part 4 takes the hand-written Euler–Lagrange system of (3.1) (with $V_3$, $k\le2$, $\sigma_3=\sigma_2\wedge\Psi/3$; lowered gradients $E_\Psi=M\Psi+\tfrac g3V_3(\sigma_2,\cdot)-\Lambda\cdot\Psi$, $E_\sigma=\tfrac g3V_3(\cdot,\cdot,\Psi)+\Lambda$, $E_\lambda=\omega^{\otimes2}(\sigma_2-\Psi^2/2)$, $\Lambda=\omega^{\otimes2}\lambda_2$) and checks that its Jacobian is symmetric, i.e. that the raised system is $\Omega_{\rm cot}$-self-adjoint (Q1, $10^{-8}$), that the hand system is the gradient of (3.1) (Q1b, independent path), and that removing the multiplier back-reaction $-\Lambda\cdot\Psi$ from the $\Psi$-row — which is what CMZ's undoubled $\Psi$-row looks like — destroys the symmetry (Q1c, asymmetry $3.6$). **Verdict: does not bite.** The back-reaction term is where the cotangent form earns its keep: it is the transpose of the $\Psi$-dependence of the constraint.

The stronger, falsifiable form of CMZ's obstruction 2 (Q2): for the *undoubled* canonical system $(Q\Psi+g\ell_2(\sigma_2),\ \sigma_2-\Psi^2/2)$ on $(\Psi,\sigma_2)$, the Helmholtz nullspace $\{\Omega:\ \Omega J(x)\ \text{symmetric at 3 random }x\}$ is computed by SVD; it is computed on the full tensor space $\mathcal H\oplus\mathcal H\otimes\mathcal H$ ($N=d+d^2$), not on $\mathcal H\oplus\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal H$; the antisymmetric block decouples with unit Jacobian and admits the identity form, so the degeneracy conclusion transfers to $\mathrm{Sym}^2\oplus\mathcal H$. It is large ($29$-dimensional for $d=3$, $92$ for $d=4$ — the constraint's $\sigma$-block is the identity, which leaves the $\sigma\sigma$ block of $\Omega$ nearly free) but **every element is degenerate** (max rank $10<12$, $17<20$ over random combinations). So their canonical equations are not Euler–Lagrange for *any* constant nondegenerate form on the type-I fields, not only for a factor-diagonal one: obstruction 2 is realised on the toy, in sharpened form. Route C escapes it by enlarging the field space; nothing weaker would do with a constant form.

**(b) R7's shifted pairing (presentation (b)).** Here obstruction 1 **does bite, and is already in the ledger.** R7 says that $\omega_1^{\otimes2}$ is graded symmetric and even, hence not an odd symplectic form on $\mathcal H^{\otimes2}$, and that the symplectic partner requires the shift $(\mathcal H^{\otimes2})[1]$; the 2026-09-14 annotation (026 §5.5) adds that no degree-diagonal pairing makes the brace *products* on the multi-string space cyclic. By (5.1) these are the same fact as CMZ's obstruction 1: a $\langle\Phi,\mathcal E\rangle$-type (BV, odd-symplectic) action on the type-I tower would need a shift $\ell-1$ at length $\ell$, and no single form is supplied. Consequence for the project, stated carefully: the *HS action* is not affected (it uses the even mass form $V_4$, §5.1, and a *classical* action needs no odd form on auxiliaries), Route C is not affected (cotangent), but the *BV formulation on the type-I space with the physical grading* — presentation (b), R18 ST5 — inherits exactly their obstruction. This is a confirmation of the ledger's caution, not a new gap in R26; but the positioning paragraph must not say "the construction supplies the cyclic structure they lack" without the qualifier "at the classical level, for the auxiliaries as mass terms".

**(c) The all-orders tower (R26).** Level $k$'s quadratic block is $a_k\hat M_k$, $\hat M_k=\tilde M_k-\tfrac g{2k+1}T_{2k+1}(\cdot,\cdot,\Psi)$, an even symmetric form on $\mathrm{Sym}^k\mathcal H$ built from $V_{2k}$ (and, dressed, $V_{2k+1}$); the Hessian in the whole tower is block-tridiagonal and symmetric because it is a Hessian — obstruction 2 cannot arise (no type-II fields, no factor number to cross; the off-diagonal blocks $L_k(\Psi)$ and $L_k^\top$ are transposes by construction, the same mechanism as Q1). Whether the $O(g)$ dressing $T_{2k+1}(\cdot,\cdot,\Psi)$ can create a $\Psi$-dependent kernel is a pole question (the $g_{\rm pole}$ of T2), not a CMZ-type structural obstruction. **Verdict: does not bite; nothing of theirs is needed across factor numbers.**

## §6 — The scalar example ($\phi^3+\phi^4$, CMZ §7)

**Their construction** (cache 2521–2677). $S=\int\bigl(\tfrac12\phi(\partial^2-m^2)\phi+\tfrac g3\phi^3+\tfrac\lambda4\phi^4\bigr)$, EOM $(\partial^2-m^2)\phi+g\phi^2+\lambda\phi^3=0$; $A_\infty$ data $V=V_0\oplus V_{-1}$ (one $c(x)$, one $d(x)$ per point), $b_1=\partial^2-m^2$, $b_2=g\,\delta$, $b_3=\lambda\,\delta\delta$, no gauge symmetry (no degree-$1$ space).
